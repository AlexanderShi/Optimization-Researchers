### S045 · A stochastic inexact sequential quadratic optimization algorithm for nonlinear equality-constrained optimization (INFORMS Journal on Optimization 2024, DOI 10.1287/ijoo.2022.0008)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: D. P. Robinson, B. Zhou · **Position**: inexact-subproblem extension of stochastic SQP (S007, 2021); vol. 6, no. 3–4, pp. 155–239 (homepage). Possibly the journal version of S037 (arXiv 2107.03512, same authors, different title), which works.json keeps separate; not checked here.
- **D1 problem/entry**: equality-constrained problems where constraint values and derivatives are exact but the objective and its derivatives are available only as stochastic approximations; the entry is large scale, where subproblem matrices are "too large to form and/or factorize" (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: an SQP method that uses only stochastic objective-gradient estimates under weak assumptions (no objective function values or estimates of them) and allows inexact subproblem solutions, with inexactness conditions tied to the stochastic gradients (abstract).
- **D3 assumptions**: exact constraints and constraint derivatives; stochastic objective gradients under "a relatively weak set of assumptions" (abstract); details not read.
- **D5 experiments**: against a stochastic subgradient method ("vastly outperforms") and against an SQP method that solves subproblems highly accurately in every iteration ("can outperform") (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: algorithm + convergence results + experiments (abstract).
**Method links**: Method 1 — evidence: subproblem termination conditions derived from what the outer stochastic method needs; SKILL.md already cites this DOI for the stochastic case (abstract). Method 5 — evidence: the rival differs only in subproblem accuracy, as in Method 1 step 4 (abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "Conditions are imposed on the inexact subproblem solutions that account for the fact that only stochastic objective gradient estimates are employed."
**Transferable**: when making an inner solve inexact in a stochastic method, benchmark against the same outer method with highly accurate inner solves, so the comparison isolates what inexactness costs or saves (abstract).

### S055 · Fair machine learning through constrained stochastic optimization and an ε-constraint method (Optimization Letters 2024, DOI 10.1007/s11590-023-02024-6)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: S. Liu, D. P. Robinson · **Position**: first of his fair-ML papers (later S069/S072, S083, S089); carries the stochastic constrained machinery of S007/S045 over to fairness; vol. 18, pp. 1975–1991 (homepage).
- **D1 problem/entry**: fair supervised learning, posed as minimizing loss subject to a prescribed bound on an unfairness measure such as disparate impact (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: embed the constrained problem in an ε-constraint method for multiobjective optimization to trace a loss–unfairness Pareto front; solve each problem with a stochastic algorithm designed to scale to large data (abstract).
- **D5 experiments**: recidivism and income prediction problems (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: modelling strategy + scalable algorithm; the claim is hedged: experiments "provide evidence that the strategy can be effective" (abstract).
**Method links**: Method 2 — variant ⚠: fairness enters as a hard constraint with a prescribed bound, swept by ε, not as a penalty weight inside the objective; consistent with the post-2024 turn away from penalties noted in SKILL.md (Inner Tensions 3) (abstract). New-pattern candidate: "constraint, not regularizer, for ML desiderata" (S055; weakly S072; S084 by title).
**Quote** (abstract): "It involves formulating an optimization problem to minimize loss subject to a prescribed bound on a measure of unfairness (e.g., disparate impact)."
**Transferable**: to show the trade-off between a training loss and a secondary criterion, make the criterion a constraint and sweep its bound (ε-constraint) to draw a Pareto front, rather than reporting a few penalty weights (abstract).

### S072 · A Bilevel Optimization Approach for Computing Synthetic Data to Mitigate Unfairness in Collaborative Machine Learning (INFORMS Journal on Optimization 2026, DOI 10.1287/ijoo.2025.0091)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: C.-Y. Wu, D. P. Robinson · **Position**: fair collaborative / federated ML; pp. 248–265 (Crossref). The homepage lists a preprint by the same authors with a different title, S069 (arXiv 2409.09532); whether it is the same work is unconfirmed.
- **D1 problem/entry**: fairness of a global model that multiple clients train together in collaborative ML (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: each client computes a synthetic data set by solving a bilevel problem so that the global model is fair. Strategy 1 sends the synthetic sets (optionally differentially private) to the server in a single round; strategy 2 runs federated averaging with local updates computed on the synthetic sets (abstract).
- **D5 experiments**: "Empirical results demonstrate that our methods are effective in reducing unfairness" (abstract); data sets and baselines not read.
- **D8 contribution**: two complementary strategies, one-shot and many-shot (abstract).
**Method links**: none to Methods 1–5 from the abstract. New-pattern candidate "constraint, not regularizer, for ML desiderata": weak; fairness is placed in the clients' bilevel synthetic-data problem, not in the server's aggregation (abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "Our two approaches (one-shot versus many-shot) are complementary to each other."
