### S006 · An inexact SQP method for equality constrained optimization (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2008, 10.1137/060674004)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–19; reference list p. 19 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: R. H. Byrd, J. Nocedal · **Position in trajectory**: Curtis at Northwestern IEMS (p. 1 affiliation); the founding paper of the SMART-test line that S025 extends (S025 p. 3) and whose proof S032 borrows (S032 p. 12).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Equality-constrained NLP at very large scale (PDE constraints) where "the exact computation of steps in contemporary methods can be prohibitively expensive" (p. 1). Entry is a gap in theory, not a new model: inexact SQP/composite-step and full-space line-search methods existed (Biros–Ghattas, Haber–Ascher, Prudencio–Byrd–Cai), but "Unlike these papers, however, we present conditions that guarantee the global convergence of inexact SQP steps" (p. 2). The question is to "determine the accuracy with which the SQP subproblems must be solved in order to ensure global convergence" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Replace the inexact-Newton forcing test on the whole KKT residual by step-acceptance tests tied to the merit model: the Model Reduction Condition Δm_k ≥ σπ_k max{‖c_k‖, ‖r_k‖−‖c_k‖} (3.2, p. 5) plus Termination Test I (model reduction for the old π and a loose residual bound, pp. 5–6) or Termination Test II (linearized-constraint progress ‖r_k‖ ≤ ε‖c_k‖ and dual residual ‖ρ_k‖ ≤ β‖c_k‖, after which π is raised to π_trial, p. 6). Primal and dual residuals are separate quantities, so one may grow if the other shrinks a lot (p. 2). Penalty increases are allowed only under Test II (pp. 5–6). Unchanged: line-search SQP with an exact penalty and Armijo backtracking (p. 4). Named "SMART tests" (sufficient merit function approximation reduction termination tests, p. 6); Algorithm A pp. 6–7.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumptions 4.1 (p. 8): iterates in a convex set with bounded f, c and derivatives; bounded multipliers (b); full-row-rank Jacobians with singular values bounded away from zero (c); bounded W_k; W_k uniformly positive definite on null(A_k) (e). Authors flag (b) and (c) as strong, adopted "to simplify the analysis in order to focus on the issues related to inexactness" (p. 8); Section 6 says Jacobian singularities and nonconvex W_k were left out to keep "the benign context of Assumptions 4.1" (p. 18), and gives a residual safeguard that would make (b) hold (p. 17).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) Lemma 3.1, a computable upper bound on the directional derivative Dφ(d;π) ≤ gᵀd − π(‖c‖−‖r‖), then used as the surrogate D̃φ in the Armijo test (3.9)–(3.10) (pp. 7–8); (ii) analysis-only normal/tangential decomposition d = u + v (p. 9); (iii) index split K1 = {‖u‖² ≥ γ4‖v‖²}, K2 = complement, and one measure Θ_k bounding both ‖d_k‖² and −D̃φ (Lemmas 4.4–4.6, pp. 10–11); (iv) penalty boundedness from the Test II dual-residual bound ‖ρ_k‖ ≤ β‖c_k‖ (Lemma 4.7, pp. 11–12); (v) steplength bounded away from zero (Lemma 4.8, pp. 12–13), summability ⇒ ‖c_k‖, ‖d_k‖ → 0 (Lemma 4.9, p. 13), then a contraction argument on the dual residual (Theorem 4.10, pp. 13–14). Global first-order convergence only; local rate discussed informally via a forcing sequence κ_k (p. 18).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Matlab, unpreconditioned GMRES (Kelley's code), exact Hessian plus identity shift when needed; 44 equality-constrained CUTEr/COPS problems with AMPL models and fewer than 10,000 variables (pp. 16–17). Baseline "ires" is identical except that GMRES stops on the full-residual test (3.3), run at κ = 2⁻¹ … 2⁻¹⁰; success 45–86 % against 100 % for isqp (Table 5.2, p. 17). Failure tests deliberately naive (p. 16). Cost of robustness measured with an isqp-ires hybrid: on the 35 problems both solve, 0.5 extra GMRES iterations in total on average, and the 9 problems ires missed are solved (p. 18). Only success rates; no iteration counts, timing or preconditioning ("this was not an issue as many of the problems are relatively small", p. 17). Efficiency on realistic applications deferred to a separate study (p. 15).
- **D6 Positioning**: Extends inexact Newton for equations (Dembo–Eisenstat–Steihaug), arguing its whole-residual merit is inadequate for nonconvex constrained problems (pp. 2, 4); distinguishes itself from Heinkenschloss–Vicente's similar model condition (composite step, trust region, reduced Hessian, p. 5); supplies the guarantee missing from PDE full-space practice (p. 2). Penalty update borrowed from Waltz et al. (KNITRO), which "has proved to work well in practice" (p. 7).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Purpose sentence early (p. 2); novelty located explicitly ("The novelty of our approach, i.e., the precise definition of what constitutes an acceptable step", p. 4); acronym coined for the device (SMART, p. 6). Candid about tuning: (κ, ε, β) "should be tuned for a specific application" and the scale dependence of β "is not ideal" (p. 7). Hedges with "we claim" (pp. 6, 15). Each test is followed by a sentence on which steps it admits (pp. 5–6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic + theoretical (global convergence of inexact line-search SQP), with an illustrative implementation. Limits stated specifically: strong assumptions 4.1(b),(c) (p. 8); possibly slow rate unless the forcing sequence is tightened (pp. 17–18); no preconditioner (p. 17); Jacobian singularity and nonconvexity as future work (p. 18). The PDE study is cited "in preparation" (Curtis–Haber, ref. [7], p. 19).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: SMART tests derive the iterative solver's stopping rule from the merit model and test primal and dual residuals separately (pp. 2, 5–7).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: baseline differs only in the stopping rule, swept over ten tolerances (pp. 16–17); naive failure tests "to aggressively challenge the robustness" (p. 16).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: penalty increase gated by which termination test passed (p. 6), an early form of the update-as-design-object, but the update formula is KNITRO's, not new (p. 7).
- Heuristic 2 evidence (not a Method): the price of robustness is measured against the naive baseline (isqp-ires, p. 18).
- New-pattern candidate: "Θ_k proof skeleton" (normal/tangential split + K1/K2 + Θ_k), reused in S032 and S025.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To make an inexact subproblem solve globally convergent, write the acceptance test as "sufficient decrease in the merit model + loose residual bound", not "small residual" ((3.2)–(3.5), pp. 5–6).
- Replace an exact directional derivative by a computable upper bound from the residual (Lemma 3.1, (3.9)–(3.10), pp. 7–8) and run the line search on it.
- Build the baseline as your own code with one component swapped, sweep its tolerance, and report the whole sweep (Table 5.2, p. 17).
- Measure the cost of robustness with a hybrid that satisfies both the naive and the new tests, and count the extra inner iterations (p. 18).

**Quotes**:
- "Our approach, on the other hand, is based on a requirement that the step decreases a local approximation of a merit function while also satisfying bounds on the primal and dual components of the residual." (p. 2)
- "Indeed, the extra cost is minimal with respect to the added robustness." (p. 18)

### S032 · Flexible penalty functions for nonlinear constrained optimization (IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 2008, 10.1093/imanum/drn003)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–21; references pp. 20–21 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. Nocedal · **Position in trajectory**: Curtis at Northwestern IEMS (p. 1); dedicated to M. J. D. Powell's 70th birthday (p. 1); the first paper of the batch in which the penalty-parameter update rule is the whole contribution.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Step-acceptance mechanisms (globalization) for constrained optimization, framed on equality constraints (p. 1). Entry is a comparison of two popular tools in the ‖c‖–f plane: a penalty function with monotonically increasing π "blocks" steps that temporarily increase infeasibility (Fig. 3, pp. 3–4); a filter can block feasibility-improving steps through historical entries, in the extreme needing restoration (Fig. 4, p. 4). The authors' "view is that the main weakness of penalty-based strategies is the blocking effect" (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Accept a step if it gives sufficient decrease in φ_π = f + π‖c‖ for at least one π in an interval [π_l, π_u] (2.1, p. 6): π_l = π_u recovers a fixed penalty; π_l ≈ 0 with π_u large gives a one-element filter (p. 6). No filter history is stored (p. 7). Two different update sources: π_u from the model quantity χ_k, raised only when the model predicts that a feasibility move costs objective ((3.8)–(3.9), p. 9); π_l from actual reductions, raised by a damped step 0.1(ν − π_l) only when the accepted step landed in region II ((3.10)–(3.11), pp. 10–11). π_m = max{π_l, χ_k} sets the directional-derivative term so as many steplengths as possible are accepted (3.14, p. 11). The line search only needs to test π_l and π_u (pp. 11–12). Algorithm 3.1, p. 11. Unchanged: line-search SQP step from the KKT system (3.4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 3.2 (p. 12): iterates in a convex set with bounded f, c and derivatives; full-row-rank Jacobians with singular values bounded below (flagged as "strong", used "to simplify the analysis in order to focus on" the flexible penalty, p. 12); bounded W_k; W_k uniformly positive definite on null(A_k). Exact SQP steps. Inequalities only sketched (Section 5, p. 19).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Explicitly "following the approach taken in Byrd et al. (2008)" (p. 12): normal/tangential decomposition, K1/K2 split and Θ_k (Lemmas 3.4–3.7, pp. 13–14). New pieces: π_u bounded and eventually constant (Lemma 3.8, pp. 14–15); π_l can then increase only finitely often, since each increase is at least ε_l or hits π_u (Corollary 3.9, p. 15); steplength bounded below using π_m ≤ π_u (Lemma 3.10, pp. 15–16); the eventual value π* of π_l serves as a fixed merit function for the summation argument (Theorem 3.11, pp. 16–17). Global first-order convergence only.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Implemented in KNITRO 5.0 (KNITRO-Direct); 85 equality-constrained CUTEr/COPS problems with AMPL models (p. 17). Trust-region fallback disabled and W_k modified so the code is a pure line search (p. 17). Baseline "pi default" = same code with (3.10) replaced by π_l ← π_u; four flexible variants with initial π_u of 1, 10, 100, 1000 (p. 17; Table 1). Measures: iterations and function evaluations, argued appropriate because the strategy "incurs little computational cost" (p. 17); logarithmic performance profiles (Figs 8–9, p. 18). Result: slightly fewer iterations and "considerable" savings in function evaluations, explained by fewer backtracks and more unit steps; initial π_u inconsequential (p. 18). No CPU time, no failure list, no infeasible or degenerate problems.
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Han (1977) and Powell (1978) penalty methods and KNITRO's update (Waltz et al. 2006, p. 5); relates the acceptance region to Chin–Fletcher's "slanting envelope" and Li (2006), and distinguishes itself by storing no history (pp. 6–7). Dismisses non-monotone penalty updates as relying "on ad hoc heuristics" (p. 3). The critique of filters is geometric and even-handed: "Penalty functions and filter mechanisms both have their own advantages and disadvantages" (p. 3). Thanks Leyffer, Toint and Wächter for a discussion of filters (p. 20).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Figure-driven argument: seven pictures of acceptance regions precede the algorithm (Figs 1–7, pp. 2–10). Rationale given for each constant (the 0.1 damping is there so that π_l increases only gradually, p. 11). Results sentence short and modest: "The results are encouraging." (p. 17). Extensions to interior-point and trust-region methods offered as claims with an explicit caveat that safeguards are needed (pp. 19–20).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic globalization device with a global convergence proof and a production-code test. Limits: equality constraints only in the analysis; IPM and trust-region versions only sketched, "various algorithmic components must be added" (p. 20); exact steps; small benchmark measured by iterations and evaluations only.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: the contribution is the update rule for penalty parameters, with π_u driven by predicted (model) progress and π_l by actual progress (pp. 9–11); one mechanism behaving filter-like early and penalty-like late (p. 5, p. 19).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the rival lives in the same KNITRO-Direct code with one rule swapped ((3.10) → π_l ← π_u, p. 17); fallback paths turned off so only the globalization differs (p. 17).
- Heuristic 6 evidence (not a Method): iterations and function evaluations chosen and justified as the metrics that expose the mechanism (p. 17).
- New-pattern candidate: "Θ_k proof skeleton", reused from S006 (p. 12).
- New-pattern candidate: "acceptance-region geometry": rival globalizations compared as regions in the ‖c‖–f plane before any algebra (Figs 1–7, pp. 2–10).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Interpolate between two rival mechanisms by making a scalar parameter interval-valued, and check that the endpoints recover each rival (p. 6).
- Give the two bounds of one parameter different update sources: a model-based one for the theory (π_u) and an actual-reduction one for practice (π_l), and damp the practical one (pp. 9–11).
- Draw each method's acceptance region in the (infeasibility, objective) plane to show which productive steps it blocks (Figs 3–4, pp. 3–4).
- Report the sensitivity to an initial parameter value as separate curves in the same profile (π_u ∈ {1, 10, 100, 1000}, p. 18).

**Quotes**:
- "Thus, our view is that the main weakness of penalty-based strategies is the blocking effect illustrated in Fig. 3" (p. 4)
- "This suggests that the initial value for πu is inconsequential compared to the effect that separate updating strategies for πl and πu have on the practical performance of the approach." (p. 18)

### S025 · A matrix-free algorithm for equality constrained optimization problems with rank-deficient Jacobians (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2009, 10.1137/08072471X)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–26; result tables pp. 23–24 read; references pp. 25–26 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. Nocedal, A. Wächter · **Position in trajectory**: Curtis at the Courant Institute, NYU (p. 1 affiliation); extends the SMART tests of S006 and of the nonconvex inexact Newton paper (ref. [5]) to rank deficiency and local infeasibility; points to the inexact interior-point follow-up (Curtis–Schenk–Wächter, ref. [10], p. 25).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Methods that cope with (nearly) rank-deficient Jacobians (penalty regularization, trust regions) need explicit storage and factorization (p. 1); the earlier SMART-test methods assume full-rank Jacobians (p. 3). Goal: a matrix-free method, globally convergent to first-order optimal points "or at least to stationary points of the infeasibility measure" (p. 2), and well defined on locally infeasible instances (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Two-stage step (p. 4): a normal step v_k from a trust-region least-squares subproblem with the unusual radius ‖v‖ ≤ ω‖A_kᵀc_k‖ (2.2), needing only Cauchy decrease (2.3) (pp. 4–5); then a perturbed primal-dual system with right-hand side −A_k v_k instead of c_k (2.6), solved iteratively (p. 5). Acceptance by three SMART tests built from a tangential component condition (2.10)/(2.11), a model reduction condition with u_k in place of d_k (2.12) and a dual residual condition (2.13) (pp. 6–7). Test 2 accepts a multiplier-only step (d = 0) so no exact solve is needed at stationary points with nonzero dual residual (p. 7). Hessian modification is triggered inside the iterative solve by a tangential curvature test, without inertia (p. 8). Algorithm TRINS, p. 8.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 3.1 (p. 9): iterates in a convex set where f, c and first derivatives are bounded and Lipschitz; {W_k} bounded; the iterative solver can make the residual arbitrarily small for each (modified) W_k. No rank assumption; finitely many Hessian modifications give W_k ≻ 2θI (p. 8). The radius form ω‖Aᵀc‖ is chosen "to simplify the analysis" (p. 4); in practice ω is updated within an interval (pp. 20–21).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Well-posedness by viewing the right-hand side of (3.6) as a concave quadratic in ‖u‖ whose maximizer tends to zero as the residual vanishes (pp. 9–11). A one-dimensional lemma (Lemma 3.4, p. 12) gives Cauchy decrease ‖c‖−‖c+Av‖ ≥ γ1‖Aᵀc‖²/‖c‖ (Lemma 3.5, p. 12) and the envelope γ2‖Aᵀc‖² ≤ ‖v‖ ≤ ω‖Aᵀc‖ (Lemma 3.6, p. 13). Key device for the unbounded-penalty case: the scaled and shifted merit φ̃ = (f − f_min)/π + ‖c‖, monotone even as π grows (Lemma 3.11, p. 14), which yields A_kᵀc_k → 0 without bounded π (Lemma 3.12, pp. 14–16). Dichotomy theorem: full-rank limit points ⇒ bounded π and KKT convergence; otherwise stationarity of infeasibility, plus dual stationarity if π stays bounded (Theorem 3.3, p. 11; proof p. 19).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Matlab; normal step by adapted LSQR (equivalent to CG with Steihaug tests, more stable near singularity), primal-dual by adapted MINRES, no preconditioner, at most 2(n+t) MINRES iterations, Hessian shifts by Ipopt's rule (pp. 21–22). Parameters fixed for all runs (Table 4.1, p. 21), "without a fine-tuning" (p. 22). Test set: all 73 CUTEr equality problems with AMPL models and at least one degree of freedom (p. 22). Three sets: original; perturbed by splitting c1 = 0 into c1 = 0 and c1 − c1² = 0 (rank-deficient everywhere, inconsistent linearization); infeasible by setting the added constraint's right-hand side to 1 (pp. 22–23). Comparator INS (the predecessor [5]) on original and perturbed sets; no infeasible column because INS rarely terminates there (p. 24). Per-problem termination flags and iteration counts (Tables 4.2–4.3, pp. 23–24), including iteration-limit failures (---) and two infeasible problems returned as "opt" because of prescaling (pp. 24–25). Admits that small problems do not show the matrix-free benefit (p. 22).
- **D6 Positioning**: Self-positioned as the extension of Byrd–Curtis–Nocedal [4, 5] (p. 3). Credits penalty-regularization and trust-region approaches to rank deficiency (Burke, Celis–Dennis–Tapia, Omojokun, Powell–Yuan), faulting only their storage and factorization demands (p. 1). Matrix-free precedents (Heinkenschloss–Ridzal, Ridzal, Ulbrich) cited as avoiding factorizations (p. 1). Points to MINRES-QLP (Choi) as a more stable solver for singular systems (p. 25).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Each condition is introduced, then explained in prose (what (2.10) versus (2.11) permit, p. 6); differences from the predecessor condition (2.1) listed explicitly (p. 6). Admissions are direct ("We admit", p. 22; the "false infeasible" risk is named and the tolerance ratio justified by prescaling, p. 20). One unverified claim is labelled as a conjecture: failures could be remedied by a better implementation, unlike INS's (p. 25).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithm + convergence theory for a regime (rank deficiency, local infeasibility) the earlier inexact methods excluded. Limits stated concretely: an extra inexact normal solve per iteration, with the suggestion to try the Newton system first and fall back only when needed (p. 25); no watchdog against the Maratos effect; preconditioners needed; large-scale experiments and inequality constraints deferred to [10] (p. 25).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the three termination tests and the Hessian-modification trigger are derived from what the penalty model needs (pp. 6–8); inertia-free modification inside the iterative solve (p. 8).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (an earlier instance than the 2010, 2012 and 2020 cases listed in SKILL.md): rank-deficient and infeasible variants of every test problem built mechanically via c1 − c1² = 0 and = 1 (pp. 22–23).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the comparator is the group's own predecessor INS in the same Matlab setting, with failures and per-problem counts printed (pp. 23–24); ⚠ one comparator only, small problems, no timing.
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: one algorithm converging to a KKT point or an infeasible stationary point, with no restoration phase (Theorem 3.3, p. 11).
- Heuristic 2 evidence: the extra normal-step solve is named as the main drawback, with a cheaper fallback design proposed (p. 25). Heuristic 10 evidence: the "rank deficiency" rung after full-rank equalities (pp. 3, 25).
- New-pattern candidate: "Θ_k proof skeleton" family (here with u_k in place of d_k and an envelope on v_k, pp. 12–14).
- New-pattern candidate: "scaled-shifted merit for unbounded penalties" (Lemma 3.11, p. 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Make a degenerate test set from any equality-constrained set by duplicating one constraint as c1 − c1² = 0 (rank-deficient, still feasible) and as c1 − c1² = 1 (infeasible) (pp. 22–23).
- Put the trust-region radius in units of the stationarity measure (‖v‖ ≤ ω‖Aᵀc‖) so the normal step's size directly bounds infeasibility stationarity (pp. 4, 13).
- When the penalty parameter may diverge, analyse φ̃ = (f − f_min)/π + ‖c‖, which stays monotone as π increases (p. 14).
- Add a multiplier-only acceptance test so an iterative solver is never forced to solve exactly at a primal stationary point (Termination test 2, p. 7).
- Report per-problem termination flags in three columns (original / perturbed / infeasible) with failures shown, not only aggregate rates (pp. 23–24).

**Quotes**:
- "We admit that by testing small problems we do not illustrate the benefits of a matrix-free approach" (p. 22)
- "A drawback of the algorithm described in this paper is that it requires the inexact solution of the normal subproblem (2.2) in addition to the primal-dual system (2.6)." (p. 25)

### S009 · Infeasibility detection and SQP methods for nonlinear optimization (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2010, 10.1137/080738222)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–19; references pp. 18–19 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: R. H. Byrd, J. Nocedal · **Position in trajectory**: Curtis at Lehigh ISE, supported while at Courant (p. 1); the infeasible-case theory behind the later PIPAL and SQuID line (SKILL.md Signature Work Anatomy).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Methods that converge fast on feasible problems can be very slow and evaluation-hungry on infeasible ones (p. 1); fast infeasibility detection matters for MINLP branch-and-bound and parametric studies (p. 1). Entry is a missing guarantee: the Sℓ1QP / elastic penalty SQP subproblem is always feasible, but the method lacks fast local convergence guarantees in the infeasible case (p. 3). Explicit rejection of the switching design (Fletcher–Leyffer; SNOPT's switch to an ℓ1 approach) because switch criteria are hard to design and iterates may stray (pp. 1–3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Write the ℓ1 penalty as ρf + v (ρ multiplies the objective, p. 4) and use the structured Hessian W = ρ∇²f − Σλᵢ∇²gᵢ (2.4), in which ρ scales only the objective curvature, so that ρ is the only control parameter and no multiplier adjustment is needed when ρ changes (p. 4). Theorem 3.6: near an infeasible stationary point, ρ_k = O(‖z_k − ẑ‖²) gives quadratic and ρ_k = o(‖z_k − ẑ‖) superlinear convergence (p. 11). Practical Algorithm II (p. 13): steering rules (solve the QP with ρ = 0; decrease ρ until linearized-feasibility progress (4.3) and penalty-model progress (4.4) hold), plus step f, ρ ≤ [E_k(λ̄, 0)/max{1, v(x_k)}]², tying ρ to the squared KKT error of the feasibility problem (p. 13).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Inequality constraints only, "for simplicity" (p. 2). Local analysis under Assumptions 3.2 at the infeasible stationary point (p. 7): C² smoothness; full row rank of active-constraint gradients; strict complementarity λ̂ᵢ ∈ (0, 1) on active constraints; second-order sufficiency for the auxiliary problem (3.5). Unit steps assumed, relying on a watchdog or second-order correction (p. 5). Indefinite QPs require picking the right local minimizer; positive definite replacements must satisfy a Dennis–Moré-type condition (3.28) (p. 12). Infeasibility is only local in the nonconvex case (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Recast the infeasible stationary point as a KKT point of the auxiliary problem (3.5), minimizing the sum of violated −gᵢ subject to active gᵢ = 0 (p. 6). Implicit function theorem on F(x, λ_A, ρ) = 0 at ρ = 0 gives a path z_ρ with ‖z_ρ − ẑ‖ ≤ Cρ and unchanged active/violated/satisfied sets (Lemma 3.3, pp. 7–8). Near ẑ the QP step solves a Newton system for F at fixed ρ (Lemma 3.4, pp. 9–10), and Newton's quadratic contraction toward z_ρ is uniform in ρ (Lemma 3.5, pp. 10–11; cf. Cores–Tapia). Triangle inequality ‖z_{k+1} − ẑ‖ ≤ ‖z_{k+1} − z_ρ‖ + ‖z_ρ − ẑ‖ ≤ C‖z_k − ẑ‖² + O(ρ) (p. 7; proof of Theorem 3.6, p. 11). Special case: n active constraints at ẑ ⇒ local infeasibility can be declared without ρ → 0 (§3.2, p. 11).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Matlab prototype, quadprog QP solver, W_k shifted to be positive definite; ρ0 = 1, τ = 0.5, η = 10⁻⁸, ε1 = ε2 = 0.1 (p. 14). Five illustrations with iteration tables (k, f, v, both KKT errors, ρ, ‖d‖, α, QP count): toy unique (pp. 14–15), CUTEr robot with added c4 = c1² + 1 (pp. 15–16), toy isolated (pp. 15–16), batch (feasible) versus batch1 = batch + tl[1] ≥ 5, simulating a branch-and-bound node (pp. 16–17), toy nactive (pp. 16–17). Single external comparison: KNITRO/direct needs 42 iterations and 151 function evaluations on unique (p. 15). Ablation: step f disabled on nactive (Table 7, p. 18). A benchmark on a standard set is declared "outside the scope of this paper as it requires a sophisticated software implementation" (p. 14).
- **D6 Positioning**: Inherits Fletcher's penalty SQP (Sℓ1QP) and the Byrd–Nocedal–Waltz steering rules, presenting Algorithm II as "alternative steering rules" that keep constraint curvature in the ρ = 0 subproblem (p. 14). Contrasts with SNOPT (switches only on trouble and does not seek a fast infeasible rate, p. 3) and with penalty updates based on several iterations' behaviour (Gill–Murray–Wright, Bertsekas, Mongeau–Sartenaer, Chen–Goldfarb), whereas steering explores the problem at a fixed iterate (p. 14). Thanks Fletcher for stressing the need for fast infeasibility detection (p. 18).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Two-tasks opening sentence (p. 1); main contribution located in the abstract ("The main contribution of this paper, however, is a set of conditions", p. 1). Early honesty about scope: "We note at the outset that infeasibility detection is difficult in the nonconvex case" (p. 3). Proof strategy previewed as a three-line inequality chain before the lemmas (p. 7). The cost of multiple QP solves is defended by an unquantified claim about "our numerical evidence" (p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Local-rate theory for the infeasible case + a practical update rule + illustrations. Limits: multiple QPs per iteration (p. 14); no systematic benchmark (p. 14); only local infeasibility in the nonconvex case (p. 3); step f forces ρ → 0 even when unnecessary (nactive, pp. 17–18), with detection of that case left as a practical suggestion (p. 18).

**Method links**:

- New-pattern candidate: "perturb from the ρ = 0 problem": analyse a parameterized method as Newton on a family F(·, ρ) with an implicit-function path, then choose the parameter decay to match the rate (pp. 7–11).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To get a fast rate to a limiting problem, prove (a) the solution path satisfies ‖z_ρ − ẑ‖ ≤ Cρ (implicit function theorem), (b) Newton toward z_ρ is quadratic uniformly in ρ, then (c) drive ρ like the square of the current error (Lemmas 3.3–3.5, Theorem 3.6, pp. 7–11).
- Couple a parameter update to the squared optimality error of the auxiliary problem (step f, p. 13) so the theory's rate condition is enforced by a computable test.
- Let the penalty parameter scale only the objective's curvature in the QP Hessian (2.4), so changing it does not require resetting multipliers (p. 4).
- Build one toy instance per case of the theory (one active constraint, none active, n active) and print full iteration tables (pp. 14–18).
- Simulate the application setting with a one-bound modification (batch → batch1 via tl[1] ≥ 5, a branch-and-bound node, p. 16).
