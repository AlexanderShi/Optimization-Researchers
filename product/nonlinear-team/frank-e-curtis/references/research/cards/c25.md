### S034 · Stochastic optimization approaches for an operating room and anesthesiologist scheduling problem (Operations Research 2025, DOI 10.1287/opre.2022.0258; arXiv:2204.11374)
**Read level**: full. Main text read completely, file pp. 1–52 (§§1–9, acknowledgments). Appendix skimmed, file pp. 53–113: A.3.3 (Prop. A.1), the proofs in A.4 (Props. 1–4), A.7, A.8.2, A.9.3, A.10.2.4, A.10.3.5 and A.10.4 were read in prose. The formula-heavy derivations (A.5, A.6, A.8.1, A.9.1–A.9.2) and the appendix result tables (A.10–A.41) were skimmed only. The reference list (pp. 113–122) was searched, not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Man Yiu Tsang, Karmel S. Shehadeh (both Lehigh ISE), Beth Hochman, Tricia E. Brentjens (Columbia University Medical Center) (p. 1). Curtis is third of five authors. The paper has no contribution statement, and the acknowledgments name only Shehadeh's dedication (p. 52), so his personal share cannot be identified from the text. · **Place in trajectory**: a Lehigh ISE applied collaboration. It uses the inexact column-and-constraint-generation (C&CG) method of Tsang, Shehadeh and Curtis (2023, Oper. Res. Lett.; reference on p. 121) for large DRO instances (pp. 24, 37). That is the bridge from his inexactness line to robust MILP.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is an application with a named partner: "our collaboration with a large health system in New York" (p. 2). The gap is integration plus uncertainty. Existing models separate allocation (open ORs, call in on-call anesthesiologists), assignment, and sequencing and scheduling (p. 2). The closest model, Rath et al. (2017), is robust optimization and ignores waiting and idle time (pp. 9–10). Box plots of half a year of partner data show duration variability within and across surgery types (Fig. 1, p. 3). The case for DRO is that estimated distributions "may inherit bias" and future durations need not follow past ones (p. 4). The problem is stated as min fixed cost + risk measure (expectation or CVaR) of recourse cost (pp. 15–16). For DRO, the recourse cost is replaced by a worst case over a mean-support ambiguity set (pp. 19–20).
  - Transferable: enter an applied problem through a named model's missing term, then show what that omission produces (here, waiting time; Prop. A.1, pp. 56–59).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Four models: SP-E, SP-CVaR, DRO-E, DRO-CVaR (pp. 16, 20). The SP models are solved by SAA with N = 100 scenarios, chosen with Monte Carlo optimization (MCO) (p. 36). For DRO, the inner sup over distributions is dualized (Prop. 1, p. 20). The resulting sup over d of recourse minus ρᵀd is turned into an MILP. The recourse LP is dualized, worst-case durations sit at the lower or upper bounds (binary b_i), and the bilinear terms are linearized by McCormick inequalities (Prop. 2, p. 21; proof pp. 60–63). The McCormick big-Ms are dual-variable bounds proved in Prop. 3 (p. 22). That gives C&CG (Algorithm 1, p. 24): the master problem carries scenario copies of the recourse constraints, the subproblem is the MILP (6), and termination is finite because there are 2^|I| vertex scenarios (p. 24). Further pieces:
  - Valid inequalities from nonnegative idle times (eq. 11, p. 23).
  - A Dirac-at-mean lower bound and d = m as the initial scenario (p. 25).
  - Bounds on ρ, used in a variable-free form (Prop. 5 and eq. 15, pp. 25–26).
  - Decomposition by groups of surgery types that share ORs or anesthesiologists (§6.4, pp. 26–29).
  - Symmetry-breaking constraints for OR opening order and load, surgery-to-OR assignment, same-type sequence and on-call order (§7, pp. 29–34).

  What is kept from the classics: the first-stage variables and constraints follow Rath et al. (2017) (p. 15, p. 19). C&CG follows Zeng and Zhao (2013) (p. 24).
  - Transferable: when an uncertainty-set sup is linear in each d_i after dualizing the recourse, reduce it to binary vertex choices, linearize with McCormick, and spend a proposition on tight dual bounds for the big-Ms (pp. 21–22, 63–65).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The model assumes:
  - Known lower and upper bounds on each surgery duration, "recommended by our collaborators" (p. 14).
  - Known means for DRO (p. 19).
  - The surgery–surgeon pairing is fixed in advance (pp. 13–14).
  - Dedicated-OR and specialty policies (p. 13, pp. 26–27).
  - Relatively complete recourse (p. 18).

  For symmetry breaking, identical ORs (and identical on-call anesthesiologists) share costs (pp. 29, 34), and same-type surgeries share a duration distribution and waiting cost (pp. 31–32). The authors say how to regroup into subtypes when these do not hold (pp. 30, 32). Experimental choices:
  - Bounds are the 20th/80th percentiles, and in-sample scenarios are lognormal (p. 35).
  - The same waiting cost for all surgeries is justified by a quoted claim that OR managers lack data for differentiated values (p. 36).

  Remark A.1 admits that the big-M bound uses a hypothetical worst case where one anesthesiologist does all surgeries (p. 56).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: There is no convergence or complexity analysis beyond finite termination of C&CG (p. 24). Devices used:
  - (a) Strong duality for the moment problem via Proposition 6.68 of Shapiro et al. (2014) (p. 59).
  - (b) The LP dual of the recourse problem (Lemma A.2, p. 60).
  - (c) Separability in i and linearity in d_i, so an extreme-point worst case gives a binary program (pp. 61–62).
  - (d) McCormick linearization of dual×binary products (p. 63).
  - (e) Dual bounds from complementary slackness and ordering of the actual start times (Lemma A.3 and the proof of Prop. 3, pp. 63–64). A worked instance shows the bound is attained, "the tightest possible constant upper bound" (Remark A.2 and Example A.2, p. 65).
  - (f) CVaR via the Rockafellar–Uryasev form plus a minimax swap (pp. 66–67).
  - (g) An exchange argument that, without waiting-time terms, start times are redundant and all surgeries of an anesthesiologist start at once (Prop. A.1, pp. 56–59).
  - (h) A two-solution example showing that (17a) and (17b) do not imply each other in the LP relaxation, which justifies using both (Example A.3, pp. 73–74).
  - Transferable: prove a structural proposition that a rival model's optimum is not implementable (g), then confirm it numerically on the rival's own solutions (p. 47).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Instances: six built from the public Mannino et al. (2010) data, 15–80 surgeries, 7–32 ORs and 5–40 anesthesiologists (Table 1, p. 35). Six more come from Min and Yih (2010) (A.10.3, pp. 103–104), plus shared-OR variants (A.10.3.5, p. 111).
  - Setup: AMPL + CPLEX 20.1 with default settings, 2 % relative MIP gap, and an inexact C&CG with a 600 s master time limit for large DRO instances (pp. 36–37). There are 20 SAA replications per SP instance and 10 random means per DRO instance (p. 37).
  - Solve times (Table 2, p. 38): medium instances solve in under three minutes, and large ones in two minutes to an hour (p. 37). SP-CVaR cannot solve instances 5–6 in 10 hours, which is attributed to non-separability (pp. 37–38).
  - Ablations of the component under test:
    - VIs (11): LP-relaxation ratio up to 2.93 and 45–75 % longer solves without them on large instances (Table 3, p. 39).
    - DRO VIs: C&CG iterations 62 vs 150 on instance 6 (Table 4, p. 40).
    - Symmetry-breaking: "5 to 145 times faster" on instance 1 over N ∈ {10,…,200} with 20th/80th-percentile bands (p. 40; Fig. 6, p. 41).
  - Out-of-sample evaluation: 10,000 scenarios under the in-sample lognormal (I), plus misspecified truncated normal, uniform with supports widened by Δ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5}, and beta (II–IV) (Table 5, p. 43). The settings are motivated by an observed −17 % to 31 % annual change in duration bounds (p. 43). The results:
    - Means are tabled for instances 2 and 6 only, "for the sake of brevity" (pp. 43–44).
    - Instance 6 is shown as CDFs of operational cost, total cost and out-of-sample disappointment (pp. 45–47).
  - Comparisons:
    - Rath's RO model, with symmetry-breaking added to every model (p. 47).
    - A sequential Denton-then-ORASP approach, whose operational cost is about twice as high on instances 5–6 (p. 48).
    - An extended RO model solved by a Rath-style decomposition, with 36–73 % gaps on instance 2 after two hours (p. 38; Table A.4, p. 89).
    - An LDR approximation: faster on instances 1–5, but it fails on instance 6 and has 4–41 % average optimality gaps (pp. 80–81).
  - Case-study sensitivity on the partner's September 2021 master schedule: 29 ORs, 35 anesthesiologists (pp. 48–51).
  - SAA solution stability is reported over 10 replications (pp. 93–94).
  - Failures are reported (SP-CVaR, LDR and RO time-outs).
  - Transferable: evaluate stochastic schedules out of sample under deliberately misspecified families and widened supports. Report full CDFs of cost and of "disappointment" (out-of-sample cost above the model's own optimal value), not only means (pp. 43–47).
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper claims to be the "first" risk-neutral and risk-averse SP and DRO models for the ORASP (p. 5). Each near rival gets an enumerated difference paragraph: Wang et al. 2019 ("First… Second… Third…", p. 11), Deng et al. 2019 (p. 11) and Rath et al. 2017 (pp. 9–10, 19). The rivals are re-described, not attacked. The symmetry constraints are positioned as generalizing earlier ones by dropping the "all ORs identical" assumption (pp. 30, 33). The paper frames DRO between SP, which assumes known distributions, and RO, which is "overly conservative" (p. 10). It cites the authors' own inexact C&CG paper for both finite termination and large-instance solving (pp. 24, 37).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**:
  - The contributions come as a numbered three-part list with sub-items (pp. 5–7). The core claim of integration plus ambiguity appears first in the abstract (p. 1).
  - Hedging is moderate. Mechanisms are offered as "One possible explanation" (p. 37) and "This may follow from" (p. 26). "For brevity and illustrative purposes" recurs to justify showing one instance or one model (pp. 38, 40, 43).
  - Reviewer-requested analyses are labelled as such ("As suggested by a reviewer", p. 25; "per a reviewer's suggestion", p. 38) and moved to a long appendix (A.8, A.9).
  - Practitioner judgement serves as a yardstick: "According to our clinical collaborators, these solution times are reasonable" (p. 38), and the collaborators "prefer a less packed schedule" (p. 51).
  - A small inconsistency: A.9.3 says only instance 1 with τ = 0.1 was solved (p. 88), but Table A.4 also lists a cost-2, τ = 0.2 solve in 6048 s (p. 89).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is mainly modelling and application, with reformulation theory and a solution method in support. The stated limits are concrete:
  - SP-CVaR does not decompose because CVaR is subadditive. The decomposition is used anyway, with a reported less-than-3 % out-of-sample cost difference "in most cases" (p. 29).
  - Variable-dependent ρ bounds can slow the solver (p. 26).
  - The largest instances need the inexact C&CG (p. 37).

  Future work: emergency arrivals, block-schedule and recovery-unit integration, and ML-based duration models (p. 52). Not stated as limits: only duration uncertainty is modelled, and all DRO results depend on a mean-support set with no dispersion information beyond support (pp. 19–20).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (pp. 38–41, 45–47, 80): the paper ablates one component at a time (VIs, DRO VIs, symmetry-breaking) inside the same model. It gives the rival RO model the authors' own symmetry-breaking constraints "For a fair comparison" (p. 47). It prints where alternatives win: SP has lower total cost at Δ = 0 (p. 46), and LDR is faster on instances 1–5 (p. 80).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant (pp. 38, 43, 81–89): the in-framework rival (extended RO with a Rath-style decomposition) was built only at a reviewer's request. The out-of-sample tables show 2 of 6 instances "for the sake of brevity". CPLEX defaults and a 2 % gap stand in for tuning, and tuning effort is not discussed.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (pp. 24, 37): the inexact C&CG (Tsang, Shehadeh, Curtis 2023) carries the inexact-inner-solve idea into two-stage robust MILP. Here the master's accuracy is set by a 600 s time limit, not derived from what the outer loop needs.
- Method 4 ⚠ variant (pp. 43–47, 56–59): solutions are judged out of sample under misspecification and by out-of-sample disappointment, not by in-sample optimal values. Prop. A.1 audits the rival model's objective, showing it rewards non-implementable schedules. This is a yardstick audit applied to the model, not to the benchmark measure.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant (p. 43): hard cases are manufactured mechanically, but as misspecified distributions with supports widened by Δ, not infeasible or degenerate constraint variants.
- new-pattern candidate: "degeneracy proof against the rival model" (prove that the competitor's optimum is not implementable, then show it numerically; pp. 47, 56–59).
- new-pattern candidate: "practitioner-set tractability bar" (solve times judged acceptable by clinical collaborators, not by a solver benchmark; pp. 38, 51).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a mean-support DRO with LP recourse: dualize the recourse, use separability and linearity in d_i to restrict the worst case to bound vertices (binary b_i), linearize with McCormick, and bound the duals by complementary slackness. Give an example showing the bound is tight (pp. 21–22, 60–65).
- Strengthen a C&CG master with the deterministic mean scenario: the Dirac measure at m lies in the ambiguity set, so Q(·, m) is a global lower bound and m is a good first scenario (p. 25).
- If a valid inequality that depends on first-stage variables slows the MILP, keep a variable-free version and report the comparison (pp. 25–26; A.10.2.3).
- Symmetry-breaking recipe for assignment and sequencing MILPs: partition resources by which job types share them, then order identical resources by opening and by load. Order same-type jobs by index across resources and within a resource. Keep both directional constraints when each cuts LP points the other does not (pp. 29–34, 73–74).
- Choose the SAA sample size by an MCO statistical gap (K = 20, N = 100, N′ = 10,000; 0.06–1.05 % gaps), not by habit (p. 36; pp. 111–113).
- Out-of-sample protocol for stochastic or robust schedules:
  - the in-sample family plus three misspecified families with supports widened by Δ ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5};
  - CDFs of operational cost, total cost and out-of-sample disappointment;
  - SAA-replication stability of the first-stage decisions (pp. 43–47, 93–94).
