## 3. Supervision style: what students and colleagues say

Each item is labelled **according to <who>, <source>** and is [observed] **secondary**, as this task requires. Era: JHU items are from his assistant-professor years, with small groups. Lehigh items come from the OptML period, where co-advising is common.

### 3.1 His own advisees

1. **Available, patient, confident in the student.** According to Hao Jiang (JHU PhD 2016, thesis p. iv): "I would like to first thank Daniel P. Robinson, my advisor, for his guidance and support throughout the years of my graduate study. He was always available for any kind of help and without his patience and confidence in me, this dissertation would have been impossible."
2. **Pushes for independence, helps with the writing.** According to Lingzhou Hong (JHU MSE 2014, thesis p. iii): "for his continuous support of my research, for his patience, motivation, enthusiasm, and most importantly, his friendship during my study in Johns Hopkins. He has helped in my research and the writing of this thesis. He encouraged me to not only to grow as a an [sic] optimization person, but also an independent thinker."
3. **Funds students and opens his network to them.** According to Yueling Loh (JHU PhD 2016, thesis p. v): "All these years, he has provided me with invaluable guidance, unwavering financial assistance, and great opportunities through his extensive professional network. Many thanks for his endless support and patience; he has made my stay at Hopkins an enriching, productive, and enjoyable experience."
4. **Shares knowledge and keeps improving the student's work.** According to Tianyi Chen (JHU PhD 2018, thesis p. iv): "He selflessly shares his knowledge, guides me to improve myself, and constantly tries to improve my work."
5. **The same pattern 2014-2018** [inferred]. Four advisees writing independently over four years all name *patience* (Hong, Jiang, Loh) or *support* (all four). Two name active work on their output: help with the writing (Hong) and "constantly tries to improve my work" (Chen). One names money and network (Loh). None describes him as directive about topic choice. Whether he assigned topics or let students choose them is **not stated anywhere** (see Gaps).

### 3.2 Colleagues' students (committee and reader roles)

6. **A skeptical reader of proofs.** According to Manolis C. Tsakiris (JHU PhD 2017, Vidal's student; Robinson was secondary reader; thesis p. v): "I am thankful to Prof. Daniel P. Robinson, for many useful conversations, for always being supportive and friendly, and, quite importantly, for not being easily convinced: this led to the identification of a few inaccuracies and the improvement of several arguments in this thesis."
7. **Listens patiently to unfinished arguments.** According to Benjamin D. Haeffele (JHU PhD 2015, Vidal's student; Robinson joined as secondary reader; thesis p. v): "Thank you, Daniel, for agreeing to join my committee late in the game. I regret that we didn't get more time to work together, but I will always appreciate you patiently listening to me as I rambled through some poorly presented proof or half thought out idea."
8. **Explains mathematics well; checks on students' well-being.** According to Qi Wang (Lehigh PhD Jan 2025, Curtis's student; Robinson on the committee; thesis p. v): "Thanks to Daniel for your inspiring approach to explaining mathematics in both classes and research, and for checking in on my well-being and sharing jokes that make life lighter."
9. **Neutral committee thanks** (included for balance). According to Mohammadreza Samadi (Lehigh PhD 2019, thesis p. iv), the committee gave "insightful comments in enriching this dissertation". According to Baoyu Zhou (Lehigh PhD 2022, thesis p. iv), the committee members, Robinson among them, shared "their invaluable experience with me selflessly". Neither singles him out.

**Tacit trait across items 6-7** [inferred]. He listens to half-formed arguments without dismissing them (Haeffele), and he does not accept an argument until it holds (Tsakiris). Both come from students in Vidal's group, about proofs in subspace learning, 2015-2017.
