### S022 · Projected Hessian updating algorithms for nonlinearly constrained optimization (SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis 22 (5), 821–850, 1985, DOI 10.1137/0722050)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: M. L. Overton · **Position**: an early constrained-optimization paper; by topic a precursor of the reduced-Hessian work with Byrd (S039, S028). Overton lists it without a copy, offering reprints by e-mail (metadata: nlp.html).
- **D1 problem/entry**: minimize a smooth function subject to m smooth equality constraints. The entry is a review of Newton-method approaches, with emphasis on Goodman's recent work (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: a Broyden-type update of an n × (n − m) one-sided projected Hessian of the Lagrangian (Q-superlinear), then updates of the symmetric two-sided projected Hessian of order n − m. New variants have a local two-step Q-superlinear property with one set of gradients per iteration (abstract).
- **D4 analysis**: includes a new short proof of the Boggs–Tolle–Wang necessary and sufficient condition for Q-superlinear convergence (abstract).
- **D5 experiments**: numerical results are presented (abstract). The zbMATH reviewer (0593.65043) remarks that all methods are run without line searches and that this may decisively affect performance (third-party metadata).
- **D8 contribution**: theory, algorithms and numerical evidence; the practical claim is hedged (abstract; second quote below).
**Method links**: Method 5 — evidence: the rate is proved for the cheap variant that evaluates one set of gradients per iteration, i.e. for the version one would run, and a known condition is re-proved more simply (abstract). New-pattern candidate "map the new method onto known methods": the new update is derived from a survey of Newton approaches (abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "We present several new variants of this algorithm and show that under certain conditions they all have a local two-step Q-superlinear convergence property, even though only one set of gradients is evaluated per iteration."
**Quote** (abstract): "Numerical results are presented, indicating that the methods may be very useful in practice."
**Transferable**: when a cheaper variant gathers less information per iteration, prove a multi-step rate (two-step Q-superlinear) rather than giving up on superlinear convergence (abstract).

### S177 · Viewing the conjugate gradient method as a trust region algorithm (Numerical Analysis, Proc. 4th IIMAS Workshop, Guanajuato 1984, LNM 1230, 118–126, 1986, DOI 10.1007/bfb0072675)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none · **Position**: ties the CG and limited-memory BFGS lines to trust regions, three years before the 1989 L-BFGS study.
- **D1 problem/entry**: read the CG method as a limited memory BFGS method, so that a trust region problem can be attached to it (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: a new search-direction formula from that trust region problem. It coincides with the limited memory BFGS step when the trust region is large enough and differs from earlier directions when the trust region is active (abstract).
- **D4 analysis**: the trust region subproblem reduces to finding a zero of a polynomial of degree 6 (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: a new direction formula; the abstract mentions no experiments (abstract).
**Method links**: none to Methods 1–6. New-pattern candidates "map the new method onto known methods" and "Collapse check to the classical case": the new step equals the L-BFGS step when the trust region is inactive (abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "In so doing we derive a new formula for computing search directions, which coincides with the limited memory BFGS step when the trust region is large enough, but which is different from other previously proposed step directions when the trust region is active."
**Transferable**: recast a classical method as a special case of another (CG as limited memory BFGS) to import the other's globalization (a trust region), and check that the new step reduces to the old one when the added constraint is inactive (abstract).
