### S064 · A family of second-order methods for convex ℓ1-regularized optimization (Mathematical Programming 159(1), 435–467, 2016, DOI 10.1007/s10107-015-0965-3)
**Read**: abstract (2012 Optimization Online preprint abstract). **Role**: supplement. **Coauthors**: Richard H. Byrd, Gillian M. Chin, Figen Oztoprak (Crossref).

**Versions**:
- Posted to Optimization Online on 2012/06/26 as an Optimization Center (Northwestern University) tech report of June 2012. OO categories: Convex and Nonsmooth Optimization; Nonlinear Optimization. OO tags: l1-regularization, quadratic programming, semi-smooth Newton (OO page).
- The journal version appeared online on 30 Nov 2015 and in print in Sept 2016, with 46 references (Crossref).
- The open preprint PDF (27 pp.) is now in papers/ under the S064 slug, for the next round. It was not read here.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: The objective is a convex f plus an ℓ1 regularization term. The entry is to bring second-order information about f in "to accelerate convergence" (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**:
  - One semi-smooth Newton framework generates the second-order methods: block active-set, orthant-based and a second-order iterative soft-thresholding method (abstract).
  - New is an active-set method that makes multiple changes to the active-manifold estimate, with a mechanism that corrects the estimates when needed (abstract).
  - Transferable: Present rival method classes as instances of one generating framework. Then transplant your new component into a second member of the family to test whether it is generic. Here the corrective mechanism "is also evaluated in an orthant-based method" (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: An algorithmic framework, a new active-set method with estimate correction, and a numerical comparison of several second-order methods (abstract). Limits and future directions: not read.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ (evidence): one component, the estimate-correction mechanism, is evaluated in two method classes, active-set and orthant-based (abstract).
- Method 3 ⚠ (variant): the active-manifold estimate gets a recovery path, a mechanism "for correcting estimates, when needed". This is outside the noise setting, and the abstract does not say what triggers the correction (abstract).
- New-pattern candidate: *generating framework for a method family*. Semi-smooth Newton is used as the generator of block active-set, orthant-based and second-order ISTA methods (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "We describe a semi-smooth Newton framework that can be used to generate a variety of second-order methods, including block active-set methods, orthant-based methods and a second-order iterative soft-thresholding method." · "We also propose a new active set method that performs multiple changes in the active manifold estimate, and incorporates a novel mechanism for correcting estimates, when needed."

### S169 · Collaborative Research: Algorithms for Large-Scale Stochastic and Nonlinear Optimization (NSF award DMS-1620022, 2016, no DOI)
**Read**: abstract (NSF award abstract; a grant record, not a publication). **Role**: supplement. **PI**: Jorge Nocedal, sole PI on this record, at Northwestern University (NSF record).

**Award facts** (NSF record):
- Programs: Computational Mathematics; OE Operations Engineering. Amount $270,000. Period 08/01/2016–07/31/2020.
- Publications the award lists:
  - *Analysis of the BFGS method with errors* (Xie, Byrd, Nocedal, SIOPT 2019);
  - *An investigation of Newton-Sketch and subsampled Newton methods* (Berahas, Bollapragada, Nocedal, OMS 35, 2020, doi:10.1080/10556788.2020.1725751);
  - *Exact and inexact subsampled Newton methods for optimization* (Bollapragada, Byrd, Nocedal, IMA JNA 2018).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: Optimization problems from machine learning that are nonlinear, high-dimensional and stochastic, involve very large data sets, and are sometimes non-convex. The entry is to get the benefits of both stochastic-gradient and batch methods without their shortcomings (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Two algorithm families (abstract):
  - gradient aggregation, which re-uses gradients computed at earlier iterations, with the challenge stated as minimizing testing error, not only training error;
  - adaptive sampling, which reduces the noise in stochastic gradients as the optimization progresses.

  Both are to include curvature information that stays effective when Hessian estimates come from inaccurate gradients (abstract).
  - Transferable: When designing an optimizer for learning, state the success criterion as test error, not training error, up front, so the algorithm is designed and judged against it (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution** (per the NSF outcomes report, Nocedal, 2020):
  - A progressive batching algorithm: it uses little data at the start and more as training proceeds. It was implemented, tested on realistic applications, and shown to converge.
  - A derivative-free line of work. A survey of existing methods led to spending computation on gradient estimation, not on grid search or interpolation models. The estimated gradients are used inside a quasi-Newton method; the report calls the resulting methods very simple to implement.
- **D7 Writing**: The abstract opens with a broad AI / machine-learning impact paragraph before the technical paragraph. S167 (2020) reuses its opening sentence (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ (evidence): the classical quasi-Newton engine is kept and fed approximate gradients in the noisy DFO setting (NSF outcomes report). Curvature updating is made robust to "inaccurate gradients" (abstract).
- Method 2 ✅ (evidence): the project started from all existing DFO methods and concluded that the dismissed gradient-estimation route was more efficient, even for noisy systems (NSF outcomes report; paraphrase).
- New-pattern candidate: *progressive sampling as variance control*. "adaptive sampling techniques to reduce the noise in stochastic gradient approximations as the optimization progresses" (abstract). Also seen in S167 (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "Two families of algorithms will be developed to garner the benefits of both stochastic gradient methods and batch methods, while avoiding their shortcomings." · "An important aspect of this research is the design of an efficient strategy for incorporating second-order information that captures curvature of the optimized loss function, even in the case when Hessian estimates are based on inaccurate gradients."

### S167 · Zero-Order and Stochastic Methods for Large-Scale Optimization (NSF award DMS-2011494, 2020, no DOI)
**Read**: abstract (NSF award abstract; a grant record, not a publication). **Role**: supplement. **PI**: Jorge Nocedal, sole PI, at Northwestern University (NSF record).

**Award facts** (NSF record):
- Program: Computational Mathematics. Amount $200,000. Period 08/01/2020–07/31/2024.
- Publication the award lists: *A trust region method for noisy unconstrained optimization* (Sun & Nocedal, Math. Program. 202, 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-023-01941-9).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: New algorithms for stochastic problems from machine learning, statistics and black-box simulation, in two projects (abstract).
  - Project 1 enters from a gap. Varying sample sizes reduce gradient variance and have been studied for unconstrained problems, but the extension to constraints "is not simple" because projections and proximal operators introduce discontinuities (abstract).
  - Project 2 is zero-order methods for noisy unconstrained problems (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**:
  - Project 1: a stochastic objective with deterministic constraints, solved with varying sample sizes (abstract).
  - Project 2: put the effort into a good gradient approximation of the noisy function, and let quasi-Newton updating build the quadratic model, not interpolation. Its efficiency is to be demonstrated on reinforcement-learning problems (abstract).
  - Combined, the two projects are to yield algorithms that scale to millions of variables and parallelize easily (abstract).
  - Transferable (writing move): A one-sentence contrast with the dominant approach that says where the effort goes: unlike [the rival class], the proposed methods invest effort in [X] and delegate [Y] to [a classical tool] (abstract).
  - Transferable (problem choice): To extend a technique proven unconstrained, name the specific obstruction (here, discontinuities from projections and proximal operators) and make it the research question (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution** (per the NSF outcomes report, Nocedal, last modified 08/11/2024):
  - Project 1: numerical experiments showed efficiency and robustness on problems with nonlinear constraints.
  - Project 2 led to what the report calls a surprising finding: a few design principles turn a classical algorithm into one that handles noise, including the zero-order setting. The report calls these principles possibly the project's most important long-term contribution (paraphrase).
- **D7 Writing**: The opening paragraph reuses and extends the 2016 award's opening sentence (S169 abstract) and closes with NSF's standard merit statement (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ (evidence): the gradient approximation feeds classical quasi-Newton updating (abstract). The 2024 outcomes report states the general claim: design principles transform a classical algorithm into a noisy one (NSF outcomes report). This matches the design guidelines of arXiv:2401.15007 that SKILL.md cites under Method 3.
- Method 2 ✅ (evidence): explicit contrast with DFO methods that build quadratic models by interpolation (abstract).
- New-pattern candidate: *progressive sampling as variance control*. "varying sample sizes to gradually reduce the variance in the gradient approximation" (abstract). Same device as S169.

**Quotes** (abstract): "Unlike derivative-free methods that construct quadratic models of the objective function using interpolation techniques, the proposed methods invest significant effort in computing a good approximation to the gradient of the noisy function and delegate the construction of a quadratic model to quasi-Newton updating." · "they have been studied in the context of unconstrained optimization, but their extension to the constrained setting is not simple because the projections or proximal operators used to enforce the constraints introduce discontinuities."

### S166 · An Iterative Approach for Solving the SCOPF Problem Applying LP, SOCP, and NLP Subproblems (OSTI technical report DOE-Northwestern-1077, 2024, DOI 10.2172/2404586)
**Read**: abstract (OSTI record description). **Role**: core (batch file).

**Record facts**:
- Sole author on the record: Nocedal, Northwestern Univ. Sponsor: USDOE ARPA-E. Contract AR0001077. Date 2024-07-19. Subjects: optimal power flow; security constraints, optimization (OSTI record; Crossref).
- OSTI links a 4-page full-text PDF. Its first page is headed *GO Challenge 1 Final Report*, *Team: NU_Columbia_Artelys*. It does not carry the record's title or any author name, so it was not placed in papers/ (reported to the operator). Only its first page was looked at, to identify it; its content is not used here.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: The security-constrained optimal power flow (SCOPF) problem. The aim is efficient algorithms and software for it (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: An iterative three-stage approach (abstract):
  - (a) linear subproblems and active-set filtering pick out the most important contingencies, which shrinks the model;
  - (b) SOCP relaxations of the reduced problem bring the iterate near the global optimum and give a lower bound;
  - (c) the nonconvex interior-point solver Artelys Knitro converges quickly to the optimal solution.
  - Transferable: Stage a large nonconvex problem in three steps: cheap linear screening to reduce the model, a convex relaxation to land near the global optimum and certify a lower bound, then a local NLP solve to finish (abstract).
- **D5 Experiments (planned)**: the plan is to "experiment with several techniques" to find the tradeoff between how complex the contingency subproblems are and how fast they solve (abstract). Results: not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: The record text is in proposal tense (it begins "We propose"); outcomes not read.

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ (evidence): three complementary algorithm classes are integrated into one pipeline (LP filtering, SOCP relaxation, interior-point NLP), and their tradeoffs are to be settled by experiment (abstract).
- KNITRO is again the NLP engine: see the Method 1 limitation on platform ownership (abstract; inference about the link).

**Quotes** (abstract): "use linear subproblems and other active set filtering techniques to identify the most important contingencies and drastically reduce the SCOPF model size" · "To identify the most effective approach, we will experiment with several techniques to identify the tradeoffs between contingency subproblem complexity and fast solvability."
