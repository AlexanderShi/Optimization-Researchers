### B002 · Numerical Optimization, Second Edition: back matter with Appendix A (Background Material), Appendix B (A Regularization Procedure), references and index (Springer free preview) (book material 2006, https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bbm:978-0-387-40065-5/1)

**Role**: core · **Co-authors**: Jorge Nocedal, Stephen J. Wright (joint voice throughout) · **Place in the trajectory**: back matter of the 2006 textbook, between the KNITRO years and the machine-learning turn (SKILL.md Research Trajectory). - **D1 Problem and entry point**: the appendix supplies the background the book assumes. It opens "In this book we work exclusively with vectors and matrices whose components are real numbers" (p. 1). Function spaces are set aside: all development "takes place in the space IRn", although many algorithms extend to Hilbert spaces (p. 5). The Preface to the Second Edition says the appendix was rearranged so that it "can be used in a more stand-alone fashion" (B001 p. 22; not part of this item).
  - Transferable technique: state the ambient space and the arithmetic model once, in the background chapter, and send readers there (pp. 1, 5, 17).
- **D2 Core idea (what the back matter contains)**:
  - **A.1 Elements of linear algebra** (pp. 1–20):
    - norms and their equivalence constants (A.3), the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with a proof, dual norms, consistent matrix norms and the condition number (pp. 3–5);
    - subspaces and the fundamental theorem of linear algebra; eigenvalues, the SVD and the spectral decomposition; trace and determinant, flagged as "used in the analysis of Chapter 6" (pp. 5–9);
    - LU with row partial pivoting (Algorithm A.1, about 2n³/3 flops), Cholesky (Algorithm A.2, about n³/3) and QR (about 4m²n/3), each with its cost and its use for null-space bases (pp. 9–13);
    - symmetric indefinite factorization with three pivoting strategies compared on cost and stability. Bunch–Parlett is stable but needs O(n³) comparisons. Bunch–Kaufman needs O(n²), but L can grow without bound, "making it unsuitable for use with a modified Cholesky strategy". Bounded Bunch–Kaufman is the compromise (pp. 13–15);
    - Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury (pp. 15–16) and the interlacing eigenvalue theorem (p. 16);
    - floating-point arithmetic, unit roundoff u ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ and cancellation (pp. 16–18);
    - conditioning as a property of the problem and stability as a property of the algorithm. Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting can show growth of about 2ⁿ⁻¹ in the worst case, yet is judged stable "for all practical purposes" (pp. 19–20).
  - **A.2 Elements of analysis, geometry, topology** (pp. 20–37):
    - sequences, lim sup and lim inf (pp. 20–22);
    - Q-linear, Q-superlinear, Q-quadratic and Q-order-p rates, then R-rates, with an R-linear sequence whose error rises at every second step (pp. 22–23);
    - topology, convex sets, cones, the affine hull and the relative interior (pp. 23–26);
    - continuity, Lipschitz continuity and the bound for products (A.43) (pp. 26–28);
    - derivatives, the chain rule (Example A.1) and directional derivatives, worked on the ℓ1 norm (pp. 28–32);
    - the mean value theorem (Example A.2) and the integral form for vector functions, with the ½L‖p‖² error bound (pp. 32–33);
    - the implicit function theorem, following Lang and Bertsekas (pp. 33–34), and order notation (pp. 34–36);
    - scalar Newton and secant steps (pp. 36–37).
  - **Appendix B** (pp. 38–39): Algorithm B.1, "Inertia Correction and Regularization", for the primal-dual matrix (19.25).
    - First factor with δ = γ = 0. On zero eigenvalues set γ = 10⁻⁸ηµ^β.
    - Start δ at 10⁻⁴ if δold = 0, else at δold/2. Multiply δ by 10 until the inertia is (n + m, l + m, 0), then store δold.
    - The procedure is credited as adapted from a more elaborate procedure of Wächter and Biegler [301] (p. 39). Reference [301] is their paper on implementing an interior-point filter line-search algorithm, Math. Program. 106 (2006) (p. 54). Wächter is also a member of this team.
    - The stated rationale: avoid large modifications, which "erase the second derivative information" and give the step "steepest-descent like characteristics". The first trial value uses δold because the needed perturbation "will often not vary much from one interior-point iteration to the next" (p. 39).
  - Transferable technique: when you present a factorization or pivoting rule, give its cost and its stability side by side, and say for which downstream use it is unsuitable (pp. 10–15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - real finite-dimensional spaces (pp. 1, 5);
  - double-precision IEEE arithmetic as the default for optimization computations (p. 17);
  - Fréchet differentiability (p. 28);
  - Lipschitz continuity of J for the linearization error bound (p. 33).
  - The appendix flags its own informal steps: the Hilbert-space details are omitted (p. 5); one chain-rule identity is "left as an exercise" (p. 31); "(Prove this statement!)" (p. 22).
