- **D1 Problem and entry point**: As in c01, the entry is an empirical gap. Limited-memory methods had not been compared with each other since Gill and Murray (1979), and little was known about how they compare with Newton's method or partitioned quasi-Newton (PQN) on large problems (p. 3). New numbers: 16 test problems "with dimensions ranging from 49 to 10000" (p. 6). They come from Gill–Murray (1979), Moré et al. (1981), Buckley–LeNir (1983), Toint (1978, 1983a), Gilbert–Lemaréchal (1988, problem 16) and the authors' matrix-square-root problems 12, 13 and 15 (p. 7).
  - Transferable: open the experiments with one table that gives every test problem with its source and the overall dimension range (Table 1, pp. 6–7).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Algorithm 2.1 (p. 5) is the BFGS iteration with a Wolfe line search, 0 < β′ < 1/2 and β′ < β < 1, always trying the unit step first. H_k comes from m̂ + 1 BFGS updates of H₀ with the stored pairs, m̂ = min{k, m − 1} (2.6). The scalings are now legible (p. 14), and they confirm the round-1 mapping, which c01 had inferred:
  - M1: H_k⁽⁰⁾ = H₀, no scaling.
  - M2: γ₀H₀, initial scaling only, with γ₀ = y₀ᵀs₀/‖y₀‖² (3.2) (p. 8).
  - M3: γ_kH₀ with γ_k = y_kᵀs_k/‖y_k‖² (4.1) (pp. 13–14).
  - M4: the same as M3 for the first m iterations, then a diagonal D_k minimizing the Frobenius norm ‖D_kY_{k−1} − S_{k−1}‖ over the last m steps (4.2)–(4.3). A safeguard (a threshold on the denominator of (4.3) and a band around γ_k, with exponents garbled) falls back to γ_k (p. 14).

  The test line search is the strong-Wolfe form (3.1), "stronger than (2.5), which is useful in practice". It uses β′ = 10⁻⁴ and β = 0.9, the values Buckley and LeNir recommend and the 1980 paper used (p. 8). The "accurate" variant forces at least one cubic interpolation per iteration (p. 12). For the analysis, Algorithm 6.1 updates a positive definite B_k⁽⁰⁾ m̂ = min{k + 1, m} times with pairs from an increasing index set; it is mathematically equivalent to Algorithm 2.1 (p. 22).
  - Transferable: give every variant a one-line formula and a label (M1–M4, p. 14) before tallying them, so that a per-label ranking means one thing.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumptions 6.1 (p. 22): f twice continuously differentiable; the level set D = {x : f(x) ≤ f(x₀)} convex; M₁‖z‖² ≤ zᵀG(x)z ≤ M₂‖z‖² for all z and all x ∈ D (6.4), which gives a unique minimizer in D. Theorem 6.1 adds that {B_k⁽⁰⁾} and {‖B_k⁽⁰⁾⁻¹‖} are bounded (p. 23). Test assumptions:
  - the stopping test (2.8) is ‖g_k‖ below a small multiple of max(1, ‖x_k‖) (exponent garbled), low accuracy "because this is common in practical applications" (p. 7);
  - H₀ = I, replaced by γ₀I after the first iteration in §3 (p. 8);
  - gradient evaluations equal function evaluations (p. 8);
  - runs on a SUN 3/60 in double precision (p. 8), and the Alliant FX/8 at Argonne for n = 5000 and 10000 (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Theorem 6.1 (p. 23): for any positive definite B₀, Algorithm 6.1 generates {x_k} converging to x*, and there is a constant 0 ≤ r < 1 with f_k − f* ≤ rᵏ(f₀ − f*) (6.5). The proof chain (pp. 23–24):
  - the averaged Hessian Ḡ_k = ∫₀¹ G(x_k + τs_k)dτ gives y_k = Ḡ_ks_k (6.6)–(6.7);
  - hence M₁‖s_k‖² ≤ y_kᵀs_k ≤ M₂‖s_k‖² (6.8) and ‖y_k‖²/y_kᵀs_k ≤ M₂ (6.9);
  - the trace bound is tr(B_{k+1}) ≤ tr(B_k⁽⁰⁾) + m̃M₂ ≤ M₃ (6.10);
  - the Pearson/Powell determinant product (6.11) gives det(B_{k+1}) ≥ M₄ (6.12);
  - together these give cos θ_k ≥ δ > 0 (6.13);
  - Powell's decrease inequality f(x_{k+1}) − f* ≤ (1 − c cos²θ_k)(f(x_k) − f*) then yields (6.5);
  - and ½M₁‖x_k − x*‖² ≤ f_k − f* gives ‖x_k − x*‖ ≤ r^{k/2}[2(f₀ − f*)/M₁]^{1/2}, so the iterates converge R-linearly too.

  γ_k is bounded above and below by constants from (6.4) and (6.9) (the printed bounds are garbled), so L-BFGS with M3 meets the theorem's conditions (p. 24). The theorem extends to backtracking by adapting Byrd–Nocedal (1987). Shanno–Phua and Buckley–LeNir are special cases with m varying in [1, m_max]. Buckley–LeNir can be made n-step quadratically convergent, and "R-linear convergence is the best we can expect" for L-BFGS (p. 24).
  - Transferable: when every iteration matrix is at most m updates of a bounded seed, the classical trace and determinant bounds become uniform constants (M₃, M₄). One line then gives a uniform cos θ bound and a linear rate (pp. 23–24).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: As in c02 (the user's question, the "as implemented in practice" test, pp. 1–2). New numbers: "remarkable progress has been made in the last 15 years" (p. 1). Trust regions are left out because most of the unconstrained theory "was obtained before 1982" and is surveyed by Moré and Sorensen (p. 3); round 1 could not read the year. Large n is fixed as "say 10,000", which is the reason unrestarted CG is studied: the problem is expected to be solved in fewer than n iterations (p. 11).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: As in c02 (four classes of results, few principles, global convergence a weak target). Legible settings and rules:
  - BFGS is best run with "a very loose line search", typical σ₁ = 10⁻⁴ (OCR "107~*") and σ₂ = 0.9 (pp. 16–17).
  - The unit trial step "should always be used in variable metric methods", because Dennis and Moré show that α_k = 1 satisfies the Wolfe conditions for all large k provided σ₁ < 1/2 (p. 7).
  - PR+ is β_k = max{β_k^PR, 0} (4.8) (p. 13).
  - L-BFGS works with small m, m ∈ [3, 7] in practice (p. 31).
  - The "subroutine library" list is tied to named codes (pp. 2–3): VA14 (PR with restarts), CONMIN, IMSL/NAG BFGS, VE08 (PQN, to be superseded by Lancelot), VA15 (L-BFGS), NAG/IMSL Newton with Nash's truncated Newton, and IMSL Nelder–Mead "for problems with noisy functions".
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - Theorem 3.1 (p. 5): f bounded below, continuously differentiable on a neighbourhood N of the level set, gradient Lipschitz with constant L (3.8), d_k a descent direction and Wolfe steps with 0 < σ₁ < σ₂ < 1.
  - Strong convexity m‖z‖² ≤ zᵀG(x)z ≤ M‖z‖² (5.6) for the BFGS and Broyden-class global theory (p. 18).
  - Theorem 5.1 (p. 21) needs nothing about f: any vectors with y_kᵀs_k/s_kᵀs_k ≥ m > 0 (5.18) and ‖y_k‖²/y_kᵀs_k ≤ M (5.19).
  - Theorem 6.1 (Conn–Gould–Toint, p. 28): f twice continuously differentiable with a bounded, Lipschitz Hessian, x_k → x*, |s_kᵀ(y_k − B_ks_k)| ≥ r‖s_k‖‖y_k − B_ks_k‖ with r ∈ (0, 1) (6.2), and uniformly linearly independent steps.
  - Theorem 6.2 (Khalfan–Byrd–Schnabel, p. 29) replaces linear independence by positive definite, uniformly bounded B_k.
  - Powell's (1986) global-efficiency study (p. 23) uses a two-variable strictly convex quadratic with G(x*) = I and unit steps. He notes that the analysis has limitations, because a search enforcing both Wolfe conditions may refuse the unit step when λ₁ is large (p. 24).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices** (statements now legible):
  - (1) Zoutendijk: Σ_k cos²θ_k‖g_k‖² < ∞ (3.9). The proof: from (3.7) and (3.8), α_k ≥ ((σ₂ − 1)/L)·g_kᵀd_k/‖d_k‖² (3.10). Then f_{k+1} ≤ f_k + c cos²θ_k‖g_k‖² with c = σ₁(σ₂ − 1)/L < 0, summed against boundedness below (p. 5). A condition number bounded by A gives cos θ_k ≥ 1/A (p. 6).
  - (2) Steepest descent on a quadratic: f_{k+1} ≤ [(λ_n − λ₁)/(λ_n + λ₁)]² f_k (3.21) via Kantorovich (p. 8). The same constant holds asymptotically with an inexact search that "always performs one quadratic interpolation" (p. 9).
  - (3) Conjugate gradients. Al-Baali's Theorem 4.1 gives g_kᵀd_k ≤ −c‖g_k‖² (4.7) for FR under strong Wolfe (p. 11). Gilbert–Nocedal extend it to |β_k| ≤ β_k^FR and show tightness for |β_k| ≤ cβ_k^FR with c > 1 (p. 12). Theorem 4.2 (β_k ≥ 0, Wolfe plus (4.7), Property (*)) gives lim inf ‖g_k‖ = 0 (p. 14). Rates: linear with exact searches (Crowder–Wolfe), and n-step quadratic ‖x_{k+n} − x*‖ = O(‖x_k − x*‖²) for general objectives (Cohen, Burmeister), sharpened to o(·) by Ritter (1980) (p. 14).
  - (4) BFGS trace and determinant (pp. 18–19). The first Wolfe condition and (5.6) give ‖s_k‖ ≤ c₂‖g_k‖cos θ_k with c₂ = 2(1 − σ₁)/m (5.9). The trace-decreasing term is then at least α_k/(c₂cos²θ_k) (5.10). The trace-increasing term is ‖y_k‖²/y_kᵀs_k ≤ M (5.13). The determinant is det(B_{k+1}) = det(B_k)·y_kᵀs_k/s_kᵀB_ks_k (5.15). For the restricted Broyden class, the trace relation (5.16) has a decreasing term proportional to 1/cos²θ_k against an uncertain-sign term proportional to 1/cos θ_k, and the determinant relation (5.17) holds for all φ ∈ [0, 1] (p. 20).
  - (5) Theorem 5.1 (Byrd–Nocedal 1989): for any p ∈ (0, 1) there is β₁ such that cos θ_j ≥ β₁ for at least ⌈pk⌉ indices j ∈ [1, k] (p. 21). The potential ψ(B) = tr(B) − ln det(B) satisfies ψ(B) ≥ ln cond(B) (5.21) (p. 21).
  - (6) Theorem 5.2 (Powell 1976a): BFGS with Wolfe steps gives lim inf ‖g_k‖ = 0 on any f differentiable and bounded below, if ‖y_k‖²/y_kᵀs_k (5.22) stays bounded (p. 22). This is the open line search question of p. 22.
  - (7) Powell (1986): when λ₁ is large, DFP may need a number of iterations of order λ₁ and may decrease λ by at most one per iteration from an unfavourable start, while BFGS needs a number logarithmic in λ₁ in the worst case (the base is garbled, "log,,"). When λ₁ is small, DFP reaches superlinear steps after one or two iterations (the BFGS count is garbled) (pp. 23–24).
  - (8) SR1. Fiacco–McCormick: at most n + 1 steps on a strongly convex quadratic without line searches, given linearly independent directions (p. 27). Conn–Gould–Toint: B_k → ∇²f(x*) (p. 28). Khalfan–Byrd–Schnabel: "2n-step q-quadratic", lim sup e_{k+2n}/e_k² < ∞ (p. 29). This corrects c02's "n-step q-quadratic", whose "2" was lost in the round-1 text layer.
