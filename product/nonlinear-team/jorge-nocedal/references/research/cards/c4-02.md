### S003 · A limited memory algorithm for bound constrained optimization (SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 1995, DOI 10.1137/0916069)
**Read level**: full (second read; round-1 card in c02). All of file pp. 1–25 was read: cover (p. 1), abstract and §1 (p. 2), §§2–5 (pp. 3–18), §6 with Tables 1–3 (pp. 18–23), references (pp. 24–25, skimmed). Tables 1–3 (pp. 20–22) were read from the page images, and the cost formulas of pp. 9–18 were also checked against the page images.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Richard H. Byrd (Colorado), Peihuang Lu, Ciyou Zhu (Northwestern) (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: as in the c02 card. The code is the NAM-11 report cited as [23] (p. 25), i.e. the S005/S143 text.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in c02: min f(x) subject to l ≤ x ≤ u for large n, with only the gradient (p. 2). The cost target is now explicit: the compact representation keeps "the computational cost of one iteration of the algorithm … of order n" (p. 3).
  - Transferable: state the per-iteration cost target as a complexity in n before designing the steps (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design (numbers)**: Compact form B_k = θI − W_kM_kW_kᵀ with W_k = [Y_k θS_k] and M_k a 2m×2m matrix built from L_k and D_k = diag[sᵢᵀyᵢ] (3.2)–(3.6) (p. 5). θ = y_kᵀy_k / y_kᵀs_k is reset every iteration and the initial matrix is the identity (step 7, p. 18). Line search: sufficient decrease with α = 10⁻⁴ and curvature with β = 0.9 (2.5)–(2.6) (pp. 4, 18). The unit step is tried first, and the Moré–Thuente routine's maximum step is the step to the closest bound (pp. 18–19). The update is skipped unless sᵀy > eps‖y‖² (3.9), with eps = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ in the tested code (pp. 6, 18). Generalized Cauchy point: breakpoints t_i from (4.1) are sorted by heapsort (p. 7). The first segment costs O(n), "all subsequent segments require only O(m²) operations", and m is "usually small, say less than 10" (Remark, p. 9). The vectors c and p are updated in O(m) and f′, f″ in O(m²) per segment. The total cost of Algorithm CP is (2m+2)n + O(m²)·n_int plus n log n for the heapsort (pp. 9–10). Subspace minimization, with t free variables and t_a = n − t active ones:
  - direct primal (Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury) costs 2m²t + 6mt + 4t + O(m³) (5.12) (p. 13);
  - CG stops when ‖r̂‖ < min(0.1, √‖r̂ᶜ‖)‖r̂ᶜ‖ and costs (2m+2)t + (4m+6)t·citer + O(m²) (5.14). The direct method is therefore cheaper for t ≫ m "unless citer < m/2", and CG ends in at most 2m iterations because B_k is a rank-2m correction of the identity (pp. 14–15);
  - dual costs (2m+1)n + O(m²) with no active bound and (2m+3)n + 9mt_a + 2m²t_a + O(m³) otherwise (p. 17). With the identity (I − θ⁻¹MWᵀZZᵀW)⁻¹M = (I + θM̄W̄ᵀAAᵀW̄)⁻¹M̄, the primal cost drops to 2m²t_a + 6mt + 4t + O(m³), and that form was used in the experiments (pp. 17–18).
  - Transferable: give every alternative subspace solver an operation count in the same units (multiplications and divisions, p. 13) and derive the crossover between them (here citer < m/2, p. 15) before running anything.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: as in c02. Smooth f, gradient only, simple bounds. B_k positive definite gives g_kᵀd_k < 0 through f(x_k) = m_k(x_k) > m_k(xᶜ) ≥ m_k(x̄_{k+1}) (p. 4). No convergence analysis (p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: there are no theorems, and the devices are cost devices. The breakpoint recursion (4.9)–(4.12) carries f′ and f″ through p = Wᵀd and c = Wᵀz (pp. 8–9). Inner products YᵀZZᵀY, SᵀZZᵀY and SᵀZZᵀS are saved and updated, and YᵀZZᵀY = YᵀY − YᵀAAᵀY is used when t ≫ n − t (p. 13). The primal–dual matrix identity brings the primal cost down to the dual's (pp. 17–18).
  - Transferable: when a step needs a quantity recomputed along n breakpoints, find the 2m-vectors whose cheap updates carry it from segment to segment (pp. 8–10).
- **D5 Numerical experiments (numbers)**:
  - *Setup*: both codes stop when ‖P(x_k − g_k, l, u) − x_k‖∞ < 10⁻⁵ (6.1) (p. 18). Runs on a Sun SPARCstation 2 (40-MHz CPU, 32-MB memory). F1 means not solved within 999 function evaluations. F2 means the next line-search iterate was indistinguishable from the current one, which happened only near the solution (p. 19). ### S005 · Algorithm 778: L-BFGS-B: Fortran subroutines for large-scale bound-constrained optimization (ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 1997, DOI 10.1145/279232.279236)
**Read level**: full (second read; round-1 card in c02). All of file pp. 1–17 was read: title and abstract (p. 1), §§1–6 (pp. 2–9), Tables 1.1–1.6 with their footnotes (pp. 10–15), acknowledgements and references (pp. 16–17, skimmed). The file is the December 31, 1994 report version (see page mapping). The tables were read from the page images.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Ciyou Zhu, Richard H. Byrd (Colorado), Peihuang Lu (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: as in the c02 card.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in c02. With numbers: L-BFGS-B needs about (12 + 2m)n storage locations. One iteration costs from 4mn + n multiplications with no active bound to about m²n with all variables at bounds. It needs 8 more n-vectors than L-BFGS (Harwell VA15) (p. 2).
  - Transferable: in a software paper, give storage and per-iteration cost as formulas in n and m in the introduction (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design (numbers)**: the calling sequence is lbfgsb(n, m, x, l, u, nbd, f, g, factr, wa, iwa, task, iprint, isbmin, csave, lsave, isave, dsave) (p. 3). Its parameters:
  - m: values below 3 "are not recommended", and 3 ≤ m ≤ 20 is recommended (p. 3).
  - nbd(i) ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} codes the bound type (pp. 3–4).
  - factr: the test is (f_k − f_{k+1})/max(|f_{k+1}|, |f_k|, 1) ≤ factr·epsmch (1). Typical values for 15-digit arithmetic are 1.d+12 (low accuracy), 1.d+7 (moderate) and 1.d+1 (extremely high). factr = 0 stops only if f is unchanged after one iteration (p. 4).
  - Workspace: wa of length (2m + 4)n + 12m² + 12m, iwa of length 3n (p. 4).
  - task returns FG, NEW_X, CONV, ABNO or ERROR, and the user may set STOP (pp. 4, 6).
  - iprint takes −1, 0, 1–98, 99, 100 or 101 (pp. 4–5).
  - isbmin = 1 (default, direct primal), 2 (dual) or 3 (CG) (p. 5).
  - User-readable state: isave(30) = iteration, isave(34) = evaluations, dsave(2) = previous f, dsave(13) = ‖proj g‖∞ (p. 5), isave(26) = skipped updates (p. 7).
  - Changes from the algorithm paper:
    - Primal and dual both use the inverse of one 2m×2m block matrix [−D − θ⁻¹YᵀZZᵀY, L_Aᵀ − R_Zᵀ; L_A − R_Z, θSᵀAAᵀS]. The matrix is indefinite but is factorized through Cholesky factors of its blocks (p. 8).
    - The skip test is now yᵀs/(−gᵀs) ≤ epsmch (p. 7), where the algorithm paper had sᵀy > eps‖y‖² (S003 pp. 6, 18).
    - A built-in second stop at ‖proj g‖∞ = 0 (p. 6).
  - Transferable: when the code's safeguard differs from the paper's (here the skip test), print the code's version in the software paper (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: as in c02. Machine precision enters twice, in the factr test and the skip test. Invariance holds under scalar multiples of variables and objective except in the first iteration and in the factr test when factr > 0 (p. 7).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: no proofs. Recovery devices: if the line search finds no sufficient decrease after 20 evaluations, all correction pairs are discarded and the iteration restarts along steepest descent. A second failure ends with ABNORMAL TERMINATION IN LINE SEARCH (pp. 6–7). The same restart is used for a singular or indefinite L-BFGS matrix or submatrix, and for gᵀd ≥ 0 (p. 7).
- **D5 Numerical experiments (numbers)**:
  - *Setup* (p. 8): bound-constrained CUTE problems with n ≥ 5 and unconstrained ones with n ≥ 100. LANCELOT SR1 and exact-Hessian options at all defaults. Sparcstation-2 with 32 Mb. All runs stop at ‖proj g‖∞ ≤ 10⁻⁵ (2).
  - *Markers* (pp. 10–11): * = 9999 evaluations reached; ** = no further progress in f; C1 = test (2) missed but f at least as good as LANCELOT-SR1's; F1 = f worse; C2 = SR1 converged to a different point; F2 = SR1 failed within 9999 evaluations. The shipped driver4 runs all 67 problems with the three subspace options (p. 6).
  - *Table 1.1* (p. 10; 33 bound-constrained problems). m = 5 has five *F1 runs (BQPGAUSS and PALMER1E–PALMER4E, all at the 9999 limit). m = 17 has one *C1 (BQPGAUSS). LANCELOT has no marks. Card writer's count, time of m = 5 against the faster LANCELOT option: faster on 16, tie on 1 (HS45), slower on 11, F1 on 5. In evaluations m = 5 never beats LANCELOT-Hessian (0 of 28 converged rows). Large rows (nfg/time, m = 5 vs SR1 vs Hessian):
    - JNLBRNGA: 332/740.33 vs 24/1263.77 vs 22/1502.96;
    - OBSTCLAE: 258/207.20 vs 7/1442.00 vs 6/1422.62;
    - OBSTCLBM: 146/353.04 vs 7/1106.37 vs 6/2017.70;
    - TORSION6: 362/707.22 vs 10/130.73 vs 9/130.31.
  - *Table 1.2* (p. 11; 34 unconstrained problems). Marks: m = 5 has **C1 on FLETCHBV, FREUROTH and PENALTY3, and m = 17 adds ARWHEAD. LANCELOT-SR1 has *F2 (FLETCHBV) and C2 (NONDIA); LANCELOT-Hessian has *F1 (FLETCHBV) and **C1 (PENALTY3). ### S049 · Large scale unconstrained optimization (The State of the Art in Numerical Analysis, IMA Conference Series 63, OUP 1997, pp. 311–338; doi:10.1093/oso/9780198500148.003.0012)
**Read level**: full (second read; round-1 card in c03). All of file pp. 1–24 was read: abstract (p. 1), §§1–5 and acknowledgements (pp. 2–20), bibliography (pp. 20–24, skimmed). Tables 1–3 (pp. 10, 15, 16) were read from the page images. The title page names Jorge Nocedal as sole author (p. 1), as noted in c03.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: as in the c03 card.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in c03. min f(x) with n large, "say n > 500" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design (numbers and equations)**:
  - Partial separability f(x) = Σ φᵢ(Uᵢx) (2.8), with gradient and Hessian sums (2.9) (p. 4). The minimum-surface element depends on 4 variables, its Hessian has 3 distinct elements, and its invariant subspace has dimension n − 2 (pp. 3–4).
  - Algorithm I is a CG trust-region step with three exits: negative curvature, an iterate leaving the trust region, and the residual test (p. 8).
  - LANCELOT's default preconditioner is an 11-band modified Cholesky factorization (p. 9).
