### S191 · Extending interior point method to nonlinear programming using trust regions (Univ. of Michigan, Ann Arbor, 1994; no DOI)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: T. Plantenga, S. Mehrotra · **Position**: a 1994 record (works.json note: an OSTI-type listing, probably a conference abstract; not verified). It comes before the NITRO/KNITRO interior-point papers S008 (1999) and S009 (2000).
- **D1 problem/entry**: extend the primal barrier interior-point method of linear programming to general nonlinear optimization (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: reinterpret affine scaling as the effect of an ellipsoidal trust region on the subproblems. The log-barrier term is kept for centring with respect to the bounds. Each iteration solves a quadratic subproblem with linearized equality constraints and an ellipsoidal trust region. Iterates stay interior to the bounds but satisfy the equalities only at convergence. On LPs the method reduces to the primal barrier method (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: an algorithm, tested with the Byrd–Omojokun large-scale equality-constrained trust-region code that Lalee, Nocedal and Plantenga had implemented (abstract). The test results themselves are not in the abstract.
**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ evidence (engine half only): the existing Byrd–Omojokun equality-constrained code is the engine, and the new part is the barrier/ellipsoidal treatment of the bounds (abstract). There is no regime-driven estimate or recovery path.
**Quotes** (abstract): "Our fundamental concept is to recast the idea of affine scaling and view it as the result of using an ellipsoidally shaped trust region on iterative subproblems." / "The algorithm reduces to the primal barrier interior point method for linear programming problems."
**Transferable**: when you generalize a method to a wider problem class, show that it reduces exactly to the established method on the special case (here LP) (abstract).

### S192 · Numerical Optimization Techniques (NSF award CCR-9400881, 1994; grant record)
**Read**: abstract (NSF award abstract) · **Role**: supplement · **PI**: J. Nocedal alone · **Position**: NSF grant, Sep. 1994–Aug. 1996, $109,998 (CV p. 4). It is not a publication but states a two-year research programme.

**Quotes** (abstract): "This approach has the advantage of leading readily into a sequence of equality constrained subproblems which can be solved by existing software." / "The algorithm is posed in the framework of trust region methods, and its relationship with sequential quadratic programming is fully exploited."

### S178 · Active-Set and Interior Algorithms for Non-Linear Optimization (NSF award 0514772, 2005; grant record)
**Read**: abstract (NSF award abstract) · **Role**: supplement · **PI**: J. Nocedal · **Position**: the 2005 agenda continuing the LP/EQP active-set line of S035 (2004) and leading to the adaptive-barrier study S029 (2009) (inferred from timing).
