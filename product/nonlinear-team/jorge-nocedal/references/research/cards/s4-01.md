### S028 · A reduced Hessian method for large-scale constrained optimization (SIAM Journal on Optimization 5(2), 314–347, 1995, doi:10.1137/0805017)
**Read level**: full (second read, OCR text). All 36 file pages were read: the abstract and §1 (pp. 2–7), §2 on bases (pp. 7–8), §3 on the corrections, update criterion, damping and penalty (pp. 8–15), Algorithm II (pp. 15–17), §4 (pp. 18–20), §5 (pp. 20–30), §6 (pp. 30–34), §7 (p. 34) and the references (pp. 35–36). Theorem statements and conditions are readable; proof algebra is partly garbled. First page checked: title and "Lorenz T. Biegler, Jorge Nocedal,? and Claudia Schmid" (p. 1); Nocedal is also listed at Argonne's Mathematics and Computer Science Division (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Lorenz T. Biegler, Claudia Schmid (Chemical Engineering, Carnegie Mellon) (p. 1) · **Place in the trajectory**: as in s02; the theory paper of the reduced-Hessian line, whose companion is S067 (card s03).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in s02. Ignoring the cross term Z_kᵀW_kY_kp_Y is "quite adequate" with orthonormal bases (p. 4) but not with the elimination bases (2.2) that large sparse problems need (pp. 4, 7).
- **D2 Key idea, with the equations**:
  - *Steps*: the range step solves (A_kᵀY_k)p_Y = −c_k (1.26, 3.42). The null step solves B_kp_Z = −[Z_kᵀg_k + ζ_kw_k] with damping 0 < ζ_k ≤ 1 and w_k ≈ (Z_kᵀW_kY_k)p_Y (1.17–1.18, 3.43) (pp. 4, 6, 17).
  - *Broyden correction*: S_k ≈ Z_kᵀW_k, an (n − m) × n matrix, with the secant equation S_{k+1}(x_{k+1} − x_k) = Z_k[∇L(x_{k+1}, λ_{k+1}) − ∇L(x_k, λ_{k+1})] (1.20). Then w_k = S_kY_kp_Y and w̄_k = α_kS_{k+1}Y_kp_Y (3.12). It needs no extra evaluations; S_k is stored explicitly, or by the compact limited-memory representation when n − m is not very small (pp. 4, 8, 10).
  - *Finite-difference correction*: w_k = Z_kᵀ[∇L(x_k + Y_kp_Y, λ_k) − ∇L(x_k, λ_k)] (3.1). With (3.2), "up to three evaluations of the objective function gradient may be required at each iteration" (p. 8).
  - *BFGS on B_k*: y_k = Z_kᵀ[∇L(x_{k+1}, λ_{k+1}) − ∇L(x_k, λ_{k+1})] − w̄_k (1.22). The multipliers are λ_k = −[Y_kᵀA_k]⁻¹Y_kᵀg_k (1.25) (p. 5).
  - *Update Criterion I* (p. 12): with Broyden, update only if s_kᵀy_k > 0 and ‖p_Y‖ ≤ γ_k‖p_Z‖ (3.18), {γ_k} summable. With finite differences, update only if s_kᵀy_k > 0 and ‖p_Y‖ ≤ γ_d‖p_Z‖/σ_k^{1/2} (3.19). Here σ_k = max{‖e_k‖, ‖e_{k+1}‖} (1.34); the code uses ‖Z_kᵀg_k‖ + ‖c_k‖ in its place (p. 17).
  - *Three regions* (p. 32): R1 = {‖p_Y‖ ≤ γ_k‖p_Z‖}; R2 = the rest of {‖p_Y‖ ≤ γ_d‖p_Z‖/σ_k^{1/2}}; R3 = {‖p_Y‖ > γ_d‖p_Z‖/σ_k^{1/2}}. The finite-difference recomputation happens only in R2, and the BFGS update is skipped in R3 (pp. 16–17).
  - *Safeguards* (p. 10): (3.13) bounds w_k by a constant Γ, with a garbled right-hand side; (3.14) keeps w̄_k only if ‖w̄_k‖ ≤ α_k‖p_Y‖/γ_k and rescales it otherwise.
  - *Penalty and damping* (p. 15): μ_k = μ_{k−1} if μ_{k−1} ≥ ‖λ_k‖∞ + 2ρ, else μ_k = ‖λ_k‖∞ + 3ρ (3.40). The damping is ζ_k = min{1, ζ̂_k}, where ζ̂_k is the largest value that satisfies (3.38) as an equality (3.39). This gives strong descent on the "good iterates" (3.37) and descent elsewhere (3.41) (p. 15).
  - *Line search*: Armijo with η ∈ (0, 1/2), backtracking within [τα_k, τ′α_k], 0 < τ < τ′ < 1 (pp. 6, 16).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumptions 4.1 (p. 18):
  - iterates in a convex set D on which f, c and their first and second derivatives are uniformly bounded;
  - A of full column rank, with ‖Y[AᵀY]⁻¹‖ ≤ γ0 and ‖Z‖ ≤ β0 (4.1);
  - (3.21)–(3.22) at every update;
  - (4.2) on w_k. Its exponent is garbled. The proof of Lemma 4.1 uses it as ‖w_j‖² ≤ κ²‖c_j‖, i.e. ‖w_k‖ ≤ κ‖c_k‖^{1/2} (read from p. 19).

  Assumptions 5.1 (p. 21): C² functions with Lipschitz Hessians, A(x*) of full rank, a positive definite reduced Hessian G*, bounded bases (5.2)–(5.3), and Lipschitz Z and A (5.4)–(5.5). Assumption 5.2 is the level-set condition: "There is no practical line search algorithm that can guarantee this condition" (p. 22). Unit steps are assumed in §6, and the watchdog is left to the companion paper (p. 30).
- **D4 Analysis (theorem statements)**:
  - *Theorem 3.1* (from Byrd–Nocedal 1989): if s_kᵀy_k/s_kᵀs_k ≥ m > 0 and ‖y_k‖²/y_kᵀs_k ≤ M (3.21–3.22), then cos θ_j ≥ β1 and β2 ≤ ‖B_js_j‖/‖s_j‖ ≤ β3 for at least half of j ∈ [1, k] (pp. 12–13; the count is printed "[$k]" in the OCR). *Lemma 3.1*: near x*, Update Criterion I implies (3.21)–(3.22) (p. 13).
  - *Semi-local*: Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 show that μ_k is eventually constant and lim inf (‖Z_kᵀg_k‖ + ‖c_k‖) = 0 (pp. 18–20). Lemma 5.3 makes x* a point of attraction when μ is large, under assumptions called "restrictive" (pp. 22–23).
  - *Rate in the number of updates*: *Theorem 5.4* gives ‖x_j − x*‖ ≤ C r^{|U_k|}, 0 < r < 1, where U_k is the set of updated iterates up to k (p. 23). *Lemma 5.5*: the finite-difference corrections are second-order accurate, ‖w_k − Z*ᵀW*Y_kp_Y‖ = O(σ_k‖p_Y‖) (5.22) (p. 25).
  - *Theorem 5.6*: ‖B_k‖ and ‖B_k⁻¹‖ stay bounded, and ‖(B_k − Z*ᵀW*Z*)p_Z‖ = o(‖d_k‖) on U. The proof uses ψ(B) = tr(B) − ln det(B) ≥ ln cond(B) (5.14–5.15) (pp. 24–29). *Theorem 5.7*: R-linear convergence "regardless of how often updating takes place" (p. 29).
  - *Safeguards fall away*: by *Lemma 5.8* the S_k are bounded and the safeguards (3.13)–(3.14) are inactive for large k (pp. 29–30). ζ_k = 1 for all large k (p. 30). *Lemma 6.1*: a Dennis–Moré condition for S_k along d_k (6.1) (p. 31).
  - *Theorem 6.3*: given R-linear convergence and α_k = 1 eventually, convergence is 1-step Q-superlinear, proved region by region (R1, R2, R3) (pp. 32–34).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (none; deferred to the companion paper, p. 34).
- **D6 Positioning** (one line): 1-step Q-superlinear against the 2-step rate of cross-term-free methods (Byrd 1985; Yuan 1985) (p. 4). For Coleman–Conn, only {x_k + Z_kp_Z} is 1-step superlinear, not {x_k} (Byrd 1990; Gilbert 1989) (p. 9). Approximating ZᵀW is preferred to Gurwitz's ZᵀWY because it gives 1-step superlinear convergence "in all cases". Coleman–Fenyes need a root choice in a cubic, and Tagliaferro's PSB matrices may become very ill conditioned (p. 11).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: as in s02. The theory paper prints no value for Γ, γ_d, ρ, η, τ, τ′ or {γ_k}: "the choices of all constants and tolerances" go to the companion paper, with the damping computation, basis changes, the criterion away from the solution and inequality constraints (p. 34), and a possible fourth region (p. 17).
- **Corrections to the s02 card**: (1) the region labels are now readable. The "middle" region where s02 places the finite differences is R2, and BFGS is skipped in R3 (pp. 16, 32). (2) s02's D3 bound "‖w_k‖ bounded by a multiple of ‖c_k‖" should read κ‖c_k‖^{1/2} (exponent reconstructed from p. 19). The other s02 statements stand.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (pre-noise; confirms s02 with numbers): the reduced-Hessian SQP engine is kept and only the cross term is repaired. The default estimate (Broyden) costs no evaluations, and the fallback (finite differences, up to three gradient evaluations) is used only in region R2 (pp. 8, 16, 32). The safeguards are proved inactive asymptotically (pp. 29–30). This is also evidence for Heuristic 5 (the cheapest adequate option wins).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the stated rate separates designs: 1-step against 2-step Q-superlinear (p. 4), and ZᵀW against ZᵀWY (p. 11). The theory covers irregular update patterns through C r^{|U_k|} and the R-linear result (pp. 23, 29). This is also evidence for Taste mark 2 (theory that separates methods).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (weak, as in s02): the group's own earlier code's instabilities motivate the paper, and the open choices are deferred by name (pp. 17, 34).
- Heuristic 5 ⚠ variant (numbers as advice): the theory paper prints no parameter values; all of them go to the companion paper (p. 34).
- Candidate pool (08, "specify by admissible conditions so the theory covers the code; proof-driven or rarely binding safeguards"): further evidence. The safeguards scale with 1/γ_k for a summable {γ_k}; they let S_k grow "in a controlled manner" and switch off near x* (pp. 10, 29–30).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Let a safeguard's bound grow along a summable sequence (here 1/γ_k with Σγ_k < ∞). Then prove that the guarded estimate stays bounded, so the safeguard is inactive near the solution and costs no rate (pp. 10, 29–30).
- When updates may be skipped in any pattern, state the rate in the number of updates, ‖x_j − x*‖ ≤ C r^{|U_k|}. Then remove that dependence with the trace–log-det potential ψ(B) = tr(B) − ln det(B) (pp. 23–29).
- With a cheap and a costly estimate of one quantity, divide the step space by the ratio of the step components into regions. Spend the costly estimate only in the region where the rate proof needs it (pp. 16, 32–34).

**Quotes**:
- "We will show that the rate of convergence of the new algorithm is 1-step Q-superlinear, as opposed to the 2-step superlinear rate for methods that ignore the cross term" (p. 4)
- "Thus, up to three evaluations of the objective function gradient may be required at each iteration." (p. 8)

### S088 · Towards a discrete Newton method with memory for large-scale optimization (Nonlinear Optimization and Applications, Plenum, 1–12, 1996, doi:10.1007/978-1-4899-0289-4_1)
**Read level**: partial (second read, OCR text). All 13 file pages were read. Settings, parameters and condition numbers are now legible. Of the tables, Table 1 keeps the ε = 0 rows for L-BFGS and DINEMO and the ε = .09 row for ALTERNATE. Table 2 keeps only its header. Table 3 keeps the ε = .05 row for L-BFGS and the ε = .09 rows for DINEMO and ALTERNATE (pp. 10–11). The other rows and the exponents of the tolerances are lost. First page checked: title, "Richard H. Byrd‘, Jorge Nocedal? and Ciyou Zhu?", "Technical Report OTC 95/01", February 15, 1996 (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Richard H. Byrd, Ciyou Zhu (p. 1) · **Place in the trajectory**: as in s02. "To appear in Nonlinear Optimization and Applications, G. Di Pillo, F. Giannessi, eds, Plenum" (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in s02 (pp. 2–3). The inner-accuracy dilemma: one inner CG iteration reduces the method to steepest descent, and high accuracy approximates Newton's method (p. 3).
- **D2 Key idea, with the equations**:
  - *Base methods*: Hessian-vector products by differences, ∇²f(x_k)v ≈ [g(x_k + εv) − g(x_k)]/ε (1.2) (p. 2). Strong Wolfe conditions with 0 < c1 < c2 < 1 (1.4–1.5) (p. 3). L-BFGS starts from H_k^(0) = γ_{k−1}I with γ_{k−1} = s_{k−1}ᵀy_{k−1}/y_{k−1}ᵀy_{k−1} (2.4), and small memories (m ∈ [3, 20]) "very often give satisfactory results" (p. 4).
  - *Inner pairs*: s^i = εv^i and y^i = g(x_k + εv^i) − g(x_k), i = 1, …, p (3.3). They satisfy (s^i)ᵀy^i > 0 because CG stops on negative curvature (p. 6). The inner CG stops when ‖r^i‖ ≤ c‖g(x_k)‖ (p. 6).
  - *Open choices*: five numbered variations (p. 7). One is the Newton-step trigger, at a regular frequency ("say 1 discrete-truncated Newton step for every 10 limited memory steps") or by an automatic criterion (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: strong Wolfe steps (p. 3); strongly convex test functions, so no negative-curvature test is needed (p. 8); no convergence theory.
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a (no proofs).
- **D5 Experiments: settings**:
  - *Codes* (p. 8): three codes share the Moré–Thuente line search with c2 = 0.9 (c1's exponent is garbled). DINEMO and ALTERNATE take a discrete Newton step at iterations 6, 16, 26, … The inner CG stops when ‖r^i‖² < 10^(exponent lost) or after 20 CG iterations.
  - *DINEMO* (p. 8): it clears the memory before each Newton step and stores the p inner pairs and the outer pair. It scales by the outer pair and updates γI p + 1 times, inner pairs first. With m = 29 and maxcg = 20, corrections are discarded only before a Newton step.
  - *ALTERNATE* (p. 8): identical, except that the matrix after a Newton step is γI updated once with the outer pair.
  - *Test family* (pp. 8–9): a quartic in n = 100 variables built on (x − 1)ᵀD(x − 1), with σ controlling the deviation from a quadratic and B = UᵀU (the formula is partly garbled), started from x0_i = (−1)^i × 50. D (4.1) with ε = 0, 0.05, 0.09 has condition numbers 1, 125 and 5073 (p. 9), and σ ∈ {0, .06, .12, .18} (table headers, pp. 10–11).
  - *Stopping and counting*: the tests are f(x_k) ≤ 1 + 10^(−?) and g(x_k)ᵀg(x_k) ≤ 10^(−?) (4.2–4.3), with "*" when only (4.3) holds. Function and gradient are evaluated together and their joint count is reported (p. 9).
- **D5 Experiments: legible results**:
  - At ε = σ = 0 all three codes need 6 evaluations, because the unit step is always tried first (p. 9).
  - *Table 1* (p. 10): at ε = 0 and σ = .06/.12/.18, L-BFGS needs 131/138/151 evaluations and DINEMO 110/115/115, i.e. 16–24% fewer (computed here). At ε = .09, ALTERNATE needs 1084, 936*, 811* and 868; the other rows are lost. The text says that L-BFGS "ends up being the winner" at ε = .09 (p. 10).
  - *Table 2*: D (4.4) keeps the 5 smallest and 6 largest diagonal entries of (4.1) and sets the rest to 1, giving 12 distinct eigenvalues. All entries are lost. The text says DINEMO "performs consistently better" (pp. 10–11), but since 20 CG steps solve the Newton equations exactly at σ = 0, this case "may be too simple" (p. 11).
  - *Table 3*: the middle diagonal entries are i/10, i = 6, …, 94, i.e. 89 values in [0.6, 9.4], with the condition numbers unchanged (p. 11). At ε = .05, L-BFGS needs 102/194/190/191 (σ = 0/.06/.12/.18); DINEMO's row is lost. At ε = .09, DINEMO needs 222/333/274/331 and ALTERNATE 364/405/444*/435, i.e. 18–39% fewer for DINEMO (computed here); L-BFGS's row is lost (p. 11). The text: DINEMO is comparable to L-BFGS at ε = .05 and better at ε = .09 (condition number 5073) (p. 12). The Table 3 caption reads "(4.1)" in the text layer, although the table belongs to the third experiment (p. 12).
  - *Also stated*: DINEMO and ALTERNATE took a "substantially smaller number of iterations" than L-BFGS. With 25 smallest and 26 largest eigenvalues as in (4.1) and the rest 1, DINEMO "clearly outperformed" both rivals for all ε. The more inner information is collected, the better DINEMO does (p. 12).

- **Corrections to the s02 card**: none. The legible entries bear out s02's D5. s02's "very stringent stopping test" now has its form (4.2)–(4.3), though not its exponents (p. 9). The eigenvalue-distribution claim rests on Table 2, lost here, and on the legible half of Table 3.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (with numbers): the ablation arm differs from DINEMO only in discarding the inner pairs. The three codes share the line search (c2 = 0.9), the Newton schedule (iterations 6, 16, 26, …), m = 29 and maxcg = 20 (p. 8). Evaluations are "the key measure", and iterations are mentioned too (p. 12). ⚠ One test family, n = 100 and one starting point (pp. 8–9).

- Report percentages only where both arms' rows are legible, and say which conclusion rests on a lost table (pp. 10–12).

### S046 · On the local behavior of an interior point method for nonlinear programming (Numerical Analysis 1997, 37–56, 1997; no DOI; author copy http://users.iems.northwestern.edu/~nocedal/PDFfiles/dundee.pdf)
**Read level**: full (second read, OCR text). All 19 file pages were read: the abstract and §1 (pp. 1–6), §2 (pp. 6–10), §3 (pp. 10–14), §4 (p. 14), §5 (pp. 14–18), §6 (p. 18) and the references (p. 19). Theorem statements and most conditions are readable. Some exponents and inequalities are garbled; where one is reconstructed from its restatement in §5, the card says so. First page checked: title, "Richard H. Byrd* Guanghui Liu # Jorge Nocedal?", "Report OTC 98/02", January 24, 1998 (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Richard H. Byrd, Guanghui Liu (p. 1) · **Place in the trajectory**: as in s02; the rate paper for the Byrd–Hribar–Nocedal algorithm [2] (OTC 97/05), whose global framework is Byrd–Gilbert–Nocedal [1] (OTC 96/02) (pp. 2, 19).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as in s02. [2] is robust and "competitive with the LANCELOT algorithm in terms of function evaluations", but "No attempt was made in [2] to make this new algorithm rapidly convergent" (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea, with the equations**:
  - *Barrier problem*: minimize φ_μ(x, s) = f(x) − μΣ_{i=1}^t ln s_i subject to h(x) = 0 and g(x) + s = 0, with s > 0 (1.2–1.3) (p. 2).
  - *Subproblem*: an SQP step with the scaled trust region ‖(d_x, S⁻¹d_s)‖ ≤ Δ and the fraction-to-the-boundary rule d_s ≥ −τs, τ "close to 1" (1.5, 1.7) (p. 3). The merit function is φ_μ + ν‖c‖₂ (1.8), and the primal-dual Hessian has a S⁻¹Λ block (1.9) (p. 3).
  - *Barrier test*: ‖F_μ(z⁺)‖ ≤ ε_μ (1.11), with F_μ = (∇L; SΛe − μe; h; g + s) (1.12) (p. 4).
  - *Inexact step*: F′(z)d = −F_μ(z) + r_μ (1.16) (p. 5). The Jacobian F′ does not depend on μ, so neither does the region of fast convergence (p. 6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumptions I as in s02 (pp. 4–5). The analysed iteration z⁺ = z + d "is an idealization" of the coded one (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis (theorem statements)**:
  - *Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2*: ‖F′(z)⁻¹‖ ≤ M with a second-order bound L on N(z*), and ‖z*(μ) − z*‖ ≤ Cμ (p. 6).
  - *Theorem 2.3*: ‖z⁺ − z*(μ)‖ ≤ ML‖z − z*(μ)‖² + M‖r_μ‖ (p. 7). *Lemma 2.4*: ‖z − z*(μ)‖ ≤ 2M‖F_μ(z)‖ and ‖F_μ(z)‖ ≤ 2Q‖z − z*(μ)‖ (p. 7).
  - *Theorem 2.5* (p. 8): let ‖r_{μ+}‖ ≤ M1‖F_{μ+}(z)‖^{1+a}. Then μ⁺ = o(‖F(z)‖) with a > 0 gives superlinear convergence, and μ⁺ = O(‖F(z)‖²) with a > 1 (as the OCR reads; possibly ≥) gives quadratic convergence. A residual bound M1(μ⁺)^{1+a} also works (p. 9). *Corollary 2.6* covers least-squares multipliers (p. 10).
  - *Theorem 3.1* (pp. 10–11): ‖z⁺ − z*(μ⁺)‖ ≤ M′(ε_μ + μ)² + M‖r_{μ+}‖, with M′ = 4ML(max{C, M})². A heuristic argument (3.5), admittedly "not rigorous", suggests that a quadratic decrease of μ can violate feasibility (p. 11).
  - *Theorem 3.2* (p. 12): if [(μ + ε_μ)² + ‖r_{μ+}‖]/μ⁺ → 0 and lim sup (1 − τ)(μ + ε_μ)/μ⁺ < 1, then s⁺ ≥ (1 − τ)s. (3.6)–(3.7) are garbled and were read from their restatement as (5.1)–(5.2) (pp. 14–15).
  - *§4*: the barrier test holds after one step if [(ε_μ + μ)² + ‖r_{μ+}‖]/ε_{μ+} → 0 (4.1). With ε_μ = θμ this fails if μ decreases quadratically (p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (none).
- **D8 Contributions and limits, with the strategies of §5**:
  - *Tolerance*: ε_μ = θμ with θ ∈ [0, √t) (5.5), where t is the number of inequalities (p. 15).
  - *Strategy 1* (pp. 15–16): μ⁺ = γμ with constant γ, τ ∈ (0, 1), θ = 1 and ‖r‖ ≤ M1μ^{1+a}. It keeps the slacks positive and passes the barrier test, for instance with θ = 1, τ = 0.995 and γ = 0.2, "which are the parameter settings in [2]". Then (1 − τ)(1 + θ)/γ = 0.05 < 1 (computed here). But the rate is "only linear".
  - *Strategy 2* (pp. 16–17): μ⁺ = μ^{1+δ} with 0 < δ < 1, τ = 1 − μ^β with β > δ, and the residual tied to a power of μ: superlinear. The exponents are partly reconstructed from the verification algebra.
  - *Strategy 3* (pp. 17–18): μ⁺ = ‖F(z)‖^{1+δ}, τ = 1 − ‖F(z)‖^β with β > δ, and ‖r_{μ+}‖ = ‖F_{μ+}(z)‖^{1+a} with a > δ. It is feasible, passes the barrier test and converges superlinearly.
  - *Complementarity variants* (5.15): μ⁺ = (max λ_is_i)^{1+δ} or [(Σλ_is_i)/t]^{1+δ}, "similar to some of the rules used in linear programming algorithms [13]", where [13] is S. J. Wright's *Primal-Dual Interior Point Methods* (pp. 18–19).
  - *Limits*: as in s02. No values are recommended for δ, β or a.
- **D6 Positioning** (one line): unlike Yamashita–Yabe [12] and El-Bakry–Tapia–Tsuchiya–Zhang [4], it keeps the slacks positive without a backtracking line search and handles an inner barrier stopping test (pp. 18–19).
- **Corrections to the s02 card**: none of substance. s02's "Report OTC" is OTC 98/02, dated January 24, 1998 (p. 1). s02's D4, written with the exponents lost, now has the exact conditions of Theorem 2.5 (p. 8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove the shipped defaults first: check that the code's current settings (θ = 1, τ = 0.995, γ = 0.2) meet the safety conditions, then state exactly which property they give up (the rate) (p. 16).
- Tie three free parameters (μ⁺, τ, the residual) to one measured quantity, the optimality residual ‖F(z)‖. A single quantity then drives feasibility, the inner stopping test and the rate together (pp. 17–18).
- Write tolerances as multiples of the barrier parameter (ε_μ = θμ). The stopping test, fraction-to-boundary and rate conditions then reduce to a few limits in μ and μ⁺ that can be checked strategy by strategy (pp. 15–18).

- **D2 Key idea, with the equations**:
  - *Ideal norm*: ‖s‖²_{|H|} = (s, |H|s) with |G| = U|Λ|Uᵀ, which needs a spectral factorization that is "likely out of the question" for large n (pp. 3–4).
  - *Proposed norm*: M = PL|B|LᵀPᵀ from H = PLBLᵀPᵀ (3.4–3.6) (p. 4). After the change of variables (3.7), the subproblem is block-diagonal and then diagonal, with D = |Θ|^{-1/2}Θ|Θ|^{-1/2} having entries ±1 (pp. 5–6).
  - *Convex case* (D = I): s = −g_δ if ‖g_δ‖ ≤ Δ, else s = −Δg_δ/‖g_δ‖. Reducing the radius then backtracks along −H⁻¹g (p. 6).
  - *Indefinite case*: λ is the root larger than 1 of a quartic, found by Newton's method, with an eigenvector term in the Moré–Sorensen hard case (pp. 6–7).
  - *Near-singular H*: Cheng–Higham's perturbation (3.13), or the alternative (3.14), "closer in spirit" to the absolute-value and Greenstadt perturbations, both acting on eigenvalues of modulus below δ (formulas partly garbled, p. 7).
  - *Algorithm 3.1*: Newton's method on the secular equation, stopping when |‖s‖ − Δ| ≤ εΔ, with ε at machine-precision level. It is "globally linearly and asymptotically quadratically convergent" from its starting values (pp. 8–9).
