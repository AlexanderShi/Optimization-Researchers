## 1. The mentoring he received (L7, L2, L3)

How he was trained explains how he trained others. All [stated, primary] unless marked.

- **Grötschel: freedom and a well-timed application.** His advisor gave him the patience and freedom to do whatever he liked; he wrote: "For almost two years, I did not publish a single paper!" (P1 p. ix). Grötschel then connected him to Infineon's chip-verification group, which became his application (P1 p. ix; P10 0:28:30). In 2023 he repeats that Grötschel gave him complete freedom, with only occasional project duties (P10 0:28:00). He believes, without being sure, that Grötschel got the organisers of the 2005 IMA MIP workshop to squeeze his conflict-analysis talk into the programme, which is where the ILOG people approached him (P10 0:35:30–0:37:00).
- **Learning by reading a working solver.** His computer-science master's thesis (begun 2000) tried to learn branching rules with machine learning. Alexander Martin let him use the source of his solver SIP, and with Thorsten Koch he learned by analysing its algorithms (P1 p. ix; P10 0:24:00). He took Grötschel's LP and combinatorial-optimisation course cycle and coded a simplex method in C in its exercises, then compared it with CPLEX (P10 0:19:30–0:21:00).
- **A negative result turned into a rule.** The learners mostly told him that one feature mattered, a pseudocost-like "history value" he had invented; he dropped the machine learning and kept the feature, which became the reliability-branching paper with Koch and Martin (P10 0:25:00–0:26:00; J1).
- **Koch: stop tuning, start writing.** After passing his self-set deadline (finish before turning 30) without having started to write, Koch convinced him that writing mattered more than another 10 % of SCIP speed (P1 p. x). [observed, secondary] Koch had listed him among the proof-readers of his own 2004 thesis (S7 PDF p. 8): proofreading ran both ways.
- **Learning a field from a person.** Yakov Novikov explained SAT conflict analysis and the two-watched-literals scheme to him during a subway ride; he implemented watched literals a few days later and generalised conflict analysis to MIP within three weeks (P1 p. x; P10 0:31:30–0:32:30; the paper is J20).
- **An industry contact who delivers data.** He thanks Raik Brinkmann, the industry partner's contact person: he received lots of test data early enough to be useful, which made the project different from most ZIB projects (P1 p. ix).
- **Readers.** Marc Pfetsch read and commented on about 75 % of the thesis; other readers were Berthold, Heinz, Koch, Markus Wedler, and Lloyd Clarke and Zonghao Gu from ILOG (P1 p. x). [practice, primary] Robert E. Bixby was the second Berichter (P1 title page).

### 2.2 Supervision style, according to the students (all secondary)

- **Recruit with a primer and a concrete solver task.** [observed, Berthold, S3 p. v] When Berthold arrived at ZIB, Achterberg gave him a ten-minute primer on how a MIP solver really works and said he was looking for a student assistant to improve SCIP's heuristics by implementing a Feasibility Pump and Local Branching. Berthold saw a diploma topic in it, and it became one.
- **Hand over readable literature first.** [observed, S3 p. v] The first research papers Achterberg gave him, the first Berthold had ever read, were works by Matteo Fischetti and Andrea Lodi, which he found accessible. (Compare Achterberg's self-description in §4 and Contradiction 3.)
- **Apprenticeship over about two years.** [observed, S3 p. v] Berthold says he was taught the secrets of computational MIP in those two years. [practice] Their first joint report came out about eight months after Berthold's first commit: Jan 2005 → ZIB-Report 05-42, Sept 2005 (P9, P4).
- **Advice, encouragement, support.** [observed] Berthold (S1 p. iii) and Wolter (S2 p. i) both put him first in their thanks, ahead of the formal supervisor.
- **An open door for other people's bugs.** [observed, S6] Orlowski kept coming into his and Wolter's offices with questions about SCIP or CPLEX behaviour and got patient help with debugging his own code. [stated, primary] Achterberg jokes about colleagues who come into his office uninvited to ask strange questions about SCIP and CPLEX (P1 p. ix). [inferred] Both describe the same habit from the two sides.
- **Reading drafts.** [observed] Raack thanks him for reading drafts in 2005 and for reading extremely large parts of his 2012 thesis (S4; S5 p. v).

### 2.3 Group habits the theses show (practice)

- **Credit is assigned per component, in prose.** [practice, primary] P1 names who implemented what: RENS, Octane and all improvement heuristics by Berthold, the feasibility pump jointly, the rest by himself (p. 118); the cut chapters partly joint with Wolter, Gomory, knapsack-cover and reduced-cost code based on Martin's SIP (p. 101). [practice, secondary] Berthold's diploma does the same from the other side: some heuristic plugins were implemented by Achterberg, the others by Berthold (S1 pp. 9, 16, 29).
- **Credit in the code.** [practice, primary] Commits add collaborators to file headers: Timo Berthold to `scip.c` and `scip.h` (87656a2c, 2006-05-15; at that commit `scip.c` lists Achterberg, Berthold, Koch, Martin and Wolter), Christian Raack to the MCF separator (ed966d2b, 2008-07-09; `sepa_mcf.c` lists Achterberg and Raack), and in 2016 Michael Winkler and himself to the Gurobi LP interface, whose header also names Marc Pfetsch (ce4f13c5) (P9). Three April 2006 commits name the external user who reported the bug; a June 2006 commit names the contributor of the updated Xpress interface (P9).
- **Benchmark every component against the commercial state of the art.** [practice, secondary] Wolter compared SCIP 0.81's separators with those of CPLEX 10.01 and Cbc 1.01.00, switching off presolve (and probing in CPLEX) so that the cuts were measured on their own (S2 §7.1). Her thesis first studies each separator basically in isolation and only then its effect on the whole solver (S2 p. 1). [practice, primary] P1 measures SCIP 0.90i against CPLEX 10 (on average only 63 % slower, p. 6), and the thesis benchmark logs were put on the SCIP website when it was submitted (S11, news of 14 May 2007).
- **A student's measurement changes the mentor's defaults, and the next measurement checks it.** [practice, primary] Wolter found SCIP's Gomory cuts inferior to CPLEX's and Cbc's in root gap closed; the separator was made more aggressive; P1's Table 8.1 then showed that this overshot, and a conservative, numerically safer variant performed better (P1 p. 113). See §7.

## 4. Collaborators and team culture in industry (CPLEX 2006–2013, Gurobi 2014–)

All [stated, primary, P10 caption text] unless marked.

- **Choosing an employer.** After the 2005 workshop talk ILOG approached him; Dash Optimization (Xpress) made a counter-offer; he chose ILOG partly because he knew CPLEX from his studies (0:36:30–0:38:30).
- **Hiring someone who wrote a solver.** At CPLEX he first implemented reliability branching, an idea Zonghao Gu had considered doing himself but kept for him, then conflict analysis and everything else he knew worked in SCIP: one success after the other. His general lesson is that someone who has written their own solver brings a large arsenal of things known to work (0:40:00–0:41:00).
- **Why Gurobi.** Bixby had asked him to join a few years before 2014; he declined because CPLEX was his baby and the team was great. When he did join, a main reason was to work again with Gu and, for the first time, with Ed Rothberg, whom he knew from CPLEX off-sites as a main source of new ideas. Bixby no longer coded and discussed ideas with him (0:43:00–0:46:00; personal reasons omitted, see the excerpt header). [observed, secondary] Bixby in 2016: the real MIP contributions at CPLEX and Gurobi were Rothberg's and Gu's, "And now by Tobias Achterberg." (S12 p. 5). [observed, secondary, caption] Bixby in 2021, on the assumption that Gurobi's first release was about as fast as CPLEX 11: he thinks "toby" would argue with that (S16; identification of "toby" inferred).
- **Division of labour on a new solver part.** For the non-convex MIQCP solver (version 9.0, by his recollection) he did most of the work on the non-convex constraints in the tree and the LP relaxation; a colleague implemented the nonlinear interior-point solver; he enjoyed designing the new data structures (0:46:30–0:47:30).
- **Infrastructure as a contribution.** One of his first and main contributions at Gurobi was convincing the team to move from Subversion to git, later with a GitHub-based issue workflow (0:48:00–0:48:30). [practice, primary] SCIP itself started in CVS with his commit of an initial directory structure on 2002-10-23 (P9).
- **Building on colleagues' ideas.** Rothberg's NoRel heuristic was implemented by Rothberg first and then extended and parallelised (0:59:30). Detecting neural-network layers in a MIP was done together with Pierre Bonami and inspired by a Fischetti paper (1:00:00–1:01:00; [inferred] probably Fischetti and Jo 2018, F1).
- **On team members of this roundtable.** On Marco Lübbecke's group's GCG in Aachen (identification inferred from caption text): mixed results, very good for some problem classes and useless for others; he does not yet see how to put it into a solver's defaults, so Gurobi offers an interface where users name the components and exploits them heuristically (1:01:30–1:02:30). On John J. H. Forrest: he doubts Forrest cares about credit and thinks he just loves to implement, like himself (0:50:00).
- **Reliance on collaborators for the literature.** He is better at thinking a problem through himself than at knowing what is out there, reading papers or following others' work, so he needs collaborators who know it; he is bad at extending other people's things: "I always want to do this myself" (1:20:30–1:21:00). Writing the literature review of a paper is the hardest part for him. Not knowing John Hooker's work when he started SCIP cost him a collaboration (1:21:30–1:22:00).

## 5. Teaching (L7)

- **2005 block course.** [practice, primary, co-authored with Grötschel and Koch] In October 2005 ZIB held a two-week block course with more than 80 hours of lectures and exercises and over 100 participants from 10 countries, using ZIMPL, SCIP and SoPlex for most exercises (P2 p. 3; the abstract places the course at TU Berlin); each morning a lecture introduced a problem area, each afternoon students modelled and solved it on their own with the data; students brought their own laptops and took the software home (P2 p. 9). In a Bayreuth course students with a few hours of experience beat the lecturer's n-Queens formulation (P2 p. 9). P2 does not say which of the three authors taught what.
- **Teaching philosophy, co-authored.** [stated, primary, joint] Black-box commercial software cannot teach scientific method because students cannot check what happened; source code lets students look behind the scenes and extend the solver, for example by writing their own primal heuristic (P2 pp. 2, 4); choosing the right model often matters more than the best solver, and mathematically equivalent formulations can behave totally differently (P2 p. 6). [inferred] The advanced-course exercise P2 proposes is how Berthold was trained (§2.2).
- **CO@Work 2015.** [practice, secondary] He lectured on integer programming (5 Oct 2015) and, with Bixby, on the Gurobi Optimizer (6 Oct 2015) at ZIB's CO@Work school (S13). Content not read.
- **Advice to users.** [stated, primary] Improve the model before tuning parameters, and tune on a set of instances, never one, to avoid over-tuning (P10 1:07:30).

## 6. Tacit knowledge: what the group knew that the papers do not say

- **Most commercial MIP progress is unpublishable.** [stated, primary] By his estimate about 10 % of the time goes to research and 90 % to software design, architecture, bug fixing, benchmarking and tweaking; changing one constant in the branching rule to 1e-5 (the old exponent is garbled in the captions) can give 2 % and is no paper (P10 0:52:30–0:53:00). Academics lack the time and the team for this; in his view reviewers wrongly demand that a new technique beat Gurobi or Xpress instead of improving its own baseline, for example SCIP (0:50:30–0:51:30, 0:53:30).
- **Defaults come from mass testing, not intuition.** [stated, primary] Gut feeling, a test, often failure, then the opposite, which sometimes works for no known reason; 192 machines run 24/7; the test set of about 6,000 instances with five random seeds takes, by his rough estimate, about 12 hours on a free cluster (P10 1:05:00–1:05:30; 0:52:00). Machine learning is used to find explainable rules, such as decision trees, which are then reimplemented by hand, for example the choice whether to dualise an LP and which simplex to use (1:16:00–1:17:30). [inferred] This mirrors his 2000 thesis outcome (§1).
- **Presolve rewards, cuts deceive.** [stated, primary] A presolve idea that shrinks the problem usually improves performance; a cut that looks great can still make performance worse (P10 0:41:30).
- **Facts that travel by personal communication.** [observed] Hidden CPLEX parameters, how the primal integral is used in tuning and which heuristics commercial solvers have without publishing them (S3; §3).
- **Benchmarking on borrowed machines (2007).** [stated, primary] His thesis runs used the spare CPU cycles of seven colleagues whose machines were identical to his (P1 p. x).

## 7. Failures, abandoned directions and negative results

- **Machine-learned branching (about 2000–2001).** [stated, primary] The learning techniques mostly pointed to one hand-made feature; he dropped them (P10 0:25:00–0:26:00). The surviving idea is J1.
- **Two years without a paper.** [stated, primary] He spent almost two years coding with no publication (P1 p. ix); a risk his advisor accepted.
- **Missed deadline.** [stated, primary] He passed his self-set thesis deadline and needed Koch's push to stop tuning (P1 p. x). He wrote the thesis while working full time at ILOG and says it is not something he would recommend (P10 0:39:30; that passage is left out of the saved excerpt for privacy).
- **Gomory overshoot.** [practice, primary] The more aggressive Gomory setting chosen after Wolter's comparison turned out worse than a conservative one (P1 p. 113).
- **Missed collaboration.** [stated, primary] He did not know Hooker's integration of CP and MIP when he started SCIP (P10 1:21:30–1:22:00).
- **Unexplained change of venue.** [practice, secondary] Berthold's diploma bibliography (September 2006) lists the feasibility-pump report as "To appear in Operations Research" (S1, ref. [3]); it appeared in the Discrete Optimization special issue on MIP (J3; P1 ref. [2]). No source read explains the change; do not call it a rejection.
