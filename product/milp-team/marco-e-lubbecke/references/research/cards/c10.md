### S019 · The SCIP Optimization Suite 10.0 (arXiv preprint 2025, arXiv:2511.18580)
**Read level**: full. pp. 1–40 read in full (all sections, Tables 1–8, acknowledgments, contributions statement) and the author affiliations pp. 46–49; the reference list pp. 40–46 skimmed (entries [15], [32], [42], [43], [46], [75], [95], [106] checked).
**Role**: core · **Co-authors**: C. Hojny (first) and 33 others in alphabetical order, among them the RWTH group J. Ehls, J. Lentz, T. Mohr, E. Mühmer, C. Reinartz Groba (pp. 1, 46–48) · **Position in trajectory**: 2015–2026 consolidation; the GCG 4.0 release.
**Voice**: 35 authors. The contributions statement credits every GCG 4.0 feature to someone else (Mohr, Mühmer, Lentz, Ehls, Reinartz Groba, and S. J. Maher of GAMS) and gives Lübbecke the role of coordinating and supervising GCG's development, the same formula it uses for the ZIB group lead (pp. 39–40). §4 (pp. 26–33) is read as his group's practice under his supervision; the SCIP, PaPILO, SoPlex, UG, Zimpl, interface and MIP-DD sections are other teams' work that he co-signs. No passage is his alone.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: A release report, not a research question: its goal is to highlight what the new release makes available, with the focus on SCIP (p. 2). The performance claims enter through the change since SCIP 9.0, measured against both the previous major release and the latest bugfix release 9.2.4, so that robustness gained by bugfixes is separated from the effect of new features (p. 4). The GCG section enters through practical frictions: an API where users had to know whether a function wants the original or a master problem (p. 27); users who want to branch on fractional sums of master variables when the master is aggregated (p. 27); a plain-text DEC format that makes advanced features hard to add (pp. 28–29); general MILP pricing problems (p. 31); pricing parallelization that used to be off at compile time (p. 32); a score refactoring that was never documented (p. 32).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: For GCG the unchanged core is the synchronized work on two SCIP instances, the original and the reformulated master, with branching, cutting, bounds, solutions and propagations mirrored and the relaxed master solved by column generation in a relaxator at every node (p. 26). What changed: (i) *extended master constraints*, rows that live only in the master and carry non-robust branching decisions or cuts; the pricing problem gets one coefficient variable y per such row, constrained to a domain Y(x, z) with auxiliary *inferred* variables z; the modifications are applied and undone per node, and dual stabilization covers them (pp. 27–28); (ii) the component bound branching rule (Vanderbeck 2000) as the example built on that interface (p. 28); (iii) JDEC, a JSON decomposition format for partial, complete and nested decompositions with symmetry representatives (pp. 28–31); (iv) a GCG pricing solver that reformulates pricing problems recursively up to the depth set by `pricingsolver/gcg/maxdepth`, a HiGHS pricing solver, and a revised Cliquer solver (pp. 31–32); (v) revised parallel pricing, compiled in by default but run with one thread by default (p. 32); (vi) the IPColGen matheuristic of Maher and Rönnberg, with new pricing callbacks (p. 32); (vii) decomposition scores as plugins (pp. 32–33); (viii) the licence moved from LGPL to Apache 2.0 with the approval of all contributing code authors (p. 26). Suite features by other authors: exact rational MILP mode with VIPR certificates (pp. 6–12), implied-integer detection via network submatrices (pp. 11–14), reflection symmetries (pp. 14–17), cut-based conflict analysis (pp. 17–18), the flower separator (pp. 18–19), (decomposition) kernel search (pp. 19–20), ancestral pseudocosts and probabilistic lookahead (pp. 21–22), Benders updates (pp. 22–24), an IIS finder (pp. 24–25), CONOPT (p. 25), PaPILO 3.0 (pp. 33–35), MIP-DD (pp. 37–38).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: SCIP solves CIPs; MILP form (1) with general integers and bounds that may be infinite, MINLP form (2) with algebraic, possibly nonconvex constraints (p. 3). GCG's Dantzig–Wolfe setting is min{c⊤x : Ax ≥ b, Dx ≥ d, x ∈ Zⁿ} with X = conv{x ∈ Zⁿ : Dx ≥ d}, a discretized integer master (4) over integer points and rays with one convexity row (one block is shown), the linking (5) and the pricing problem min{(c⊤ − π⊤A)x − π₀ : x ∈ X} (p. 27); as extracted, the ray term of (5) carries λp where λr is meant (p. 27). Structure-specific parts: Ryan–Foster branching for set-partitioning-type masters (p. 28), IPColGen only for set covering, packing or partitioning masters (p. 32), Cliquer for weighted independent set pricing problems and close variants (p. 32). Benders: linking variables are matched by identical names in master and subproblem; integer and no-good cuts need binary linking variables (p. 23). Exact mode: MILPs with rational data only (pp. 7, 11). Implied integrality needs integral A and d, integral or infinite bounds and a totally unimodular block B, detected only as network or transposed network matrices (pp. 12–13). Test data: 349 MILP instances from MIPLIB 2003, 2010, 2017 and COR@L and 169 MINLPLib instances, in both cases those that earlier releases could solve (p. 4); the MIPLIB 2017 benchmark for exact mode (p. 11) and 1,035 collection instances for implied integers (pp. 13–14).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: GCG's robust/non-robust device: through (5), integrality of λ implies integrality of x but not conversely; a branching decision on a fractional original variable becomes a master row via (5) or simply a variable bound in pricing, so tailored pricing usually stays applicable; non-robust rows need y ∈ Y(x, z) in pricing, may add real cost or destroy a tailored pricing algorithm, and can be stronger (pp. 27–28). The JDEC reader ignores unknown fields (p. 29); the recursive pricing solver detects structure only when no nested structure is supplied for all blocks and the depth limit is not reached (p. 31); the Cliquer solver can reject requests by properties of the constructed graph such as node count or density (p. 32); multithreaded pricing may change the solving path, so one thread is the default until a deterministic mode exists (p. 32). Suite devices (other authors): plugins carry an exact-safe flag and only flagged ones run in exact mode (pp. 7–8); safe dual bounds by bound shift and project-and-shift, with the exact LP as fallback and at listed points (root end, depths 2^k, near-integral solutions, bounds near the cutoff, all integers fixed) (p. 9); VIPR certificates built from conical combinations, Chvátal–Gomory rounding and disjunction unsplitting, with presolve left uncertified (pp. 10–11); implied-integrality validity by Hoffman–Kruskal after fixing x (p. 13); flower separation brought from O(|E|^{k+1}) to O(|E|) with overlap sets (p. 18); ancestral pseudocosts with a conservative fallback to hybrid branching when either level is unreliable (p. 21); domination arcs compressed into a directed forest so PaPILO's parallel transactions do not conflict (p. 34); delta debugging that keeps the error reproducible while shrinking the instance (pp. 37–38).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Suite protocol (§2): SCIP 10.0 against 9.0 and 9.2.4 with every linked library version stated; 349 MILP instances × 3 seeds and 169 MINLP instances × 5 seeds (1,047 and 845 runs); identical single-threaded machines (Intel Xeon Gold 5122, 96 GB), one run per machine; 5,400 s (MILP) and 3,600 s (MINLP), MINLP counted solved at 10⁻⁴ relative or 10⁻⁶ absolute gap (p. 4); solved counts and shifted geometric means of time (shift 1 s) and nodes (shift 100) on *all* (numerically inconsistent results excluded), *affected* (dual simplex iteration counts differ), difficulty brackets [t, T] and *both-solved* (p. 5). Results: MILP 888 vs 886 solved, time ×0.98 and nodes ×1.02 against 9.0, time ×0.92 on [100, timelim]; 888 vs 883 and time ×0.96 against 9.2.4 (pp. 5–6); MINLP time ×0.94 and ×0.91 (pp. 6–7). Losses are reported (bugfixes slowed 9.2.4 against 9.0; more nodes; p. 5); exclusions show only as count differences (1,047 runs vs 1,046 in *all*; 845 vs 809 and 816; pp. 4, 6–7); no significance test. Component studies give less protocol: exact mode on the MIPLIB 2017 benchmark with 2 h and 3 seeds solved 161 instances against 235 (fp-reduced) and 342 (fp-default), slowdown 2.6–3.6 against the reduced and 6.8–10.8 against the default variant, the table taken from Eifler's thesis, no hardware stated (pp. 11–12); implied-integer detection statistics on 1,035 instances (5 excluded for memory, 25 solved in presolve), its performance impact only called relatively neutral, with no table (pp. 13–14); the flower separator gains on affected subsets of 14, 21 and 11 instances (p. 19); CONOPT against Ipopt on MINLPLib instances SCIP 8.0.0 solved within an hour (p. 25). **GCG: no computational result for any GCG 4.0 feature** (pp. 26–33); the acknowledgments thank ZIB and RWTH for the computing used for countless test runs (p. 39).
- **D6 Positioning**: One report in the SCIP series (the 7.0 report cited for decomposition information, p. 20; the 8.0 report for nonlinear branching scores, p. 22). GCG is cited through Gamrath and Lübbecke 2010 [42] (p. 2), and decomposition theory is handed to the *Branch-and-Price* cahier G-2024-36 [32] as the recent reference (p. 26), also for the component bound rule (p. 28); rules are credited to Vanderbeck [106], Ryan–Foster [95] and Maher–Rönnberg [75] (pp. 28, 32). Suite level: Gurobi's published 13% time and 18% node gains from implied integers are set against SCIP's neutral result (pp. 12–13); priority claims for MIP-DD as the first open-source delta debugger for MIP solvers (pp. 1, 37) and Pseudo-Boolean competition wins (pp. 3, 33, 39).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Release-report form: one subsection per feature with its parameter (e.g. `pricing/masterpricer/nthreads`, `heuristics/ipcolgen/freq`, `detection/scores/selected`; pp. 32–33), status hedges (experimental, disabled by default, planned for future releases; pp. 27–29, 32), and a closing statement of who implemented what, introduced as making contributions and contact points transparent (pp. 39–40). The GCG section opens by promising users that no decomposition expertise is needed while assuming the reader knows decomposition (p. 26); a footnote admits that the score refactoring already happened in GCG 3.6 without being documented (p. 32). Slips in co-signed, non-GCG text: the abstract claims an improvement in node counts (p. 1) while Table 1 shows MILP nodes ×1.02 against 9.0 (p. 6), which the body calls +3% (p. 5); the CONOPT text swaps the overall time and node changes against Table 8 (p. 25); a 5% mean on the MINLP *both-solved* subset (called both-optimal in the text) is described as *consistently* faster (p. 6).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contribution level: software (interfaces, file format, pricing solvers, licence) and documentation of suite features. Limits are written concretely but, for GCG, never measured: separator support for master-only rows is basic and flagged experimental (p. 28), deterministic parallel pricing is a future plan (p. 32), IPColGen is off by default (p. 32), the GCG object and JDEC are to be extended (pp. 27, 29). Suite limits: exact mode makes only reliability pseudocost branching exact, does not use its unsafe strong-branching results, offers only Gomory mixed-integer cuts and leaves presolve uncertified (pp. 9, 11); implied-integer detection stays off (p. 13); decomposition kernel search needs finetuning (p. 20); ancestral pseudocosts stay off until conclusive evidence for general MILPs (p. 21); the PBSolver lacks problem-type detection, a feasibility definition and an exact mode (p. 39).

**Method links**:
- ✅ Method 1 (steps 4–6): GCG's design keeps the original model as a synchronized SCIP instance (p. 26), branches robustly on original variables so tailored pricing stays applicable (p. 27), and lets users hand over known partial or nested structure in JDEC files (pp. 28–29); supplied decompositions also drive SCIP's decomposition kernel search and Benders relaxator (pp. 20, 23–24).
- ⚠ Method 1 (step 6, stop rule): GCG 4.0 opens non-robust master-only rows that can be stronger but may destroy tailored pricing (p. 28) and adds general-MILP pricing solvers (recursive GCG, HiGHS; pp. 31–32): the generic tool invests exactly where the stop rule tells a user not to decompose.
- ✅ Method 3 (steps 1, 2, 5; co-signed): the suite protocol (two baselines, affected subset, difficulty brackets, seeds, identical machines, stated shifts, losses reported; pp. 4–7) and components kept off by default when gains are neutral or narrow (pp. 13, 19–21, 32) are the SCIP release-report norm, so neither this protocol nor H7 is distinctive to him.
- ⚠ Method 3 (step 6, checkpoint): his group's section reports the changes of eight subsections without one measurement and makes a usability promise (pp. 26–33); co-signed text claims more than its tables (pp. 1, 5–6, 25).
- ⚠ Method 2 (step 5): decomposition scores become selectable plugins (pp. 32–33), but no score is tested against measured outcomes here and no learned ranker is cited (references pp. 40–46).
- ✅ Method 4 (weak): the software report delegates decomposition theory and a branching rule's details to the book cahier [32] (pp. 26, 28, 42).
- Outside the methods: H9 and Misreading 1 (coordinator and supervisor, not implementer; p. 40); H7 (one thread by default for determinism, IPColGen off; p. 32), now in print rather than only in code; Tension 2 (no expertise needed, unmeasured; p. 26); Tension 7 (GCG now under Apache 2.0; p. 26).
- New-pattern candidates: *nested decomposition of the pricing problem* (pp. 29–31; the book defers nested column generation, D004 p. 8); *interface plus one worked example* (p. 28; also the IPColGen callbacks, p. 32); *structure as an exchangeable file* (pp. 20, 23–24, 28–31).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D5) Benchmark a release against both the previous major release and the latest bugfix release, so slowdowns caused by bugfixes are not charged to the new features (pp. 4–5).
- (D5) Next to *all* and *both-solved*, report an *affected* subset (instances whose dual simplex iteration counts differ between versions) and difficulty brackets [t, T]; drop numerically inconsistent runs from *all* (p. 5).
- (D5) Cost a new mode that lacks features against the default and against a reduced default with exactly those features switched off (p. 11).
- (D4) Enforce a branching decision on an original variable as a bound in the pricing problem, so a tailored pricing solver stays usable; model a non-robust master row in pricing by a coefficient variable y restricted to a domain Y(x, z) (pp. 27–28).
- (D4) Ship a new extension interface together with one complete rule built on it as the code example and a how-to section in the documentation (p. 28).
- (D3) Store decompositions as JSON with nested block decompositions and symmetry representatives, and let the reader ignore unknown fields so users can attach their own data (p. 29).
- (D4) Gate a specialised pricing solver by properties of the instance graph (number of nodes, density) so it can reject requests it would handle badly (p. 32).
- (D4) Keep parallel pricing at one thread by default while it makes the solving path nondeterministic, and say so in the release text (p. 32).
- (D4) Shrink each bug-triggering instance with a delta debugger and add the shrunken instance to the regression tests (pp. 37–38).

### D004 · Conclusion (Branch-and-Price, pp. 545–554, Springer 2026, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-96917-1_8)
**Read level**: full. All 10 file pages (printed pp. 545–554): abstract and contents, outroduction, §8.1 GENCOL by decade, §8.2 deferred subjects, exercises, licence; Figure 8.1 seen only through its caption and axis labels (p. 3).
**Role**: core · **Co-authors**: Jacques Desrosiers, Guy Desaulniers, Jean Bertrand Gauthier · **Position in trajectory**: closing chapter (chapter 8) of the book S017, the end of the 2015–2026 book project; open access under CC BY 4.0 (p. 10).
**Voice**: joint. The first page names J. Desrosiers et al. with the chapter DOI (p. 1); the outroduction is signed Marco, Guy, and Jean Bertrand and dedicates the history to Jacques Desrosiers, whose career it traces and whose legacy the book is meant to be (p. 2); §8.1 builds on an earlier text by Desrosiers (2010) (p. 3), so it is the Montreal group's self-history, in which Lübbecke appears among the collaborators who joined in the last 20 years (p. 7). No passage is his alone.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: How to end a book on column generation without summarizing it. The chapter opens with an epigraph from Valéry's *Rhumbs* saying that nothing beautiful can be summarized (p. 1) and, instead of a recap, gives a history of five decades of the Montreal GENCOL group, a look at the future, a list of open subjects and two exercises (pp. 1–9); the outroduction frames the open subjects as suggestions for readers interested in writing (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: A lineage-and-agenda conclusion. Column generation's rise from almost no papers fifty years ago to a major tool for large integer programs is told through one group's decades (pp. 2–7), and the open topics are handed on: a short list of 25 advanced subjects for a second (and third) book, in the hope that their authors keep this book's notation (p. 8). It does not recapitulate the book's chapters (pp. 1–10).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Integer programs with set-partitioning-type masters and resource-constrained shortest-path pricing: the SPPTW (1981) generalized to the SPPRC, solved by dynamic programming with non-decreasing resource extension functions that may be discontinuous and non-convex, which encode rules set by administrators and lawyers (pp. 3–4); ESPPRC pricing with richer labels, extension functions and dominance rules for rich vehicle routing (p. 7). Under mild assumptions, which the chapter does not state, a compact formulation exists for any column generation scheme, with block-diagonal structure and identical subproblems each contributing one column to an integer solution (pp. 5–6; Villeneuve et al. 2005, his works entry S021). Instance scales quoted: school bus problems with up to 151 trips (p. 3), VRPTW with 25 to 100 customers (p. 5), a monthly crew pairing instance with 46,588 flights (p. 7).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: The master–pricing split of a node's in- and out-degree constraints: one is kept in the master and a transformation of the pair, e.g. their difference, which is flow conservation, goes to pricing (pp. 3–4). Further devices, all attributed to the Montreal group: dual stabilization against tailing-off; dynamic constraint aggregation for set-partitioning masters, generalized to the improved primal simplex so that degeneracy is exploited rather than fought; the positive edge rule, implemented in COIN-OR Clp by John Forrest; an interior-to-extreme-point crossover (p. 6); pricing problems from alternative optimality conditions (maximize the smallest reduced cost, or find a negative-cost augmenting cycle) and ISUD, which makes integrality the pricing problem's responsibility, extended to integral column generation with side constraints (p. 6); arc-based ng-route neighbourhoods and new stabilization (p. 7); rolling horizon with constraint aggregation for large crew pairing (p. 7); machine-learned column or arc selection, and labels extended for stochastic resources (p. 8). Proof devices: n/a; nothing is proved, the compact-formulation result and Propositions 4.16–4.17 of chapter 4 (not read) are cited (pp. 5–6).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: n/a: no experiments. The one data figure, Fig. 8.1, counts publications per year 1966–2024 from a Web of Science topic search whose three phrases are stated (pp. 2–3). Historical results are repeated without protocol: in 1992 a crew pairing problem with over 190 million million variables solved to optimality and 6% better than the best available software systems, with no reference given (p. 4); US$54 million savings in two years for Flexjet (Hicks et al. 2005) (p. 5); about 400 industrial implementations and over 150 papers by the team (p. 5).
- **D6 Positioning**: Places the book in the GENCOL lineage and its community: an edited column generation book (2005), schools from Montréal 2006 to Paris 2025, workshops from Aussois 2008 to Montréal 2023 (pp. 2–3). Column generation is presented as better understood in its virtues and its shortcomings (p. 5). The 2005 survey by Lübbecke and Desrosiers (S001) is placed in the third decade as relating Dantzig–Wolfe reformulation and column generation and stressing the dual point of view (p. 6). Lübbecke appears among the collaborators who joined in the last 20 years (p. 7). Future trends: larger instances (p. 7), machine learning as a strong tendency, citing Kruber et al. 2017 (his S009) first (p. 8), and stochastic models (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: A playful frame for a serious text: epigraphs from Valéry and from Queen and David Bowie's *Under Pressure* (p. 1); a coined *Outroduction* announced by a talk-show line and signed by three co-authors (p. 2); a history told by decade with prizes, companies and sale prices (pp. 3–7); two exercises, a quiz on who François Soumis is and the instruction to find all mistakes in the book (p. 9), followed by a German song epigraph (von Brücken 2015) (p. 9). Small slips survive (e.g. a stray word in the cross-reference to Propositions 4.16 and 4.17, p. 6), the kind exercise 8.2 hands to readers.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contribution level: synthesis and agenda; no new result. Limits come as a concrete numbered list of 25 subjects left for later books, among them volume and bundle methods, Benders decomposition and its combination with Dantzig–Wolfe, interior point algorithms, nested column generation, machine learning, stochastic aspects, implementation, and the histories of SCIP and BaPCoD (p. 8); errors are handed to readers (p. 9). Implementation and the history of SCIP, the framework his group builds on, are among the deferred subjects (p. 8).

**Method links**:
- ✅ Method 4 (steps 2, 5, 6): open subjects published as a numbered list for later books, with the hope that they keep this book's notation (p. 8); the future framed as suggestions for writers (p. 2); mistakes left to readers (p. 9). SKILL.md's book pp. 552–553 (printed) are file pp. 8–9 here.
- ✅ Method 1 (steps 3 and 6): paths priced by dynamic programming absorb non-convex industrial rules (p. 4); a compact formulation exists for any column generation scheme, so branching and cutting can always be defined on its variables (pp. 5–6; S021).
- ⚠ Method 3 (step 6): the history repeats success numbers without protocol, the 1992 one without a reference (pp. 4–5), in a section built on Desrosiers's 2010 text (p. 3); it also keeps a relative failure in the record, the first VRPTW branch-and-price on small instances, as the opening of fundamental research on cutting planes (p. 5).
- Outside the methods: B3 (a conclusion that does not summarize; pp. 1–9); G2 (a failure kept in the record; p. 5); G3 (research told as implementations, companies and savings; pp. 4–5); Tension 5 (machine learning as a strong tendency in the joint 2026 text, against his 2024–26 posts; p. 8); Academic Lineage (p. 7).
- New-pattern candidates: none of its own; nested column generation is deferred here (p. 8), which S019 implements as nested decomposition (S019 pp. 29–31).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D3/D4) When a node must be visited exactly once, keep one of its in- or out-degree constraints in the master and give the pricing problem their difference as flow conservation (pp. 3–4).
- (D3) Encode operational rules, even discontinuous or non-convex ones, as non-decreasing resource extension functions of a dynamic-programming pricing problem rather than as master rows (p. 4).
- (D8) End a long synthesis with a numbered list of the subjects you deferred, ask later authors to keep your notation, and put the errata invitation into the text as an exercise (pp. 8–9).
- (D5/D6) When you show a field's growth, give the database and the exact search phrases behind the count (pp. 2–3).

**Quotes**:
- "hence branching and cutting decisions can always be defined on the variables of this formulation" (p. 5)
- "by various authors hopefully following the notation introduced in this one" (p. 8)
