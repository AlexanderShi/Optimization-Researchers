### S031 · Row-reduced column generation for degenerate master problems (European Journal of Operational Research 236(2) 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2013.12.016)
**Read level**: full. This is the author preprint (dated November 9, 2011, revised February 5, 2013; p. 1). All 17 pages were read: abstract, §§1–5 and the appendix proofs (pp. 15–17). Figure 1 (p. 12) is legible as its caption only, and the references were skimmed. Checked on p. 1: the title, and "Marco E. Lübbecke" (RWTH) as third author after Desrosiers and Gauthier.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jacques Desrosiers and Jean Bertrand Gauthier (GERAD & HEC Montréal, p. 1). An NSERC grant supported the first author, and François Soumis commented on a draft (p. 14). · **Place in trajectory**: a paper of the LP-degeneracy side line (2011–2022 in the skill's Research Trajectory); the preprint is dated November 2011 (p. 1). It generalizes the Montréal methods IPS and DCA (p. 2) and cites a contraction-expansion working paper by the same three authors (p. 15); S049 surveys it later (S049 p. 5).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Column generation "has a bad reputation for its slow convergence, known as the tailing-off effect", blamed mainly on degenerate restricted master solutions, above all in LP relaxations of combinatorial problems (p. 2). The paper enters by carrying recent anti-degeneracy advances of the primal simplex into column generation (p. 1). The authors call it a methodological paper (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea**: When the restricted master problem (RMP) has p < m positive variables, a basis with m − p artificials (eq. 3, p. 4) lets the authors drop the zero rows. The result is a row-reduced master (RrRMP) with a p × p working basis (eq. 8, p. 5). A column is compatible when its transformed part on the dropped rows vanishes (Definition 1, p. 6). A compatible column with negative reduced cost gives a nondegenerate pivot (p. 6).
  - Pricing has two problems. cSP is the usual pricing problem plus linear compatibility constraints (eq. 11, p. 6). rSP is a convex combination of incompatible columns, solved itself by column generation over X (eqs. 10, 13; pp. 6–7).
  - Algorithm RrCG (p. 9) runs classical column generation while the solution is nondegenerate. Once it turns degenerate, RrCG alternates RrRMP and cSP, then calls rSP and returns to the RMP. The master–pricing alternation and the pricing set X stay as they were; the master's row set and the pricing objective and constraints change (pp. 5–7).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: The master is an LP min cᵀλ, Aλ = b, λ ≥ 0 with m artificials, so A has full row rank (p. 3). Columns encode combinatorial objects x ∈ X, and the cost c(x) is "typically non-linear" in routing and crew scheduling (p. 3). The columns of A_F must be linearly independent, "no restriction" when the solution comes from a simplex code (p. 4). Inequalities are handled with surplus variables (§3.4, pp. 7–8). The construction is interpreted for set partitioning and for minimum cost flow (p. 11). No instances or data (n/a).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (with proofs)**: Theorem 1 gives a necessary and sufficient LP optimality condition: λ_F > 0 is optimal iff some π_Z makes the rSP value nonnegative. The proof builds a dual-feasible solution one way and an improving combination the other (p. 10). Proposition 1: a column is compatible iff it lies in the span of A_F (pp. 10–11). Propositions 2–3: cSP and rSP do not depend on the choice of working basis, proved by duplicating rows and left-multiplying (appendix, pp. 15–17). Restricting the search to rows with large b̄_i (a row subset Q ⊆ P) is called "a partial pricing strategy", and leaving the row partition unchanged after a pivot still gives an exact algorithm (p. 13). Caveat: the compatibility constraints in cSP "may destroy the structure of the subproblem" (p. 6).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none. The computational promise is credited to the cited IPS and DCA experiments (pp. 1–2, 14).
- **D6–D7 (brief)**: The paper generalizes IPS (Elhallaoui et al. 2010a; Raymond et al. 2010) and DCA (Elhallaoui et al. 2005, 2008, 2010b), likens row reduction to Pan's deficient basis (p. 2), and treats IPS as a special case of RrCG (p. 9). Claims are hedged: the method "may take advantage" of degenerate solutions (p. 1).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is methodological: a row-reduced column generation framework with an LP optimality condition as a by-product (pp. 1, 14). There is no implementation. Open questions listed: when to update the row partition, efficient solvers for cSP and rSP, which pricing problem to call, many incompatible columns or their single convex combination, and relaxed compatibility (p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 evidence ✅ (steps 1, 2, 4; co-authored, third author). The paper starts from the tailing-off effect of degenerate masters (p. 2), the effect the skill names as his starting point, although here it is the field's effect, not his own code's. It puts IPS and DCA into one notation and one algorithm (pp. 2, 9, 14). It states as a theorem a result "not explicitly stated in previous IPS papers" (p. 10).
- H1 variant ⚠ (a heuristic, listed here and not in the digest). Pricing is staged: the restricted cSP first, rSP only when no compatible column with negative reduced cost remains (p. 9), and a row subset gives partial pricing (p. 13). The first stage, however, is chosen because it guarantees a strict improvement, not because it is cheap, and it can be harder than the original pricing problem (p. 6).
- Taste G4 evidence ✅: the paper reads structure in the solution as well as in the model (p. 14; Quotes).
- New-pattern candidate "turn the complicating feature into an advantage": degeneracy, the cause of tailing-off, becomes the source of a smaller working basis (pp. 1–2, 14). The same idea appears in S049 (p. 1) and S026 (p. 10) below.

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a master LP solution has p < m positive variables, drop the m − p zero rows and price for columns in the span of the positive columns. Every such compatible column with negative reduced cost gives a strictly improving pivot (pp. 4–6).
- If no single compatible column exists, price a convex combination of incompatible columns whose image on the dropped rows is zero (rSP). Its optimal value is a necessary and sufficient optimality certificate (pp. 6, 10).
- Before relying on a reformulated pricing problem, prove that it does not depend on an arbitrary choice, here the working basis, with a row-duplication argument (appendix, pp. 15–17).

**Quotes**:
- "This methodological paper generalizes the improved primal simplex and dynamic constraints aggregation methods." (p. 1)
- "Combining column generation and dynamic row-partition during the solution process allows for exploiting both the modeling structure of its formulation and the algebraic structure of its solutions." (p. 14)

### S039 · Decomposition theorems for linear programs (Operations Research Letters 42(8) 2014, DOI 10.1016/j.orl.2014.10.001)
**Read level**: full. This is the GERAD Cahier G-2014-67 version (September 2014). All 13 file pages were read: cover (p. 1), title page (p. 3), abstract (p. 4), §§1–6 (pp. 5–12) and references (p. 13). Checked on file p. 3: the title, and "Marco E. Lübbecke" (RWTH) as third author after Gauthier and Desrosiers.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jean Bertrand Gauthier and Jacques Desrosiers (GERAD & HEC Montréal, p. 3) · **Place in trajectory**: degeneracy side line. The paper cites S038 for the improved complexity analysis (p. 5, ref. [5]) and announces an LP version of minimum mean cycle-canceling (MMCC) (pp. 11–12).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Network flow theory has the Flow Decomposition Theorem, the Augmenting Cycle Theorem and the Negative Cycle Optimality Theorem, the tools behind strongly polynomial cycle-canceling algorithms (p. 5). The entry is to carry them over to LP (p. 5; Quotes).
- **D2 Core idea**: The flow decomposition is proved by Dantzig-Wolfe instead of by the usual construction (pp. 6–7). The network becomes a circulation and the bounds stay in the master. Flow conservation forms a cone subproblem whose extreme rays are paths and cycles, and counting the variables of a basic solution gives the bounds.
  - For an LP with bounded variables, adding v = b and Ax − v = 0 makes the subproblem the cone {Ax − v = 0, x, v ≥ 0}. Its extreme rays are "weighted paths" (v ≠ 0) and "weighted cycles" (v = 0) (pp. 7–8).
  - Theorem 2: every feasible x⁰ is a nonnegative combination of at most m + n weighted paths and cycles, at most n of them cycles (p. 8).
  - Theorem 3: two feasible solutions differ by at most n weighted cycles of the residual problem LP(x⁰) (p. 9).
  - Theorem 4: x⁰ is optimal iff LP(x⁰) has no negative weighted cycle, iff some π makes all reduced costs on LP(x⁰) nonnegative, iff complementary slackness holds (p. 10).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: The LP is min cᵀx, Ax = b, l ≤ x ≤ u with m ≤ n and b ≥ 0 without loss of generality (p. 7). The network case is the capacitated minimum cost flow problem with balanced supplies (p. 5). No instances (n/a).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (proof devices)**: A Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation, Minkowski–Weyl and the size of a basic solution replace a constructive argument (pp. 6–8). The residual problem comes from the change of variables x = x⁰ + (y→ − y←) (p. 9). The pricing step is a primal–dual pair of LPs: the dual maximizes the smallest reduced cost µ over free π, the primal finds a weighted cycle under a convexity constraint, and duality links the two (pp. 10–11). The authors flag that they reuse network notation for LP objects on purpose (p. 7). LP extreme rays cannot be scaled to a unit cycle, which is why the paths and cycles are "weighted" (p. 8).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none.
- **D6–D7 (brief)**: The paper extends the network theory of Ahuja, Magnanti and Orlin and the MMCC analysis of Goldberg and Tarjan, with S038 as the refined analysis (p. 5). It is a short theorem–proof paper whose discussion is a research programme with hedges: "remains to be proven", "deserves an analysis" (p. 11).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is three LP generalizations and a Dantzig-Wolfe proof of the network theorem, which the authors call "an interesting result in itself" (p. 12). Two directions stay open. An MMCC-type algorithm for LP has unproven convergence and unanalysed complexity; the scaling impact of the convexity constraint is the named obstacle (p. 11). A column generation algorithm on MP(x⁰) would stay at x⁰ and bring rays into the master (p. 12). The two differ in degeneracy: "While the first guarantees degeneracy immunity, the second makes no such promises." (p. 12).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 evidence ✅ (steps 2 and 5; co-authored, third author): network and LP results are written in one notation on purpose (p. 7), and the open steps are published as a programme: the LP adaptation of MMCC, its convergence and complexity, and a column generation variant (pp. 11–12).
- New-pattern candidate "Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation as a proof and explanation device": Dantzig-Wolfe replaces the constructive proof of flow decomposition and yields the LP theorems (pp. 6–8). See also S038 (p. 19) and S049 (pp. 18–20) below.
- New-pattern candidate "harvest the pricing problem beyond one column": the authors cite this as one of column generation's most successful ideas (p. 12; Quotes). See also S049 (p. 20) and S068 (pp. 7–8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To prove a representation theorem in terms of paths, cycles or rays, write a Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation whose subproblem is a cone, drop the null extreme point, and count the variables of a basic solution of the master system (pp. 6–8).
- State an optimality test as a primal–dual pair of pricing LPs: the dual maximizes the smallest reduced cost over free duals, the primal finds a normalized improving direction. Equal optimal values prove that the primal and dual optimality conditions are equivalent (pp. 10–11).

**Quotes**:
- "This paper generalizes these network flow theorems to linear programming." (p. 5)
- "One of the most successful ideas of column generation is to harvest a lot of information from the subproblem even though some of it might turn out irrelevant" (p. 12)

### S065 · A Branch-Price-and-Cut Algorithm for Packing Cuts in Undirected Graphs (Experimental Algorithms, SEA 2014, LNCS 8504 2014, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-07959-2_4)
**Read level**: full. This is the published version: all 12 file pages (LNCS pp. 34–45) were read, with Tables 1–2. Figures are legible as labels and captions only. Checked on p. 1: the title, and "Marco E. Lübbecke" as second of three authors (RWTH).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Martin Bergner and Jonas T. Witt (RWTH). The work was funded by the DFG Priority Program "Algorithm Engineering", grants LU770/4-1 and LU770/4-2 (p. 1). · **Place in trajectory**: written in the generic branch-and-price years, but this is a tailored code on SCIP 3.0.1, not GCG (p. 6). The W[1]-hardness proof and the proof that pricing with diff constraints is hard are left to "the full version of this paper" (pp. 1, 5).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Cut packing is known from duality theorems, complexity results (NP-hard, also on planar graphs; polynomial on chordal and bipartite graphs) and approximation bounds (α(G) ≤ γ(G) ≤ 2α(G) − 1) (p. 1). No experimental results exist, so the paper claims the first computational study (p. 2; Quotes). It also tests how complexity and approximability results "match with computational experience" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: The compact model stacks m copies of a min-cut formulation (pp. 2–3). The extended model has one binary per cut plus edge slacks. Formally it is the Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation of the compact model with the m identical subproblems aggregated into one, which removes the symmetry (p. 3).
  - Pricing is a min-cut with arbitrary weights. Stoer–Wagner solves it while the duals are nonnegative; once branching makes some duals negative it becomes a max-cut and is solved as a binary program (p. 4). A greedy heuristic prices disjoint cuts, and max-cut heuristics and a column pool are tried before exact pricing (p. 4).
  - Branching goes first on the original y_ij, then Ryan–Foster on edge pairs (pp. 4–5).
  - Clique cuts are separated, and their duals are moved to hyperedges so that the root pricing stays a hypergraph min-cut (p. 5). A bound from an edge clique cover is added as cuts before column generation starts (p. 6).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: The graphs are connected and undirected (p. 1). The test set has about 100 instances: 10th DIMACS challenge graphs, Trick's coloring graphs, random graphs from Rudy, Randgraph and NetworkX 1.7 (seed 1 unless stated), and MUSKETEER-edited twins of the real graphs (p. 6). Bipartite and chordal graphs are tested because the problem is polynomial there (pp. 7–8).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Branching rules are chosen so that pricing stays combinatorial: in the child with x̄_ij = 1 the duals stay nonnegative, while pair branching stays combinatorial only for a few pairs (pp. 4–5). On a clique the LP value is n/2 against an optimum of 1 (p. 5). The hardness reductions are not in this text (pp. 1, 5).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: SCIP 3.0.1 with CPLEX 12.4.0.1 as LP solver, Intel Core i7-2600, 16 GB, 3600 s (p. 6). On 12 random graphs the compact model solves 2, the basic extended code 8 and the full code 12; the solver used for the compact model is not named (pp. 6–7). By class (Table 1, p. 8), random graphs fail beyond 40 nodes while bipartite graphs up to 320 nodes and chordal up to 160 are solved; times are shifted geometric means with the shift not given. Real graphs: 5 of 9 solved against 1 of 9 random twins (pp. 8–9). Benchmarks with hidden optima: no root node solved within the hour (p. 8). Each component is ablated, off and on, in performance profiles (pp. 9–10).
- **D6–D7 (brief)**: The paper presents itself as the first computational study after a literature of theory only (p. 2), and reads its results against that theory (pp. 8, 10).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The limits are stated as sizes: up to 80 vertices on random graphs, 500 on partitioning benchmarks, 5000 edges on coloring graphs (p. 10). The combinatorial pricing mattered "not that much" (p. 9). Future work: better clique edge covers, other min-cut formulations for pricing, and dual variable stabilization (p. 11).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 evidence ✅ (steps 2, 3, 5, 6): the compact model is run and loses, 2 of 12 against 12 of 12 (p. 7). The variable is a whole cut because its pricing problem is a min-cut (pp. 3–4). The code is built on SCIP (p. 6), and branching goes on original edge variables first (p. 4).
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (steps 5–6): classes where the method should do well and where it should struggle (pp. 7–8), real graphs next to random and edited twins (p. 8), per-component ablations in both directions (pp. 9–10), and failures and size limits stated in the text (pp. 8, 10).
- Method 3 variant ⚠: the random graphs come from a single seed (p. 6) and the shifted geometric means lack their shift (p. 8). The only baseline is the authors' own compact model, as no earlier code exists (pp. 2, 7).
- H1 evidence ✅ (a heuristic, listed here and not in the digest): several disjoint columns per round from a greedy pricing heuristic, plus max-cut heuristics and a column pool, before any exact pricing (p. 4).
- Tension 6 evidence (dated 2014): problem-specific clique inequalities matter most here. Disabling the clique cover and its inequalities "causes the largest performance drop" (p. 9), which supports the "indispensable" side that the skill dates to 2024.
- New-pattern candidates:
  - "pricing-compatibility filter for cuts and branching" (already in the pool, from S077): y_ij branching and the hyperedge transfer of clique duals keep the pricing combinatorial, while Ryan–Foster pairs do not scale combinatorially (pp. 4–5).
  - "check theory's predictions against computation": the polynomial classes are solved faster, and the factor-2 gap is not observed (pp. 2, 8, 10).
  - "first computational study of a theory-only problem or proposal" (p. 2; S068 p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When identical subproblems repeat (m copies of a cut), derive the extended formulation by Dantzig-Wolfe and aggregate the copies into one pricing problem; the symmetry goes with them (p. 3).
- Prefer branching rules whose children keep the pricing duals nonnegative, so that a combinatorial pricing algorithm survives. Fall back to binary-program pricing only where it cannot (p. 4).
- Carry cut duals into the pricing structure: map each clique cut to a hyperedge that carries its dual, and solve the pricing as a hypergraph min-cut (p. 5).
- Compute a cheap combinatorial bound (an edge clique cover) once and add its inequalities before column generation starts (p. 6). Here, switching it off cost the most performance (p. 9).
- For a problem with known easy classes, generate instances of the easy and the hard classes, and pair each real instance with a random twin and an edited twin of equal size (pp. 7–8).

**Quotes**:
- "We are not aware of any experimental results, neither exact nor approximate, for cut packing; thus we provide the first computational study of the problem." (p. 2)
- "This comes as a surprise as there is a theoretical gap of a factor of 2 that we do not observe in the instances in our experiments." (p. 10)

### D006 · Dantzig-Wolfe Reformulations for the Stable Set Problem (ResearchGate, unpublished 2015, DOI 10.13140/rg.2.1.3470.0887)
**Read level**: full. All 12 pages were read: abstract and §§1–5 with all proofs (pp. 1–11). Fig. 1 (p. 8) is legible as its caption only, and the references (pp. 11–12) were skimmed. Checked on p. 1: the title, and "Marco E. Lübbecke" as second author after Jonas T. Witt (RWTH). The file carries no date and no report number; the year comes from the works list.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jonas T. Witt (RWTH, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: an earlier version of S052 (Discrete Optimization 2018). It has the title and authors of the 2015 RWTH report that S058 names as its starting point (recorded in the S052 card, c06). Whether this file is that report cannot be told from the file.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: In general only the sandwich P_IP ⊆ P_DW(I′) ⊆ P_LP and Geoffrion's necessary condition are known (p. 2; Quotes). The stable set problem is chosen for three reasons: it is well understood, it matters for general IPs through conflict graphs and set packing, and it has been used to compare other relaxations (Sherali–Adams, Lovász–Schrijver, Lasserre, corner) (pp. 2–3). Earlier Dantzig-Wolfe and Lagrangian work convexified only specific subgraph classes (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Each set of convexified constraints is a subgraph G′ = (V, E′), and both extremes are characterized. DW(G, G′) = ESTAB(G) iff G′ is bipartite (Theorem 1, p. 5). DW(G, G′) = STAB(G) iff G′ contains all odd holes, with triangles counted as odd holes (Theorems 2–4, pp. 6, 9–10). Sufficiency rests on new facet-graph lemmas:
  - every critical edge lies in an odd hole (Lemma 2, pp. 7–8);
  - the same holds for minimal critical edge sets (Lemma 3, pp. 8–9);
  - every facet-graph has a spanning α_π-critical subgraph whose edges all lie in odd holes (Lemma 4, p. 9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Pure IPs with bounds (p. 1), and the maximum weight stable set problem in the edge formulation with weights in Z≥0 (pp. 3–4). "a cycle on three nodes is also considered an odd hole in this paper" (p. 6). The characterizations are polytope identities (pp. 4–10). No instances (n/a).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (proof devices)**:
  - The half-integral point (1/2 on an odd cycle) lies outside STAB(G′) by the odd cycle inequality (Theorem 1, p. 5). When one hole edge is not convexified it lies inside DW(G, G′), as the average of two alternating stable sets (Theorem 2, p. 6).
  - A five-node counterexample shows that odd cycles cannot replace odd holes (p. 6).
  - Lemma 2 follows Andrásfai's proof idea: two maximum weight stable sets, a component exchange and a shortest path (pp. 7–8).
  - Lemma 3 is an induction that adds back the edges of a minimal critical set (p. 9), and Lemma 4 fixes a deletion order for building a critical subgraph (p. 9).
  - Sufficiency is then shown facet by facet (Theorem 3, pp. 9–10).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none. Computational implications are "out of the scope of this paper", and experiments are announced for future work (p. 11; Quotes).
- **D6–D7 (brief)**: Geoffrion 1974 is the only general predecessor (pp. 1, 3). The priority claim carries "To the best of our knowledge" both in the abstract (p. 1) and on p. 3, and the authors call their focus "a seminal step" (p. 10). The paper also gives a weakened answer to a question from Sewell's thesis (p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is the two characterizations and the facet-graph results. A separation remark follows: facet-inducing cuts need only the subgraph spanned by the edges that lie in odd holes (pp. 10–11).
  - Limits: deciding whether an edge lies in an odd hole is NP-complete, and W[1]-complete when parameterized by hole size. Even the strongest reformulation's LP is expected to be "too hard to solve effectively in practice" (p. 11).
  - Future work: reformulations between the extremes, such as convexifying all holes up to size k (p. 11). The authors believe the results extend directly to node covering and linear ordering, and name general IPs their "long-term vision" (p. 11).
  - Compared with the S052 card (c06), several results recorded there are absent here: the clique, node cover, matching and set packing transfers and Reed's Theorem 8 (S052 pp. 9–17). This text names node covering and linear ordering as believed extensions instead (p. 11). The S052 card records no linear-ordering result.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 variant ⚠ (order, dated): the theory of both extremes is complete in 2015 without any computation, and experiments are only announced (p. 11); the enumeration study (S058, SEA 2018) comes three years later. This moves the skill's note "The 2018 study starts from a theorem" back to 2015 (SKILL.md Method 2 Limitations; S052 card).
- Method 2 evidence ✅ (exit B): the pattern is proved for one class, extensions to other problems are stated as beliefs, and the family between the extremes is named as the next step (p. 11).
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (claim sized in a theory paper): computation is declared out of scope, and both the hardness of recognizing odd holes and the expected difficulty of the LP are stated (p. 11).
- Tension 3 context: here the priority claim is hedged in the abstract itself (p. 1), while the S052 card records the 2018 abstract without that hedge, so the hedge was lost between versions (S052 card, c06). The self-description "a seminal step" (p. 10) is already in the 2015 text.
- New-pattern candidate "extremes first, then in between" (already in the pool, from S052 and S058): both extremes are characterized and the family between them is named next (pp. 4, 10–11). This is an earlier version of S052, so it does not count as an independent paper for the three-paper rule.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before characterizing when a reformulation strengthens the bound, prove the never-helps half from a known integrality result applied to the convexified part. Here it is Nemhauser and Trotter's result that ESTAB = STAB iff the graph is bipartite (pp. 4–5).
- To show that leaving a structure unconvexified loses strength, write a fractional point as the average of two integer solutions of the convexified part such that it violates a valid inequality of the whole problem (pp. 5–6).

**Quotes**:
- "Interestingly, this is already all we know about the strength of Dantzig-Wolfe reformulations of integer programs in general." (p. 2)
- "We will report on experiments in future work." (p. 11)

### S026 · A Branch-Price-and-Cut Algorithm for Multi-Mode Resource Leveling (European Journal of Operational Research 245(1) 2015, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2015.02.043)
**Read level**: full. This is the published version: all 11 file pages (printed pp. 70–80) were read, covering the abstract, §§1–6, Appendix A and Tables 1–4. Figures are legible as captions and axis labels only, and the references were skimmed. Checked on p. 1: the title, and "Marco E. Lübbecke" (RWTH) as second author between Coughlan and Schulz (TU Berlin; Schulz, the corresponding author, is also at BASF SE).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Eamonn T. Coughlan and Jens Schulz (TU Berlin, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: a TU Berlin application; the turnaround scheduling problem comes from Megow, Möhring and Schulz 2011 (p. 1). This is the journal version of an SEA 2010 paper, and new cuts and branching raise the critical size "from before 30 to 50 jobs" (p. 1, footnote).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Turnaround scheduling in chemical plants (p. 1) becomes, for a fixed horizon, a multi-mode resource leveling problem with shift calendars and the cost of hired capacity as objective (pp. 1–2). Settings: SCIP separators in aggressive mode, root node only, p_min = 1, i_max = 100, and α(x̄) computed by QR with column pivoting from the GNU Scientific Library (p. 8).
