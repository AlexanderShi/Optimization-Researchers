### S030 · Minimizing the stabbing number of matchings, trees, and triangulations (Proceedings of the 15th ACM-SIAM Symposium on Discrete Algorithms (SODA) 2004, pp. 430–439 per the arXiv comment; no DOI; arXiv cs/0310034v1)
**Read**: abstract. The abstracts.json entry is word for word the abstract of the final arXiv version, which is the DCG 2008 text read for card D001 (compared with the DataCite record of 10.48550/arXiv.cs/0310034). The SODA version's own abstract (arXiv v1, 16 October 2003, comment "to appear in Symposium on Discrete Algorithms", 13 pages, 7 figures, 2 tables) was read on the arXiv abstract page and on the title page of the v1 PDF while checking its identity · **Coauthors**: S. P. Fekete, H. Meijer

- **D1 problem/entry**: The complexity of minimum-stabbing-number perfect matchings, spanning trees and triangulations of a point set was a long-standing open question, one of the original 30 problems on the Demaine–Mitchell–O'Rourke list (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: Hardness of several minimum stabbing problems by one general proof technique, with lower bounds on approximability; then a cut-based IP for matchings and spanning trees whose LP relaxation gives lower bounds in polynomial time and has an optimal fractional solution with an edge of at least constant weight, the step towards iterated rounding (abstract). The v1 abstract states NP-completeness, says the heavy edge "suggests" constant-factor approximations, and ties the polyhedral approach to another open problem from a combinatorial-optimization list (arXiv v1 abstract).
- **D5 computational evaluation**: problems with up to several hundred points solved optimally or near-optimally (abstract); no test set, solver or times in the abstract. v1 has two tables (arXiv comment), while the journal text leaves the details out (card D001, D5, citing D001 p. 3), so the computations are probably in this SODA version (inferred; to be read next round).
- **D8 contribution**: answers the complexity question negatively for several of the problems and opens one IP/LP route to bounds, approximation and exact solution (abstract); the constant-factor approximation is claimed only as a step towards (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 6): the approximation is sized as "a crucial step towards" a constant-factor guarantee rather than claimed (abstract), and v1 said the heavy edge only "suggests" one (arXiv v1 abstract).
- new-pattern candidates (existing, from D001): LP relaxation as approximation-design instrument (abstract: polynomial-time LP bounds and a provably heavy fractional edge); first exact IP in a theory-dominated area (abstract: the cut-based IP that solves instances of several hundred points).

**Transferable**: After a hardness proof, write one cut-based IP whose LP relaxation gives polynomial-time lower bounds, whose optimal fractional solutions provably contain a heavy variable for iterated rounding, and which also solves instances of realistic size exactly (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "The (axis-parallel) stabbing number of a given set of line segments is the maximum number of segments that can be intersected by any one (axis-parallel) line." / "in fact, it is one of the original 30 outstanding open problems in computational geometry on the list by Demaine, Mitchell, and O'Rourke"

### H001 · Improved Column Generation for Highly Degenerate Master Problems (Les Cahiers du GERAD G-2011-66 2011, no DOI)
**Read**: abstract, from the GERAD landing page https://www.gerad.ca/en/papers/G-2011-66 (published November 2011, 26 pages; no PDF offered there) · **Coauthors**: J. Desrosiers, J. B. Gauthier (Lübbecke third)

- **D1 problem/entry**: Column generation is known to suffer from degeneracy, which shows as the tailing-off effect; the entry is a transfer of recent advances against degeneracy in the primal simplex method to column generation (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: Improved column generation (ICG) reduces the master's rows to the number of non-degenerate basic variables, so the basis is smaller and the master re-optimizes faster; pricing must then generate columns compatible with the row reduction, and when it cannot, incompatible columns are added and the reduction is updated dynamically; in either case the objective strictly improves (abstract). That strict improvement is the invariant the paper proves (D4; abstract).
- **D5 computational evaluation**: none of its own in the abstract; it cites recent experiments with IPS and DCA on highly degenerate LPs (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: a methodological generalization with the improved primal simplex method and dynamic constraints aggregation as its two special cases, with extensions and implementation issues discussed (abstract). S031's text is dated "November 9, 2011; revised: February 5, 2013" and renamed row-reduced column generation (S031 p. 1), so it reads as the revision of this Cahier (inferred from the dates, authors and abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 evidence ✅ (steps 1–2): starts from the tailing-off effect and puts two published remedies, IPS and DCA, under one method (abstract); co-authored, Lübbecke third.
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 6, across versions): measured against this abstract, the revision drops "Improved" and "Highly" from the title, turns "takes advantage" into "may take advantage of degenerate solutions", makes the strict improvement conditional on a negative weighted combination of reduced costs, and drops the comparison with the column reduction of standard column generation (abstract; S031 p. 1).
- new-pattern candidate (existing, from S031): turn the complicating feature into an advantage (abstract: an ICG method "that takes advantage of degenerate solutions").

**Transferable**:
- When the restricted master is degenerate, keep only the rows of its non-degenerate basic variables, price columns compatible with that row set, and when none exists add incompatible columns and update the row set, so that every iteration strictly improves the objective (abstract).
- Before a working paper becomes a journal paper, rewrite each unconditional statement of its abstract as the condition under which it holds, and drop comparisons the paper does not measure itself (abstract against S031 p. 1).

**Quotes** (abstract): "We show that, in either case, a strict improvement in the objective function value occurs." / "Two special cases of ICG are the improved primal simplex method and the dynamic constraints aggregation."
