### S078 · Optimization of the reconfiguration planning of cyber-physical production systems with delta-like architecture (ReMAR 2018, IEEE, DOI 10.1109/remar.2018.8449867)
**Read level**: full. File pp. 1–9: abstract and introduction (p. 1), Delta reconfiguration (pp. 2–3), modelling and model formulation (pp. 3–7), analysis and results (pp. 7–8), conclusion (pp. 8–9), references (p. 9). The extraction scrambles most formulas (pp. 4–7) and keeps figures only as labels (Figs. 1–5), so formulas are described from the surrounding text, never quoted.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jan Brinker, Dominic Ring, Burkhard Corves (RWTH Aachen, Institute of Mechanism Theory, Machine Dynamics and Robotics), Yukio Takeda (Tokyo Institute of Technology) (p. 1) · **Voice**: engineering-led; four of five authors are mechanical engineers and Lübbecke is the one OR author (p. 1); no passage can be attributed to him. · **Place in trajectory**: second paper of a collaboration that began with the RA-L 2017 p-median paper [6] (S047 in INDEX.md, not carded), which this one extends (p. 3); S094 is the extended abstract of the same work.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A line of p handling systems serves a known, highly diverse task stream; p identical systems that each serve all tasks "may be less efficient" than p optimized configurations, so select p configurations and allocate the tasks at minimum energy (p. 1). Entry: the earlier p-median model is "limited due to high memory usage in case of large data sets" and was shown feasible only for 200 tasks and 2,250 configurations (p. 3). Contributions: column generation against the memory limit, a configuration space reduced without discarding viable configurations, a minimum input-transmission requirement (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Following Lorena and Senne's column generation for the capacitated p-median, the binary p-median (1)–(6) (p. 4) is reformulated as a set partitioning problem and relaxed (p. 4); the next page calls the restricted master a set covering problem (p. 5). Columns are configurations with the tasks they serve; the RMP gives a dual per task and one for the cardinality row (p. 5). Pricing is one problem per configuration over binaries; with capacity neglected the "knapsack problems" have no capacity row, so a task enters the column when its reduced cost is negative, and the column is added when its value is below the cardinality dual (p. 5). Branch-and-bound follows because the RMP solution "may not be integral" (p. 5). A flow chart keeps the original model when memory suffices and switches to column generation otherwise (Fig. 3, p. 4). [My reading] Pricing without any row beyond binaries (p. 5) has the integrality property, so by the book's Proposition 4.7 the master LP bound equals the compact LP bound [S084 p. 27]: this reformulation buys memory, not bound, which is the motive the paper states (p. 3).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Pure binary, uncapacitated p-median; infeasible task–configuration pairs get a penalty, in the end an infinite cost (pp. 4, 7). n = 5,150 synthetic tasks from market shares of workspace, payload and degrees of freedom (Table I), paths randomly rotated in steps of π/6, every workspace–dof combination present (p. 5); the data "would need to be adjusted" with process figures and product streams that are "currently unavailable" (p. 5). m = 6,552 configurations after discretization (11,232 combinations before incompatible ones are dropped) (p. 6). 33,742,800 energy costs from inverse dynamics by Kane's equations, link masses and inertias scaled by length ratios, no recuperation of braking energy, and a minimum input transmission index of cos 50° ≈ 0.642 (pp. 6–7).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (brief): The configuration space is cut first by a constraint satisfaction problem: eleven mandatory constraints (derivation "not shown here"), each dimension minimized and maximized, solved in AIMMS with CP Optimizer; sampling or gradient methods are rejected because they "do not provide exact bounds" (p. 6). Costs in MATLAB, the p-median in Python with Gurobi, column generation in MATLAB with Gurobi (p. 7). The text does not say whether columns are priced inside the tree or branch-and-bound runs on the final columns only (p. 5); only the first would back the optimality claim of p. 8 [my reading]. No proof.
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: One illustrative instance, p = 6 of 6,552 configurations for 5,150 tasks (p. 7): Table III, per-configuration coverage 6.5–34.3%, energy savings of 4.5%, 12.1% and 17.1% against single-configuration baselines, a degressive curve to 0.779 for p < 15 (p. 8). Compact model against column generation: one sentence, memory down "at the cost of solving time" (p. 8); no time, memory, iteration or column counts, hardware or limits.
- **D7 Writing**: "innovatively employed" for the OR techniques (pp. 1, 8) and "a novel and rigorous approach" for the authors' own earlier paper (p. 3).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: An application (energy-efficient selection and allocation of configurations) with a CSP pre-reduction and a column generation variant (pp. 8–9). Limits: an economic evaluation needs set-up times and costs, procurement and operating costs, capacities and occupancy (p. 9); finer discretization is possible but its "practical benefit appears questionable" (p. 9). Future: better starting solutions for faster column generation (p. 8); factory layout planning with fixed configurations (p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (co-authored, engineering-led): the compact p-median stays the first choice and column generation is the fallback when memory runs out (Fig. 3, p. 4; p. 3), which is Method 1's order; but the go reason is memory, which Workflow B's checkpoint (bound gain, named pricing algorithm) does not list, the pricing cannot raise the bound [my reading, S084 p. 27], and the probe's numbers are not reported (p. 8).
- Method 3 ✗ contradiction (voice caveat: first author Brinker, Lübbecke's share not identifiable): "both approaches yield optimal solutions" and "This proves to be beneficial when dealing with large problems and limited resources" (p. 8) rest on one instance (p. 7) with no memory or time figure; the two approaches run in different environments (Python, MATLAB; p. 7); the text praises its own techniques (pp. 1, 3, 8), the self-praise of Taste B3.
- New-pattern candidate: **textbook column generation carried into an engineering partner's model, unmeasured**: column generation introduced from the cutting-stock textbook case and the Desrosiers–Lübbecke Primer [28] (p. 4), motive memory (p. 3), gain not measured (p. 8). S094 is the same project, so it is not independent evidence.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before building column generation, check whether the pricing problem has the integrality property (here: binaries with no other row, p. 5). If it has, the master bound equals the compact LP bound [S084 p. 27], so the reformulation can only buy memory or speed, and those are what you must measure.
- Before enumerating candidate configurations, compute exact ranges of each design parameter by minimizing and maximizing it subject to the mandatory constraints only (a CSP in a CP solver), instead of cutting the space by experience, which can discard valid candidates (p. 6).

**Quotes**:
- "excessive memory usage of solving the original model formulation can be decreased at the cost of solving time" (p. 8)
- "Note that both approaches yield optimal solutions." (p. 8)

### S094 · Optimization of the Reconfiguration Planning of Cyber-Physical Production Systems (Vierte IFToMM D-A-CH Konferenz 2018, extended abstract, DOI 10.17185/duepublico/45329)
**Read level**: full. File p. 1 is the DuEPublico cover sheet (title, five authors, conference, DOI); pp. 2–3 are the whole two-page abstract ("Kurzfassung", written in English) with Fig. 1, acknowledgments and four references.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jan Brinker, Dominic Ring, Yukio Takeda, Burkhard Corves (p. 2) · **Voice**: same team and first author as S078; Lübbecke is listed with the Chair of Operations Research (p. 2); no passage attributable to him. · **Place in trajectory**: announces the two techniques of S078 and builds on the RA-L 2017 paper [2] (S047) (pp. 2–3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The S078 scenario (p. 2). Energy as the target is expensive to optimize because "each potential allocation needs to be evaluated", so larger data sets need "more elaborate modeling and solution techniques" (p. 2).

First, reduce the configuration space by analysing mandatory requirements (practical implementation, kinematics, performance) as a constraint satisfaction problem, "without discarding viable candidates based on poor user experience" (p. 2). Second, column generation for the p-median, which had been implemented in its standard formulation in Python and solved with Gurobi: relax the binary problem, restrict it to configuration subsets and add variables iteratively (p. 2). Fig. 1 orders the pipeline: CSP reduction, cost matrix, selection and allocation by column generation (p. 3).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Binary p-median; extended Delta robots with rotational actuation, with the claim that the approach applies to "any production line with potentially adaptable systems or workstations" (p. 2). No data.
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (brief): no detail beyond the two named techniques; column generation is cited to the Primer [4] (pp. 2–3).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Announcement only, no results or limits. The expected gain, that efficiency "may be improved while still solving optimally" (p. 2), becomes in the full paper a memory gain at the cost of solving time, still without numbers (S078 p. 8).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: as in S078, compact model first and column generation for larger data (p. 2); the bet is stated as an expectation before any measurement (p. 2), and the full paper resizes it to memory against time without numbers (S078 p. 8).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant (voice caveat as in S078): a hedged "may" for the new technique, but "a novel and rigorous approach" for the group's own earlier paper (p. 2).
- New-pattern candidate: textbook column generation in an engineering partner's model (see S078; same project, not independent).

**Transferable techniques**:
- none beyond S078's (same project; the abstract adds no device).

**Quotes**:
- "using the total energy consumption as target value for optimization leads to expensive computational effort" (p. 2)
- "efficiency may be improved while still solving optimally" (p. 2)

### S080 · Neueinteilung der Bundestagswahlkreise mithilfe mathematischer Optimierung. Szenarien: 250 und 200 Bundestagswahlkreise (repORt 2018–050, RWTH Aachen, Lehrstuhl für Operations Research, 2018; no DOI)
**Read level**: full. File pp. 1–18: summary and introduction (pp. 1–3), legal basis and modelling (pp. 3–4), the software (pp. 4–5), appendix A.1–A.5 on evaluation, optimality, scoring functions, apportionment and data (pp. 6–18). Scatter plots, histograms and maps (Abb. 1–6) survive only as axis labels. The "?" after the title is the mark of the footnote on p. 1, not a question mark. The report is in German; the card quotes German.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Sebastian Goderbauer (doctoral researcher) and Georg Wicke (student assistant), both at Lübbecke's chair (pp. 1, 18) · **Voice**: the chair's collective "wir", Lübbecke the senior author as chair holder (p. 18); no passage is his alone. · **Place in trajectory**: a computation done in July and August 2018 for the Bundeswahlleiter (p. 1, footnote) inside the chair's districting line, which dates from 2013 and includes Goderbauer's master's thesis (Springer 2016), a VolkswagenStiftung grant (2016/17), a Goderbauer–Lübbecke working paper on the software and a Goderbauer–Winandy review (p. 3). INDEX.md lists a 2019 journal paper, S064, without open text.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: After the 2017 election produced the largest Bundestag ever, fewer than 299 districts are debated (p. 1). Revisions usually start from the last division and change it minimally for continuity; a new number of districts forces a complete re-division, the admissible divisions are too many to survey, so it should follow objective criteria derived from the law (pp. 1–2). The client fixed the scenarios at 250 and 200 districts (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: Separate what must hold from what is scored (p. 4). Hard: the ±25% population limit, contiguity, state and (where possible) Regierungsbezirk boundaries, Gemeindeverbände and small municipalities kept whole (p. 4); the Regierungsbezirk and Gemeindeverband rules come from practice and are declared as the authors' additions (p. 3). Scored: respect for the boundaries of counties and kreisfreie Städte, and population deviation (p. 4); continuity drops out for a complete re-division (p. 3). Each district gets a score in [0, 1] per criterion: piecewise linear in the deviation, so small deviations score alike and large ones are penalized disproportionately; administrative conformity 1 for whole counties or cities (or a district inside a city that must be split, above 1.25 times the average), otherwise the share of the district's outer boundary that is a county boundary (p. 9). The objective is the weighted sum over districts with weights the client's office set equal (pp. 4, 9). The model itself is "hier nicht ausgeführte" (p. 4).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: A districting MILP on a contiguity graph of Gemeindeverbände, municipalities and city subdivisions, computed from geometry (pp. 12, 17), with virtual adjacencies for islands and exclaves listed by official key (pp. 17–18). Districts are apportioned to states and Regierungsbezirke by the divisor method with standard rounding (BAZI 2014.11) (p. 10); in the 200 scenario RB Gießen has no admissible division into its 2 districts and is merged with RB Kassel (p. 10; Table 8, p. 11). Data: the Bundeswahlleiter's Basiskarte (shapefile, 30.09.2017) (p. 12), extended by sub-municipal geometry and population for the 17 largest cities, from the cities or OpenStreetMap, with licences tabulated (pp. 12–15); register counts scaled to official totals by the divisor method (pp. 15–16); Hannover treated as a kreisfreie Stadt (p. 13). German population 73,165,489 (p. 10).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (brief): MILP solution methods and local searches based on them, solver unnamed (p. 8). Runs stop for time reasons; the best solution value L and an upper bound U bracket the optimum, L = U proves optimality, otherwise a guaranteed percentage is reported, explained as a pessimistic estimate because the solution may already be optimal (p. 8). A decision-support tool on a free GIS shows and edits divisions, sets criterion weights and calls the algorithms for change proposals; users keep "die Hoheit über alle Entscheidungen" (pp. 4–5).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Per-district scatter plots and histograms of deviation against conformity for the current 299 and the computed 250 and 200 districts (pp. 6–7); 230 of 250 and 168 of 200 districts within 10%; four districts over 20% in the 200 scenario forced by apportionment (Saarland 21.7%, Bremen 23%); boundaries kept about as well as now, but fewer districts fully conforming (p. 6); one weight-variation example for Schleswig-Holstein (p. 8); quality guarantees mostly 5% or better within minutes, optimality for a few states or Regierungsbezirke (p. 8); no solver, hardware or run times.
- **D6 Positioning**: Optimization is set sharply against heuristic and manual procedures that determine and evaluate only a few plausible alternatives (p. 2); its own solutions come from MILP-based local searches, but each with a bound-certified quality (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing**: Written for a public client: the excluded inputs are listed (no political, cultural or economic data; no admissible division excluded or favoured in advance, p. 1), optimality is explained to lay readers (p. 8), data repairs are documented down to single municipalities (pp. 12–18). One summary sentence is stronger than the appendix: the legal principles "werden bestmöglich erfüllt" (p. 1).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Two computed divisions, an extended data set called the most comprehensive and current of its kind (p. 1), and the decision-support software (p. 5). Limits: few optimality proofs, more expected from longer runs (p. 8); with 200 districts small population moves could leave Saarland or Bremen without any admissible division (p. 10, footnote 2). Future: lower the upper bound faster for this problem class (p. 8); ask which number of districts fits the population structure best, instead of the rather arbitrary round numbers 200 and 250 (p. 2).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the claim is sized in the appendix in the manner of Taste G1 and Heuristic H2: the MILP-based local searches hand the client solutions with certified gaps, optimality is claimed only where proven, the bound is explained as pessimistic (p. 8), and losses with their structural causes are reported (p. 6). But the summary says the principles "werden bestmöglich erfüllt" (p. 1), beyond gaps of up to 5% (p. 8), and the only comparison is the current 299-district division, a different number of districts, shown "zur Einordnung" (p. 6), not a competing method.
- Heuristic H3 ✅ practice evidence (the skill has it only from abstracts and chair pages): requirements sorted into hard and scored, practice-based additions declared (pp. 3–4); the owner sets the weights (p. 4); a weight shift is shown and the trade-off handed back to the decision makers (p. 8). Taste B1 ⚠ variant: the objective is a weighted sum of two scores (p. 9), but an owned one. Workflow A step 3 ✅: the data pipeline takes seven of the eighteen pages (pp. 12–18).
- New-pattern candidate: **neutral decision support for a public client**: hard law as constraints, criteria as owner-weighted scores, the trade-off displayed, excluded inputs listed, the user keeps every decision in the tool (pp. 1, 4–5, 8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn each legal or client rule into either a hard constraint or a scored criterion, and declare every rule you add from practice (pp. 3–4).
- Score each unit per criterion on [0, 1] (piecewise linear for deviation, boundary-length share for administrative conformity), let the owner set the weights, and show one example of what a different weight does to the optimum (pp. 4, 8–9).
- When runs stop at a time limit, report each solution's guaranteed gap, tell lay readers it is a pessimistic estimate, and call optimal only what is proven (p. 8).
- Keep contiguity constraints satisfiable with virtual adjacencies for islands and exclaves, listed by official key (pp. 17–18).
- Reconcile register counts with official totals by the divisor method with standard rounding, and document the rule (p. 16).

**Quotes**:
- "Das Büro des Bundeswahlleiters gab vor, dass für unsere Arbeit beide Einteilungsziele als gleich gewichtig anzusehen seien." (p. 4)
- "Für wenige Bundesländer/Regierungsbezirke konnten wir Optimalität nachweisen." (p. 8)

### S090 · SCIP8: rethinking nonlinear constraint representation and recent progress (ROADEF 2022, 23e congrès annuel, INSA Lyon; HAL hal-03595412; no DOI)

**Method links**:

### S118 · Eliminating redundant columns from column generation subproblems using classical Benders' cuts (talk slides, SCIP workshop 2018, Aachen, 08/03/2018; no DOI)
**Read level**: full. All 56 file pages, i.e. 23 slides with overlay builds (mapping in the header). Plots survive only as axis labels and legends (pp. 46–47, 51–53), formulas as fragments (pp. 2–30), so formulas are described, not quoted.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Stephen Maher (Lancaster University), Jonas Witt (RWTH Aachen) (p. 1) · **Voice**: the three-author deck; the speaker is not named (p. 1). · **Place in trajectory**: the 2018 talk version of the work published as S074 (Discrete Optimization 2021, card in c09): same three authors, strong redundancy, Farkas-based subproblem cuts, temporal decompositions (S118 pp. 12, 23–30, 41). The title slide supplies the venue and year that the work list lacks (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The deck quotes Vanderbeck and Savelsbergh's definition of a redundant column, proposes to refine the pricing problem so that some redundant columns are eliminated, and notes that "until now" this was done only by domain propagation for tighter variable bounds in the subproblems (Vanderbeck–Savelsbergh 2006; Gamrath–Lübbecke 2010); "this talk" adds cuts to the subproblems; a column is strongly redundant if it is in no optimal solution of the master IP (p. 12).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: For two blocks, a column of block 1 is strongly redundant if no block-2 solution completes it to a feasible solution of value at most z* (p. 15). That feasibility problem is hard and says nothing about how to refine pricing, so z* is replaced by an upper bound z^UB, integrality of block 2 is dropped, and classical Benders feasibility cuts refine the subproblem (pp. 18–22). The dual polyhedron does not depend on the column; a Farkas ray of an infeasible check gives an inequality valid for every column that is not strongly redundant (pp. 23–26). A zero multiplier on the objective row gives a "feasibility subproblem cut", a negative one normalized to −1 an "optimality subproblem cut"; without objective constraints, duals instead of Farkas values maximize the violation (pp. 27–30). Pricing loop: solve the subproblems, check each solution by one LP, add a cut when it is redundant, price again when all found columns are redundant; plus: a potentially stronger master LP bound and possibly better columns for the master IP; minus: one LP per subproblem solution and possibly harder subproblems (p. 31).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: IPs in bordered block form, written for two blocks, discretization with binary λ (pp. 2, 15). Preliminary tests: no cuts on bin packing, capacitated p-median, coloring; the bullets that follow (no feasibility cuts "because of problem structure", information from the original LP relaxation, weak original LP relaxations) read as the reasons [my reading] (p. 37). Classes with cuts: capacitated lot sizing (Tempelmeier–Derstroff; Trigeiro et al.) and linearized thermal unit commitment (Frangioni et al. 2009) under period and horizon decompositions, and the MIPLIB subset of Bergner et al. (2015) with GCG's automatic detection by maximum white score (pp. 41–44). In lot sizing the cut for period t and a product subset requires at least one setup y_it of that subset (p. 48).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (brief): In GCG, a separator in the master problem whose SEPAEXECLP and SEPAEXECSOL callbacks return a domain reduction (SCIP_REDUCEDDOM); because cuts are separated only in the pricing loop, the pricer calls the separator's public methods; domain reductions are propagated; separation runs in probing mode of the original problem with changed bounds and objective; a new parameter, misc/alwaysgetduals, forces SCIP to compute duals (p. 34). Device: Farkas' lemma on an LP whose dual feasible set is independent of the fixed column (pp. 23–26).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Root node only, a check in every pricing iteration, other separators and stabilization disabled (p. 37); 11 sets (Derstroff period and horizon 2 with 98 instances each, Trigeiro horizons 2/4/8 with 69, unit commitment period and horizons 2–12 with 42, MIPLIB 39), solved counts and geometric mean times of subcuts against "default" (p. 45), where the plot legends nostab-nosepa (pp. 46–47, 53) suggest "default" is that reduced setting [my reading]. Subcuts is faster on 7 sets, slower on 3 (ls-trigeiro-horizon8, uc-period, miplib-maxwhite), equal on 1; it solves more on 3 sets and fewer on 1 (ls-derstroff-period, 61 against 62) [my count, p. 45]. Root time "usually increases", iteration behaviour "unpredictable" (p. 45). Also profiles (p. 46), gap of the original LP closed per instance (pp. 47, 53), pricing time per round with cut counts on two instances (pp. 51–52). No shift, hardware or limit printed; 3,600 s inferred from the 3600.00 entries (p. 45).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A method (Benders cuts in pricing), its GCG implementation and a first study (pp. 23–34, 45). Limits: the costs of p. 31, no cuts on the classical classes (p. 37), longer root times (p. 45). Future work: control of pricing difficulty, more classes with time-dependent structure, other decompositions for MIPLIB, the effect on the price-and-branch heuristic (p. 54). Against the journal version: S074 compares with GCG's default settings (S074 p. 23), keeps four of these sets (Derstroff period and horizon 2, unit commitment horizons 8 and 12; S074 p. 26) with 81 Derstroff instances where the talk has 98 (S074 p. 28; S118 p. 45), and has no MIPLIB set (no mention anywhere in S074's text).

**Method links**:
