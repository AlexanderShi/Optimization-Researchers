- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - Entry from a problem that is already solvable: the LP (3.1) is given, so the chapter asks why one should reformulate at all, and answers only that there might be a structure to exploit, which ch. 4 develops (p. 3).
  - Principle against algorithm: the decomposition principle is presented as a reformulation by Minkowski-Weyl, not a solution method, in the abstract (p. 1) and again in the Reference Notes (p. 56). Exercise 3.2 makes the reader say how column generation relates to it (p. 59).
  - Pedagogical entry: ch. 2 built master and pricing problems by construction; here they are derived by a direct mathematical reformulation, which also identifies the constraints of the pricing problem (p. 3).
  - Problem statement: min c⊺x subject to Ax ≥ b [σ_b], Dx ≥ d [σ_d], x ≥ 0, assumed feasible with a finite optimum, with the constraints grouped into two non-empty sets A and D (pp. 3–4).
  - Transferable: state a decomposition as one compact formulation plus one grouping of its constraints, and name in advance the original-space objects (primal solution, dual solution, basis) that the method must hand back (pp. 3–4, 12–13).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Key idea: write D by its extreme points and rays (Theorem 3.1, cited from Nemhauser and Wolsey 1988, Thm 4.8) and substitute them into the objective and the constraints of A. The master MP (3.7)/(3.9) has one convexity row, and z⋆_MP = z⋆_LP by construction (pp. 5–8). The pricing problem minimizes the adjusted costs over D, minus π0. It must be solved by an algorithm that returns an element of X (a simplex-type method, or an interior-point method with crossover); when it is unbounded it returns some negative ray, not necessarily the one of minimum reduced cost (pp. 10–11).
  - *Kept* from ch. 2: the RMP–pricing loop and its information flow (Fig. 3.5, pp. 9–10); the lower bound, proved from Corollary 2.1 (Prop 3.2, p. 17); Note 2.17's rule that heuristic pricing gives a bound only from under-approximations of the reduced costs (p. 17); the adjusted-cost convention of Note 2.24 (pp. 11–12).
  - *Changed*: the master and its pricing domain come out of the reformulation (p. 3); the generic dual π becomes (π_b, π0) (p. 10); the x–λ link (3.9f) is dropped while solving and used once afterwards (p. 8); the pricing problem's own duals complete the dual of the LP (Prop 3.1, p. 12).
  - *Why decompose an LP at all*: every grouping gives the same optimum (3.21), so the chapter names three other reasons (pp. 13–14):
    - (i) decentralization over a block-diagonal structure, Dantzig's historical motive (Dantzig 1963, his pp. 448–449), which is lost when blocks are merged;
    - (ii) a pricing structure that a specialized algorithm solves well, such as network flow, for which integer solutions come easily from the LP;
    - (iii) the groups need not be disjoint, and redundant or implied constraints may be added, so that a structure is created in the pricing problem while duplicates stay in the master.
    - Integrality, under which groupings stop being equivalent, is left to ch. 4 (p. 14).
  - Extensions built on the same substitution:
    - polytopes and cones, where a cone loses its zero point and its convexity row (p. 9);
    - block-diagonal structure with one pricing problem and one convexity row per block (pp. 14–16);
    - static variables left outside the reformulated set (pp. 20–21);
    - a restricted compact formulation grown by the positive variables of pricing solutions, with no reformulation at all (pp. 22–26);
    - positive edge, IPS and MMCC read as pricing strategies on a degenerate simplex dictionary (pp. 26–28);
    - Benders as Dantzig-Wolfe of the dual (pp. 28–30);
    - the flow-decomposition theorem proved by a reformulation (pp. 31–33).
  - Transferable: derive the pricing problem from a compact formulation instead of guessing it. If two compact formulations give the same master, prefer the one whose pricing domain is a cone. For the SDVSP, the formulation with a return arc (d,o) gives the set-partitioning LP relaxation directly, while the o–d formulation needs the rescaling λ = vθ (pp. 46–51).
- **D3 Problem class & data model** (team.json):
  - Class: LPs only.
    - (3.1) is feasible with a finite optimum (p. 3). Theorem 3.1 needs a polyhedron with an extreme point, which a non-empty region in the non-negative orthant has (p. 5). Integer ray scaling relies on rational data (Note 3.3, p. 6).
    - Integer programs appear only through their LP relaxations (TCSPP (3.78), p. 41; cutting stock, Exercise 3.16, pp. 65–66), as the MILP (3.59) with complicating integer variables that Benders reformulates (p. 28), and in forward pointers to chs. 4 and 7 (pp. 14, 43).
  - Structures exploited:
    - block-diagonal D with linking constraints A (pp. 14–15); identical blocks (a homogeneous fleet), whose use is deferred to §4.4 (p. 17);
    - polytope and polyhedral-cone pricing domains (pp. 5–6, 9); network-flow pricing (p. 14);
    - static variables, as in inventory routing (Desaulniers et al. 2016b) and network design (Frangioni and Gorgone 2014) (p. 21);
    - standard-form equality systems for column activation, and the homogeneous case Dx = 0 (pp. 22, 25);
    - the capacitated minimum cost flow problem (p. 31) and time-space networks for aircraft routing (pp. 51–52).
  - Correctness conditions stated:
    - the pricing solver must return an element of X (p. 11); Prop 3.1 needs the MP solved to optimality (p. 12);
    - the lower bound needs exact or under-approximated minimum reduced costs (p. 17);
    - in column activation only (σ_b, π_d) is guaranteed dual optimal, because the RLP's σ_d can fail at a degenerate solution (Note 3.11, pp. 24–25).
  - Data: no model library. Toy instances only: a two-variable LP (p. 35), a TCSPP on 6 nodes and 10 arcs, used twice (pp. 40–41, 53), the SDVSP as a generic network without numbers (pp. 47–51) and 15 daily flights between three cities (pp. 51–52).
  - Transferable: let the two groups overlap, and add redundant or implied constraints to the pricing domain when they create a structure that a specialized algorithm can exploit; the duplicates stay in the master (p. 14).
- **D4 Algorithm & implementation design, proofs and devices** (team.json):
  - Proof devices:
    - Theorem 3.1 is cited, and its proof replaced by three ideas: both sets are finite, P is non-empty, and the two inclusions (p. 5). Note 3.1 adds that λ determines x but not conversely (p. 5).
    - Dual recovery (Prop 3.1, Walker 1969): strong duality in the LP and the MP, dual feasibility read off the pricing problem's non-negative reduced costs, and c̄ = 0 at the MP optimum (Note 2.6) give π0 = π_d⊺d (pp. 12–13).
    - Basis recovery: fix the zero-valued x-variables and re-solve the restricted LP (3.20) with a simplex-type method; the basis may contain degenerate variables (p. 13).
    - Bounds (Prop 3.2): z_RMP + Σ_k c̄^k ≤ z⋆_MP = z⋆_LP ≤ z_RMP, from Corollary 2.1 plus the −∞ case and κ^k = 1. The arbitrary-dual form (3.33) is the Lagrangian dual bound, which in the authors' account appeared historically only in the 1970s (p. 17).
    - Ray normalization: an added constraint 1⊺x = 1 turns ray pricing into point pricing, so that the grouping with no constraints in D reproduces the primal simplex with Dantzig's rule (Note 3.9, pp. 19–20). IPS normalizes its rays the same way (p. 27), and Note 3.17 fixes x_do = 1 (p. 51).
    - Column activation (Prop 3.3): the RLP gives an upper bound by strong duality (3.47) and (3.46) gives a Lagrangian lower bound. A negative pricing value forces a positive variable outside J, so J grows at every iteration until c̄ = 0 (pp. 23–24).
    - A four-variable counterexample (3.50): at a degenerate RLP solution, the RLP's dual σ_d is not optimal while the pricing problem's π_d is (Note 3.11, pp. 24–25).
    - Positive edge's compatibility test uses one random vector: an incompatible column passes only with probability zero, and w⊺ = v⊺A_2B^−1, computed once, lets the test run on the original columns (p. 27).
    - Benders master: a bilevel rewrite, the dual passage to a polyhedron that does not depend on the first-stage variables, feasibility cuts from its rays and the epigraph form of the inner minimum yield (3.65), the dual of the MP, and its optimality-cut form (3.66) (pp. 28–30).
    - Flow decomposition (Prop 3.4, a simplified version of Gauthier et al. 2014): transform to a circulation, reformulate over the cone of circulations, whose rays are cycles in the augmented network (paths of G when x_do = 1), and count a basis of size |A| + |S| + |D|. This gives at most |A| + |N| positive paths and cycles, at most |A| of them cycles (pp. 31–33).
  - Implementation rules stated:
    - Scale extreme rays to integer vectors with relatively prime components: a matter of taste in this chapter, important in ch. 4 and possibly a matter of performance in ch. 7 (Note 3.3, p. 6).
    - Drop the x–λ link while solving and compute x⋆ once at the end (p. 8). Track the zero extreme point explicitly (Note 3.4, p. 8), or drop it and the convexity row for a cone (p. 9).
    - Do not reformulate easy bounds: separate pricing problems for x and v would replace 2n_v bound constraints by 2^{n_v} extreme points, so keep v static in the master (pp. 20–21).
    - Partial pricing over blocks: call only a fraction of the pricing problems per iteration, chosen by past success, by time since the last call, or by the convexity dual π0^k, an optimistic predictor of the next column's value (Note 3.7, pp. 16–17).
    - Column activation: initialize with a Phase I; give an omitted row a zero dual if it is satisfied, and otherwise a big-M dual with an artificial variable; deactivate variables no longer used (Note 3.13, p. 25).
    - Benders: start with no cuts and an arbitrary π_b ≥ 0 (p. 30).
    - Price SDVSP circulations by a shortest o–d path closed with x_do = 1, which yields the true minimum reduced-cost ray (Note 3.17, p. 51).
  - Transferable: after column generation, solve the pricing LP once more at the final master duals. Its duals complete the dual of the compact LP (Prop 3.1, pp. 12–13), and they may differ from the compact LP's own duals, by a translation of +29 in Example 3.2 (p. 43).

Here the extremes are limit-case checks: all constraints in D (the pricing problem solves the LP) or none (pricing reproduces the primal simplex). The generic framework of Gauthier et al. (2018) [= S044, inferred] places IPS and MMCC at its two ends, with none or all positive variables changed. pp. 18–20, 28.
- New-pattern candidate: after a primal trick, ask for its dual analogue (same name as the S060 card). Benders is derived as Dantzig-Wolfe applied to the dual (pp. 28–30), and Exercise 3.17 restates PS, IPS and MMCC by the dual LPs of their pricing problems (pp. 66–68).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Dual completion after column generation: solve the pricing LP once more at the final master duals; its duals π_d, with π_b, form an optimal dual of the compact LP (Prop 3.1, Walker 1969, pp. 12–13). Expect them to differ from the compact LP's own duals (a translation by +29 in Example 3.2, p. 43).
- Basis recovery from a master solution: fix the zero-valued variables of the recovered x⋆ and re-solve the restricted LP (3.20) with a simplex-type code (p. 13).
- Partial pricing over blocks ranked by the convexity duals π0^k (an optimistic predictor of a new column's value), by past success or by time since the last call. Report the Lagrangian bound only when every block's reduced cost is exact or under-approximated (Note 3.7 and the remark after Prop 3.2, pp. 16–17).
- Normalize ray pricing, so that an unbounded pricing LP returns the ray of minimum reduced cost under a stated scaling instead of an arbitrary negative ray (the caveat of p. 11): add 1⊺x = 1 (Note 3.9, pp. 19–20; IPS, p. 27) or fix the return-arc flow x_do = 1 (Note 3.17, p. 51).
- Static variables: keep in the master the variables that do not appear in the structured constraints; reformulating their bounds would replace 2n_v bound constraints by 2^{n_v} extreme points (pp. 20–21).
- Return arc: add (d,o) to an o–d path model so that pricing runs over the cone of circulations. The zero point and the convexity row drop, rays are the columns, and a shortest o–d path closed with x_do = 1 prices them (pp. 50–51; Example 3.4, pp. 53–54).
- Column-and-row activation of the compact LP: add the positive variables of each pricing solution, and the rows they touch, to a restricted compact LP; start with a Phase I; give omitted rows a zero dual if satisfied, otherwise a big-M dual with an artificial variable; stop at pricing value 0 (Algorithm 3.1, Prop 3.3, Notes 3.12–3.13, pp. 22–25; Example 3.4, pp. 53–56). Take the final dual from the pricing LP (π_d), not from a degenerate RLP (σ_d) (Note 3.11, pp. 24–25).
- Positive edge compatibility test: draw one random vector v, compute w⊺ = v⊺A_2B^−1 once, and treat a column as compatible (non-degenerate pivot) when w⊺a_j = 0; an incompatible column passes with probability zero (p. 27).
- Support-size bound by reformulation: to bound how many objects (paths, cycles) a solution needs, reformulate over a cone whose rays are those objects and count the rows a basis of the master needs (Prop 3.4: at most |A| + |N| paths and cycles, at most |A| of them cycles, pp. 31–33).
- Triangulated toy instance for a column-generation code: solve the compact LP, the fully enumerated MP and the MP by column generation, and check that x, the duals up to translation and the optimum agree. Log z_RMP, the duals, the minimum reduced cost, the generated column and lb = z_RMP + c̄ per iteration, and plot z_RMP only once the RMP is feasible (pp. 35–46; Table 3.1, p. 44; Fig. 3.14, p. 46).
