### S074 · Avoiding redundant columns by adding classical Benders cuts to column generation subproblems (Discrete Optimization 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.disopt.2021.100626)
**Read level**: full. All 36 file pages read: ORE cover sheet (p. 1), body pp. 2-34 (introduction, theory, generalisation, algorithm, computational results, conclusions, acknowledgments), references pp. 34-36. The text is the ORE accepted manuscript (p. 1); the publisher's version was not compared.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Stephen J. Maher (Exeter), Jonas T. Witt (RWTH Aachen) (p. 2); Maher funded by EPSRC EP/P003060/1 (p. 34) · **Place in trajectory**: pricing-side domain reduction in the GCG line; it extends the domain propagation of Gamrath and Lübbecke (SEA 2010), which is part of GCG's default (pp. 5-6, 28, 33). It is the one Benders-related paper in the skill's record (Routing table; Misreading 3). An earlier title of the work, "Eliminating redundant columns from column generation subproblems using classical Benders' cuts", is indexed separately as S118 and was not read in this batch.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is a bound effect of column generation. Pricing can generate columns that are not needed to express any integer optimum, and the master LP bound is potentially stronger if they are never generated (abstract, p. 2). Avoiding all redundant columns would close the whole integrality gap at the root; the authors grant that this may be too much to aim for in practice but take the potential bound gain as motivation (p. 5). The second entry is a generality gap. Earlier remedies (reduced-cost fixing for shortest-path pricing, constraint-programming filtering, enumeration of every column with reduced cost below UB - LB) were built for specific problem types, mainly vehicle routing, knapsack and crew assignment; only the Gamrath-Lübbecke propagation is general, and "a general implementation that could be tested across applications is missing" (pp. 5-6). The problem is stated as: reduce the pricing domain generically so that strongly redundant columns are not generated (pp. 6-7).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**:
  - Redundancy in the sense of Vanderbeck and Savelsbergh depends on a reference optimum (Def. 1.1, pp. 4-5), which makes it as hard as the problem itself (p. 7). The paper uses *strong* redundancy instead: the column is zero in every optimal integer solution (Def. 2.1, p. 7). It notes that earlier methods in fact remove only strongly redundant columns (p. 7).
  - A verification IP with the column fixed is infeasible if and only if the column is strongly redundant (Lemma 2.2, p. 9); any relaxation of it gives a sufficient test (Lemma 2.3, p. 9). Dropping integrality and replacing z* by the incumbent z̄_UB gives the verification LP (Thm 2.1, p. 10).
  - A Farkas ray of an infeasible verification LP becomes a classical Benders feasibility cut added to the pricing problem, valid for every column that is not strongly redundant (Thm 2.2, p. 11); rays with positive weight on the bound row give optimality cuts (13) (pp. 11-12).
  - The "practical verification LP" drops the upper-bound row, so it is the original LP relaxation with the column's variables fixed (p. 12). Its cuts are as strong as those of the full verification LP (Thms 2.5-2.6, p. 15), and its dual solution maximizes the violation at the column (p. 16). The reason given for this choice: in the authors' Benders experience optimality cuts tend to be stronger than feasibility cuts, and an infeasible LP has infinitely many dual rays with no rule for picking the best one (p. 12).
  - Changed: the pricing problems (cuts are added) and the pricing loop (Algorithm 1, p. 22). Unchanged: the master problem, branching and every other GCG default (p. 23).
  - Generalised to more than two blocks (Thms 3.1-3.3, pp. 19-20) and to MIPs by fixing only the integer variables of the column (pp. 20-21). The authors note that the cuts use global information from the master constraints, which "is contrary to the decomposition principle" (p. 33).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - Pure IPs in bordered block-diagonal form, with disjoint subsystems linked by the rows (1b), reformulated by discretization (p. 3); MIPs follow by fixing only integer variables (pp. 20-21). The theory is written for two blocks (p. 8); the theorems for more blocks are proved "analogously", one line each (pp. 19-20).
  - Correctness needs only z̄_UB ≥ z* (proof of Thm 2.1, p. 10); without a finite primal bound only feasibility cuts can arise (p. 9).
  - Classes tried first: bin packing, cutting stock, vertex coloring, capacitated p-median, generalized assignment and single-source capacitated facility location (p. 23). Classes where the cuts appear: period and horizon decompositions of multi-level lot sizing (Tempelmeier-Derstroff, 4 periods) and of linearized thermal unit commitment (Frangioni, 24 periods) (pp. 25-26). Lot-sizing horizons are linked by variables, unit-commitment horizons by constraints (p. 26); in lot sizing the feasibility cuts take the readable form of a sum of setup variables of period t that must be at least 1 (p. 27).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**:
  - Proof devices: Farkas' lemma turns infeasibility of the check into a cut (p. 11); a weak-duality chain (20a-c) gives the optimality cut (p. 14); scaling a ray by its last component, w̃ = ũ/ũ^c, maps cuts between the two verification LPs (proof of Thm 2.6, p. 15); reduced-cost fixing is rebuilt as a Farkas ray (w, 1) of a verification LP with x_i fixed to 1, so subproblem cuts generalise it from single variables to whole columns (Thm 2.7, pp. 17-18).
  - Algorithm 1 (p. 22): solve the pricing problems; check every column with negative reduced cost by one LP; if the check fails, add a feasibility or optimality cut to that pricing problem; re-solve only the refined pricing problems; stop when some column passes or nothing was refined. The right-hand sides of optimality cuts are tightened whenever the incumbent improves (p. 23).
  - Build: a development version of GCG 2.1.4 on a development version of SCIP 5.0.1, with CPLEX 12.7.1.0 for the restricted master LP and for the pricing MIPs; cuts only at the root; heuristic pricing first (20% gap limit, 1,000-node limit), exact pricing when heuristic pricing finds no column (p. 23).
  - Side effects named: the cuts are typically more numerically difficult than the original rows and link the periods inside a horizon, which can destroy the separability of the pricing problem (p. 31).
  - Notational slips: the proof of Thm 2.6 concludes w̃ ∈ T, the ray set, for a dual solution that belongs to S (p. 15); Thm 3.1(ii) writes c_1^T x̄^k for the k-th block (p. 19); the sentence before Thm 3.3 introduces a dual solution as w̃ ∈ T^k (p. 20).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - Baseline: the setting Default, i.e. GCG's defaults, which already contain the Gamrath-Lübbecke propagation, so the comparison is against the only earlier general method (pp. 23, 28). Subcuts is Default with Algorithm 1 switched on at the root (p. 23). Xeon L5630 2.13 GHz, 16 GB, 3,600 s (p. 23). No outside solver, no run of the compact model in a MILP solver, no seeds or permutations (pp. 23-32).
  - Classical classes: "(almost) no subproblem cuts were generated"; no instance counts, table or timing is given (p. 23).
  - Temporal test sets: ls-Derstroff-period and ls-Derstroff-horizon2 with 81 instances each, uc-Frangioni-horizon8 and -horizon12 with 42 each (Table 1, p. 28). Metrics: affected instances, cuts per affected instance, solved counts, and shifted geometric means (shift 1) of time, iterations and gap closed relative to the original LP relaxation, over the instances solved by both settings (p. 27).
  - Master LP (Table 1, p. 28): gap closed 8.10 to 13.69, 84.78 to 87.94, 78.76 to 83.88 and 97.97 to 98.04. The text reports these as "an increase in the dual bound of 5.59% and 3.16%" and of 5.12% and 0.07% (p. 28); the figures equal the differences of the gap-closed means, i.e. percentage points of gap closed [my reading]. Master-LP time rises on every set (p. 28). Only feasibility cuts were generated, even when the optimum z* was supplied in place of z̄_UB (p. 28).
  - Integer solve (Table 2, p. 30): time 72.50 to 68.08, 13.62 to 9.91 (-27.24%), 95.15 to 79.11 (-16.04 s) and 31.86 to 34.59 s (+2.73 s); nodes 865.69 to 793.79, 35.06 to 18.97 (-45.89%), 6.56 to 4.52 and 1.32 to 1.32 (pp. 29-30).
  - Sample sizes: on the unit-commitment sets the master LP is solved within the limit on 10 of 42 instances and the integer problem on 6 and 8 (Tables 1-2, pp. 28, 30), so the unit-commitment means rest on few instances.
  - Pricing cost: Table 3 (p. 31) and per-round pricing times with cumulative cut counts for three lot-sizing instances (Fig. 4, p. 32). Table 3 gives 42 affected instances for both unit-commitment sets, where Tables 1-2 give 40 and 36 (pp. 28, 30, 31); the text does not explain the difference.
  - Losses reported: the classical classes (p. 23), higher root time on all sets (p. 28), the slowdown on uc-horizon12 (p. 30) and harder pricing there (p. 31).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: It continues Vanderbeck-Savelsbergh [27] and Gamrath-Lübbecke [13] (p. 33). The weaker notion of columns redundant for the LP is credited to Lübbecke-Desrosiers [17] and Sol [23] (p. 5). The reduced-cost-fixing line (Beasley; Ceria et al.; Hadjar et al.; Irnich et al.) and constraint-programming filtering (Fahle and Sellmann) are described neutrally, then judged limited in general applicability (pp. 5-6). It sets itself apart from Baldacci et al., Rönnberg-Larsson and the integral simplex "by defining necessary and sufficient conditions for identifying non-redundant columns" (p. 6). It claims, "to the best of the authors' knowledge", to be the first general approach that uses Benders-type inequalities against redundant columns, and calls it "a novel, interesting integration of Benders decomposition and Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation" (p. 33).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contributions are listed (a)-(e) (pp. 6-7); the theory is set out on the two-block case first and generalised afterwards (pp. 8, 18-21); one diagram ties together the original problem, master LP, pricing problem, partial LP relaxation and cuts (Fig. 1, p. 21). Hedging is moderate ("We expect", pp. 7, 9, 16). The limitation stands in the abstract (p. 2); the null result on classical classes is framed as valuable (p. 23); the extra root time is called "unavoidable" (p. 28). The conclusion interprets rather than summarises: the bound improvement "is not as great as expected", and two causes are named (p. 33). One overreach: "can significantly improve the performance of the branch-and-price algorithm", with no statistical test (p. 30).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution levels: a concept (strong redundancy), theory (validity, equivalence of the two checks, the link to reduced-cost fixing), an algorithm (modified pricing), an implementation in GCG that is "planned to be made available to the academic community" (p. 7; the text does not say it was released) and a computational study (pp. 6-7). Limits are concrete: no cuts on the classical classes (p. 23); only feasibility cuts (p. 28); higher root time (p. 28); harder, less separable pricing on long horizons (p. 31); a check built on the original LP relaxation, which can be much weaker than the master LP, and one LP per column (p. 33). Future work: cuts from partial pricing solutions (p. 18); the limits of pricing difficulty up to which the cuts pay (p. 31); the master LP as the check, solved by column generation, with the open question of what to do with columns generated during checking; Benders enhancement techniques (pp. 33-34).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (steps 1, 3, 5 and 6): the limitation is in the abstract (p. 2); the null result on classical classes is reported and explained (pp. 23-24); losses are reported (root master-LP time up on every set, p. 28; uc-horizon12 slower, p. 30); shifted geometric means with their shift and the "solved by both" rule are stated (p. 27); the baseline is the host solver's default including the group's own earlier general method (pp. 23, 28). This confirms Misreading 3 from the full text: the efficacy is called limited on classical problems (p. 2) and the improvement "not as great as expected" (p. 33).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the null result on classical classes carries no counts or table (p. 23); there is no general-purpose set such as MIPLIB to show no harm, and no outside competitor (pp. 23-32); the unit-commitment means rest on 10, 6 and 8 solved instances (pp. 28, 30); the "5.59%" dual-bound increase is a difference of gap-closed means [my reading] (p. 28); "can significantly improve" has no test (p. 30); Table 3's affected counts differ from Tables 1-2 (pp. 28, 30, 31); no seeds, although these are deterministic but variable runs [inferred].
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (step 6, original variables in view): the redundancy check is the original LP relaxation with the pricing solution fixed (pp. 12, 21-22), and the bound gain is measured as gap of the original LP relaxation closed (p. 27).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (step 2 not taken): no run of the compact model in a MILP solver; the yardstick is GCG's default branch-and-price (pp. 23-30).
- H4 ✅ [inferred link]: a weak step of the group's own solver, redundant columns left by GCG's propagation, is turned into a paper that generalises it (pp. 5-6, 28, 33). Routing row "his one Benders paper puts Benders cuts inside pricing" ✅ (pp. 11, 33).
- New-pattern candidate: **null result explained from the textbook model, then the predicted structure tested**. The generic component does nothing on textbook classes; a bin-packing argument explains why (the check LP is feasible under mild conditions and the original LP is weak, p. 24); the explanation says dependence between subproblems is needed, so temporal decompositions are tested (p. 25), and varying the horizon length checks the mechanism (fewer subproblems, fewer cuts, p. 29).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To show that a candidate column cannot be in any optimum, fix its variables in the original LP relaxation (plus the incumbent bound). Infeasibility gives a Farkas ray, i.e. a classical Benders feasibility cut to add to the pricing problem; a dual solution whose value exceeds z̄_UB - c^T x̄ gives an optimality cut (pp. 9-14, 22).
- Drop the upper-bound row from the check LP: its dual solution then maximizes the violation at the column and gives optimality cuts instead of an arbitrary ray (pp. 12, 15-16).
- Show that the closest classical technique is a special case of your device (reduced-cost fixing as a subproblem cut with x_i fixed to 1, Thm 2.7) to establish generality (pp. 17-18).
- When a generic component does nothing on the textbook classes, derive the reason from the textbook model and let it choose the next test class (pp. 23-25).
- Test the mechanism by varying the decomposition granularity on the same instances (period against horizon 2; horizon 8 against 12): fewer subproblems should mean fewer cuts and less gain (pp. 26, 29).
- Plot per instance the gap closed by the reformulation and the extra gap closed by the component (Fig. 3, p. 29), and per pricing round the pricing time with cumulative cut counts (Fig. 4, p. 32), to show where the gain and the cost arise.
- Take the host solver's default, including your own earlier method, as the baseline setting and switch on only the new component (pp. 23, 28).

**Quotes**:
- "While their efficacy is limited on classical problems, the benefits of applying the cuts is demonstrated on structured models to which a temporal decomposition can be applied." (p. 2)
- "While this is an undesired result, it is valuable in highlighting a limitation to the proposed methods and the subproblem cuts, and it also gives an insight into the nature of (strong) redundancy." (p. 23)

### S043 · An image-based approach to detecting structural similarity among mixed integer programs (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2022, DOI 10.1287/ijoc.2021.1117; SSRN DOI 10.2139/ssrn.3437981)
**Read level**: full. All 43 file pages read: title and author page (p. 1), body pp. 2-35 (introduction, MIPLIB comparison, methodology, data and training, results, conclusions), references pp. 35-42, appendix Table 10 of the 64 sources (pp. 42-43). The text is the SSRN preprint dated April 20, 2020 (p. 1); the IJOC version was not compared, so tables and wording may differ there.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Zachary Steever (corresponding author, PhD candidate), Chase Murray, Junsong Yuan and Mark Karwan, all University at Buffalo; Lübbecke fifth of five, listed as "Professor and Head of Group" at RWTH Aachen (p. 1). The paper does not say which part is his · **Place in trajectory**: one of the two machine-learning papers in his record (Misreading 4); his group's instruments supply the data: strIPlib (Bastubbe, Kirchhart, Lübbecke, Rieken, Witt) and GCG 3.0 detection and visualization (pp. 4, 8-9, 12, 36).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The paper poses "a new question": can pictures of constraint-matrix (CCM) structure identify similar MIP models and instances (abstract, p. 2)? The gap it names: classification schemes for models (Kendall notation and its analogues) are siloed by application (pp. 2-3), and the only quantitative instance comparison is MIPLIB 2017's metric over instance statistics (pp. 3, 6). CCM structure has long been used to solve MIPs (Dantzig-Wolfe, Benders, cuts, total unimodularity) but "has not been well exploited as a basis for MIP comparison" (p. 3). Uses claimed: similar structure may share solution approaches and modelling techniques and may hint at what makes problems hard (pp. 3-4).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: ICIP has three steps: render the nonzero pattern of A, after GCG's structure detection, as a black-and-white image; encode it with a convolutional autoencoder; compare codes by ISS, the Euclidean distance between feature vectors (pp. 4-5; eq. (2), p. 19). Against MIPLIB's 110 hand-defined statistics in 11 groups (pp. 6-7), it uses no manually defined features, which the authors say reduces human bias (p. 10). Not changed: GCG's detection is used as it is, taking the decomposition with the greatest maxwhite score (p. 12). Rejected alternatives: Fisher vectors and bag-of-words, because sparse binary CCM images lack the rich local structure those methods need (pp. 13-14). The paper sets itself apart from ML for solving: "we use CNNs to study optimization problems, rather than to help solve them" (p. 14).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - Only the matrix A of max{cx : Ax ≤ b, x ∈ Z^n_+} (eq. (1), p. 3), and only its nonzero pattern: coefficient values, c, b and variable types are ignored (pp. 10, 13).
  - Stated assumption: the decomposition with the greatest maxwhite score "best reflects the underlying structure" (p. 10, restated p. 35); hence the method is not invariant to row and column permutations (pp. 10, 35).
  - Data: strIPlib, the 19 problem types with at least 50 instances, 50 instances per type, at least one per source, files of at most 1 MB, 950 instances drawn at random from 64 sources (pp. 20-21; Table 10, pp. 42-43); 12 MIPLIB 2017 instances with clear problem descriptions, kept out of training (pp. 31-32).
  - Images fixed at 640×480 pixels by GCG's gnuplot output, compressed by binary max pooling (pp. 12-13).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: GCG 3.0 default detection on MPS files, maxwhite choice (p. 12); gnuplot scripts stripped of axes and shading, colours inverted (pp. 12-13). Autoencoder: 3×3 kernels, 2×2 max pooling, ReLU, 22 layers, code taken from layer 12 (15×20×8) (pp. 16-19; Table 2, p. 19); binary cross-entropy loss (p. 18); TensorFlow 2.1.0, 6,721 trainable parameters, 3 epochs, SGD with Nesterov momentum, batch 32, learning rate 0.01; Adam, AdaGrad and RMSProp gave the same accuracy with higher loss (p. 22). Transformations the network must absorb: shading, pattern density, proportionality (pp. 15-16). Problem- and source-level vectors are averages of instance codes (pp. 23, 29); 2-D layout by R's qgraph (p. 23); impurity index = number of constraint classes present in some but not all sources of a type, at most 17 (p. 28); top-49 match rate (p. 28). Proof devices: n/a (no proofs).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - No solver runs. The evaluation is face validity: known relations are recovered (cwl and cpm, ISS 0.45, the lowest pair; pcp and col, 0.49) (pp. 25-26); graphs at ISS thresholds 2.00 and 4.00 (pp. 23-24) and 2.00 and 4.50 (p. 33), with no reason given for the thresholds (choosing one is future work, p. 36). Mean ISS 5.81 (sd 2.38) at problem level and 7.45 (sd 3.41) at instance level (pp. 23, 28).
  - Training: Dell OptiPlex 3046, Core i7-6700, 16 GB; 3 epochs in about 7 minutes; loss 8.2837 to 0.3852, accuracy 0.4277 to 0.9750 (Table 4, p. 22). One training run, no seeds (p. 22).
  - Baseline: no quantitative comparison with the MIPLIB metric; constraint-class tables are shown as contrast (Tables 5, 6 and 8; pp. 26, 28, 30), and the authors state that it "is impossible to define whether" ICIP's relations are better (p. 10).
  - Out-of-training check: f2g links to two gap sources; ger has ISS 1.16, 1.18, 1.32 and 1.64 to cpmC, cwl, cpmE and bppA (pp. 31-32).
  - Inconsistencies: the text gives clp "the lowest %-Match (16.82%)", while Table 7 lists 18.98% for clp and 18.61% for map (p. 29). The statement that high-impurity types "perform poorly on the Mean ISS and %-Match measures" (pp. 28-29) has no statistic; in Table 7 inr has impurity 0 with Mean ISS 7.89 and 22.37%, and col impurity 4 with Mean ISS 1.62 (p. 29) [my reading].
- **D6 Literature positioning**: Against model ontologies (pp. 2-3) and against MIPLIB 2017's instance-statistics similarity, "the only other documented method" of its kind (p. 6). It criticises that metric's constraint classes (hierarchy ignored; the catch-all class covers over 9% of MIPLIB constraints) (pp. 7-8) but grants its advantages (it covers A, x, c and b and depends less on GCG) (p. 10), and offers ICIP as "a supplement to MIPLIB's methodology" (p. 10). It distances itself from ML-for-solving work (Bengio et al. 2018; Gasse et al. 2019) (p. 14).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The abstract frames a new question (p. 2); the three-step pipeline with picture examples comes early (pp. 4-5); a tutorial on CNN design follows (pp. 16-18). Hedges: "may allow" (abstract, p. 2); the speculation that all MIPs may be definable by a unified set of image primitives is labelled as not claimed but hoped for (p. 34). Spin: problem types that connect to nothing are called "a positive result" (p. 27). The conclusion uses "often" on a handful of examples and makes two "first" claims (p. 35). Figure 16's caption has "gatC" for a gap source (p. 31).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: a methodology and exploratory analyses; the paper claims the "first known use of automated feature engineering to compare MIPs" and the "first documented analyses of a quantitative measure for problem-to-problem and instance-to-problem similarity" (p. 35). Limits stated concretely: heuristic detection and the maxwhite assumption, so no permutation invariance (pp. 10, 35); A only (pp. 10, 36). A data-curation result: the six clp sources are different formulations, "later corroborated by the maintainers of strIPlib", and clpD resembles a generalized assignment model (pp. 30-31). Future work: matrix seriation and permutations (p. 35); interpreting the learned features and combining them with human-defined ones (pp. 35-36); larger data sets, an empirical validation by running solution methods on related instances, a meaningful ISS threshold, and x, c and b (p. 36).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ⚠ variant (exit A and its checkpoint): a learned proxy for "structure" is checked for face validity only, by recovered known relations (pp. 25-26), constraint-class tables (pp. 26, 28, 30) and 12 held-out MIPLIB instances (pp. 31-34); validation against solver behaviour is deferred (p. 36) and the thresholds are unexplained (pp. 23, 33, 36). The checkpoint "a proxy that looks right is not evidence" applies to the paper as written.
- Method 3 ✗ contradiction (co-signed; fifth of five, his share unknown): "often" in the conclusion rests on a few cases (p. 35); the text-table mismatch on clp (p. 29); the impurity relation asserted without a statistic (pp. 28-29); two "first" claims (p. 35); no quantitative baseline (p. 10).
- Misreading 4 ✅ and G5 ✅: the CNN is used to study optimization problems, not to solve them (p. 14); the study is Buffalo-led and makes no solver-performance claim (pp. 1, 36).
- G4 ✅: the images depend on GCG's heuristic detection and on the maxwhite choice, so the paper itself documents that a decomposition picture can be an artefact of the detector (pp. 10, 35).
- New-pattern candidate: **impurity audit of a benchmark library before treating a named problem class as one test set**. Count the constraint classes present in some but not all sources of a type (9 of 19 strIPlib types impure); inspect the worst class per source; the strIPlib maintainers corroborated that the six clp sources are different formulations (pp. 27-31). Co-signed, Buffalo-led; it sits next to Method 3 step 2 (retire or label test sets).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Validate a new similarity or structure measure first on pairs known to be related (cwl against cpm, pcp against col) before reading new relations off it (pp. 5, 25-26).
- Impurity index for a benchmark class: with SCIP's 17-class constraint classifier, count the classes that occur in some but not all sources of a problem type; a nonzero count flags mixed formulations, which a per-source table then locates (pp. 28-31).
- Keep instances from a second library out of training (12 MIPLIB instances) to check that a learned representation transfers (pp. 31-34).
- Stack the detected-structure images of all instances of a class into one composite image to see whether the class has one structure or several (pp. 25, 29-30).

**Quotes**:
- "we use CNNs to study optimization problems, rather than to help solve them." (p. 14)
- "These analyses reveal that structurally similar MIPs often share a related underlying problem, even if those problems originate from seemingly disparate applications." (p. 35)

### S014 · Enabling research through the SCIP optimization suite 8.0 (ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 2023, DOI 10.1145/3585516; arXiv:2303.07101)
**Read level**: full. All 25 file pages read: body pp. 1-19 (introduction, performance of SCIP 8.0, core solving engine, interfaces, extensions, final remarks, acknowledgements), author affiliations pp. 19-20, references pp. 21-25. The text is arXiv:2303.07101v1 (title page dated March 14, 2023; arXiv stamp 13 Mar 2023; p. 1); the TOMS version was not compared.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: 34 co-authors, Lübbecke 18th of 35 (p. 1); the RWTH Lehrstuhl für Operations Research authors are Donkiewicz, Gaul, Lübbecke, Mühmer and Schlein (pp. 19-20). The decomposition-heuristics part of §3.3 is SCIP's, not GCG's.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Not a research question but a positioning: the paper is about "the role of the SCIP Optimization Suite in supporting research" (abstract, p. 1); the 8.0 developments serve as examples of SCIP as a research tool and as a platform (p. 1). The evidence offered for the research-tool claim is a list of independent works that used SCIP: symmetry handling, branching rules, ML inside branch-and-bound, application algorithms and MINLP uses (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: The design idea is a central branch-cut-and-price core with plugins (constraint handlers, heuristics, branching rules, separators), so that users can "test their techniques within a general-purpose branch-cut-and-price framework" (p. 3). The 8.0 items: a new MINLP framework with expression handlers for basic operations only and implicit reformulation by annotating expressions instead of adding auxiliary constraints, so that original constraints are kept and branching on auxiliary variables is avoided (pp. 6-7); nonlinear handlers and new separators (RLT, principal 2×2 minors, rank-1 intersection cuts, the last off by default) (p. 8); symmetry handling for MINLPs, Schreier-Sims-table cuts for general variables, suborbitopes and a faster symresack separation (pp. 10-11); the decomposition heuristics PADM (with re-optimization of the original objective) and DPS (pp. 11-13); dual postsolve in PaPILO and PaPILO as a presolving option in SoPlex (p. 13); GCG 3.5 with a refactored detection loop, strong-branching-based candidate selection, the Python interface PyGCGOpt and a visualization suite (pp. 14-15); SCIP-SDP 4.0, SCIP-Jack 2.0 and UG (pp. 16-18).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - MINLP form (1) with g given in algebraic form, inside the class of constraint integer programs (p. 2). A decomposition is a partition into k blocks plus linking rows and columns (pp. 11-12), and "In general, there is no unique way to decompose an MILP" (p. 12). GCG's subproblems are "usually formulated as MILPs" (p. 14).
  - Test sets: 347 MILP instances from MIPLIB 2003, 2010 and 2017 and COR@L that SCIP 7.0 or 8.0 solved with at least one of five seeds, and 113 MINLPLib instances (p. 4); 1,678 MINLPLib instances (version 66559cbc, 2021-03-11) for the nonlinear rewrite, flagged as "not designed to be a benchmark set" (p. 9); 194 MISDP instances of Gally et al. (p. 16); the 200 PACE 2018 Steiner-tree instances of Tracks A and B (p. 17).
  - Correctness and numerics: the old explicit reformulation gave solutions feasible for the reformulated but infeasible for the original problem on 7% of MINLPLib instances (p. 7); symmetry detection is limited to symmetries that keep the formulation invariant (p. 10).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Invariants and devices: keep the original constraints and apply only transformations that "in most situations" do not relax the feasible set under tolerances (p. 7); PaPILO's transaction-based design lets presolvers run in parallel, with reductions checked for validity and applied in a deterministic sequential order (p. 13); SoPlex offers 80-bit extended precision, iterative refinement and exact rational LP solutions (p. 13); symresack cover separation goes from quadratic to linear time because the auxiliary graph's components are paths or cycles (p. 11); DPS splits the linking-row right-hand sides into block shares and updates the partition until the blocks are feasible (eqs (2)-(3), pp. 12-13). GCG (pp. 14-15): Dantzig-Wolfe and Lagrangian decomposition, Benders supported; automatic structure detection; column generation or Benders cut generation at every node; heuristics and cuts that use both the original and the reformulated model; detectors as plugins; a classification-based detection concept called "empirically very successful" with no data in this paper; branching on original variables [71], Vanderbeck's generic branching and Ryan-Foster, plus the new strong-branching-based selection heuristics [24].
- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - Suite benchmark (§2): SCIP 7.0 (SoPlex 5.0, PaPILO 1.0) against SCIP 8.0 (SoPlex 6.0, PaPILO 2.0) "without parameter tuning or algorithmic variations"; GCC 7.5, Ipopt 3.12.13 with MUMPS 4.10.0, CppAD, bliss 0.73; 7,200 s; Xeon E5-2690 v4 2.60 GHz, 128 GB, one job per machine, single thread; five seeds, giving 1,735 MILP and 565 MINLP runs; numerically inconsistent results excluded; solved counts and shifted geometric means of nodes (shift 100) and time (shift 1 s) (p. 4).
  - MILP (Table 1, p. 5): on 1,708 runs SCIP 8.0 solves 1,478 against 1,445, time 231.3 against 271.3 s (ratio 1.17); on both-solved only 1.12; 84 against 51 on diff-timeouts. MINLP (Table 2, p. 5): 454 against 435 of 558, time ratio 1.17, node ratio 0.76; SCIP 8.0 needs more nodes on every subset except both-solved (1.32), and the text mentions nodes only for both-solved (p. 5). The excluded runs are not counted; by arithmetic 27 MILP and 7 MINLP runs (1,735 - 1,708; 565 - 558) [my arithmetic] (pp. 4-5).
  - Nonlinear rewrite (§3.1.5, pp. 8-10): classic against new on 1,678 instances in three orderings each (5,034 jobs); CPLEX 20.1.0.1 as LP solver, Ipopt 3.14.4; one hour, 100,000 MB, gap tolerances 1e-6 absolute and 1e-4 relative; Xeon E5-2660 v3 (p. 9). Failure is defined (bound conflicting with best known bounds, fatal error, no termination at the limit) and infeasible final incumbents are counted separately: 481 to 49 infeasible, 143 to 70 failed, 2,929 to 3,131 solved, with about 400 gained and about 200 no longer solved (Table 3, p. 9). "Fastest" means not more than 25% slower (p. 9); performance profiles for time and gap at termination (Fig. 1, p. 10).
  - Extensions: SCIP-SDP 3.2 against 4.0, 185 to 187 optimal, nodes 617.3 to 497.3, time 42.9 to 26.6 s, Mosek 9.2.40, with tolerances the text "recall[s]" were relaxed (p. 16). SCIP-Jack against Gurobi 9.5 and SPDP run on the same hardware, arithmetic means with time-outs counted as one hour; 67, 176, 198 and 199 solved (Fig. 2, p. 17). GCG: no computational result (pp. 14-15).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: SCIP is positioned by its users: independent works [7, 9, 11-13, 15-17, 32, 48] (p. 3); the design is credited to Achterberg [1] (p. 3); details are deferred to the release report [8] (pp. 3-4). SCIP-Jack is set against the Polzin-Vahdati solver, "unchallenged for almost 20 years" (p. 17), and GeoSteiner 5.1 (p. 18). GCG is framed as complementary to tailored decomposition algorithms implemented in SCIP (p. 14).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The abstract states the focus (p. 1); performance comes first (§2), then components (p. 3). The claim is sized in §2, where the improvement on both-solved instances is "only of 12 %" and the overall speed-up is "more due to newly solved instances" (pp. 4-5), and in §3.1.5, which reports instances lost and says the new version "can be slower" (pp. 9-10). A "words of caution" paragraph and a footnote on wrong SDP results precede the SDP table (p. 16). Promotional phrases occur elsewhere: "award-winning" (p. 2), "significantly improved" (p. 16), "one commonly observes a run time difference of more than six orders of magnitude" (p. 17). The Final Remarks restate the sections and give no future work (pp. 18-19).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: software and its use in research; no new theory. Limits stated: MINLPLib is not a benchmark set (p. 9); the new nonlinear handling is slower on some instances and loses about 200 (pp. 9-10); 49 final incumbents remain infeasible with small violations (p. 9); suborbitopes "did not turn out to always be beneficial" (p. 11); PADM's penalty objective "can lead to arbitrarily bad solutions" (p. 12); SDP results depend on tolerances (p. 16). No future-work list beyond the platform framing (pp. 1, 18-19).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence ([stated-joint], the GCG section): GCG "can be used to quickly assess the potential" of a decomposition-based algorithm for any problem with a compact MILP formulation (step 4, p. 14); tailored decomposition algorithms are built in SCIP, with GCG complementary (step 5, p. 14); heuristics and cuts use both the original and the reformulated model, and branching is on original variables among others (step 6, pp. 14-15). The MINLP rewrite keeps the original constraints and avoids branching on auxiliary variables (p. 7), the same principle outside decomposition.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (Tension 2): the GCG philosophy is routine use without the user's interaction or knowledge (p. 14), while the same paper's decomposition-heuristics section says "Users might be aware of decompositions and know which are most useful for a specific problem" (p. 12) and PyGCGOpt accepts custom decompositions (p. 15). The GCG section states no stop rule and reports no GCG run (pp. 14-15).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence ([stated-joint], weak): no unique decomposition of an MILP, and different decompositions give different solver behaviour (p. 12); detection usually finds many candidate decompositions, and a decomposition report lists them (pp. 14-15). No enumeration study in this paper.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the co-signed suite protocol has what the skill finds missing in his own studies: five seeds (p. 4), three orderings per instance, failure definitions and losses reported (p. 9), a test library labelled as not a benchmark set (p. 9), and the speed-up traced to newly solved instances (pp. 4-5). His group's own component study in this batch has no seeds (S074, pp. 23-32), and the GCG section reports no runs (pp. 14-15). Unsized claims sit in other groups' sections [inferred]: SCIP-SDP (p. 16) and SCIP-Jack (p. 17); exclusions are not counted (pp. 4-5).
- H7 ✅ (suite level, not his group specifically): PaPILO is only an option in SoPlex 6.0 and the old presolving stays default (p. 13); rank-1 intersection cuts are off by default (p. 8); suborbitopes are added by a strategy because they were not always beneficial (p. 11); GCG 3.5's strong-branching heuristics are "added" with no performance data or default status (p. 15).
- H9 ✅: a GCG 3.5 component, strong-branching-based selection, comes from an RWTH master's thesis (Gaul [24]) (pp. 15, 22); detectors are plugins so new ones can be added (pp. 14-15). G2 ✅: a software paper presented as a research contribution, with outside uses as its evidence (p. 3). B3 ⚠: the co-signed Final Remarks summarise (pp. 18-19), the pattern he later criticises in conclusions.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Version-against-version protocol: identical machines, one job per machine, single thread, five seeds per instance, shifted geometric means (time shift 1 s, node shift 100), solved counts, and the subsets "affected", difficulty buckets [t, tilim], both-solved and diff-timeouts (pp. 4-5).
- Say where a speed-up comes from: set the all-instances ratio beside the both-solved ratio (1.17 against 1.12 on MILP) and attribute the rest to newly solved instances (pp. 4-5).
- For a redesign, run each instance in its original and two permuted orderings, define failure explicitly (bound conflicting with best known bounds, fatal error, no termination at the limit), count infeasible final incumbents separately, and report instances lost as well as gained (p. 9).
- Count a version as "fastest" on an instance only if it is not more than 25% slower than the other, and add performance profiles for time and for gap at termination (pp. 9-10).
- Compare solvers on instances presolved by a standalone presolver (PaPILO), so that the comparison does not depend on each solver's own presolve (p. 13).
- Ship a new component as an option and keep the old default until the evidence is in (PaPILO in SoPlex 6.0, p. 13).
