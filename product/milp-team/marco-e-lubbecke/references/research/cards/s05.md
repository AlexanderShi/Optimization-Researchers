### S073 · A Branch-Price-and-Cut Algorithm for Packing Cuts in Undirected Graphs (ACM Journal of Experimental Algorithmics 21 2016, DOI 10.1145/2851492)
**Read level**: full. All 16 pages: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-2), formulations and complexity (pp. 2-3), algorithmic ingredients (pp. 3-10), computational setup and results (pp. 10-13), summary and conclusions (pp. 13-14), acknowledgements and references (pp. 15-16); Figures 1-6 only from captions and text. First-page check: title, and Marco E. Lübbecke (extracted as "MARCO E. LUBBECKE", the umlaut displaced) among the three authors (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Martin Bergner and Jonas T. Witt, RWTH Aachen OR, in an alphabetical author list; DFG Priority Program "Algorithm Engineering", grants LU770/4-1 and LU770/4-2 (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the generic branch-and-price period (2008-2015 in SKILL.md). A preliminary version appeared at SEA 2014 (p. 1; ref. p. 16), indexed as S065 (not read here). This is a tailored code on SCIP 3.0.1, measured against GCG 2.0.1 (pp. 10-11).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A problem with no computational record. Packing the largest number of pairwise edge-disjoint cuts is NP-hard, W[1]-hard in its parameterized form (Corollary 2.1, p. 3), with uses in bioinformatics and network reliability, and "So far it could not be solved exactly" (abstract, p. 1). No experimental results exist, exact or approximate (p. 2). A second aim: to see "how complexity results match computational experience" (abstract, p. 1; p. 2). The reliability view that NP-hardness "limits their applicability" is answered with an exact IP approach (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: The compact model copies a min-cut formulation n−1 times and fixes vertex 1 on one shore, "mildly reducing symmetry" (p. 2). The extended set-packing model over all cuts (known from Caprara et al. 2004) is derived as its Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation: the packing rows stay in the master, and the identical subproblems are aggregated, which removes the symmetry (pp. 2-3). Pricing is a min cut with nonnegative weights at the root (Stoer-Wagner, in its hypergraph version once clique duals enter). After branching, duals may be negative, and pricing becomes a binary program (pp. 4-5, 9). A priced cut with a disconnected shore is split into its components (p. 6). A greedy heuristic prices several disjoint cuts, max-cut heuristics price in the tree, and a column pool is searched first (p. 6). Branching is on aggregated original edge variables first, then Ryan-Foster on edge pairs (p. 6). Clique inequalities are separated at the root, since on a clique the LP bound is n/2 against an optimum of 1 (p. 8). Odd-cycle inequalities are derived but not separated (pp. 9-10). An edge clique cover gives a combinatorial upper bound, and its cliques enter the master before column generation (p. 10). The success "mainly builds on" that bound (p. 2).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Connected undirected graphs, cuts with nontrivial shores (p. 1); bipartite and chordal graphs are the polynomial special cases (p. 1). About 100 test instances (p. 11): random graphs from Rudy, Randgraph and NetworkX 1.7 with seed 1, MUSKETEER copies of real graphs, real graphs of the 10th DIMACS challenge with at most 500 vertices, and Trick's coloring graphs with fewer than 5,000 edges (pp. 10-12); BHOSLIB was tried and dropped (p. 12).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: Pricing under diff-branching is NP-complete, by reduction from independent set (Theorem 3.1, p. 7). A cut forced by a same-branch that cannot be split is added anyway: it may slow the search but does no harm (pp. 7-8). Odd-cycle duals change only the pricing objective, so the combinatorial pricer stays usable (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: SCIP 3.0.1 with CPLEX 12.4.0.1, Intel Core i7-2600 with 16 GB, 3,600 s (p. 10). On 12 Randgraph instances the compact model solved 3 in SCIP and 5 in GCG, the basic tailored code 8 and the full code 12 (pp. 11-12). Table I gives solved counts and shifted geometric means with no shift stated (p. 12). Five of nine DIMACS graphs were solved, against one of nine matched random graphs (pp. 12-13). Ablation profiles (p. 13, Fig. 5 p. 14). Failures are printed: random graphs above 40 vertices and every BHOSLIB root node (p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The first exact algorithm and computational study (pp. 2, 13). Stated size limits: 80 vertices on random graphs, 500 on partitioning graphs, 5,000 edges on coloring graphs (p. 14). The 80 does not match "we fail to solve instances larger than 40 vertices" (p. 12), on the text alone. γ(G) is mostly equal to α(G), far below the bound 2α(G)−1 (pp. 13-14). The verdict on tailored against generic code is in the quotes (p. 14). Future: better edge clique covers for stronger dual bounds, and other IP or semidefinite min-cut formulations for pricing (p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 evidence ✅ (steps 1, 2, 5, 6): compact model first, extended model derived as its Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation (pp. 2-3). The compact model is run in SCIP 3.0.1 and in GCG 2.0.1 on the same machine and limit as the tailored code (pp. 10-12). The code is built on a framework that manages the tree (p. 10), and aggregated original variables are branched on first (p. 6).
- Method 1 variant ⚠ (dating of the increment): the skill dates GCG as "a first try and an estimate" to 2017 and the tailored SCIP studies to 2024-26. Here, in 2016 (preliminary version 2014, p. 1), GCG is a competitor, not a probe. The paper concludes that a problem-specific code still pays and expects generic solvers to catch up (p. 14). No probe step precedes the build (pp. 10-14).
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (steps 1, 3, 5): random sets stand beside literature graphs, and each real graph is paired with a random graph of the same size and a MUSKETEER-edited copy (pp. 10-12). The gap to GCG is explained: same pricing problem, other branching rules (p. 11). Ablation runs in three directions (pp. 13-14), and failures are printed (p. 12).
- Method 3 variant ⚠ (steps 5-6): shifted geometric means without the shift (p. 12); one generator seed (p. 11), as in the skill's Limitations; the conclusion's 80-vertex limit against the 40 of §4.2 (pp. 12, 14); "almost every instance" (p. 11) before the counts (pp. 11-12).
- H1 evidence ✅: several disjoint columns per round, heuristic pricing in the tree, a column pool before any pricer (p. 6). H1 variant ⚠: the exact combinatorial pricer mattered less than expected (p. 13), while the combinatorial dual bound carried the success (pp. 2, 13).
- Tension 6 evidence: the problem-specific clique inequalities and the clique cover give the largest ablation effects (p. 13), while a generic family (odd-cycle) is skipped on a literature argument (pp. 9-10).
- New-pattern candidate "theory-to-computation check": the study tests whether complexity and approximation results show in computation. The classes solvable in polynomial time also run faster in the exponential algorithm (p. 12), and the factor-2 gap between α(G) and γ(G) does not appear (p. 13). It is an aim stated in the abstract (p. 1; p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Derive the extended formulation as the Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation of a replicated compact model and aggregate the identical subproblems, which removes the replication symmetry; branch first on the aggregated original variables (here the sum of the edge variables over all copies) (pp. 2-3, 6).
- When branching breaks the combinatorial pricer (negative duals), switch to a binary-program pricer only in the nodes that need it, and prove the branched pricing problem hard to justify the switch (pp. 4-7).
- Split any priced column whose shore is disconnected into its components and add those; the original column cannot be in an optimal LP solution (p. 6).
- Compute a combinatorial upper bound (an edge clique cover) before column generation and put its clique inequalities into the master from the start (p. 10).
- Ablate in three directions: disable each component in the full code, enable each in the basic code, and disable several together (pp. 13-14).
- For each real instance, add a random graph of the same size and a structure-preserving edited copy (MUSKETEER), to tell structure effects from size effects (p. 12).

**Quotes**:
- "In our case, the combinatorial pricing is a nice add-on but nonetheless has not that much influence on the solution time as expected." (p. 13)
- "We conclude that at the current state of the art, it still considerably pays to consider a problem-specific implementation, even though generic solvers may become competitive soon." (p. 14)

### S047 · Optimization of the Reconfiguration Planning of Handling Systems Based on Parallel Manipulators with Delta-Like Architecture (IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters 2(3) 2017, DOI 10.1109/LRA.2017.2705286)

### S061 · A strongly polynomial Contraction-Expansion algorithm for network flow problems (Computers & Operations Research 84 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.cor.2017.02.019)
**Read level**: full. All 17 pages: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-2), network problem and contracted network (pp. 2-4), properties (pp. 4-10), behavioural study (pp. 10-12), the Contraction-Expansion algorithm with complexity analysis and experiments (pp. 12-17), conclusion and references (p. 17); proofs read as stated; Figures 1-20 only from captions and text. First-page check: title, and Marco E. Lübbecke (RWTH Aachen) as third author (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jean Bertrand Gauthier (first and corresponding author) and Jacques Desrosiers, GERAD and HEC Montréal (p. 1); Lübbecke third of three · **Place in trajectory**: the LP-degeneracy side line (SKILL.md Research Trajectory, 2011-2022); received 2016-03-23, accepted 2017-02-27 (p. 1). It builds on the authors' 2015 MMCC paper (ref. p. 17; S038, not read here) and is the network basis of S044 (S044 p. 2). S044 p. 14 names Gauthier's prize-winning 2016 dissertation on primal algorithms for degenerate linear and network flow problems; that S061 is part of it is [inferred].

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A gap between theory and practice. The primal network simplex works well although few of its pivots are non-degenerate, while MMCC is strongly polynomial but "no match" for other methods in practice (p. 1). The aim is "a better understanding of algorithms with strictly positive step size at every iteration", not beating leading codes; column-generation oracles are named as the setting where a more involved pricing is worth it (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Contract each tree of a linearly independent arc set P (by default the free arcs) to its root, and keep the restricted arcs visible (pp. 3-4). Duals fixed along tree paths give the hidden arcs zero reduced cost, so the visible arcs carry the cost (Procedure 1, Proposition 4, pp. 6-7). A minimum mean cycle on the smaller network extends uniquely to the residual network and is cancelled (Propositions 3 and 5, pp. 6-7; Fig. 9, p. 8). P = ∅ gives MMCC, and P = the basic arcs gives the primal network simplex with Dantzig's rule (§3.6, p. 9). Contraction-Expansion shrinks P inside a phase to the arcs that are free both before and after each cancellation. When a Type 3 cycle gives a measurable jump, the phase ends and the network is contracted fully again (§§5.1-5.2, pp. 12-13).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Capacitated minimum cost flow with arc bounds, balanced supplies and no multiarcs (p. 2). P must be linearly independent, which is easy to keep even from a nonbasic solution (Proposition 2, p. 5). The complexity proof assumes a basic initial phase solution and caps the cancellations before the full residual network at n (p. 14).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: Cost equals reduced cost on any cycle, by telescoping (Proposition 1, p. 2). P inside the free arcs gives necessary and sufficient optimality conditions, otherwise only sufficient ones (Proposition 6, p. 8). The contracted mean bounds the true minimum mean from below (Proposition 10, p. 10). Phases end on a jump of a lower bound, not of the oscillating optimality parameter, and arc fixing is restated on that bound (Proposition 11, Definitions 3-4, Proposition 12, pp. 12-13). A phase costs O(m²n) and the algorithm O(m³n²) (Proposition 13, Theorem 1, pp. 13-14). Whether the expansion is needed for strong polynomiality is left as a conjecture (p. 12).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: C++ on an Intel i7-4770K with 16 GB under Windows 10; DIMACS families, with version A of GRIDGRAPH, NETGEN and GRIDGEN profiled (pp. 15-17). The baselines are MMCC and Cancel-and-Tighten (CT). CE beats MMCC more as size grows (1,907 s against 15,629 s on gridgen_sr_13a), but CT beats both (36 s) (Table 1, p. 16). Losses are printed: no contraction on road_paths_05_WI_a (CE 10,355 s, MMCC 9,553 s) and β mostly above 1 on NETGEN (pp. 15-16). Instances up to 2^22 nodes are left out because MMCC was too slow (p. 17); no time limit is stated.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A generic contraction algorithm with MMCC and the network simplex as extreme cases, and a CE variant with MMCC's O(m³n²) bound, so "no improvement of the runtime complexity" (abstract, p. 1; p. 17). Phases become markers the algorithm checks, not only devices of the proof (p. 17). Limits stated: not competitive yet, the practical leaders not compared, and the omitted families, the authors "believe", would not change the picture (p. 17). Future: whether the adaptation is necessary (p. 12), and strongly polynomial algorithms combined with practical behaviour (p. 17).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 6, with step 5's losses): the purpose is understanding, not beating leading codes (p. 1), and the paper "does not pretend to propose a competitive algorithm just yet" (p. 17). The losses to CT, on ROAD and on NETGEN are printed with their mechanism (pp. 15-16), and the exclusion of the largest instances is explained (p. 17).
- Method 3 variant ⚠ (step 3): the baselines are the algorithm being generalized (MMCC) and its sibling (CT). The network simplex and cost scaling, which the paper itself calls the practical leaders (pp. 1, 17), are not run, and only version A of three families is profiled (pp. 15-17).

### S044 · Vector Space Decomposition for Solving Large-Scale Linear Programs (Operations Research 66(5) 2018, DOI 10.1287/opre.2018.1728)
**Read level**: full, except §4.3 (illustrative examples, pp. 10-12), which was skimmed. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-2), §2 on the residual problem, cycles, the oracle and the linear algebra (pp. 2-6), §3 on the framework (pp. 6-9), §§4.1-4.2 (pp. 9-10), §4.4 and §5 (pp. 12-13), author notes (p. 14). The text is the Articles in Advance version, published online 2018-08-02 (p. 1). First-page check: title, and Marco E. Lübbecke (RWTH Aachen) as third author (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jean Bertrand Gauthier (first author) and Jacques Desrosiers, HEC Montréal and GERAD (p. 1); Lübbecke third of three · **Place in trajectory**: the LP-degeneracy line; received 2016-09-08, accepted 2017-12-23 (p. 1). It is built on S061: the framework is "motivated and enabled by network flow analogies" from it (p. 2). The INDEX note lists an earlier Optimization Online version (2015/02) under another title (not read).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Degeneracy in the primal simplex. Dantzig's rule measures the objective rate but ignores which basic variables can move, a "visibility problem", so degenerate solutions breed degenerate pivots (p. 1). Dantzig and Thapa's call for dual-guided pivot rules is reread (p. 1). The practical motive is column generation, which encodes the primal simplex in its pricing and inherits its degeneracy (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: From a basic feasible solution, choose a subspace basis A_P of some basic columns and transform the rows with T_P⁻¹ (p. 5). The duals of the rows tied to P are fixed, and the others are optimized to maximize the minimum normalized reduced cost. In primal form the pricing picks a nonnegative combination of entering variables (a projected cycle), which is lifted uniquely to the hidden variables and followed with a ratio-test step (pp. 7-8; Fig. 5, p. 7). The one parameter P spans known methods: P = B gives the primal simplex with Dantzig's rule, P = F the improved primal simplex (IPS), P ⊇ F dynamic constraint aggregation (DCA), and P = ∅ minimum weighted cycle-canceling (MWCC), which is MMCC on networks (pp. 9-10, Fig. 6).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: The bounded LP min cᵀx, Ax = b, l ≤ x ≤ u with m < n, A of full row rank, feasible with a finite optimum (p. 2); a basic feasible start (p. 6); positive normalization weights, fixed or updated dynamically (pp. 3, 8).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: The cost and reduced cost of a cycle are equal (Lemma 1, p. 4). Optimality conditions hold on the residual problem (Proposition 1, p. 4). Compatibility is membership in the span of A_P (Definition 4, p. 5). The step is positive whenever P lies in the free variables (Proposition 2, p. 9). Interior directions are nonnegative combinations of edge directions, by Minkowski-Weyl (Proposition 3, p. 10). MWCC solves the Klee-Minty polytope in one iteration (p. 12). Transferring the network complexity results is "left for another paper" (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none: illustrative examples only (pp. 10-12); §4.4 cites earlier timings and makes observations without numbers (p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A unifying framework with two properties, positive steps and edge-only directions; IPS is the only variant with both (p. 13). Limits stated: no claim of a competitive generic implementation (p. 12), and the quality of a direction is hard to judge (p. 11). Future: a cancel-and-tighten analogue for LPs (p. 12); stabilization, dual-optimal inequalities and heuristic pricers that use original costs in column generation (p. 13).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 variant ⚠ (step 2 inside a research article): published remedies for degeneracy (IPS, DCA, MWCC and the simplex itself) are put into one notation under one parameter and dated in one figure (Fig. 6, p. 10). It is not a survey, and it is the first author's line (pp. 1-2, 14).
- Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 6): the framework does not imply a competitive generic implementation (p. 12); "By no means do we imply" that one variant gives better directions, and a modified example reverses the effect (p. 11).
- Method 3 variant ⚠ (checkpoint, counts): two experimental claims come without numbers: "we observe the same behavior for the linear programming counterpart MWCC" (p. 12), and pricing with original costs is "already proving elegant in our recent experiments" (p. 13).
- H1 variant ⚠: since cost and reduced cost of a cycle are equal, a heuristic pricer may rank candidates by original costs or by any approximate duals (p. 13); this extends H1's cheap columns before exact pricing.
- New-pattern candidate "one-parameter family with known algorithms as its extremes" (with S061): pp. 1-2, 9-10.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Classify pricing rules by which duals they fix and which they optimize; fixing the duals of the nondegenerate basic variables (P = F) yields positive steps along edges (pp. 7, 9-10, 13).
- Normalize the direction-finding LP with weights: norm-based weights make the choice independent of variable scaling, and an infinite weight is partial pricing (p. 8).
- Return a nonbasic iterate to a basic one by a restricted LP over the free variables, with the others fixed (eq. 26, p. 8).
- In a heuristic pricer for cycle-like columns, rank candidates by original cost when the duals are unreliable, since cost equals reduced cost on cycles (pp. 4, 13).

**Quotes**:
- "In column generation, however, the primal simplex algorithm is inherently encoded in the pricing mechanism, regardless of which algorithm is actually used to reoptimize the linear restricted master programs." (p. 2)
- "Having a generic framework that conceptually hosts, for example, PS, IPS, DCA, and MMCC does not imply that we believe in a generic competitive implementation of the framework." (p. 12)

- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: Necessity: an even induced v-w path closes an odd induced cycle through vw, whose odd-cycle inequality is valid on one side and not on the other (p. 3). Sufficiency, by Meyniel's technique: for a stable set S1 containing v and w and a stable set S2 containing neither, the component K of G[S1 ∪ S2] that contains v avoids w. Swapping S1 ∩ K and S2 ∩ K then gives two stable sets that each contain one of v, w (p. 3). Corollary 1 follows by induction on |F| (p. 3), and Theorem 2 by replacing edge addition with contraction (p. 4).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: n/a.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A polyhedral characterization of even and odd pairs (abstract, p. 1). No limits or future work are stated, and neither Dantzig-Wolfe nor computation is mentioned (pp. 1-4).

**Method links**:
