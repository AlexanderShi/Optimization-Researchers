## D. Recruiting and onboarding: what applicants are told (all [stated], primary unless noted)

### D1. There is no PhD program; a PhD is a job with teaching

The chair FAQ (English, Wayback 2016-07-31; unsigned chair text, so group-level):

- On the program: "We do not have a particular "PhD program." Typically, working towards a PhD goes with a paid position as a research/teaching associate. Such a position may imply teaching duties or administrative tasks assigned to you. That is, being a PhD "student" usually means that you are "studying" next to your regular "working day." A simple consequence is that we need to have such a position available."
- On unsolicited requests: "we delete (and do not answer) any request that does not personally address one of our team members. We ignore any request that does not specifically refer to our team, and why you would like to work with exactly us (and not any other team in the world). We ignore any request that does not precisely specify a research question you would like to work on with us ("I read your paper about..." is not sufficient!)."
- On recommendation letters: "It is easy to truly recommend a person if he/she is known to us, e.g. because they have been teaching/research assistant in our group or because of having completed a thesis with us."
- On internships (sic "internhips"): "If you are seriously interested in an internship with our group, then specify precisely what research project you would like to pursue, and why with us."

## F. How experiments are done in the group: the tacit rules, written down [practice, primary, group-authored]

The source is `doc/resources/devs/howtouse/conduct-experiments.md` and `diverse-testset-generation.md`, scipopt/gcg at commit `c0e230a`. Era: GCG 3.x–4.x, about 2020–2026. This is the closest thing to "what every student knows" that exists in writing.

- **Check the installation before the big test set:** "we recommend checking that your installation and configuration of GCG is correct by performing a test on the default (called `short`) testset." The default report prints shifted geometric means with shifts "[ 100/10.0/5.0/2.0]" (nodes, time, pricing time, LP time).
- **Choose instances where the change can show, then check for harm elsewhere:**
  - "It is crucial that you determine with which instances your modifications of settings or code can be seen"
  - "to show that your changes do not influence other instances negatively, you should also test using a general-purpose testset."
- **Diversity over convenience:** "test sets should cover a variety of characteristics in order to correctly represent the changes done." The current set is "`experiment.test`, version May 2021. Instances with decompositions that in total take around 25 CPU hours to test, are very diverse and cover most of the algorithmic machinery." It was curated with the group's own strIPlib (structured integer programming library) following "Donkiewicz (2021)", a student thesis.
- **An abandoned test set, labelled as such** [practice]: "`goodinstances.test`: a selected set of instances with at most 1 hour of runtime. Not recommended for use anymore."
- **Comparability is the researcher's job:** "In this manual mode, you yourself have to pay attention to the general comparability of your runtime data (same instances, settings, time limits, computer used, ...)." Settings matter too: "by default, the arrowhead structure detectors (and others as well) are disabled ..., which could lead to very different results than anticipated."
- **Don't break the parsers:** "you may not change any existing GCG logging formats to not get differently formatted `.out`-files that will not be parseable anymore by the parsers."
- **The same habits in a dissertation** [practice, primary]. Franzen's 2022 thesis reports solver versions, compiler versions, the cluster project ID and core hours (PDF pp. 189–190). It also reports plainly what failed (section H).
