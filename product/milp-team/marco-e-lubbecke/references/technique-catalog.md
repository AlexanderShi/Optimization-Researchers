## 1. Proof devices (PD)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| PD1 | Kill a formulation on paper | Before computing, exhibit a fractional point the formulation cannot cut off (a uniform solution at the trivial bound, a half-integral odd cycle, the average of two integer solutions of the convexified part) | [S027 pp. 90–91; S052 in-press pp. 5, 12–13, 17; D006 pp. 5–6] |
| PD2 | Compare bounds through Dantzig-Wolfe | To prove a column model stronger than the compact one, decompose the compact LP and map solutions into it; for a column model without a compact formulation, reverse Dantzig-Wolfe to construct one | [S027 pp. 93–94; S021 report pp. 6–9; S007 manuscript pp. 6–7; S084 pp. 53–58] |
| PD3 | Integrality-property screen before building | For each candidate grouping, check whether its pricing problem has the integrality property; if it does, the reformulation cannot raise the LP bound, and any reason to decompose must be another one (memory, speed, symmetry) | [S084 pp. 27, 39; S088 pp. 19, 60–61; S051 p. 7; S078 p. 5] |
| PD4 | Hull facet by facet | To show that a convexified part gives the hull, attach a critical subgraph to each facet; show necessity by an exchange on two solutions and a valid inequality the weaker relaxation misses; reuse complete descriptions through gadget graphs | [S052 in-press pp. 8–9, 11–12; S071 p. 3; D006 pp. 4–5] |
| PD5 | Correctness by a hereditary property | When an enumeration recurses over faces, show that every face it enters stays in the class, so one LP per node decides; count the work per output | [S020 pp. 4–5] |
| PD6 | Dual-bound fathoming in the pricing DP | Drop a label when its cost minus all positive duals it can still collect cannot beat min{0, incumbent}; scale the test by γ for a pricing heuristic with a proven relative error, and cite the precedent (*rediscovering*) | [S027 pp. 121–127; S033 report pp. 2–4] *(sole)* |
| PD7 | Price a restriction twice | When the method works on a structured restriction (restricted pickup and delivery paths), count its search space against the fairest alternative and give a tight worst-case factor for what the restriction loses | [S066 manuscript pp. 4–6; S027 p. 158] *(sole)* |
| PD8 | Rounding from a structural property of an optimal LP vertex | Prove a property of optimal vertices (planar support after uncrossing, a heavy variable by counting) and round on it; round switch binaries by pigeonhole; get a ρ-approximation for choice constraints over a scalable polyhedron | [D001 arXiv pp. 19–23; S030 arXiv p. 9; S112 p. 5; S046 pp. 7–8; S051 pp. 7–8] |
| PD9 | Integrality only where needed | Construct an explicit integral optimum to prove that the extended variables may be continuous; or project a dimension out by a combinatorial feasibility theorem (Hall's condition) and recover it exactly | [S089 pp. 8–11, 34–35; S010 pp. 2–4] |
| PD10 | Representation theorems by a cone reformulation | Drop the null extreme point and count the master basis to turn network-flow decomposition theorems into LP ones | [S039 pp. 6–8; S086 pp. 31–33] |
| PD11 | Exactness by two-way solution-transfer lemmas | To prove an LP-free subproblem solver exact, chain lemmas that carry solutions both ways between formulations, show the system matrix nonsingular (diagonal dominance of a reduced Laplacian) and justify zero duals by relaxing a bound by ε | [S076 pp. 7–13, 15–16, 21] *(unreviewed)* |
| PD12 | A minimal counterexample before a remedy | Before proposing a fix, build the smallest instance that breaks the standard rule (three variables and one row against a pricing rule), audit any published refutation too, and rule out a whole class of constructions by one small cyclic example | [S021 report p. 5; S063 pp. 1–2; S018 manuscript pp. 21–22] |
| PD13 | Disaggregation certificate for branching on aggregated variables | With identical blocks, branch on aggregated original variables and certify that an integral aggregate disaggregates, with Ryan-Foster branching as the fallback | [S050 preprint pp. 4–6, 22–23] |
| PD14 | Exactness of a stabilized master | Accept the λ-part of a boxed or penalized master's optimum as the master's optimum only when the box variables are zero | [S088 pp. 39, 43] |
| PD15 | Inapproximability by blow-up | Replace each element by s copies so that a hardness gap transfers to the multiplied instance | [S034 pp. 5–6] |

## 2. Design moves (DM)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| DM1 | Pricing cascade | Order the column generators from cheap to exact: column pool, a greedy dual-based heuristic, the exact algorithm stopped at the k-th negative column, the full exact run only to prove optimality; heuristic pricers first, exact ones when they fail; return several diverse (complementary) columns per round, not only the most negative ones | [S027 pp. 133–135; S024 p. 10; S060 pp. 15–18, 41–42; S005 p. 35; S050 preprint pp. 8–9] |
| DM2 | Branching enforced in pricing | Impose branching on original variables as bound changes, arc deletions or infinite-capacity arcs inside the pricing problem, so that its algorithm is unchanged (H11) | [S007 manuscript p. 11; D002 pp. 23–24; S089 p. 19; S115 p. 19] |
| DM3 | Cut duals carried into the pricing structure | Put the duals of robust cuts on the arcs or hyperedges the pricing algorithm already handles; relax master equalities to covering form when costs are non-negative | [S065 p. 5; S089 p. 14; S115 pp. 10, 12] |
| DM4 | Aggregate identical blocks | Merge identical pricing problems into one with a multiplicity and remove their symmetry; then check what branching needs (PD13) | [S013 p. 60; S065 p. 3; S073 pp. 2–3, 6; S084 pp. 36, 50] |
| DM5 | Two synchronized formulations | In a generic solver, let the original problem coordinate the search and keep the master as a relaxation inside it, so that branching, cuts and heuristics act on original variables | [S011 pp. 4–5; S013 p. 60; S019 p. 26] |
| DM6 | Structure as an exchangeable file | Let users and detectors exchange a decomposition as a file (blocks, linking rows and columns, nested blocks, symmetry information) | [S013 p. 60; S005 p. 33; S019 pp. 28–31] |
| DM7 | Detection | Build a hypergraph with one node per nonzero and costed row and column hyperedges and partition it for a bordered block-diagonal form; work with whole constraint classes and vote on the number of blocks | [S032 manuscript pp. 3–5; S070 pp. 5–6; S005 pp. 32–33; S084 p. 63] |
| DM8 | Nested decomposition of a structured pricing problem | When the pricing problem is itself a structured MIP, decompose it again (labelling for routes, an inner column generation for quantities) and keep the stronger outer master | [S029 pp. 3–6; S115 pp. 13, 26; S019 pp. 29–31] |
| DM9 | Feasibility of the restricted master | Start with Farkas pricing (zero costs against the dual ray of an infeasible master), with a heuristic that is exact for feasibility used as Farkas pricing, or with one trivially feasible column per demand | [S008 manuscript pp. 6–7; S060 pp. 10–11; S115 p. 15; S023 p. 10] |
| DM10 | Dual-side accelerators (H12) | Penalized dual boxes updated at each new best dual, the box narrowed to a share of the gap, smoothing with a mis-price check, dual-optimal inequalities, duals seeded by estimates; choose by the master's share of time. Smoothing (Wentges): price at π̂_t = απ̂ + (1−α)π_t with α ∈ [0, 1) and π̂ the stability center; add a column only if it prices out at the RMP duals; after a misprice the dual bound still rises to at least L(π̂) + α(v(RMP) − L(π̂)) | [S001 report pp. 15, 21–22; S032 manuscript p. 5; S088 pp. 36–37, 51–52, 55–58; S008 manuscript pp. 11–12] |
| DM11 | Column activation of the compact LP | Solve the compact LP by activating columns, which is tractable under two stated conditions | [S086 pp. 22–26, 53–57] |
| DM12 | Benders subproblems without an LP | When a subproblem's solution is determined (a unique flow), solve linear systems instead of LPs and reuse factorizations across scenarios by rank-1 updates; keep the base case in the master; solve a scored subset of subproblems per round with periodic full rounds | [S076 pp. 14–27; S075 pp. 15–19, 23] *(unreviewed)* |
| DM13 | Tiered strong branching for branch-and-price | Score candidates by re-optimizing the restricted master only, then run column and cut generation with heuristic pricing on a shortlist; stop when the cheap rule keeps agreeing | [S006 pp. 72–74; S077 p. 58] |
| DM14 | Every component a switch | Give every component a parameter and print the defaults; implement a family of rules as one parameterized code and ship the rules as settings files | [S027 pp. 130, 142; S006 pp. 73, 113–114] |
| DM15 | A heuristic from the exact model | Relax the coupling resource and scale lengths; decouple the cells of a slow dynamic program; let one model's relaxation fix another model's variables, then release the fixing | [D002 pp. 15, 19; S035 pp. 12–13; S012 report pp. 9–10] |
| DM16 | Keep the dual bound correct inside a framework | Switch off presolve, cut generation and propagation during pricing wherever they would invalidate the dual bound: "During branch-and-price we have to disable SCIP's preprocessing, cut generation, and propagation functionalities to ensure a correct dual bound." | [S029 p. 10] |
| DM17 | Tolerances at the master–pricing interface | Name each tolerance (negative reduced cost, bound match, perturbation size, typically 10^-3 to 10^-6), keep one tolerance for master and pricing, never test reduced costs against 0.0, round new columns at the pricing solver's integrality tolerance and recompute their reduced cost; exact rational arithmetic only for mission-critical programs | [S008 manuscript p. 13; S060 pp. 23–24] *(S008 sole)* |
| DM18 | A risky component behind a calibrated gate | Gate a component that can fail badly by a conservative rule that keeps false positives low, with the default solver as the exit | [S009 pp. 2–3, 6] |
| DM19 | Robustness as an added block | Keep the original integer model unchanged and link a recovery LP through slacks; evaluate worst cases at the vertices of the scenario set | [S004 pp. 19–21, 24–25] |
| DM20 | Stop column generation by the Lagrangian bound | Use v(RMP) + κ·v(PP) ≤ v(MP) when an optimal master solution has Σλ ≤ κ (κ = 1 with a convexity row), and only in iterations with exact or relaxed pricing (v(PP) is not available from a heuristic); with integer costs stop when the rounded-up bounds meet; when column generation tails off, branch early and give the children the parent's bound | [S008 manuscript pp. 3, 6] *(sole)*; [S060 p. 20] |
| DM21 | Harvest the pricing problem | Turn what pricing computes into valid inequalities for the original LP: a reduced-cost cut from each pricing optimum (valid for conv(X), hence for the Dantzig-Wolfe relaxation), cuts separated inside a branch-and-cut pricer, the original objective cut | [S068 pp. 7–8] |

## 3. Experiment and evaluation protocols (EP)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| EP1 | Test where it should fail | Include instances on which the method should fail, and state the success criterion, failure included: no win expected on the average instance, failure unless it wins where it should | [S027 pp. 137, 140; S015 preprint pp. 1, 8–9; S070 pp. 5, 7] |
| EP2 | Reruns on own hardware | Re-run every competitor on your machine with one limit and one thread count; ask authors for unpublished code; explain gaps to their published counts; define *newly closed* against the union of all methods and list every instance others solve and you do not; attribute a gain by rerunning with older and open-source solvers | [S089 pp. 22–23, 27–29, 42; S010 pp. 10–11; S068 pp. 2, 5, 8] |
| EP3 | Isolate the effect | Give the baseline your own problem-specific components; add a control arm with the competitor's choice for one component; swap only the component under study; keep the variant without the acceleration in every table | [S115 pp. 17, 22; S089 p. 24; S076 pp. 28, 30] |
| EP4 | Ablation in three directions | Switch features off one at a time, both off and both on, plus a best-of row; rerun a whole table without one component under a stated cap | [S073 pp. 13–14; S016 p. 4; S024 p. 7; S027 p. 141] |
| EP5 | Instances from one real case | When real data are scarce, inconsistent or confidential: a size ladder by truncating one real case, perturbed replicates per class, stress variants that each break one property, random and edited twins of the same size; publish the generator's parameters and the reason the data are synthetic | [S027 p. 137; S036 preprint p. 9; S028 pp. 10–12; S025 preprint pp. 15–16; S081 pp. 1–2; S065 pp. 7–8; S073 p. 12; S075 pp. 22, 28; S091 p. 2] |
| EP6 | Means and noise | State each shift and its reason; average over commonly solved instances with their counts; set a noise threshold on shared machines and an absolute threshold below which an improvement is not counted | [S016 p. 4; S025 preprint pp. 17–18; S026 pp. 6, 8–9; S075 p. 23] |
| EP7 | A tuning set apart from the benchmark | Tune on a separate set (31 against 130 instances in the 2026 study) and report only on the benchmark | [S075 p. 22] |
| EP8 | Ablation chain | One row per added component with its predecessor named, the compact model in row 1, failures charged, a performance profile | [S075 pp. 22–23] |
| EP9 | Time-split accounting | Report master, pricing and remaining time before and after each component, and aim the next component at the dominant share | [S075 p. 23; S076 pp. 28–29; S088 pp. 41, 52; S027 p. 145] |
| EP10 | Exhaustive enumeration of a reformulation choice | Take textbook formulations with weak coupling and at most 18 constraints, switch cuts and presolve off, normalize by gap closed, and validate the cheap proxy against the enumeration before using it at scale | [S058 pp. 3, 5, 7–8, 10] |
| EP11 | Mechanism test | Vary the decomposition granularity on the same instances: if the mechanism holds, fewer subproblems mean fewer cuts and less gain | [S074 manuscript pp. 24–26, 29] |
| EP12 | Expectation, then observation | Write the expected result before each table and flag every mismatch | [S015 preprint pp. 13–21; S050 preprint p. 14] |
| EP13 | Variability protocols | Five seeds or permuted copies, an instance solved only if solved in all, the seed spread reported | [S013 pp. 14, 70–71; S014 arXiv pp. 4–5, 9; S005 pp. 13–15] *(co-signed)* |
| EP14 | Practice as the baseline, done fairly | Emulate the practitioners' procedure in the model and report savings as an interval; re-realize human plans under the model's rules before comparing | [S036 preprint pp. 9–10; S018 manuscript p. 29] |
| EP15 | Validate a new measure on known pairs first | Check a similarity measure on pairs known to be related before using it; keep a second library out of training; count formulation impurities inside one named class | [S043 preprint pp. 5, 25–26, 28–34] |
| EP16 | Release code and data | Release code and data so that readers can check claims the printed numbers do not cover | [S012 report p. 9; S089 p. 23] |
| EP17 | Budget parity against randomized competitors | Give a deterministic method the competitors' total budget across their seeds, in a named unit | [S010 pp. 10–11] |

## 4. Writing moves (WM)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| WM1 | Proof-of-concept framing up front | Say in the introduction that the study is a proof of concept, not a competitive tool: "It is the purpose of this study to evaluate the potential of such a hybrid and to give a proof-of-concept." | [S016 p. 2; S032 manuscript p. 2; S015 preprint p. 27; S028 p. 2; S029 p. 6] |
| WM2 | Limits where they arise | State what the result does not imply in the sentence where a reader could over-read it: "We should also mention that the goal of this research is not to beat leading algorithms on network problems." | [S052 in-press p. 3; S061 p. 1; S044 p. 12] |
| WM3 | Open questions as the last section | End with numbered open questions; list deferred subjects for later authors; invite errata as an exercise | [S020 p. 8; S053 p. 11; S112 p. 8; D001 arXiv p. 23; D004 pp. 8–9] |
| WM4 | Untested points and one running example | Close each survey topic with what is untested; carry one tiny example through every component and have its numbers checked by a third person | [S001 report pp. 10, 18–19; S002 pre-publication pp. 1–21, 27; S086 pp. 35–46; S088 pp. 9, 24, 33] |
| WM5 | Credit in the sentence | Write *rediscovering* with the precedent cited; list all authors with all first names; turn a student's observation into a named proposition; thank objectors by name | [S033 report p. 2; S086 p. 57; S084 p. 112; S027 pp. 125, 151] |
| WM6 | Openings | Open with an imagined solver log as the vision, with the reader types and their questions, or with the reader's sceptical question answered in numbered parts | [S011 pp. 1–2; S070 p. 4; S091 p. 1] |
| WM7 | Bias in the recommendation sentence | State your own bias in the sentence that makes the recommendation | [S048 p. 2] *(sole)* |
| WM8 | One notation with bracketed duals | Write master, pricing and later reformulations in one notation, each constraint's dual in brackets beside it | [S087 pp. 8, 11; S060 p. 5] |
| WM9 | Name where a release got worse | Name the subsets on which a new release degraded performance in the abstract itself: "but also some subsets where the new framework degraded performance" | [S090 p. 2] *(co-signed)* |
| WM10 | Evaluation questions before the results | Number the questions the experiments answer before the first table, and answer them in that order | [S115 pp. 3, 24, 30] *(unreviewed)* |
| WM11 | Recompute before submission | Recompute every number in the abstract and the conclusion from the final tables, check captions against their rows, and keep the general no-harm set in every version of a study | a general lesson from several cards' reading notes; no work is named (08 §7.4) |
