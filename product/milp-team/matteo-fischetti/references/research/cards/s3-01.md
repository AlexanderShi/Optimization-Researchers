**Read level**: full (all 16 slides as text; formulas and the results of slides 13–15 are images missing from the text layer, so those slides give titles only). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Michele Monaci · **Position in trajectory**: the conference talk of the bilinear branch-and-cut published as S079 (EJOR 2020, card c10), given between the preprint of February 22, 2019 and its revision of September 20, 2019 (S079 p. 1); the last slide points to his papers page for the paper (p. 16).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The goal is a solver for non-convex MIQP. Of two routes, enumeration on top of a continuous QP solver or a MILP branch-and-cut customized with McCormick inequalities and spatial branching, the talk takes the second, with the pros and cons left as ellipses (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Rewrite the MIQP as a MILP with bilinear equations (p. 3); McCormick inequalities, with gradient cuts when x = y (p. 4); spatial branching when a node solution satisfies every McCormick inequality but violates some z = xy (#bilinear_infeasibility, p. 5); a shifted spatial branching that moves the threshold by δ to balance the violations of the two children and picks the equation with the largest δ (p. 6); and intersection cuts (Balas 1971) whose convex set comes from any branching disjunction the point violates, here the 1- and 2-level, possibly shifted, spatial-branching conditions (pp. 7–8).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: A fully general MIQP restated with bilinear equations (p. 3; formulas not in the text); McCormick inequalities are tight only at the variable bounds (p. 5); an intersection cut needs the point to be an LP vertex, whose basis gives the cone (p. 7). Test bed: all quadratic MINLPlib instances (700+), minus those with an unbounded root LP, 620 left (p. 12).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: CPLEX 12.8 callbacks: lazy constraints (McCormick inequalities for integer solutions), user cuts for fractional solutions (optional and sometimes detrimental), a branch callback for the new rule, and an incumbent callback as the last resort when tolerances defeat everything else; the solver's MILP heuristics stay at their default level, and MIQP-specific heuristics are not implemented (p. 11). Numerically safe cuts: one valid inequality from the worst left-hand-side coefficient and right-hand side of each disjunction, taken in a reduced form where the basic variables have zero coefficients (p. 10).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: SCIP 5.0.1 (CPLEX 12.8 as LP solver, IPOPT 3.12.9) against basic (no intersection cuts) and with-IC (cuts at nodes whose LP solution is integral); every arm single-thread because SCIP allows no parallel runs; 1 hour on an Intel 3.10 GHz PC with 16 GB (p. 12); the three results slides are images (pp. 13–15). The slide's "408 of which solved by all methods" (p. 12) does not match the paper, where 408 is the count solved by at least one method and 248 by all (S079 p. 14).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: No conclusions slide (p. 16). Limits named: always cutting instead of branching is not advisable because of numerics, slow convergence, tailing off and cut saturation, as with Gomory cuts (p. 8); reading the cone rays from the tableau makes numerical accuracy a big issue (p. 9); there are no MIQP-specific heuristics yet (p. 11).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (steps 1 and 3, joint): the solver is built on MILP branch-and-cut, not around a QP code (p. 2); the MILP solver's heuristics run at their defaults, and the new code is four callbacks (p. 11).
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (step 5, weak): fractional (user-cut) separation is called optional and sometimes detrimental (p. 11), the ingredient the paper leaves off by default after a root-only test (S079 pp. 10, 13).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (step 1): every arm runs single-thread because the rival cannot run in parallel (p. 12).
- new-pattern candidate (existing): own toolbox as building blocks (the intersection-cut slides reuse the 2017 bilevel deck: slide 7 repeats S194's slide 12 nearly word for word, slide 9 repeats S194's slide 19 with "bilìnear-free set" for "bilevel-free set", and slide 4 still says "generic bilevel eq."; pp. 4, 7, 9; S194 pp. 12, 19).

- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: The thought experiment switches the solver's internal heuristics off and solves node LPs by simplex (p. 10). - **D5 Computational evaluation**: none in the deck; the conclusions say only that many instances from the literature are solved in a satisfactory way (p. 21).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Conclusions: a MIBLP is a MILP plus additional constraints, intersection cuts carry valuable information at the nodes, heuristics and preprocessing exist but are not discussed, and many literature instances are solved satisfactorily (p. 21). No limits or future work.

**Method links**:

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before writing a solver for a harder class, list the node cases a MILP branch-and-cut already handles (fractional: branch; integer and feasible: incumbent) and write code only for the remaining one, here integer but bilevel infeasible, in a lazy-constraint callback (pp. 10–11, 18).
- Introduce a new cut on the field's standard two-variable example first (the LP vertex, why it is infeasible, the cut it yields), then state the general separation (pp. 13–16).
- When an intersection cut may miss an integer point on the frontier of the free set, use integrality of the defining quantity to enlarge the set so that the point is interior and always cut (p. 18).
