### S109 · Fast training of support vector machines with Gaussian kernel (Discrete Optimization 22, 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.disopt.2015.03.002)
**Read level**: full (all 19 file pages of the author preprint). First-page check: title and sole author Matteo Fischetti match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (sole author); the telecommunication interpretation, the LOO 1 evidence and the HINGE error figures come from Lucia Petterle's 2012 Padova master's thesis (pp. 4, 8, 14–15, 19) · **Position in trajectory**: the 2015–2022 machine-learning line of the skill's trajectory table; S054's reference list places it in the ISCO 2014 special issue (S054 p. 13).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Hinge-loss SVM training fixes C and γ and tunes them in an outer cross-validation loop, e.g. "5*5*5+1=126 convex problems" per training (p. 2). The entry is to put cross validation inside the model: a MILP whose objective is the leave-one-out estimate of misclassification (pp. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea**: The NAIVE misclassification MILP counts each point's own signal and so prefers an "almost impulsive" kernel that is useless off the training set (p. 6); the LOO MILP measures the net signal without x_j's own term (pp. 6–7). Overfitting is then controlled by a-priori constraints instead of penalty terms: all α_i = 1 and β0 = 0 (LOO 1), all α_i = 1 with β0 free (LOO 2), one α per class (LOO 3) (pp. 3, 7–8); γ moves inside the training (p. 13).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: binary classification with a Gaussian kernel (pp. 3–4); LOO MILP with binaries z_j, big-M M = 10,000 and ε = 10⁻⁵ (pp. 7, 15); the leave-one-out estimate is reliable only if training is blind to the left-out point, which the unrestricted MILP violates (p. 7); V(γ) assumed (almost) unimodal (p. 13). Testbed: Brooks's ten data sets (Adult omitted for size), 10 random 70/30 splits each, features standardized on the training part (p. 14).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Algorithm 1 finds the best β0 exactly: net signals in O(p²), a sort, a scan of the breakpoints −ν_j (pp. 11–12); LOO 3 by 11 grid values of α⁺ (p. 9); γ by a three-point bisection (pp. 13, 15). The full LOO MILP: CPLEX 12.5, cuts off, EPINT 0.0 and EPRHS 1e-9, the LOO 2 solution as warm start, polishing after 10 s, 30 s per call (p. 15).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: single-thread notebook; test error HINGE 18.33%, LOO 1/2/3 19.74/18.22/17.45%, LOO MILP 20.80% (its training objective underestimates the test error by 79%); training about 600 s for HINGE (CPLEX barrier over the 126 QPs) against 0.3/0.6/7.1 s, LOO MILP 216.6 s (pp. 13–17).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: four contributions: the telecommunication reading, the LOO MILP, constraints against overfitting, sorting algorithms (p. 3). Limits printed: speedups only against a general-purpose solver (p. 15); competitive "at least, on our testbed and by using the Gaussian kernel" (p. 18); LOO 1 fails on Ionosphere (23.38% against 6.30%, p. 17). Future: a controlled increase of degrees of freedom, a-priori properties imposed as constraints, the linear kernel (p. 18).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: thinning applied to degrees of freedom to cure overfitting, not solver difficulty: LOO 1 needs no optimization at all yet classifies reasonably (p. 8); fewer degrees of freedom make the estimate more reliable (p. 9); the unrestricted LOO MILP reaches a 4.20% training objective and 20.80% test error (p. 16).
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: the MILP is the modelling device, and its CPLEX run (warm start, polishing) is kept "mainly for benchmark purposes" (p. 15); the delivered method needs no solver (pp. 11–12, 18): the step-5 retreat from a slow MIP, taken to the end.
- Method 4 ✗ contradiction (conceded in print): the HINGE arm is a general-purpose barrier solver with error figures taken from the thesis, while specialized SVM codes exist (pp. 14–15); step 2 (the practitioner's baseline) is not met, step 6 (print the bias) is (p. 15).
- Heuristic 2 ✅ (move one piece): the overfitting control moves from the objective into constraints (pp. 3, 18).
- Academic Lineage ⚠ variant: a 2012 master's thesis feeds a 2014 preprint (pp. 1, 19), but the paper is sole-authored and credits the student in the text (pp. 4, 8, 14–15).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Fold an outer validation loop into the model: make the leave-one-out error the objective and exclude each point's own contribution from its own prediction (pp. 6–7).
- Control overfitting by equality constraints that remove degrees of freedom, and check that the model's optimal value tracks the test error across the hyperparameter range before trusting it (Figure 2, pp. 7–10).
- When everything but a scalar threshold is fixed, optimize the threshold exactly by sorting the scores and scanning the breakpoints (Algorithm 1, pp. 11–12).

### S123 · Mixed-Integer Linear Programming Heuristics for the PrePack Optimization Problem (Discrete Optimization 22, 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.disopt.2015.03.004)
**Read level**: full (all 20 file pages of the author preprint submitted on 11 March 2015, p. 1). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti, Monaci, Salvagnin match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Michele Monaci, Domenico Salvagnin · **Position in trajectory**: an applied matheuristic of the 2010–2016 randomness period (acknowledged project "Exploiting randomness in Mixed Integer Linear Programming", p. 18); model and instances from Hoskins et al. (CPAIOR 2014), instances provided by L.-M. Rousseau (pp. 3, 18–19).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: POP arises when a warehouse pre-packs items for many stores and the packing workforce costs as much as the goods: a hard limit on the number of box configurations, with over- and understocking penalized (pp. 2–3). Prior heuristics (CP, MILP, patents) fix parts of the solution step by step (p. 3). Entry: the published model defeats global MINLP solvers and a black-box MILP solver (pp. 5–6).
- **D2 Core idea**: matheuristics that fix variable subsets so that each restriction is "(reasonably) easy" for a commercial MILP solver (pp. 6–7): (a) fixing all x or all y makes the bilinear model a MILP, alternated by REOPT (p. 7); (b) a neighbourhood that frees one box configuration at a time, in a random circular order, stopping after N_B non-improving calls (pp. 7–8); (c) a constructive scheme over growing store subsets, each partial solution turned into a full one by REOPT, stores ordered randomly, most dissimilar or most similar by the Hamming distance of demand vectors (pp. 8–9). Tabu Search and VNS are named and not investigated (p. 7).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: integer variables x_bs and y_bi with bilinear products q = x·y (p. 4), linearized by a binary expansion of x (p. 5); bins completely filled; overstock bounds (pp. 3–4); with even capacities a redundant parity constraint (18) strengthens the LP bound and yields the lower bound (19) (pp. 5–6, 11). Testbed: the real AllColor58 instance (58 stores, 24 items = 6 sizes × 4 colours, capacities 4, 6, 8, 10, β = 1, α = 10) and its sub-instances, plus 50 random store subsets (pp. 11, 16–17).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: x and q declared continuous free so that presolve removes them (p. 5); aggressive artificial bounds on x shrink the binary expansion and are relaxed when no improvement is found (p. 8); multi-start with the internal seed not reset, 5 random pair swaps of the store order and a new CPLEX seed at each restart (p. 10); abort when the parity bound is met (p. 11).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: C with CPLEX 12.6 on a quad-core Xeon, 900 s (p. 10); black-box CPLEX ends with 90–99% gaps on the three large instances (p. 6); single runs against the published values and times of [6] "assuming the PC used are substantially equivalent" (pp. 11–13), then 100 seeded runs per instance (time, time to best, primal integral, #opt, box plots) and 50 new instances × 10 seeds (pp. 14–18); every instance of [6] reaches the parity bound (pp. 13–14, 17).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: MILP-based heuristics that get solutions out of an unsolvable model and prove optimality of every instance of [6] through the bound (p. 17). Limits: fast heu loses to most similar on the largest instance, so a hybrid is suggested (pp. 14–15); instances with more stores are harder (p. 17). Future: other substructures, more sophisticated metaheuristics (pp. 17–18).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (steps 1–2): global solvers first ("none of the global solvers that we tried, namely Couenne [11] and SCIP [12], could solve even the smallest instances", p. 5), then the black-box MILP table (p. 6), then each sub-decision solved as a restricted MILP by the same solver (pp. 6–7).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (steps 3–4): the seed dependence is stated before the tables, results come as 100-seed statistics with box plots (pp. 10, 14–16), and the solver seed changes at every restart to diversify (p. 10). No placebo arm.
- Method 4 ✗ contradiction (a dated instance of the listed lapse, 2015): the rival's single-run values and times come from [6] on another machine (pp. 11–13). The headline claim does not depend on them: optimality is certified by the parity bound (pp. 13, 17).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: thinning as a heuristic restriction: presolve deletes the redundant variables (p. 5), a cap on x shortens the expansion, and the cap is released when the search stalls (p. 8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a model bilinear in two variable blocks, fix one block to get a MILP and alternate between the blocks until no improvement (REOPT, p. 7).
- Search a neighbourhood that frees one configuration at a time in a random circular order, and stop after one full round without improvement (pp. 7–8).
- Cap integer variables aggressively to shrink a binary expansion, and release the cap when the neighbourhood stops improving (p. 8).
- Grow the instance: solve on an increasing subset of entities, convert each partial solution into a full one and update the incumbent at every step; adding the most similar entity next injects little novelty per step (pp. 8–9).
- Stop a heuristic at a trivial valid bound, so that a hit becomes a proof of optimality (pp. 11, 13).
- Report heuristics by the primal integral and by 100-seed statistics with box plots of final value and time to best (pp. 11–16).

**Quotes**:
- "Working with a subset of the variables (after fixing the other) we were able to squeeze useful solutions out of an impossible-to-solve MILP model." (p. 17)
- "Due to their heuristic nature, our methods—through deterministic—exhibit a large dependency on the initial conditions, including the random seeds used both within our code and in CPLEX." (p. 10)

**Read level**: full (all 28 file pages of the author preprint submitted on 15 January 2018, printed title "Self-split parallelization for Mixed-Integer Linear Programming", p. 1). First-page check: authors Fischetti, Monaci, Salvagnin match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Michele Monaci, Domenico Salvagnin · **Position in trajectory**: the journal extension into MILP of the CPAIOR 2014 SelfSplit paper [25] (pp. 4, 27); the erraticism-and-sampling line carried to parallel search.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: MIP solvers are "only deceitfully easy to parallelize": root-heavy work, order-dependent performance, unbalanced trees, global information, users who want determinism (p. 2). - **D2 Core idea**: every worker reads the input plus (k, K), runs the same deterministic sampling phase, colours the open nodes by a deterministic rule and skips the other workers' nodes; only the final results are merged (pp. 6–7). Vanilla colouring is pseudo-random; the paused-node variant parks nodes judged easy enough by the log-volume of their integer domains relative to the root, adapts the threshold, then sorts the parked nodes by a difficulty score and deals them round-robin (pp. 7–9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: a deterministic original algorithm on identical workers (same hardware and OS), no fault tolerance, no hash check of the sampling phase (pp. 5, 7); sampling and merging assumed not to be bottlenecks (p. 7). - **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: two in-vitro experiments before any real code: (i) synthetic subproblem sizes (uniform with a 2× or 10× spread, Pareto α = 0.5 and 1.5), N = K·M, 10,000 repetitions, speedup boxplots (pp. 9–12); (ii) 32 MIPLIB 2010 trees of at least 500,000 nodes recorded once with CPLEX 12.5.1 callbacks (default, single thread, optimum given, heuristics off) and replayed with pause rules (volume, depth), sortings (ideal oracle, score, volume, random) and offline or online colouring, the pause parameter chosen a posteriori per instance (pp. 12–15). Real codes: 46 and 11 new lines (pp. 16, 18); for the callback code three passes (deterministic sampling, a dummy callback that kills wrong-colour nodes, restore and run), with the callbacks installed once and told the pass number (pp. 19–20); in CPLEX one parameter set (ρ = 5, N = 2,000, δ = 10) for all instances (p. 21).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: identical single-thread Xeon machines (p. 15): ATSP branch-and-bound (20 instances, optimum on input) speedups 7.76/13.37/21.56/30.55 for K = 4/16/32/64, ATSP branch-and-cut (44 instances) 6.74/10.89/14.54/17.91 (pp. 16–18); the Benders UFL code 9.1 on average over its own one-worker version and 5.4 over the original code (pp. 20–21); in CPLEX the one-worker overhead is 1–2% under each of 5 seeds, and with 16 workers SelfSplit is fastest overall against CPLEX's distributed RAMPUP and DYNAMIC, but DYNAMIC solves more (208 against 193) and leads on the hardest classes (pp. 22–24). Tables 7 and 8 give the same CPLEX(1) reference different counts in class "all" (81 against 174, pp. 23–24); the text read does not explain this.

Limits: static balancing fails on heavy-tailed subproblem distributions, where bet-and-run is suggested instead (p. 12); branch-and-cut codes with small trees of heavy nodes pay for the sampling overhead (pp. 18, 25); DYNAMIC wins on hard instances (p. 24); the in-vitro figures are "somewhat optimistic" (p. 15). ### S027 · On the use of intersection cuts for bilevel optimization (Mathematical Programming 172 (1), 2018, DOI 10.1007/s10107-017-1189-5)

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ivana Ljubić, Michele Monaci, Markus Sinnl · **Position in trajectory**: the 2014–2020 bilevel line (WWTF ICT15-014, p. 27), next to S090.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: instead of ad-hoc bilevel methods, a general-purpose MIBLP solver built from "non-invasive supplements" to a branch-and-cut MILP code, inheriting its cuts, heuristics, numerics and threads (pp. 3–4); the generic methods in the literature are illustrated on small examples (p. 4) and need a compact HPR region or all-integer variables (pp. 11–12).
- **D2 Core idea**: (i) the minimal modifications of branch-and-bound on the high-point relaxation (HPR) needed for correctness: no incumbent update or unbounded fathoming without a bilevel check, a follower MILP at integral nodes, branching on the linking leader variables until fixed, a refinement MILP at fully fixed nodes (Algorithms 1–2, pp. 9–11); (ii) intersection cuts from bilevel-free sets S(ŷ) built from one follower solution, enlarged to S⁺(ŷ) (right-hand side b + 1) under integrality, with every facet deleted whose opposite closed half-space holds no bilevel-feasible point (Theorems 3–5, Corollaries 1–2, pp. 13–16), ŷ chosen by SEP-1 (the follower optimum, free from the feasibility check) or SEP-2 (an auxiliary MILP that maximizes the removable facets) (pp. 17–18); (iii) basis-free "informed no-good" cuts for all-binary problems (pp. 18–20).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: linear MIBLP in value-function form, optimistic version (pp. 5–6); Assumption 1: integer variables bounded; Assumption 2: continuous leader variables do not enter the follower (p. 7); Assumption 3: Ax + By − b integer, needed for S⁺ (p. 14); HPR need not be compact (p. 12). Testbed: 302 instances in four classes (CARAMIA-MARI, DENEGRE, INTERDICTION treated as general bilevel, and instances derived from 19 binary MIPLIB 3.0 models by making the first 10/50/90% of the variables follower variables, up to about 80,000 HPR variables) (pp. 20–22).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Theorem 1 decides follower unboundedness for every HPR point by one LP over box-normalized directions, scaled to an integer ray by rationality (p. 8); Theorem 2 gives finiteness because each branching strictly shrinks a bounded integer domain (p. 11); the cuts are local, from the node's optimal basis (p. 13), and need a minimum distance from the facets to be numerically reliable (pp. 13–14). C on CPLEX 12.6.3 callbacks (usercut and lazyconstraint), CPLEX heuristics and preprocessing off, tolerances 10⁻⁹ (p. 20).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: 4 threads, 3,600 s (p. 20); BENCHMARK, DeNegre's cuts inside the authors' own CPLEX code, is first checked against the original implementation's published results (10–100 times faster, two more solved, pp. 22–23); performance profiles over 232 instances: SEP-2a fastest on 28% and solves 71%, BENCHMARK 8% and 42% (p. 24); shifted geometric means with shifts of 10 s and 100 nodes, gaps clipped at 100% (pp. 25–26); single runs, no seeds.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: a finitely convergent branch-and-cut for MIBLP and the first use of intersection cuts in bilevel optimization, outperforming previous proposals "by a large margin" (pp. 4, 26–27). Limits: S⁺ needs Assumption 3, informed no-good cuts work only for binary problems (pp. 14, 23); separating at every node does not pay on the tiny DENEGRE instances (p. 26). Future: other bilevel-free polyhedra, cuts that need no LP basis (p. 27).

**Method links**:

### S090 · A dynamic reformulation heuristic for Generalized Interdiction Problems (European Journal of Operational Research 267 (1), 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2017.11.043)
**Read level**: full (all 31 file pages: IRIS cover sheets pp. 1–2, accepted manuscript pp. 3–31). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti, Monaci, Sinnl match (p. 3); the cover identifies the file as the final peer-reviewed accepted manuscript (p. 2).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Michele Monaci, Markus Sinnl · **Position in trajectory**: the bilevel line (WWTF ICT15-014, p. 28); the optimal values of the literature instances come from the same group's exact MIBLP solver [4] (p. 23).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: interdiction problems are zero-sum Stackelberg games whose exact solution is hard (p. 3); dropping the assumption that leader and follower objectives are opposite gives the Generalized Interdiction Problem (GIP), which cannot be cast as min-max (p. 5). The only generic heuristic in the literature, a greedy, is "blind" to the follower's structure (p. 7).
- **D2 Core idea**: relax the follower's integrality and replace the follower LP by its dual (SIP), or by primal and dual feasibility plus a linear duality-gap condition (GIP), to get a compact single-level MILP (Theorems 4.1–4.2, pp. 12–13); solve it with a black-box MILP solver and repair each leader vector by REFINE (the follower MILP, then a MILP with the follower value fixed) (pp. 14–15); ITERATE refines every pool solution and adds a no-good cut per leader vector (p. 16); DYN-REF appends the root cuts CPLEX generates while solving follower MILPs to the follower system, which adds dual columns to the reformulation without any pricing code (pp. 16, 18).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: binary interdiction variables, with y_j ≤ UB_j (1 − x_j) as the only x-dependent follower constraints, and a follower assumed bounded and feasible for every x (pp. 9–10); interdiction enforced by big-M penalties M_j > max{d_j, Φ^UB − Φ^LB} (Theorem 3.1, p. 11); the relaxed problem is in general neither a relaxation nor a restriction (an upper bound for SIP, exact when J_y = ∅, p. 12). Testbed: 1,286 instances in ten classes (knapsack, multidimensional knapsack, clique interdiction with and without {0,1/2}-CG redundant rows, firefighter, random GIP), with network-flow and shortest-path classes excluded because a continuous follower makes the reformulation exact (pp. 18–20).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: proofs by bounding the follower's best value (Theorem 3.1, p. 11) and by strong duality with equality relaxed to ≥ through weak duality (Theorem 4.2, p. 13); the solver's own big-M strengthening is relied on (p. 14). CPLEX 12.6.3 with POLISHING after half the time limit, the solution pool and optionally POPULATE, all other parameters at defaults (p. 18); cuts are collected only when the best pool solution is refined (p. 19).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: 4 threads on a Xeon E3-1220V2 (p. 18); on 656 literature instances with known optima and a 10 s limit, DR+ is optimal on 92% (average error about 3%), I+ on 88%, the literature greedy on 17% (pp. 23–24); the greedy also gets a randomized 4-thread GRASP variant "For a more fair comparison" (p. 19); new instances at 60 s against the best value found, with dual gaps from the group's exact solvers (pp. 24–26); single runs.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: the GIP class, its compact reformulation, and a dynamic reformulation fed by solver-generated cuts (pp. 5–6, 27). Limits: large percentage errors on clique instances with tiny optima (p. 24), uninformative dual gaps on random GIPs (p. 26). Future: inside an exact method, min-max regret, interdiction penalties, general MIBLPs through KKT conditions, where preliminary runs find the reformulation slow (pp. 27–28).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (steps 2–3): the black-box solver does all the work, on the reformulation, inside REFINE, and as the source of the cuts DYN-REF recycles (pp. 5, 16, 18).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (steps 2 and 5): the weak literature baseline is upgraded to a randomized multi-thread variant with the same time (p. 19), and solver parameters stay at defaults "To avoid overtuning" (p. 18).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: LP duality projects the follower variables out of the SIP reformulation, "thus removing the y variables from the model" (p. 13), paid for with the loss of exactness (p. 12).
- Heuristic 3 ✅ (borrow the cure): the relax-dualise-reformulate idea comes from min-max regret heuristics [18, 19] (p. 5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a bilevel or min-max problem with an integer follower, relax the follower's integrality, dualize its LP into a compact single-level MILP, solve it with a black-box solver, and repair each leader vector with two small MILPs (the follower value, then the best response with that value fixed) (pp. 12–15).
- Strengthen the reformulation on the fly by appending the root cuts the solver generates while solving follower subproblems, which adds dual columns without pricing code (pp. 16, 18).
- Iterate over the solver's solution pool, refine each solution, exclude seen leader vectors by no-good cuts, and keep 5% of the remaining time for refinement (p. 16).
- Before comparing, give a naive literature baseline a randomized multi-start version with the same threads and time (p. 19).
- Pair instance sets that differ only by redundant valid inequalities (TRSC against TRSC+) to show how much each method depends on the formulation (pp. 22, 24).
- Derive safe big-M penalties from variable bounds (Theorem 3.1, p. 11).

**Quotes**:
- "A peculiarity of our scheme is that the generation of the new columns of BT does not require an ad-hoc pricing algorithm, in the sense that we just re-use the cutting planes generated by the MILP solver invoked with function REFINE." (p. 18)
- "To avoid overtuning, all the other CPLEX parameters (including those related to cut generation) are left at their default values." (p. 18)
