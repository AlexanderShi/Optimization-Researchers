### S180 · Combining complete and partial cell suppression methodologies in statistical disclosure control (Statistical Journal of the United Nations ECE 2001, DOI 10.3233/sju-2001-18410)
**Read level**: full (all 7 file pages). First-page check: title and authors Matteo Fischetti and Juan-José Salazar-González match (p. 1). The relation symbols (≤, ≥) of the models on pp. 5–6 are lost in the text layer; they are read off the matching constraints in S093 (p. 8).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Juan-José Salazar-González · **Position in trajectory**: statistical disclosure control line inside the EU project IST-2000-25069 (p. 1); a practitioner summary of their partial cell suppression working paper [1] (1998), whose journal version is S093, plus a new combined model (pp. 3, 7).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Classical (complete) cell suppression hides cells, but an attacker with any LP code (the paper names MS Excel, Maple and Mathematica) computes a feasibility interval for each hidden cell, so the published output is in effect a table of intervals (p. 3). Entry: if the optimizer already chooses which cells become intervals, it can choose the interval endpoints too (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Three methodologies side by side: complete CSP (one binary per cell), partial CSP (continuous decrements z⁻ and increments z⁺ per cell), and a combined model that adds a binary x_k per cell, linked by z⁻_k ≤ (a_k − lb_k)x_k and z⁺_k ≤ (ub_k − a_k)x_k, so that the office's controls become linear constraints: at most MAX INTERVALS intervals (Σx_k ≤ MAX INTERVALS) and a minimum width (z⁻_k + z⁺_k ≥ MIN WIDTH·x_k) (pp. 5–6).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Tables with linear links (marginal totals), external bounds lb_k and ub_k known to the attacker, lower, upper and sliding protection levels per sensitive cell (typically percentages of the nominal value), fixed suppression costs w_k for complete CSP and per-unit width costs w⁻_k, w⁺_k for partial CSP (pp. 2, 4–5).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: No algorithm is given: the protection requirements are written as linear constraints by LP duality, with the details referred to [3] and [1] (pp. 5–6); complete CSP is strongly NP-hard, partial CSP polynomial, the combined model NP-hard in general (pp. 4, 6); a branch-and-cut along the lines of [3] "can be implemented" for it (p. 6), but none is reported.
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: One table, taken from [1], on three real 2-D tables (41×31 to 359×46): partial CSP losses 153, 140,297 and 9,357 against complete CSP losses 486, 281,398 and 19,099; partial times 4.78–77.67 s against 11.42–367.62 s for complete CSP plus 0.07–2,486.07 s for its auditing phase, on a "Pentium 133" (p. 4). The paper itself warns that the two losses have different meanings (p. 4); the combined model is not run.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: The combined methodology is called the main contribution (p. 7): it keeps partial suppression's lower loss and built-in auditing while bounding the number and width of intervals (pp. 5–6); its price is NP-hardness (p. 6); no computation for it and no future work (pp. 6–7).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the thinning happens in the problem definition, not in a model: publishing intervals instead of asterisks turns a strongly NP-hard 0-1 problem into a polynomial one (p. 4), and binaries return in the combined model only to carry the office's controls (p. 6).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: the one table compares losses that the authors say mean different things (p. 4); the commensurable weighting appears only in S093 (p. 8). The same times are attributed to a Pentium 133 here (p. 4) and to a Pentium/75 in S093 (pp. 7–8).
- H1 (heuristic, not a method) ✅: the field's yes/no suppression reflex is inverted into continuous intervals (p. 3).
- Research Taste warning sign 2 ✗ (dated, practitioner venue): the combined model's branch-and-cut is described as implementable but is neither implemented nor tested (p. 6); compare the s01 card on S139.
- new-pattern candidate: "Adversary's output as decision variable" (the intervals an attacker would compute by LP become the published decision variables, so the auditing phase disappears, pp. 3–4; see S093 pp. 2, 4).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a requirement is checked by an LP that an adversary or auditor would solve, ask whether that LP's output can be the decision itself; here the published intervals are the variables and the separate auditing step disappears (pp. 3–4).
- Merge a fixed-charge method and a continuous method into one MIP by gating each continuous width with a binary (z ≤ range·x), then write the operational controls (a count, a minimum width) as linear constraints on x and z (p. 6).

**Quotes**:
- "If the optimisation problem in classical (complete) cell suppression looks for cells to publish intervals instead of the exact original values, why not to look for the extreme values of the intervals too?" (p. 3)
- "It looks like the optimisation problem of the new methodology (partial CSP) is more complicated than the old one (complete CSP), but it is not!" (p. 4)

### S077 · Exact algorithms for minimum routing cost trees (Networks 2002, DOI 10.1002/net.10022)
**Read level**: full (all 13 file pages). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti, Lancia, Serafini match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Giuseppe Lancia (partly at Sandia National Labs, p. 1), Paolo Serafini · **Position in trajectory**: Padova period, an exact-methods paper with a computational-biology application under a MURST bioinformatics project (p. 1); more experiments are in a 2001 Padova technical report [4] (pp. 9, 13).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The minimum routing cost spanning tree (the minimum communication cost network with equal demands), NP-hard also in the metric case, with a PTAS and Wong's 2-approximation known (pp. 1–2). Entry: exact algorithms, and Wu et al.'s suggestion that trees of small routing cost could beat Gusfield's star-based multiple alignment (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: A path formulation IP1 with exponentially many path variables priced by Dijkstra per vertex pair and O(mn²) linking rows generated at run time, integrality only on the edge variables (pp. 2–4); "LP shortcutting": the column phases give lower limits l′ and the row phases upper limits l″ on the node bound, so a node is branched as soon as l″ < u and fathomed as soon as l′ ≥ u, and column generation stops after the sixth phase (pp. 5–6). Also a combinatorial bound from a maximum spanning tree on path savings that improves Dionne–Florian's (p. 4), and an alignment heuristic built on the minimum routing cost tree (pp. 8–9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Undirected graphs with nonnegative edge lengths (p. 2); Euclidean graphs (points in the unit square, lengths ×n² rounded) and random graphs (lengths in 1..n²), complete, dense (max{C(n,2)/3, 4n} edges) or sparse (3n edges), complete Euclidean the hardest (p. 9); protein families from PRINTS with 10–20 sequences of 200–600 amino acids (p. 12).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Edge variables stay in every LP so that pricing never regenerates a variable fixed by branching (p. 3); a modified Dijkstra returns many negative-reduced-cost paths per call (pp. 3, 11); node tightening by MST-based upper bounds on edge loads (p. 6); fixed-order and variable-order branching rules (pp. 6–7); an inequality family found by enumerating the extreme feasible vectors of (L_ij, D_i, D_j, x_ij) and computing their facets with PORTA (p. 7); local search with linear-time moves from 100 starting trees (p. 6). C with CPLEX 5.0 on a Pentium Celeron 300 MHz (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Settings chosen one factor at a time (p. 9): bounds normalized to l_IP1 on 20 complete Euclidean graphs (p. 10); the Dionne–Florian bound reimplemented and too weak for 15 vertices within an hour (p. 10); a one-instance degeneracy trace (20 vertices: 19 pricings and 1,066 s before the LP value first moves; 2,631 s with all rows against 351 s with row generation) (p. 10); LP shortcutting faster on all 100 hardest instances, mean saving 44% (p. 11); final runs on 210 graphs with a 1-hour limit: Euclidean graphs solved up to 30 vertices (2 of 5 complete ones at that size), random ones up to 40 and rarely 50 (p. 12); hardness falls as d_max/d_min grows (100 instances with 30 vertices) (p. 12); alignment cost about 6% below the best star's (p. 12).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Models, bounds and search devices that extend to communication-cost networks (p. 13); the problem stays hard (about 30–40 vertices in an hour on a slow PC), but beyond that size the bound certifies solutions within 1% of optimality on average after an hour (p. 13); the branching-rule results are only in the report [4] (p. 7).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: the column-generation loop is traced on one 20-vertex instance (p. 10) and fixed at loop level: rows are generated too, so the LPs are smaller and less degenerate, the loops alternate, and column generation stops after six phases (pp. 4–5, 10–11); the pricing oracle (Dijkstra) stays and is only asked for more columns (p. 11). It is a column-generation loop, not a cut loop.
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (step 5, credit the lineage): intermediate bounds for fathoming are credited as known, and only preemptive branching is claimed as new (p. 5).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (step 1): the rival Dionne–Florian bound is reimplemented and run in the same code on the same machine instead of being quoted (p. 10).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the spread is in instance hardness, not in runs; it is noticed (p. 12) and turned into a designed factor (edge-weight ranges, 10 classes of 10 instances, p. 12). No seeds.
- new-pattern candidate: "Lower bound as an audit of a heuristic" (beyond the solvable size the bound certifies the local-search solutions within 1%, p. 13; also S135).
- new-pattern candidate: "Facets of a small aggregate by enumeration and PORTA" (the extreme feasible vectors of a four-variable aggregate are enumerated, their facets computed for several n, and a general inequality derived, p. 7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- In branch-and-price with row and column generation, track l′ after each column phase and l″ after each row phase; branch when l″ < u and fathom when l′ ≥ u, unless the gap to the incumbent is below a threshold (0.5% here) (pp. 5–6).
- Keep the branching variables permanently in the LP and price out only the others, so branching never conflicts with pricing (p. 3).
- Print a table of bound against time per column-generation phase and truncate where the tail starts (here six phases give about 99% of the bound) (pp. 5, 11).
- Find candidate inequalities for a small aggregate of variables by enumerating its feasible extreme vectors and running PORTA for several sizes, then prove the general form (p. 7).
- Measure a node-level speed-up over the whole tree, since work saved at one node can be needed later (p. 5).

**Quotes**:
- "Although the idea of using intermediate bounds for fathoming the node is known, the use of preemptive branching is (to the best of our knowledge) new." (p. 5)
- "It is worth noting that at a single node the speed up can be greater than 90%; however, the work saved at a node (e.g., generation of useful columns) may be needed at a later time, so the overall saving is less impressive than the one obtained, for example, at the root node." (p. 5)

- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Proof devices only: facet proofs by induction on the number of clones with affinely independent tours built explicitly (Theorem 3.3, pp. 17–18); support-reducedness read off the connectivity of a bipartite graph B[G₀] (Theorem 2.8, p. 15); validity by rank-1 Chvátal derivations (odd CAT, p. 29; SD, p. 31) and by a case split on x(Q₁) ≥ 1 with degree inequalities added (pp. 44–46); a fractional point that certifies Chvátal rank above 1 (p. 40); unbounded Chvátal rank because exponentially many subtour elimination constraints need positive multipliers in any derivation (Theorem 7.11, p. 42).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: n/a (no computation).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: The Grötschel–Padberg research problem on monotone versus flat facets is answered by a simple condition (three isolated nodes, Corollary 2.10, p. 16); 2-liftable chord sets are characterized (Theorem 7.2, p. 36); new rank-1 shell and fork classes and the curtain inequalities (pp. 40–47); limits: separation is not addressed (pp. 4–5), most proofs are left to the cited papers, and the chapter ends with Corollary 7.16 without conclusions or open questions (p. 47).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (polyhedral, not computational): the case for staying in the original directed space instead of transforming to the STSP (p. 3); it is the theory behind S056's computational finding (S056 p. 34).
- new-pattern candidate (existing): "Reduce to the known special case and lift" (facets carried from smaller graphs by cloning nodes into cliques with closed-form, sequence-independent coefficients, pp. 16–19; T-lifting, merge lifting and 2-cycle cloning, pp. 20–24).
- new-pattern candidate (existing): "Chvátal–Gomory derivation as validity proof and separation handle" (rank-1 derivations prove odd CAT and SD validity, pp. 29, 31; Chvátal rank classifies lifted cycle inequalities, pp. 40–42).
- new-pattern candidate: "Prove in the full-dimensional relaxation, then transfer back" (monotonization removes the equations that complicate interchange arguments, and simple graph conditions carry the facets back to P, pp. 13–16).
- No link to the computational content of the six methods: the chapter is polyhedral theory, which team.json lists as not yet represented by a voice of its own.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before using sequential lifting, look for a lifting with closed-form, sequence-independent coefficients (clique lifting: δ_k = max{α_ik + α_kj − α_ij}) (pp. 16–19).
- Prove facets in the full-dimensional monotone polytope, where interchange arguments are short, then transfer them with an easy sufficient condition (three nodes isolated in the support graph G⁺ of the nonzero coefficients, Corollary 2.10) (pp. 13–16).
- Put inequalities into a canonical form before claiming that a family is new or that two members differ (pp. 24–26, 34).
- To show that an inequality is not of Chvátal rank 1, exhibit a point that satisfies the degree and subtour elimination constraints but violates the inequality by more than 1 (p. 40).

### S093 · Partial cell suppression: a new methodology for statistical disclosure control (Statistics and Computing 2003, DOI 10.1023/a:1021927424942)
**Read level**: full (all 9 file pages). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti and Salazar-González match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Juan-José Salazar-González · **Position in trajectory**: statistical disclosure control line after their exact methods for 2-D tables (Mathematical Programming 1999) and general tables (JASA 2000) (p. 2); preliminary version presented in Washington in May 1998 (p. 3), received February 2000, accepted March 2001 (p. 1); EU project "Computational Aspects of Statistical Confidentiality" (p. 9).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Complete cell suppression is strongly NP-hard (p. 2), and an attacker computes the range of each hidden cell with two LPs (pp. 1–2). Entry: publish intervals instead of missing values, with three claimed advantages: a smaller loss, polynomial solvability, and the auditing ranges for free (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: An LP in the published decrements and increments (z⁻, z⁺) of each cell (p. 3). Model 1 certifies each protection level with auxiliary tables f^k and g^k per sensitive cell, more than 10,000,000 variables and 20,000,000 constraints for a 100×100 table with 5% sensitive cells (p. 4). Model 2 projects these tables out, Benders-style, by LP duality of the attacker subproblems, leaving capacity constraints Σ(α_i z⁺_i + β_i z⁻_i) ≥ z⁺_{i_k} over the extreme points of the dual polyhedra (pp. 4–6).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Any table given by My = b with external bounds lb ≤ y ≤ ub known to the attacker (k-dimensional, linked and hierarchical tables; network structure only for k = 2) (p. 3); lower, upper and sliding protection levels per sensitive cell and nonnegative weights w⁺, w⁻ (p. 4); with nonnegative weights the optimal ranges coincide with the auditing ranges (p. 4).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: A Kelley-type cutting-plane loop: a master LP with the trivial bounds, then for each sensitive cell an attacker LP whose dual gives a violated capacity cut (p. 6); polynomial in theory by the equivalence of optimization and separation (p. 6), whereas Model 1, polynomial too, is unusable in practice (p. 6); a constraint pool with removal of slack cuts and a pool check before separation (pp. 6–7); a preprocessing pass at the trivial solution that both drops the protection levels already met and fills the pool (p. 7); variable pricing above 10,000 variables (p. 7).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: A restricted model (z⁺_i = UB_i x_i and z⁻_i = LB_i x_i, whose optimum "can be strictly worse") solved by the authors' complete-CSP code with its integrality features off and stopped after the root, on a PC Pentium/75 with CPLEX 3.0, three real 2-D instances; each complete-CSP weight set to the width of the a priori interval so that the losses are comparable; partial losses 31–50% of the complete ones, and times 4.78–77.67 s against 367.69–2,603.00 s for complete CSP including auditing (pp. 7–8).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: A new methodology plus a polynomial algorithm (pp. 1, 8); the computation is called preliminary and uses a slight modification of the model (p. 8); future: a fixed penalty for any replaced entry via 0-1 variables, with a maximum count and minimum widths at the master level, unifying complete and partial suppression (p. 8), which is the combined model of S180.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (step 3, practice, joint, 1998–2003): the auxiliary certificate tables are projected out Benders-style, so the model lives in the space of the published widths only (pp. 4–6). Unlike the 1999 cell suppression paper (s01 card S069), this one names Benders (p. 4).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (steps 4–6): the two losses are made commensurable by setting each complete-CSP weight to the width of the a priori interval (p. 8); the auditing time is counted in the classical arm (p. 8); the new method runs in a restricted version whose optimum can only be worse (p. 7), so by my reading the savings shown are a lower bound.
- Method 6 ⚠ variant (step 3): the smallest code is the authors' own branch-and-cut with its integrality features off and stopped at the root (p. 7), not a general-purpose MIP solver.
- new-pattern candidate: "Adversary's output as decision variable" (the attacker's LP ranges are the published intervals, and at an optimum they coincide with the auditing ranges, pp. 2, 4; see S180 pp. 3–4).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace per-scenario certificate variables (one auxiliary solution per sensitive cell) by cuts in the master variables derived from the dual of each scenario LP, separated by solving that LP at the master point (pp. 4–6).
- Let preprocessing and pool initialization share one pass: evaluate each requirement at a trivial solution, drop those already met, and store the dual cut of each of the others in the pool (p. 7).
- Before comparing two methods whose objectives measure different things, choose weights that make the measures commensurable and state the choice in the setup (p. 8).

**Quotes**:
- "We next derive an equivalent model inspired by Benders’ decomposition (see e.g. Nemhauser and Wolsey (1988)), which is based on the same technique we used in Fischetti and Salazar (2000) in the context of complete CSP." (p. 4)
- "This produces complete CSP instances which can fairly be compared with their corresponding partial CSP versions." (p. 8)

### S135 · Optimisation of the interconnecting network of a UMTS radio mobile telephone system (European Journal of Operational Research 2003, DOI 10.1016/s0377-2217(01)00383-6)
**Read level**: full (all 12 file pages). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti, Romanin Jacur, Salazar González match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Giorgio Romanin Jacur, Juan José Salazar González · **Position in trajectory**: industrial application with CSELT, Telecom Italia's research laboratory, which supplied the instances and its tabu-search code (pp. 1, 8, 12); received November 2000 (p. 1); the same model is the test problem of S064, his first local-branching follow-up (S064 pp. 9–11).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A three-level star network (BTSs as leaves, cell site switches, local exchanges) whose switches and exchanges must be chosen and connected at minimum cost (pp. 3–4); strongly NP-hard because it generalizes facility location (p. 4). - **D3 Problem class and data model**: Traffic and channels per BTS, modules of Q channels (e_i = ⌈d_i/Q⌉), CSSs of two types with traffic, module and BTS capacities, LEs with traffic and module capacities, BTSs that are basic, isolated or free, and forbidden connections removed (pp. 3–6); the hardness is attributed to the interaction of the 0-1 substructure with the integer module variables (p. 6); 14 CSELT instances with 85–120 BTSs, 6–25 CSSs and 3–5 LEs (p. 9).

- **D8 Contributions and limits**: A new model, cut families and a branch-and-cut that give tight lower and upper bounds and certify the partner's tabu search (p. 12); the argument that the ad hoc cuts drive the heuristics to better solutions is post hoc, resting on 11 of 14 improvements against CPLEX's one (p. 11); future: a better lower bound, to solve medium and large instances exactly (p. 12).

### S064 · A local branching heuristic for mixed‐integer programs with 2‐level variables, with an application to a telecommunication network design problem (Networks 2004, DOI 10.1002/net.20017)
**Read level**: full (all 23 file pages of the author preprint dated April 2003, revised October 2003; the figure on p. 15 is not in the text layer). First-page check: title and authors Fischetti, Polo, Scantamburlo match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Carlo Polo, Massimo Scantamburlo · **Position in trajectory**: the first follow-up of local branching (2003) by its author, taking up the metaheuristic framework that paper's concluding remarks anticipated (pp. 1, 3); the preliminary implementation is Polo's 2002 Padova master's thesis [19] (pp. 12, 18); the test problem comes from S135 (p. 9); Lodi supplied his local-branching code (p. 16).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The original local branching did worse than CPLEX on the UMTS instances, and this failure motivated a variant that detects and exploits MIP structure (p. 14). Target: MIPs whose binaries split into two levels, so that fixing the first level leaves an easier but still hard subproblem, as in facility location with side constraints or models with big-M rows (pp. 1, 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: DRT, a variable-neighbourhood scheme written entirely as linear constraints around a black-box MIP solver: refining fixes the first level (Δ₁(x, x̄) ≤ k₁, k₁ = 0 in the tests); tight refining adds second-level rings Δ₂(x, x̄) = k₂ for increasing k₂ (typically steps of 2 up to 10, p. 4; the test files use 1 to 4 in steps of 2, p. 22); diversification asks for any first feasible solution with k₁min ≤ Δ₁ ≤ k₁max; Δ₁ ≥ 1 is added statically as a tabu constraint (pp. 4–6, 13); DRT2 adds big diversifications when the search stalls (pp. 5–6). With no first-level variables DRT becomes a descent method on (9) (p. 5).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MIP (1)–(5) with a nonempty set of binaries (p. 2); first-level variables found by two row criteria, logical dependency (a binary whose range covers the row's maximum slack δ_max, (19)–(20)) and big-M (a coefficient above bigM_perc = 5 times the row average), or supplied by the modeller (pp. 8–9); test bed UMTS-1 to UMTS-20 with 50–300 BTSs, 334–12,307 columns and 10–66 first-level variables, root gaps 0.13–6.29% to the best known value (p. 12).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: ILOG CPLEX 7.0 as the external solver with per-phase time limits (initial search 60 min, diversification 30 min, refining 5 min, tight refining 3 min; in DRT2 10-minute diversifications and at most 4 big ones after 5 non-improving iterations) given as parameter files (pp. 21–23); pseudo-code in the appendix (pp. 20–21); Microsoft Visual C++ 6.0 on a Pentium III/1 GHz (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: One 3-hour run per instance: DRT against CPLEX 7.0 with MIPEmphasis = 1, DRT2, DRT with no first-level variables, and local branching at the UMTS setting of its paper; improvements δ and δ% = 100δ/(DRT value − root bound), which the paper calls relative to the initial MIP gap (pp. 9, 13–14); a preliminary table from Polo's thesis on a Pentium III/350 MHz also compares a CPLEX-based VNS and the problem-specific tabu search, and there DRT loses on UMTS-3 to all three (pp. 12–13).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Claimed: better solutions than all the alternatives on all the instances (pp. 2, 14), although Table 3 has ties (δ = 0 against CPLEX on UMTS-3 and UMTS-6, against local branching on UMTS-1 and UMTS-3) and DRT2 beats DRT on several large instances (p. 14); one application class only (p. 9); future: other network design problems and better detection of first-level variables (p. 16).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (steps 2–3, practice, joint): the critical sub-decisions (which neighbourhood, which first-level setting) become linear constraints added to the model and are explored by the unchanged black-box solver (pp. 3–5); the general codes need fewer than 2,000 lines against about 6,000 for the problem-specific tabu search (p. 12).
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (step 5, subtraction): the arm with no first-level variables removes the paper's key ingredient and performs poorly (p. 14), so the ingredient stays.
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: his own method's failure on a problem class is printed and becomes the next study (p. 14); a follow-up, not a re-test of a published claim.
- Method 3 ✗ contradiction (dated lapse, 2003–04): one run per instance, with no seeds and no placebo arm; gains as small as δ = 7 (0.02%) against local branching on UMTS-2 count toward outperforming on all the instances (p. 14). The skill dates seeds in his designs from 2009.
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: the baselines are the ones Method 4 asks for (the solver's heuristic emphasis, H5; the rival at its own published setting and in its author's implementation, pp. 13, 16), but ties are reported as wins (pp. 2, 14) and the preliminary table mixes an earlier implementation and another machine (p. 12).
- H2 (heuristic, not a method) ✅: DRT is local branching with the distance constraint split into a first-level and a second-level part (pp. 4–5).
- Research Taste warning sign 1 ✅ (2003 text): the conclusion criticizes building MIP models, dropping them when the solver fails, and turning to ad hoc move-based heuristics (p. 16).
- new-pattern candidate: "Detect the model's structure, then specialize the generic method" (first-level variables found from row slacks and big-M coefficients, pp. 7–9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Detect "first-level" binaries automatically: a binary with a negative coefficient whose range covers the row's maximum slack forces the other variables to their bounds when set to 0; a coefficient above 5 times the row's average flags a big-M switch (p. 8).
