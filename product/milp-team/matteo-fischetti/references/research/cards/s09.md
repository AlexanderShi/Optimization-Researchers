### S137 · Chasing first queens by integer programming (CPAIOR 2018, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-93031-2_16)
**Read level**: full (all 14 file pages of the author preprint dated 10 January 2018; the appendix solution lists, pp. 9–12, read as data). First-page check: title and authors Matteo Fischetti and Domenico Salvagnin match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Domenico Salvagnin · **Position in trajectory**: a puzzle put to him by Knuth (private communication, November 2017, ref. [16], p. 12); the 2019 arXiv paper D019 names this one as its preliminary version (D019 p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The decision version of n-queens is trivial and the counting version known only to n = 27 (pp. 1–2); the lexicographically first solution (OEIS A141843) is known only for n ≤ 55, with partial solutions beyond (p. 2). The entry is an outsider's question: Knuth asked whether ILP can compute open instances (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Four solvers on one clique model: a Gecode CP model with three alldifferent constraints and DFS (p. 5); ILP-ITER, n black-box ILPs, one per row, minimising the column index with earlier rows fixed (p. 5); ILP-TRUNC, a DFS that takes as the next value the rounded-up bound of the solver's root node (cuts and preprocessing on, node limit 0) and backtracks on infeasibility (p. 6); LEX-DFS, an enumeration on a lexicographic simplex with pruning, a "mini-cutloop", n-way row branching, a row-by-row lex simplex, a clique propagator and clique/odd-cycle separation (pp. 6–8). Encoding the order in the objective is set aside because of coefficient size (p. 2).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Pure binary x_ij with row and column equalities and diagonal at-most-one rows, all cliques (p. 2); from the stable-set view, the polynomial clique family (3)–(5) and odd-cycle inequalities separated as {0,1/2}-cuts (pp. 3–4). Testbed: every n from 21 to 60 (p. 8).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Lex-optimality is the device: an integral lex-optimal node LP is the lex-optimal integer solution of its subtree, and a lex-optimal value below 1 for the first unfixed variable proves it cannot be 1 there, so it is fixed to 0 (p. 7); the lex simplex is built on a black-box simplex by fixing nonzero-reduced-cost variables (pp. 6–7). An exact clique separator written as an auxiliary ILP found no further violated cliques (p. 4).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: CPLEX 12.7.1 and Gecode 5.1.0 on 24 identical Xeon E3-1220 V2 machines, 2-day limit, shifted geometric mean (shift 10 s) and solved counts: CP solves 21 of 40 sizes, each ILP method 35, shmean 2945.76 / 780.20 / 251.85 / 263.79 s (CP / ILP-ITER / ILP-TRUNC / LEX-DFS), n = 42, 46, 48 unsolved by all; one run per method and size, no seeds mentioned (p. 8; Table 2, p. 13).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Lex-first solutions for 22 open sizes from 56 to 115 are listed (pp. 9–12; every listed size above 60 is odd, my reading of the list); the conclusion claims two outcomes, open cases solved "sometimes in unexpectedly-short computing times" and unsolved cases as benchmarks for ILP technology (p. 9). Limits: even n "consistently harder", size no guide to difficulty (p. 8); how the n > 60 solutions were computed (method, time) is not stated. Future: explain the even/odd gap (p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the solver is the subroutine twice over: each row decision is a black-box ILP (ILP-ITER, p. 5), and the solver truncated at its root, with preprocessing and cuts, is the bounding oracle of a custom DFS (ILP-TRUNC, p. 6); the exact clique separator was tried as an auxiliary ILP and found nothing new (p. 4). [joint]
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the subtraction test is run and reported against the authors' own extras, cut separation halving the nodes but switching it off giving a faster code (246 s, p. 9), yet the cut version stays in Table 2 (p. 13); the finding is reported, not applied.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: the lexicographic simplex of his Gomory-cut studies (refs [3], [22], pp. 12, 14) is reused not to stabilise a cut loop but as the node LP of an enumeration whose lex-optimality gives pruning and fixing (p. 7).
- new-pattern candidate: "Lexicography as a structural device" (lex simplex as node LP, p. 7; again in D019 pp. 6–7, 9–10).
- new-pattern candidate: "Open public records as target and yardstick" (open OEIS values, pp. 1–2; new solutions listed for checking, pp. 9–12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a lexicographic objective over a permutation, solve one ILP per position with the prefix fixed, n ILPs instead of n² yes/no tests (p. 5).
- Use the solver's root bound, with its preprocessing and cuts but no branching, as the value guess of your own DFS and backtrack on infeasibility; plain LP bounds were correct and fast but backtracked too often (p. 6).
- Before coding a separator, run an exact auxiliary-ILP separator once to see whether the cheap polynomial family already finds everything (p. 4).
- Print the ablation that hurts your own ingredient, with the reason (cuts halve nodes but cut node throughput) and the faster time (p. 9).

**Quotes**:
- "Note that this is not equivalent to solving the LP relaxation of the ILP, as cutting planes and (most importantly) preprocessing play a crucial role here." (p. 6)
- "As for the advanced techniques implemented in LEX-DFS, we have to admit that for some of them the overall effect was rather disappointing." (p. 9)

### D018 · Embedded hyper-parameter tuning by Simulated Annealing (arXiv 2019, arXiv:1906.01504)
**Read level**: full (all 9 pages of arXiv 1906.01504v1, 4 June 2019, marked "Preprint. Under review."; figures read through their captions). First-page check: title and authors Matteo Fischetti and Matteo Stringher match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Matteo Stringher · **Position in trajectory**: the precursor of S110 (OLA 2021), which reports the same experiments with an identical Table 1 (p. 7; S110 p. 10).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: SGD hyper-parameters are set by hand, rules of thumb, grids or random trials (pp. 1–2); SA had been used for training only where gradients are missing (p. 2). The entry is to search a discrete neighbourhood of gradient moves with a metaheuristic, so that tuning happens inside one SGD run instead of an external loop (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea**: First a naive derivative-free SSA (random ±εΔ(w) moves, Metropolis acceptance), reported as a failure: no overfitting but poor accuracy, since random directions cancel out in high dimension (pp. 3–4). Then SGD-SA: each minibatch step draws a learning rate at random from a discrete set H, which should include unusually large rates, and the Metropolis test on the minibatch loss accepts or rejects the step; temperature cooled per epoch (pp. 4–5, Algorithm 2). Momentum and Nesterov left out to keep the setting "simple and clean" (p. 5).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: A differentiable loss or proxy is required (p. 2); CIFAR-10 split 50,000 training / 10,000 test, VGG16 and ResNet34 (p. 5). Algorithm 2 returns the best w on the validation set at the end of each epoch (p. 5), while Table 1 is captioned "Final validation accuracy" (p. 7); no validation set separate from the 10,000 test examples is described (p. 5; my reading).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: No proofs; a first-order Taylor argument motivates trying both ±εΔ(w) in the naive move (p. 3). Random numbers drive discrete decisions (rate choice, acceptance), so the search path depends strongly on the seed, unlike the initialisation seed of plain SGD (p. 5).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: PyTorch, 100 epochs, batch 512, one TITAN Xp; baseline Scheduled-SGD without momentum (η = 0.1/0.01/0.001 over 30/40/30 epochs), SGD-SA with α = 0.8, T0 = 1 and 14 rates; 10 seeds per arm reported seed by seed: lower validation loss on every seed, higher accuracy except seeds 3, 4, 6 on ResNet34, best-of-10 better by 1–2% (pp. 5–7); my arithmetic from Table 1: mean accuracy 86.16 vs 85.11 (VGG16) and 82.24 vs 82.11 (ResNet34), SGD-SA's ResNet34 runs spanning 76.31–84.11 (p. 7).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: A training scheme used as embedded hyper-parameter tuning, shown on one test problem (p. 8). Limits stated: slower convergence on the training set and seed-dependent curves (p. 6). Future: a differentiable objective for the moves and a black-box one for acceptance, to favour robust solutions; other metaheuristics (tabu, VNS, GA) (p. 8).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the spread is measured on both arms (Scheduled-SGD's seed curves "almost overlapping", p. 6) and turned into a resource, the seed as a hyper-parameter for an external loop (p. 6, quoted below); but no placebo arm, and the best-of-10 gain (p. 6) is smaller than SGD-SA's own seed spread on ResNet34 (Table 1, p. 7; my arithmetic).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: both arms stripped of momentum, so the idea is compared and not the code (p. 5; step 4), but the baseline is "quite basic" (p. 5) rather than what a practitioner would run (step 2), and the selection rule (best epoch on the validation set, p. 5) and the reported set are not separated (p. 5; my reading).
- new-pattern candidate: "Metaheuristic acceptance over a method's own moves" (the neighbourhood is the gradient method's own steps at different rates; the metaheuristic only accepts or rejects; pp. 4–5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Report the naive version first, with its failure and the diagnosis that motivates the real design (random directions cancel out in high dimension) (pp. 3–4).
- Put deliberately risky candidates (unusually large learning rates) in the move set and let the acceptance test discard them, instead of tuning them away beforehand (p. 4).
- Run both arms over the same number of seeds and print a seed-by-seed table, losses included (pp. 5–7).

**Quotes**:
- "An important feature of our method is that H can (actually, should) contain unusually large learning rates, as the corresponding moves can be discarded by the Metropolis’ criterion if they deteriorate the objective function too much." (p. 4)
- "In our view, this dependency on the seed is in fact a positive feature of SGD-SA, in that it allows one to treat the seed as a single (quite powerful) hyper-parameter to be randomly tuned in an external loop." (p. 6)

### D019 · Finding First and Most-Beautiful Queens by Integer Programming (arXiv 2019, arXiv:1907.08246)
**Read level**: full (all 14 pages of arXiv 1907.08246v1, 18 July 2019, "Preprint. Under review."; appendix solution lists, pp. 10–13, read as data). First-page check: title and authors Matteo Fischetti and Domenico Salvagnin match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Domenico Salvagnin · **Position in trajectory**: extension of S137, named as its preliminary version presented at CPAIOR, Delft, 5–8 June 2018 (p. 2); §§2–4 repeat S137 nearly word for word (pp. 2–8; S137 pp. 2–9).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: As S137 (pp. 1–2), plus a second question from Knuth (private communication, June 2018): the "most-beautiful" placement, minimising the multiset of distances to the centre, known only for n ≤ 48 (p. 2; ref. [18], p. 14). Entry for the new part: sorting cells by cost and taking the lex-minimal solution is correct with distinct costs but wrong with ties (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two additions. A pure cutting-plane method on the lex simplex: Gomory cuts with lex simplex converge finitely, but a preliminary implementation was numerically unstable here, so the cut family is replaced by combinatorial "lexicographic nogoods" (10) built from the first fractional variable and the earlier variables at 1 (pp. 6–7). For the bottleneck variant, Algorithm 1 scans the distinct costs in decreasing order, solves one ILP per level minimising the number of chosen cells at that cost, and adds an equality fixing that count (p. 9); a preprocessing step jumps 100 levels at a time by asking the solver for any solution avoiding all cells with d_ij ≥ D_{k+100}, with an upper cutoff of 0.01 to abort it early (p. 10).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: The same pure 0-1 clique model (pp. 2–3); costs d_ij = (2i − n − 1)² + (2j − n − 1)² with many ties, fingerprint sorted non-increasingly (p. 9); the bottleneck scheme is stated for lexicographic min-max problems in general (p. 9).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: The nogood loop converges because each cut forces a strict worsening of the lexicographic objective (no cycling) and the cuts are finitely many (p. 7). Algorithm 1 fixes counts, not cells, since the optimum at a level is not unique (pp. 9–10); levels with z* = 0 let the cells be fixed to 0 (p. 9), and the loop stops when the fixed counts reach n (p. 10). Version note: the exponent of objective (1) reads ni + j here (p. 2) and i + j in S137 (S137 p. 2).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: §4 repeats S137's Table 1 unchanged (pp. 7–8); the nogood method is reported only in words, slower than LEX-DFS and solving "almost as many" boards (p. 7); most-beautiful solutions for n = 16, 32, …, 176 with notebook wall-clock times from 0.2 s to 38,757.7 s, not monotone in n (pp. 12–13); for n = 128, 397 of 1,464 cost levels give z* = 0 (p. 10).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Adds a convergent pure cutting-plane method, "theoretically interesting" but not competitive, and new most-beautiful solutions for n > 48 (pp. 10, 12). Limits: no numbers for the nogood method (p. 7); the bottleneck scheme's speed rests on the solver's parametric heuristics (p. 10). Future: the even/odd gap, lexicographic variants as a topic, nogoods for general binary programs (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the bottleneck variant is a sequence of ILPs on one model that relies on the solver's "parametric heuristics" (p. 10); the 100-level jump uses the solver as a feasibility oracle, aborted by a tiny cutoff (p. 10). [joint]
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: with the loop already lexicographic, the instability is blamed on the cut family and Gomory cuts are swapped for combinatorial nogoods (p. 7), so the oracle changes and the loop stays, the reverse of Method 2's step 3.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the S137 subtraction finding is repeated (switching cuts off is faster, p. 7) and the cut version kept in the table (p. 8).
- new-pattern candidate: "Lexicography as a structural device" (lex simplex as node LP and cut loop, lex bottleneck by count-fixing; pp. 6–7, 9–10).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a lexicographic bottleneck objective with tied costs, solve one ILP per cost level minimising the count at that level, and fix the count by an equality rather than fixing cells (pp. 9–10).
- Skip long runs of zero-count levels by asking the solver for any solution that avoids a whole block of levels, aborted by a tiny objective cutoff, and fix the block if it succeeds (p. 10).
- When Gomory cuts are numerically unstable in a lexicographic loop, try a combinatorial cut that excludes the current lex prefix; finiteness follows from the strict lex worsening per cut (p. 7).

**Quotes**:
- "Unfortunately, a preliminary implementation of the method proved to be numerically unstable on our n-queens models." (p. 7)
- "Though not competitive in our case, this method is theoretically interesting and can be possibly extended to more general binary integer programs." (p. 10)

### S038 · Interdiction Games and Monotonicity, with Application to Knapsack Problems (INFORMS J. Comput. 2019, DOI 10.1287/ijoc.2018.0831)
**Read level**: full (all 28 pages of the author preprint titled "Interdiction Games and Monotonicity", without the journal subtitle; Figures 2–3 survive only as axis debris, Tables 1–6 read). First-page check: main title and authors Fischetti, Ljubić, Monaci, Sinnl match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ivana Ljubić, Michele Monaci, Markus Sinnl · **Position in trajectory**: Vienna bilevel period (WWTF project ICT15-014, p. 22); benchmarked against the group's own general bilevel solver MIX++ (Fischetti et al. 2016b, pp. 20–21).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Single-level reformulations of interdiction games need big-M coefficients that make the cuts weak (p. 7); for knapsack interdiction the tight choice M_j = d_j had been used without proof by Caprara et al. (2016) and Ralphs (2015) (pp. 3, 9). The entry is the structural property that removes big-M, downward monotonicity of the follower (Assumption 1, p. 3), shown to hold for knapsack, matching and packing interdiction and for follower models with extra non-interdictable variables (facility location, prize-collecting TSP) (pp. 2, 4–7).
- **D2 Core idea**: Project all follower variables out and branch-and-cut on (w, x) with interdiction cuts (43) whose coefficients are the profits, separated by solving the follower with profits d_j(1 − x*_j) (pp. 3, 8–9, 13); add modified and lifted cuts (pp. 10–12), static dominance inequalities x_s ≤ x_i (pp. 12–13), and a primal heuristic that adds invalid leader constraints (54) making the follower's answer analytic, solves the compact MILP for an upper bound, then imposes the cutoff (60) and a disjunction (61) forcing (54) to be violated (pp. 14–15). Earlier uses re-solved a MILP after each cut; here cuts enter one branch-and-cut (p. 9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Min-max game (1)–(4) with binary interdiction variables, follower LP or MILP, finite bounds u (p. 2), follower feasible and bounded for every policy (p. 3); monotonicity leaves knapsack interdiction Σ2-hard (p. 3); Theorem 1: monotone iff some formulation has Q_N ≥ 0 (p. 3); binary interdicted follower variables for the §3.3 cuts (Assumption 2, p. 10). Data: 360 KIP instances (CCLW 50, TRS 150, DeNegre 160) and 144 new multidimensional instances from SAC-94, split -0/-50/-100 by which constraints the leader owns (pp. 15–16).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Validity by construction: zero the interdicted entries of any follower point and invoke monotonicity (Theorem 2, p. 9); maximal follower solutions dominate (Theorem 4, p. 10), favoured by an ε = 0.001 perturbation of zero profits in separation (p. 13); a three-node assignment-interdiction counterexample shows the cuts invalid without monotonicity (p. 10); item-flipping arguments give the modified (disjunctive) and lifted cuts (Theorems 5–6, pp. 11–12); an exchange argument keeps one optimum under the dominance inequalities (Theorem 7, pp. 12–13). Exact separation (knapsack DP or MILP) is needed only at integer points; fractional points get greedy or root-node MILP separation (p. 13).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Python on CPLEX 12.6 defaults, 3,600 s, single thread, Xeon E3-1220V2 (p. 15); cumulative ablation over six settings by root-gap and time-to-optimality profiles (pp. 16–18); all 360 KIP solved within 84 s, 4 above 10 s (p. 18); CP/CCLW and TRS times copied from other machines (pp. 18, 20), MIX++ run on the same machine with four threads (pp. 20–21); 10 of 144 multidimensional instances unsolved in an hour, all solved with longer limits up to about 30,000 s (pp. 21–22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Generalised cuts with proofs, three new cut families, a heuristic preprocessing, and "by far the largest computational study on interdiction games" (p. 4). Limits: the facility-location and prize-collecting applications are modelled but not computed, as a customized code is expected to be needed (p. 15); CCLW is faster on several easy instances (e.g. 50-5: 0.60 s vs 4.82 s; Table 1, p. 19); MIX++ wins once (pb5-50, p. 21). Future: the non-monotone case and customised codes for the §2 applications (p. 22).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the thin move is a Benders-like projection of every follower variable, made tight by a structural assumption instead of big-M (pp. 3, 8–9). [joint]
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the ablation is cumulative, not by subtraction: setting M leaves runtime "nearly identical" to the basic setting (p. 18) yet stays in the default MHDLP, which is never run without it (pp. 16–18).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: rivals' published times from other machines (pp. 18, 20), the lapse the skill already dates to the bilevel work; but MIX++ ran on the same machine with four threads against one (pp. 20–21), its single win is printed, and it is called "not specialized for interdiction" (p. 21).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the primal heuristic is an auxiliary compact MILP solved by the same solver, whose value then serves as a cutoff together with a reverse disjunction (pp. 14–15); CPLEX parameters left at defaults (p. 15).
- new-pattern candidate: "Restrict, solve, then exclude" (solve the problem restricted by invalid constraints, then add its cutoff and a disjunction forbidding the restriction, pp. 14–15; the same shape as local branching's two branches, my reading).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a cut with tight coefficients circulates without proof, name the assumption that makes it valid, prove it by a feasibility-preserving construction, and give a smallest counterexample outside the assumption (pp. 3, 9–10).
- Separate exactly only at integer points, where correctness needs it, and heuristically at fractional ones to raise node throughput (pp. 13, 18).
- Perturb the zero objective coefficients of a separation problem by ε·d_j so that it returns maximal, dominating solutions (p. 13).
- Add invalid constraints that make the inner problem trivial, solve the compact MILP as a heuristic, then add its cutoff and a disjunction excluding the restricted region; if the result is infeasible, the heuristic value is optimal (pp. 14–15, 21).

**Quotes**:
- "However, in most of the cases, the quality of derived cuts is not satisfactory, since large big-M coefficients (or indicator constraints) must be used." (p. 7)
- "The explanation of this behavior is that a much high node-throughput can be achieved in branch-and-bound when using heuristic separation, while slightly improved bounds (M) may not be crucial to quickly solve an instance to optimality." (p. 18)

### S078 · Heuristic algorithms for the wind farm cable routing problem (Applied Energy 2020, DOI 10.1016/j.apenergy.2020.115617)
**Read level**: full (all 40 file pages: the DTU Orbit cover, p. 1, and the peer-reviewed manuscript dated 23 June 2019, pp. 2–40; the figures survive only as captions). First-page check: title and authors Davide Cazzaro, Martina Fischetti, Matteo Fischetti match (p. 2; cover p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Davide Cazzaro and Martina Fischetti (Vattenfall BA Wind; Martina Fischetti also DTU, p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: the wind-farm line with Vattenfall (its 2019 Edelman finalist status is cited, p. 3); Matteo Fischetti is third author, and the references write both Fischettis as "Fischetti, M.", so which earlier wind papers are his is not settled by this text (pp. 35–37).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Cable routing is NP-hard and hard for MILP solvers in practice, while preliminary what-if analyses need good solutions fast, which motivated heuristics "not using MILP technology" (p. 3); electrical infrastructure is 4–5% of CAPEX (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Six heuristics on a penalised cost over a successor representation (pp. 5–7): a new construction, Sweep, which orders turbines by angle around the substation, cuts them into groups of TPG turbines (a bin-packing view, trivial with unit power) and runs Prim-Dijkstra with inter-group edges forbidden, in multistart over all O(n²) parameter combinations (pp. 11–14); and SA, Tabu Search, VNS, ACO and GA, all but ACO warm-started from Sweep, with 1-opt moves evaluated incrementally in O(n) instead of O(n²) (pp. 10–12, 14–28). Crossings are penalised, so their real cost could be priced in, which "would be prohibitive in a MILP model" (p. 7).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: One offshore substation, no obstacles; an anti-arborescence with one outgoing cable per turbine, cable types by capacity and unit cost, at most C substation cables, no crossings, unit power after normalisation (pp. 3–5). Training set: 24 real instances from five farms [26] (p. 8). Test set: 220 layouts kept from 300 generated cases (n ∈ {50, 80, 120}, 5 sites, 4 turbine types, 5 wind scenarios), turbine positions from a 1-hour proximity-search MILP matheuristic [17–19], 80 infeasible layouts dropped (p. 9); three substation positions, and C = Cmin + 1 by default "to have difficult instances" (pp. 9–10).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: No proofs; Prim-Dijkstra with the cheapest cable gives a rough lower bound (p. 11), used to normalise SA's temperature (p. 17); Tabu list on arcs (p. 18); VNS restarting from the incumbent (pp. 18–20); ACO rebuilt from Nedlin [43] in three readings because details were missing, keeping the one judged closest to the original (pp. 21–23); GA with successor encoding and Kruskal repair beats Prüfer numbers, and Sweep solutions are kept out of its initial population (pp. 24–26).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Parameters tuned only on the 24 training instances by performance profiles (pp. 8, 16–28); 10-minute single-thread runs on a Xeon E5-2623 v3, the limit read off a 1-hour cost trace on one 100-turbine instance (pp. 28–29); training compared with the 1-hour MILP matheuristic of [26], test set only among the six heuristics (pp. 29–34), with VNS best on 203 of 220 (p. 31); no seeds or repeated runs are mentioned (text search).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Sweep, five tuned metaheuristics, and a 220-instance test set (pp. 2, 35); ranking VNS, then Tabu; GA and SA rarely improve Sweep; ACO hardly reaches feasibility, "a critical weakness of the method" (pp. 30–31). Limits: no heuristic beats the matheuristic on training instances (p. 29); VNS without Sweep beats Sweep alone on 160 of 220 but never with 120 turbines (p. 32). Inconsistency: the test set is "publicly available" in the abstract and conclusion (pp. 2, 35) but "available, on request, from the authors" in the introduction (p. 4). Future: priced crossings, a multi-thread GA, obstacles and Steiner nodes, joint layout and cable design (p. 35).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: "To overcome the risk of overtuning", tuning and testing use separate instance sets (p. 8, step 5); the method of choice has "basically just one parameter" (p. 19) and is preferred as "easier to implement and tune" (p. 34), while Tabu's drop without Sweep is put down to "the many parameters" (p. 32). [joint; applied partners first]
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: MILP was the earlier route ([26], whose matheuristic is the reference, pp. 7–8, 29); the requirement of fast what-if answers turns the non-MILP heuristic into the lesson (p. 3), as in Method 6's step 5.
- Method 3 ✗ contradiction: five randomised metaheuristics are ranked from runs for which no seed or repetition is mentioned (pp. 28–34; text search), unlike the seeded designs the skill records in his own papers since 2009.
- new-pattern candidate: "Warm-start ablation" (every improvement heuristic rerun without the constructive start, and VNS with and without Sweep compared directly, pp. 31–33).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Tune every parameter on a literature training set and report on a separate, new, realistic test set (pp. 8–9).
- Fix the time limit by tracing each method's cost over a long run on one hard instance and cutting where all but one have stopped improving (p. 28).
- Rerun each improvement heuristic without the constructive warm start to separate what the construction contributes from what the improvement phase adds (pp. 31–33).
- When re-implementing a rival from an underspecified paper, implement each plausible reading, keep the best and say so (pp. 21–23).

### S130 · An integrated local-search/set-partitioning refinement heuristic for the capacitated vehicle routing problem (Math. Program. Comput. 2022, DOI 10.1007/s12532-022-00224-2)
**Read level**: full (all 27 pages of the MPC manuscript dated 14 June 2022; the box plots of Figs. 2–4 survive only as labels). First-page check: title and authors Francesco Cavaliere, Emilio Bendotti, Matteo Fischetti match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Francesco Cavaliere (first author), Emilio Bendotti · **Position in trajectory**: Padova applied-MIP period; lists the De Franceschi–Fischetti–Toth ILP refinement heuristic among earlier set-partitioning heuristics (p. 4) and uses a per-customer column rule akin to Caprara–Fischetti–Toth's for set covering (p. 13); author order not alphabetical, student first (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Exact decomposition solves only CVRPs with a few hundred customers (p. 2); the entry is a "powerful (yet time consuming)" refinement heuristic, meant to run on top of a state-of-the-art heuristic and judged by improving top-quality solutions (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: LS-CGH rounds: (1) a reengineered, parallel LKH-3 ("newLKH": reentrant thread-local code, balanced runs, cache-first cost function, a penalty function restricted to the routes a move touches, a Flip with an update step, SA acceptance on top of LKH's kicks) fills a route pool (pp. 4–12, 14–15); (2) LP pricing on the SP relaxation filters the pool, from the cheapest 8,000 routes, adding routes below 0.8·c_min plus the ten smallest-reduced-cost routes per customer, capped at 16,000 (pp. 12–13); (3) CPLEX 12.10 solves the restricted SP with an aggressive time limit and early polishing, and the next round starts from the best solution (pp. 4, 13). SP is kept over set covering, which solved "significantly slower" (p. 13).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Symmetric CVRP with a fixed number of routes, SP formulation (1a)–(1d) (pp. 1–2); extends to variants with independent routes that LKH supports (pp. 13–14). Data: the 57 largest X instances, after Queiroga et al., and the 10 Belgian XXL instances up to 30,000 customers (p. 15).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Profiling drives the redesign: the penalty test rejects more than 95% of proposed tours, so rejection is made cheap and a rare update step restores the route data (pp. 10, 15); SA with trial- and time-based temperatures, taking the minimum, scale factors fixed "after some preliminary computational tests" (pp. 11–12); the route pool keys routes by customer set and keeps the best sequence (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Xeon E3-1220 V2 with up to 4 threads, gap to BKS (p. 15); speedups on identical trajectories over 5 seeds, 1.73–5.41 on 57-X and up to 56.01 on XXL (pp. 16–17); single 200-minute runs of original LKH (Helsgaun's default parameters), newLKH and LS-CGH, average gaps 1.16% / 0.48% / 0.32% (pp. 18–19); 10-seed box plots on 9 instances (pp. 18–21); FILO's best of 10 runs refined from 0.101% to 0.076% (57-X) and 0.092% to 0.079% (XXL) (pp. 22–24).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: LS-CGH, an open faster LKH-3 (github.com/c4v4/LKH3), gains over FILO, and 105 CVRPLIB best-known-solution improvements on 30 instances, 14 unbeaten in March 2021 (pp. 7, 22). Losses visible in the tables but not discussed: LS-CGH is worse than newLKH on 9 of 57 single runs (Table 2, p. 19) and leaves FILO's solution unchanged on 20 of 57 (Table 3, p. 23) (my counts). The record runs had no time limit and were stopped by hand after 24 hours without improvement (p. 22). Future: CVRPTW, CARP, VRPB with LKH as route generator (p. 24).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the critical sub-decision, which pooled routes to combine, is an auxiliary SP MIP solved by the black-box solver with an aggressive limit and its polishing (p. 13), and the formulation is chosen by what the solver solves faster (SP over set covering, p. 13). [joint; student first author]
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: speed is isolated from search, since the modified LKH visits the same solution sequence as the original under one seed in single-thread mode (p. 16); each arm adds one layer (LKH, newLKH, LS-CGH) under the same time limit and threads (p. 18).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: his own old devices go to a student with new tools (a column rule akin to Caprara–Fischetti–Toth's, p. 13, and the ILP-refinement lineage, p. 4, on LKH-3 and CPLEX 12.10); a revival, not a re-test.

- new-pattern candidate: "Set-partitioning recombination of a heuristic's by-products" (pp. 4, 12–13).

**Transferable techniques**:

### S168 · A Vehicle Routing heuristic based on accelerated LKH-3 coupled with Set Partitioning (12th DIMACS Implementation Challenge 2022, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (all 7 pages; no printed page numbers). First-page check: title and authors Francesco Cavaliere, Matteo Fischetti, Keld Helsgaun match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Francesco Cavaliere (corresponding author), Keld Helsgaun (author of LKH-3) · **Position in trajectory**: the challenge entry of the S130 line; team "Vavavuma!", solver LKHSP, CVRP and VRPTW tracks, code at github.com/c4v4/LKH-SP (p. 1).

- **D8 Contributions and limits**: A generic refining heuristic, with new best-known solutions for 6 CVRP and 134 VRPTW instances before Phase I (p. 6).
