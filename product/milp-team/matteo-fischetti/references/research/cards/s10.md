### S100 · Safe distancing in the time of COVID-19 (European Journal of Operational Research 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2021.07.010)
**Read level**: full (all 24 file pages: repository cover p. 1, accepted author manuscript pp. 2–22, references pp. 21–24; the figures are not in the text layer, so only numbers stated in the prose are used). First-page check: title and the authors Martina Fischetti, Matteo Fischetti and Jakob Stoustrup match (pp. 1–2); the manuscript is dated 3 February 2021, revised 30 April and 16 June 2021 (p. 2).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Martina Fischetti (Vattenfall BA Wind, first author), Jakob Stoustrup (Aalborg University) (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: applied MIP of the 2020–2024 period, the second author's share partly funded by a PRIN project (p. 22); a preprint circulated from May 2020, and the second author (Matteo Fischetti) presented it at INFORMS 2020 as IFORS Distinguished Lecturer (p. 5). The model and the matheuristic come from the first author's wind-farm work with Monaci (2016) (pp. 5, 8–9, 23), so this card is evidence of habits only, as the skill's Honest Boundary already says of the distancing work.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Distancing rules make restaurants, offices and theatres place tables or seats under a minimum distance, a kind of circle-packing problem (p. 3); the OR work cited packs as many as possible or assigns seats on airplanes and in transit, by MILP, p-dispersion or circle packing (p. 3). The entry: fix the number of facilities and minimize the total risk of infection (pp. 3–4), and test the intuition that maximal, equal spacing is safest (p. 4), through a parallel with offshore wind-farm layout, the first author's professional field (p. 2; pp. 5–7).
- **D2 Core idea**: Read wake interference as virus-spread interference, both falling with distance (p. 7), and reuse the wind-farm layout MILP as it is (pp. 8–9): binaries on sampled positions, one conflict row per pair closer than Dmin, and one continuous w_i per position that collects the interference caused by i through a big-M row with M_i = Σ_{j:(i,j)∉E_I} I_ij (p. 9); it is solved by the first author's matheuristic (p. 9), and Double-Click's wind-farm optimization software is thanked for its use (p. 22). Variants are data of the same model: maximum packing with Nmin = 0, Nmax = +∞ and P_i = 10^3 (p. 15), a fixed count with Nmin = Nmax = 30 (p. 17), and family groups as configuration variables with one cardinality row per group size, (14) (pp. 20–21).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: 0-1 location with a cardinality row (4), conflict-edge packing rows (5), big-M linking rows (6) and w_i ≥ 0 (pp. 8–9). The interference I_ij is an input left to the decision maker (pp. 10, 22); five forms are compared: linear d_max − d, Gaussian with σ² = 1, 1/d and 1/d^1.5 (from Mittal et al.), and 1/d³ (from Jones et al., aerosol spread uniformly in a sphere) (pp. 10–11). Constant factors drop out, so the optimum does not depend on emission rate or exposure time (p. 10); risk is additive over pairs (p. 22). Toy runs set Dmin = 0, P_i = 0 and Nmin = Nmax = k, so that only the objective spreads the facilities (p. 11). Theatre case: 506 configurations and 11,726 forbidden pairs under the Norwegian seating rules, with distances taken between middle seats (pp. 20–21).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: No new algorithm and no proof. The modelling device is the single-index aggregated w_i, which the 2016 wind-farm paper found to solve larger instances than two-index models (p. 9). One analytic check rejects the linear form (9): with 3 facilities on a segment, two go to the endpoints and the third can sit anywhere without changing the value (p. 10). Solver, settings and stopping rule of the matheuristic are not given, and layouts are called optimal (pp. 4, 13) without a gap or bound anywhere in the text.
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Illustrations, not a benchmark: 10 facilities on a line and 20 in a 10 m × 10 m square under the five forms, with induced minimum distances of 2.57, 1.80, 2.40 and 2.50 m for (10)–(13), and 1/d^k for k ∈ {1.5, 2, 3} giving essentially the same layout (pp. 11–14); real cases against the layout a practitioner uses: 36 tables against a manual 3 m grid's 30 at the Brus brewpub, Copenhagen (p. 16), 211 umbrellas against the owner's 203 at Bagni Alberoni, Venice (p. 18), and a tables-against-risk Pareto curve, risk under 20% of its maximum up to 22 tables, about 60% at 30 and 80% at 33 (p. 17); no solver, hardware, run time, gap or seed appears in the text (pp. 2–22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: the analogy that lets a wind-farm optimizer place facilities under distancing (pp. 2, 21), and the lesson that the safest layouts are not equally spaced but load the border of the area (pp. 2, 4, 13). Limits stated concretely: optimized layouts look counter-intuitive and are hard to explain to a non-technical audience (p. 21); pairwise-additive risk excludes risk that is a nonlinear function of the whole layout (p. 22); the paper does not decide which interference form is right (pp. 10, 22). Future: direction-dependent interference from air conditioning or Plexiglas (p. 21).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (weak; joint, and the model is the first author's, with Monaci, 2016): one aggregated continuous variable per position replaces two-index interference variables, kept because it solves larger instances (pp. 8–9).
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: the general-purpose machinery tried first is an existing MILP model plus matheuristic from another application, reused unchanged; each new variant is data (P_i, Nmin, Nmax, Dmin) or a change of what a binary stands for (configurations), not new code (pp. 9, 11, 15, 17, 20–21). No run of a plain solver on the plain model is reported.
- Methods 2–5: no bearing; the paper reports no computational protocol beyond the practitioner's layout as the comparison (pp. 2–22).
- Outside the numbered methods: Research Heuristic 1 ✅ (the belief that maximal equal spacing is safest is put to the test, p. 4, and overturned, pp. 2, 13); Heuristic 3 ✅ (a solved twin problem, wind-farm layout, supplies model and solver, pp. 2, 5–7, 21); Heuristic 7 ✅ (the abstract closes with *A lesson learned*, p. 2). The Honest Boundary's authorship note holds: the wind-farm expertise and the model are the first author's (pp. 5, 8).
- new-pattern candidate: Toy-instance check of a modelling choice. A candidate objective is rejected because the smallest instance with an obvious answer has a degenerate optimum (3 facilities on a segment, p. 10), and the surviving forms are compared on 10 points on a line and 20 in a square before any real case (pp. 11–14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before trusting a penalty or interference function, solve the smallest instance whose right answer you know and drop forms whose optimum is degenerate there (3 facilities on a segment, p. 10); then compare the survivors on 1-D and 2-D toy layouts (pp. 11–14).
- Model pairwise interference with one continuous variable per location and one big-M row, M_i = Σ I_ij over the compatible j, instead of one variable per pair (pp. 8–9).
- Express problem variants as data of one model: maximum count with Nmin = 0, Nmax = +∞ and a large P_i (10^3) (p. 15); a fixed count with Nmin = Nmax (p. 17); Dmin = 0 and P_i = 0 to see what the objective alone does (p. 11).
- For grouped entities (families), let each binary stand for a feasible configuration, precompute the incompatible pairs, and add one cardinality row per group size (pp. 20–21).
- Drop constant factors from the objective so the optimum does not depend on parameters nobody knows, and test robustness to the functional form (1/d^k for k ∈ {1.5, 2, 3}) (pp. 10, 14).
- Compare with the layout the practitioner actually uses, on the same area and rule (203 against 211 umbrellas at 4 m) (p. 18).

**Quotes**:
- "The intuition would tell us that, placing facilities at the maximum distance one from each other would ensure the safest layout. But is this really the case?" (p. 4)
- "using a single index variable wi allows this model to solve larger instances compared with equivalent two-index models in the literature" (p. 9)

### S195 · Thinning out facilities: a Benders decomposition approach for the uncapacitated facility location problem with separable convex costs (author report 2015, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (all 31 file pages: text pp. 1–23 and 26–28, Tables 1–5 pp. 22 and 24–27, references pp. 28–31; Figures 1 and 2 survive only as axis labels and cut counts, pp. 16, 18). First-page check: the authors Matteo Fischetti, Ivana Ljubić and Markus Sinnl and the main title *Thinning out facilities* match, dated April 1, 2015 (p. 1). The subtitle does not match the work list's Scholar title, *Thinning out facilities: Lagrange, Benders, and*, which is the title of the MIP 2015 talk (Chicago, June 2015), *Thinning out facilities: Lagrange, Benders, and (the curse of) Kelley* (RESOURCES.md row 168). The work's note names this file (thinning_out_facilities.pdf, 2015), so it is carded as that report; see the batch problems.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ivana Ljubić, Markus Sinnl (University of Vienna) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the thinning-out line (2014–2020); an earlier version of S011, ten months before that preprint (February 11, 2016, per card S011) and two months before the MIP 2015 talk. The Steiner-tree paper of the 11th DIMACS challenge is cited as its starting point (p. 2; ref. [9], p. 29), and the first author's share was funded in part by a Padova project on exploiting randomness in MILP (p. 28). Same paper as S011: count the two once.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The huge number of allocation variables makes UFL hard for general-purpose MILP solvers, so Lagrangian methods are generally preferred (pp. 1–2). The entry is a transfer: the Steiner-tree work [9] had shown that known MILP models can be thinned out with a large boost, and the same is tried on UFL (p. 2); Benders for linear UFL is folklore, mentioned by Magnanti and Wong together with the polynomial size of its cut family (after Cornuéjols, Nemhauser and Wolsey), then forgotten for 30+ years without a computational study (pp. 2, 6); qUFL enters as a standard MINLP test problem and a CBLIB member (p. 2). The conclusions add the practical motive: a sound Lagrangian code is far from trivial, hence a simpler approach on an off-the-shelf MILP solver (p. 26).
- **D2 Core idea**: Remove the x_ij, the bottleneck for MIP solvers, and bound one w_j per customer (fat) or a single w_sum (slim) by (generalized) Benders cuts separated inside a stock branch-and-cut (pp. 5, 12–13); for linear costs the Benders model is compact, |I| + |J| variables and |J|(|I| + 1) constraints (p. 2). Changed: the formulation, by projection, and the root cut loop, by in-out stabilization (pp. 14–15). Kept: CPLEX 12.6 for node LPs, heuristics, general cuts, branching and the cut pool (p. 13), and Kelley's loop in the tree (p. 17).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: UFL with c_ij ≥ 0 (∞ when an allocation is forbidden), the integrality of x being redundant (p. 3); qUFL with unit demands, c_ij > 0 and costs c_ij x_ij², whose compact baseline is the perspective SOC model (5)–(11), chosen for its tighter relaxation (pp. 4–5). Master with at least 2 open facilities: the 1-facility case is preprocessed, and all 1- and 2-facility solutions are enumerated for linear instances with n ≤ 1000 (p. 5). The GB cut assumes convex, twice-differentiable slaves on a closed convex set and constraint qualifications (p. 6); the QP derivation assumes y* > 0, the general case being handled numerically (pp. 8, 13–14). Data: UFLLIB M* (100 × 100 to 2000 × 2000) and Körkel-Ghosh (n = m ∈ {250, 500, 750}, symmetric and asymmetric, cost classes a, b, c), Barahona-Chudak's 18 instances, ORLIB with zero costs set to 10^-5, random qUFL as in [19, 20], and new ones with n up to 2000 and m up to 10000 (pp. 20–21, 23).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design (brief)**: GB cuts from Geoffrion's formula (18), explained through the Lagrangian at the optimal multipliers (p. 6); for linear costs the slave is a continuous knapsack solved by Dantzig's critical item, so only n distinct cuts exist per customer (pp. 7–8); for quadratic costs z is eliminated through *perspective costs* γ_i = c_i/y*_i, and a small QP routine (Algorithm 1) is proved correct by Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.1, written out to make the results reproducible (pp. 9–12); fat and slim give the same bound as projections of one formulation (p. 13). Root: in-out with the stabilizer at all ones, moved halfway towards each LP optimum, separating at λ y* + (1 − λ) ỹ + δ(1, …, 1) with λ = 0.2, δ = 2ε and a 5/5/5 reset schedule back to Kelley (p. 15). Tree: Kelley through the user-cut callback, 20 rounds per node (2,000 at the root), cuts added as local (p. 17); one aggregated optimality cut (43) in the lazy callback (p. 19); threshold rounding, local branching and proximity search with 5,000 ticks per call (p. 19). Numerics: perspective strengthening skipped for y*_i < 10^-5 or > 1 − 10^-5, the 2ε trick, EPINT = 0, EPGAP and EPRHS at 10^-9, and a Margot-style validity test (pp. 13–14, 20).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: CPLEX 12.6 on Xeon E3-1220V2 nodes (3.10 GHz, 16 GB), wall-clock seconds, 4 threads, basic untuned settings (p. 20); single-thread traces on gs250a-1 with one separator under three loop policies (fat: Kelley stopped at the root after 15,000 s, in-out root 0.19 s and done at 0.41 s; slim: in-out root 0.04 s with 4 cuts) and fat against slim on MT1 (pp. 15–18); 7 open linear instances solved (Table 1, p. 22); 22 best-known values improved, 22 matched and 6 worse by under 0.04%, from four seeded single-thread local-branching runs (pp. 22, 24); qUFL speed-ups of up to four orders of magnitude over the perspective model run with MIQCPSTRAT = 1, which hits the memory limit from n, m ≥ 200 (pp. 22–23, 25); slim beats fat at scale with average root gaps under 0.04%, and all KG qUFL instances are solved (pp. 23, 26–27); no time limit is stated for the exact runs and no seeds for the timings; per-instance results sit in an online appendix (p. 28). Where card S011 reports the same items (gs250a-1, Table 1, Table 2, MT1), the numbers are identical.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic and computational: 7 open UFL instances solved, 22 best-known values improved, and qUFL speed-ups of four orders of magnitude on much larger instances (pp. 2, 26–27); the impact claims are that location, allocation and network-design problems (connected facility location, ring-star, median cycle, traveling purchaser) can be thinned out the same way, and that stabilized loops matter for convex MINLP solvers (pp. 27–28). Limits stated concretely: no real bundle method (p. 15); Kelley stays in the tree because a better loop inside CPLEX is not immediate (p. 17); for linear costs the root bound is not tight, so many nodes remain (p. 18; 200,184 nodes on ga250a-3, p. 22); 50 KG linear instances are still out of reach for exact methods (p. 21); capacitated facility location is not immediate and left to future work, with nonlinear stochastic programming (p. 28).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (same paper as S011, not independent): thinning out is transferred from the Steiner-tree work (p. 2); the x_ij are projected out (p. 5); the w_j are aggregated into w_sum against the MIP reflex, with the same-bound projection argument (pp. 12–13); fat or slim is decided per cost class by measurement (pp. 17–18).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence, dated to April 2015: §3.2 blames Kelley's loop rather than cut strength and names the *curse of Kelley* (p. 14); the cure is derived from the analogy with Lagrangian dual minimization (pp. 14–15); one separator runs under three policies on gs250a-1 (p. 15), with the numbers of the 2016 preprint (pp. 15–17; card S011, D5), so the showcase instance dates from this report; stabilization stays at the root because a better loop inside CPLEX is not immediate (p. 17); the lineage credit is [5, 12] only, Ben-Ameur and Neto and Fischetti and Salvagnin (p. 15), narrower than in S011 (card S011, D6).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the quick shot, one GB separator plugged into CPLEX 12.6, already solved open instances (p. 13); a simple in-out variant instead of a bundle method (p. 15); no qUFL-specific heuristics because none were needed (p. 19); his local branching and proximity search reused as sub-MIP heuristics (p. 19). The recipe then grew for much better results (p. 13), and only the loop and the fat or slim choice are ablated (pp. 15–18).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (partial): the perspective baseline runs with the CPLEX option that suits it, better than the default (MIQCPSTRAT = 1, p. 22); the new code runs with basic, untuned settings (p. 20); the linear case is compared through solved status and best-known values from the literature (pp. 21–22), not through a common harness.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: seeds only as a resource, four concurrent seeded single-thread runs with the best solution kept (p. 22); no seeds or repetitions are reported for the timings (pp. 15–18, 20–27).
- Outside the numbered methods: Taste mark 1 ✅ (the open Barahona-Chudak instances 2500-10 and 3000-100 solved in 824.76 s and 225.25 s, pp. 21–22); Taste mark 4 ✅ (Benders for UFL as forgotten folklore, pp. 2, 6); Heuristic 1 ✅ (the slim model against the aversion to aggregated formulations, p. 13); Heuristic 3 ✅ (the master LP is Lagrangian dual minimization in disguise, so its stabilization is borrowed, pp. 14–15); Heuristic 8 ✅ (one-instance traces before the tables, pp. 15–18); Heuristic 10 ✅ (a Margot-style validity test that caught tolerance issues of earlier versions, p. 20). **Transferable techniques**:
- When a cut loop stalls, ask whether the master LP is a known problem in disguise and borrow that field's stabilization: minimizing a convex function over the 0-1 box is Lagrangian dual minimization, where bundle methods beat Kelley, so a simple in-out loop replaces Kelley at the root (pp. 14–15).
- Before any table, compare loop policies with the same separator on one instance, on a log time axis with the cut counts at the end of the root and of the run: Kelley, Kelley separating at y* + 2ε, in-out (pp. 15–17).
- In-out constants and reset schedule: stabilizer at all ones, moved halfway each iteration; separate at λ y* + (1 − λ) ỹ + δ(1, …, 1) with λ = 0.2 and δ = 2ε; set λ = 1 after 5 iterations without bound improvement, δ = 0 after 5 more, stop and drop slack cuts after 5 more; drop slack cuts every 5th iteration (p. 15).
- When only the sum of per-customer costs matters, aggregate them into one variable, argue that the bound is unchanged because both masters project the same formulation, then choose fat or slim per cost class by measurement (pp. 12–13, 17–18).
- If the Benders slave is a continuous knapsack, write the cut in closed form from the critical item: only n distinct cuts exist per customer, and the model stays compact (pp. 2, 7–8).
- Stress-test the cut generator after each run: fix the incumbent, perturb 80% of its entries by 10^-9 to 10^-1, separate, add the cuts statically, re-solve, 10,000 times, and write the model to a file on infeasibility (p. 20).
