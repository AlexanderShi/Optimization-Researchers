### S126 · A fix-propagate-repair heuristic for mixed integer programming (Mathematical Programming Computation 2025, DOI 10.1007/s12532-024-00269-5)
**Read**: full, pp. 1–29: text, Tables 1–9 and the reference list. Figs. 1–5 (pseudocode) and Fig. 6 (a plot) have no text layer. Only their captions were read, together with the prose that walks through their line numbers (pp. 6–7, 10–11, 15, 17, 23).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Domenico Salvagnin (first and corresponding author), Roberto Roberti; all DEI Padova (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**:
- Dates: received 15 December 2022, accepted 14 September 2024 (p. 1).
- Origin: an extended version of the group's entry to the MIP 2022 Computational Competition, where it ranked second (p. 3).
- Funding: the PRIN 2022 project REWIND supported "the last two authors", i.e. Roberti and Fischetti (p. 27).
- Fischetti is third author. The Xpress reimplementation is the first author's (p. 26). [joint]

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - The entry point is a competition call: novel general-purpose primal heuristics. Its rules encouraged LP-free methods, allowed auxiliary convex problems and forbade subMIPs; the authors kept to these rules (p. 3).
  - The starting point is the folklore fix-and-propagate heuristic (p. 3). Its weakness, as the authors name it: it cannot recover from past mistakes, since 1-level backtracking catches only the most recent wrong turn; and its success depends heavily on the variable and value strategies (p. 3).
  - The alternative tradition is solution repair, as in shift-and-propagate and in WalkSAT for SAT. The authors report that WalkSAT applied to MIP is in general not very effective, though some of its ideas are kept (p. 3).
  - The problem as stated: a diving heuristic based on constraint propagation that repairs the solution after each step (abstract, p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Keep fix-and-propagate and add a repair step. The repair restores feasibility of the current partial assignment without backtracking, and it may change older fixings, not only the latest one (p. 6).
  - What was moved: WalkSAT's repair, from complete assignments to partial assignments inside the dive.
  - Why: a diagnosis. A WalkSAT generalised to MIP behaved like public implementations on SAT-encoding MIPs but poorly on the competition instances. It cut the violation of the random start quickly, then could not bring it to zero (pp. 13–14).
  - Kept from WalkSAT: a violated row chosen at random, zero-damage moves first, a noise parameter, and scoring by damage only (pp. 13, 15–16).
  - Changed: flips become shifts (pp. 13–14). Violation is computed from the min/max activities of the current domain, the same quantities that propagation maintains (p. 14). Intervals are shifted, not values (p. 14).
  - Second level (§5.1): the random walk is replaced by a search over move sequences (pp. 16–17):
    - each repair move becomes a disjunction;
    - a second propagation engine derives what the moves imply;
    - when progress stalls, the search jumps to the most promising open node, giving up completeness.
  - Unchanged: the problem itself is never modified. All state sits in the propagation engine and the node stack (p. 7).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - Class: general MILP in ranged form, with an integer subset J ⊆ I (p. 2).
  - Bounds: all variable bounds are assumed finite, which the authors call acceptable for a primal heuristic (p. 2). In the runs, any infinite bound left after presolve gets an artificial box [−100000, +100000]. The authors note this is not valid in general but acceptable for heuristic use (p. 19).
  - Structures exploited (p. 5; pp. 5–6; pp. 9–10):
    - clique constraints, kept in a clique table, with a greedy clique cover;
    - variable locks;
    - the analytic center of the LP relaxation as a corepoint, computed by barrier without crossover on the LP without objective.
  - Propagation cost is polynomial for binaries, exponential for general integers, and possibly non-terminating for continuous variables; hence the limits used in practice (pp. 4–5).
  - Test sets: the 240 MIPLIB 2017 benchmark instances (p. 19); inside Xpress, an internal set of 817 public and commercial instances (p. 26).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**:
  - One limited-DFS engine with three switches: propagate, repair, backtrack. Its settings give the four parametrizations dfs, dfsrep, dive and diveprop; classic fix-and-propagate is one of them (pp. 6–8). A trail (a LIFO record of domain changes) gives partial undo (p. 6). Node limit = number of variables + 1, and the search stops at the first feasible solution (p. 7).
  - Strategies (pp. 8–12):
    - variable orders: 7 static ones (LR, type, random, locks, and three clique-cover orders that the authors believe novel);
    - value rules: 9, including LP-based ones that round up with probability equal to the fractional part, clipped to the current domain.
    - Of the 63 combinations, 14 were kept after preliminary tests (p. 12).
  - RepairWalk: noise p = 0.75 and 200 iterations per call. The best state is tracked and returned (p. 16). Enlarging a domain is not allowed as a repair move (p. 14, footnote 1).
  - RepairSearch: two propagation engines (pp. 17–18).
    - Changes go from the repair engine to the partial-solution engine, by intersection or by fixing to the nearer endpoint; a numerical example works through this (p. 18).
    - A non-binary shift becomes a disjunction split at one end of the shifted interval, chosen by comparing the gaps to the repair domain (p. 18).
  - Safeguards (p. 19):
    - a deterministic work limit of at most 100 × (number of nonzeros) matrix accesses for propagation and repair;
    - a greedy 1-opt step on every solution found, which matters on set-covering models, where the all-ones solution tends to be found first.
  - Portfolio: three classes by what a method needs (nothing; a zero-objective LP point; the optimal LP). The classes run one after another, the members of a class in parallel, until one method finds a solution. The members were hand-picked from the preliminary results, and the full order is on p. 24 (pp. 22, 24).
  - No proofs. One completeness remark: the DFS is complete if limits are large enough (p. 7).
  - Source issue: the text says that locks sorts variables by non-decreasing maximum lock count (p. 8), while Table 1 lists it as non-increasing (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - Setup (p. 19):
    - C++, with CPLEX 12.10 as LP solver and MIP presolver;
    - 24 identical Xeon E3-1220 V2 machines at 3.10 GHz with 16 GB, multi-threaded runs;
    - 10 minutes per instance-seed run, as in the competition;
    - several random seeds to limit performance variability, citing Danna 2008;
    - the same CPLEX presolve applied in every run.
  - Preliminary study, dfs only (pp. 19–20):
    - 3 seeds × 240 instances = 720 pairs, at most 100 trial dives each;
    - columns: solutions found, average primal gap, shifted geometric mean time with shift 1 s.
    - Solutions found range from 334 (goodobj) to 481 (zerolp) (p. 19).
    - A pure random strategy (random2) is among the worst; this is the argument that the differences are not noise (p. 20).
    - 103 easy models; 58 that no strategy solves; no dominance among the rest (p. 20).
    - Single-strategy wins are named (rail01, peg-solitaire-a3, unitcal_7). On tbfp-network the fastest strategy needs 0.5 s while the slowest finds nothing in 400 s (p. 20).
    - Virtual best: 547 pairs (p. 20).
  - Effect of repair (Table 5, pp. 21–22):
    - dfsrep beats dfs on every strategy at similar time.
    - dive (repair without propagation) matches dfs on success rate but costs more.
    - Models with no solution fall from 58 to 36; virtual best 606.
  - Portfolio over 5 seeds (pp. 24–25):
    - 969 of 1200 runs (slightly above 80%), against 75% for the best single method; primal gap 0.66; 1.24 s; virtual best 1010 (p. 24).
    - Stage-wise truncation: the LP-free stage alone finds 898 in 0.72 s. For scale, MIP presolve alone takes 0.62 s, and presolve plus the first LP 1.04 s (pp. 24–25).
  - Repair search vs repair walk inside the portfolio (p. 25):
    - 969 vs 945 found, 1.24 vs 1.09 s.
    - A contingency table (42 vs 18 discordant pairs) is tested with "a standard McNemar's test" and called significant; no p-value is printed.
    - A per-instance check over the 5 seeds: repair search wins by at least 3 seeds on 8 instances; repair walk does so on 3.
    - My note: the 1200 instance-seed pairs are clustered by instance, so the test treats correlated pairs as independent; the per-instance count is the cleaner check.
  - Inside Xpress (pp. 26–27):
    - The strategies run sequentially, in a background root task. This setting was reached "After extensive tuning"; the tuning set is not stated.
    - 817 instances × 5 seeds; Xeon Gold 5218, one socket, 20 threads; 1 h.
    - Metrics: solved count, plus time, nodes and primal-dual integral as ratios to the reference; shifted geometric mean with shift 10; difficulty brackets [t, 3600}; a subset of affected models.
    - Ratios (time / nodes / primal-dual integral), Table 9, p. 26: 0.996 / 0.993 / 0.995 overall; 0.990 / 0.990 / 0.994 on [100, 3600}; 0.869 / 0.769 / 0.856 on the 116 affected runs of 4085.
    - The heuristic is to be on by default in the next Xpress release (p. 27).
  - Tuning-test overlap: the portfolio members were picked from the preliminary results on the same 240 instances on which the portfolio is then scored (pp. 19, 24). The paper does not flag this.
  - Data and code are available only on request (pp. 20, 27).
- **D6 Positioning**:
  - Places the work in a three-way taxonomy of primal heuristics (LP-free, LP-based, subMIP) and in the split between constructive and improving heuristics (p. 2).
  - Credits (pp. 2–3): the folklore fix-and-propagate and its recent variants (structure-driven fix-and-propagate, Scylla); the LP-free revival around the competition (feasibility jump, Scylla, Local-ILP); and shift-and-propagate.
  - Self-citations are few: the pump; local branching; and a local-branching repair of infeasibility, described as a more expensive approach to repair (pp. 2–3). FP 2.0 is cited for propagation non-termination (p. 5).
  - The corepoint follows Berthold et al. and Bonami–Salvagnin–Tramontani (p. 5).
  - A division of labour is stated: solution quality is better obtained by subMIP local searches such as RINS or local branching (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**:
  - Low-key claims: the abstract calls the method relatively inexpensive, the single strategies not very robust, and the Xpress gain small (p. 1); the conclusion calls the effect small but positive (p. 27).
  - Novelty is claimed narrowly: the clique-cover strategies are novel to the authors' knowledge (p. 9); the combination is called novel, while similar to shift-and-propagate and WalkSAT (p. 27).
  - Negative preliminary results are printed as motivation: WalkSAT on MIP (p. 14); a tabu list and soft restarts, of limited effect (p. 16).
- **D8 Contributions & limits**:
  - Contribution levels: an algorithm (fix-propagate-repair, repair search); a study (the strategy × parametrization grid); software in a commercial solver (pp. 26–27). Adoption by Xpress is the authors' own statement (p. 27).
  - Limits stated:
    - single strategies are not robust (p. 1);
    - solution quality is quite poor whatever the method (p. 19);
    - 36 models remain without a solution (p. 22);
    - the portfolio selection is manual and could probably be improved (p. 24);
    - the effect inside Xpress is small, which the authors attribute to conservative settings (p. 27).
  - Future work: more variable-value strategies (p. 27); portfolio selection cast as an optimization problem (p. 24, footnote 3); infinite bounds (p. 2); cliques extracted from knapsack constraints or by probing (p. 5).

**Method links**:

There is no content-free perturbation arm, and the runs are multi-threaded (p. 19), unlike step 1's single-thread protocol.
- Method 4 (blind harness), evidence ✅: every arm gets the same presolve and the same deterministic work limit (p. 19). The virtual best is printed as a ceiling (pp. 20, 22, 24). A per-instance consistency check stands next to the pooled test (p. 25).
- Method 4, contradiction ✗, on two counts:
  - (i) The portfolio members were selected from preliminary results on the same 240 instances on which the portfolio is scored, and the overlap is not flagged (pp. 19, 24). This goes against step 5 (keep tuning instances out of the evaluation).
  - (ii) The paper, accepted in September 2024, uses a McNemar test (p. 25). That contradicts the skill's statement "No significance test before 2026". The paper is joint, and the first author is Salvagnin.
- Method 2 (blame the loop), variant ⚠: WalkSAT's move logic is kept, and the place where the loop calls it is moved, from complete assignments to every node of the dive. The move follows a diagnosis of where the walk stalls (pp. 13–14). Here the "oracle" is a repair routine, not a separator.
- Method 1 (thin out, subtract), evidence ✅ for step 5:
  - the 63 combinations were cut to 14 and the three worst dropped (pp. 12, 20);
  - stage-wise truncation shows what each stage adds (pp. 24–25).
- Method 6 (ask the solver first), variant ⚠: the rules forbade subMIPs (p. 3). So the solver enters only as presolve and LP engine (pp. 19, 24), and solution quality is left to subMIP heuristics (p. 8). The finished method then goes into the solver (Xpress), the reverse direction (p. 26).
- Heuristic links (for synthesis; not counted as Methods):

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) Write the heuristic as one search engine with independent switches (propagate / repair / backtrack). The classic method and each variant are then parameter settings of the same code, and every comparison isolates one switch (pp. 6–8).
- (D4) Measure the violation of a partial assignment from the propagation engine's min/max activities, so that repair and propagation share one incrementally updated data structure (p. 14).
- (D4) When a random-walk repair cycles, or needs several steps for a simple swap, turn each move into a disjunction and search over move sequences with a second propagation engine. Propagation then performs compound moves such as clique swaps by itself (pp. 16–18).
- (D4) Cap propagation work deterministically, at 100 × (number of nonzeros) matrix accesses, rather than by time (p. 19).
- (D5) Add a pure-random strategy as a noise-floor arm, and print the virtual best as the ceiling a portfolio could reach (pp. 20, 24).
- (D5) For success/failure outcomes, print the 2×2 contingency table of the two arms and test the discordant cells (McNemar); then confirm per instance over the seeds (p. 25).
- (D5) Judge a heuristic inside a solver by time to optimality, nodes and primal-dual integral as ratios to the reference, in difficulty brackets [t, T} and on the subset of affected models (pp. 26–27).

### S143 · EndoWake: Modeling wind by linear programming for wind farm layout optimization (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2609.40157)
**Read**: full.
- Main text pp. 1–30, with the references.
- Supplementary material pp. 31–42: Algorithms S1–S2, Lemma S1, Remark S1, Proposition S1, and seed tables S1–S5.
- Figure 1 (calibration field) and Figure 2 (bands) are only partly extracted (axis labels); their captions were read (pp. 10, 14).
- I spot-checked the median rows of main Tables 1 and 3, and the posting figures on p. 25, against the seed tables (pp. 35–41). They agree.

**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Martina Fischetti, University of Seville, first and corresponding author (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**:
- Dated 1 October 2026; arXiv v1 of 30 September 2026; unrefereed (p. 1).
- It returns to the authors' wind-farm line from the dual-bound side. That line comprises proximity search (2014, 2016), the Vattenfall deployment (2020) and the 2023 Management Science paper (p. 4).
- CRediT: Matteo Fischetti, conceptualization, formal analysis, methodology, software, review and editing. Martina Fischetti, conceptualization, data curation, investigation, methodology, validation, original draft (p. 27).
- AI declaration (p. 27): Claude Opus 5 and Claude Fable 5 served as programming assistants, as adversarial readers of the mathematical statements and as editors. Independent adversarial audits came from ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. The authors checked every proof.
- Replication package on Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22017424 (p. 28). [two-author; joint]

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - The mathematical-programming line prices wakes with an interference matrix: pairwise deficits computed offline and combined by a superposition rule fixed before the layout is known (pp. 2–4).
  - This includes the authors' own earlier work, where, they note, the layout side has certified results only through incumbents and matheuristics (p. 4).
  - Engineering wake models have moved away from fixed superposition, but they serve as evaluators or gradient methods and return no dual bound (p. 4).
  - The entry point: write the wind field into the MILP itself, with linear propagation (pp. 2, 5–6).
  - The stated difficulty is the dual bound, not the incumbent: greedy construction plus local search finds good layouts quickly (p. 2).
  - Scope: the paper is methodological, and "exact" means exact with respect to the calibrated model (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - The model:
    - The wind speed of each cell and scenario is a continuous decision variable.
    - It is bounded by a convex combination of five upwind speeds (the stencil, weighted by the kernel) plus a recovery term.
    - A turbine multiplies the speed it passes downstream by ρ < 1 (pp. 5–8).
  - The structural fact (Proposition 1, p. 9): the feasible field has a greatest element, computable by one forward sweep, and that element is antitone in the layout. The validity of every cut rests on this (p. 10).
  - Size: the model is linear in the number of grid cells, against Θ(|C|²) for an interference matrix (p. 9).
  - On this model:
    - band inequalities couple layout and power in aggregate. They come from a DP over chains of cells in a strip narrower than the separation, through the facets of an upper concave envelope (pp. 14–16);
    - a branch-and-check drops the flow and power blocks and keeps the sites, the packing, the band inequalities and one hypograph variable per cell and scenario. It scores incumbents with the sweep and adds upwind cuts (pp. 17–19).
  - Kept from the classical line: discrete sites and clique packing. Removed: the interference matrix and the superposition rule (pp. 2, 5).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - Model (pp. 6–8):
    - binary site variables on the allocatable cells;
    - continuous wind variables w and power variables z;
    - SOS2 weights λ for the piecewise-linear power curve (the device of Beale and Tomlin);
    - a big-M constant V_s in the wake rows;
    - clique packing over 3×3 windows, and cardinality bounds.
  - Standing assumption: every free-stream speed V_s is below the cut-out speed (p. 6).
  - Boundary convention: cells outside the grid read the free-stream speed, which is mildly optimistic. A frame of cells without binaries is the remedy, not used in the experiments (pp. 7–8).
  - Calibration: weighted least squares against the Bastankhah–Porté-Agel wake model, residual 7.7% at Δ = D; the fit drives β to zero (p. 10).
  - Instances (p. 20):
    - a synthetic ladder of n×n grids with N_max = n + 2;
    - four axial, equiprobable sectors at 10 m/s, zero installation cost;
    - an idealized 3 MW power curve, chosen to avoid proprietary data.
  - Published benchmarks are deliberately not used. They rest on pairwise wake models, so comparing objective values would compare wake models, not methods (p. 20).
  - The instances are deliberately symmetric. The authors argue that this is conservative, because Proposition 2 depends on neither the sectors nor the kernel (pp. 20–21).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (proof devices and design):
  - Proposition 1, maximal field (pp. 9–10):
    - device: T_x is monotone in w and antitone in x; the propagation planes are acyclic, which gives a forward substitution and an induction (p. 9);
    - it is proved in the paper rather than cited, because everything rests on it (pp. 9–10).
  - Proposition 2, vacuous root bound (p. 11):
    - a uniform fractional point satisfies every relaxed row and attains N_max·P_max;
    - this holds whenever the average density is below roughly one turbine per nine cells, which is the operating regime, not a technicality;
    - the bound even exceeds the wake-free value.
  - Two local repairs fail:
    - the perspective reformulation provably stops at the wake-free value, and measures as no effect on top of the band inequalities (p. 12);
    - a componentwise-maximal lifted wake row (Proposition 3) leaves the bound at N_max·P_max (pp. 12–13).
    - The diagnosis: lifting acts on the wind, while the objective reads power (p. 13).
  - Proposition 4, band inequalities are valid (pp. 14–16):
    - a band narrower than the separation orders its turbines into a chain;
    - the DP makes three safe relaxations: separation enforced only between consecutive turbines, one deficit per turbine, and that deficit at its floor value;
    - validity follows from antitonicity.
    - Two details decide validity: the upper hull (not the lower one) and a DP horizon of N_max (p. 16).
  - Proposition 5 (p. 17):
    - the subproblem is solved in O(|C|) time; it is a function evaluation, cheaper than an LP;
    - the power curve is non-concave and discontinuous at cut-in, so the subproblem has no LP duals. Hence logic-based Benders, in its branch-and-check form.
  - Proposition 6, upwind cut (pp. 18–19):
    - valid by antitonicity, with its support restricted to the upwind cells;
    - argued to dominate full-support no-good cuts and integer L-shaped cuts.
  - Supplement (pp. 31–34): Algorithm S1 (the DP), Algorithm S2 (the callback, with a fallback to full support), Lemma S1 (the support of the field), Proposition S1 (correctness and finite termination, and what survives the solver's tolerances).
  - Numerical safeguards:
    - facets are anchored at their left vertex; in exact rational arithmetic they fall below their points by at most 9.1e-13 kW (p. 16);
    - the band inequalities were evaluated 1,054,476 times on feasible integer layouts, with no violation (p. 16);
    - Gurobi's NumericFocus is at its maximum for the big-M model (p. 20);
    - returned layouts are re-scored, the separation is re-checked, and the field is recomputed as an LP with x fixed, agreeing to 5e-8 m/s (p. 24);
    - the fallback cut never fired, in 45 runs and 4,414,254 earlier cuts (p. 19).
  - In-search design: how the band inequalities enter the model decided the outcome (p. 22).
    - As static rows they tighten the root by 2.7–4.6%, but good incumbents stop coming.
    - The monolithic model therefore pools the inequalities that are inactive at the root (option c, selected on a preliminary run).

### S144 · Models and algorithms for the time window assignment traveling salesperson problem with stochastic travel times (European Journal of Operational Research 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2025.07.034)
**Read**: full, pp. 1–16: text, Tables 1–18, Algorithms 1–2 and the reference list. Figs. 1–5 (performance profiles and plots) have no text layer; their captions were read (pp. 5, 6, 7, 11, 13).

- Funding: the PRIN 2022 project REWIND, on home delivery (p. 14).
- CRediT: Fischetti, conceptualization, methodology, writing and funding acquisition. Software and data curation are by Cavaliere alone (p. 14).

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - Source: attended home delivery and last-mile delivery. Time windows must be assigned and communicated in advance, and the route planned under stochastic travel times, trading operating cost against service quality (p. 1).
  - Structure (p. 1; p. 3):
    - here-and-now decisions: the tour and the time windows;
    - wait-and-see decisions, per scenario: departure times, earliness, lateness and overtime.
  - Prior work: one paper (Çelik et al. 2025). Its model combines DFJ routing with a big-M temporal second stage and solves instances up to n = 20, occasionally 25 (pp. 2–3).
  - The entry point: the big-M relaxation is weak, and a model that knows each arc's position can express times without big-M (pp. 3–4).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Key idea: position-indexed arc variables (Fox–Gavish–Graves 1980) index all time variables by tour position, so time propagation needs no big-M (pp. 3–4).
  - Mixing formulations: add the aggregated 2-index variables and separate SECs on the fly. Where callbacks are unavailable, add Claus's flow model instead, which gives the same LP bound (p. 4).
  - The Benders design is fixed by measurement:
    - ten master/subproblem layouts are screened with CPLEX's built-in Benders (Table 2, p. 6);
    - in the best layout, the 2-index and 3-index binaries and θ stay in the master, and every time-related variable moves to one subproblem holding all scenarios (pp. 5–7);
    - this gives up the common practice of one subproblem per scenario, which here proved inefficient and unstable (p. 6).
  - Compared with the prior Benders of Çelik et al., what changes is the model and the split. What stays is the standard LP-duality optimality cut (pp. 4–5).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**:
  - Model (p. 3): a complete asymmetric digraph; service times folded into arc times; waiting allowed; a shift duration T; a finite set of scenarios with probabilities.
  - Four cost terms with weights α, β, δ, fixed at 1, 10 and 10 in all tests (p. 3; p. 9).
  - The first stage is binary and the recourse continuous, so LP-duality Benders cuts apply (pp. 4–5). The 3-index variables may be relaxed to continuous without losing validity (p. 5).
  - Instances (pp. 9–10):
    - customer locations from the generator of Queiroga et al. and Uchoa et al.;
    - 10,000 scenario matrices per instance, half with localized and half with spread disruptions;
    - localized: arc disruption probability 1/(2n̄), four magnitudes taken from Jabali et al. 2015, so that about 60% of scenarios leave a given tour undisrupted;
    - spread: gamma-distributed delays with mean 0.35 c_ij and shape 16, as in Çelik et al.;
    - T is calibrated from the optimal ATSP duration (d_ATSP for localized, 1.35 d_ATSP for spread), because a large T makes instances trivial (p. 10).
  - The instances are public on GitHub (p. 10).
  - A further set on the Padova road network (OpenStreetMap) has correlated disruptions (pp. 13–14).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**:
  - Master: the variables x_ij, x^p_ij and θ; everything else goes to the subproblem (p. 7).
  - Separation (pp. 7, 9; Algorithm 2):
    - SECs are separated with Concorde code on fractional and on integer points;
    - Benders cuts are separated only at integer points, and only when SEC separation finds nothing.
  - The D+k / D−k facets of Balas–Fischetti were implemented after the SECs and then dropped: they gave no average gain, and the authors chose to keep the approach as simple as possible (p. 7).
  - Root loop (p. 7):
    - runs showed slow bound progress at the root, leaving a large gap, and a faster rise once branching started;
    - so Benders separation is done at integer solutions only;
    - stabilization was not implemented, its benefit being marginal in preliminary tests.
  - Warm start (pp. 7–8):
    - ATSP++ (α = δ = 0, β kept) is a 2-index MILP. Its overtime is linear in x, because departures need no waiting;
    - fixing x* and solving the LP gives the time windows;
    - the LP's reduced costs give an initial Benders optimality cut, after Fischetti–Ljubić–Sinnl 2016;
    - device: x is fixed through its bounds, not through equations, so that its reduced costs are not zero (p. 8).
    - Algorithm 1 gives the overall scheme (p. 8).
  - Each Benders separation LP yields a full primal solution, which is posted as a heuristic incumbent (p. 8).
  - Scenario aggregation at separation time (p. 8):
    - with x* fixed, scenarios that are identical on its support are merged with probability weights;
    - validity comes from a surrogate relaxation (Crainic et al. 2021), and tightness at x* holds by design;
    - this reduces 100 scenarios by 50–70% and 1000 scenarios by 75–90% (localized instances).
  - Proof devices: the surrogate-relaxation argument (eqs. 6–9, p. 8); the LP equivalence of Claus and DFJ, cited from Padberg–Sung (p. 4).
  - Implementation: CPLEX in opportunistic parallel mode, chosen because the callbacks are slow; barrier for the separation LPs (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - Setup (p. 9):
    - C/C++, GCC 12.3.1, Fedora 37, CPLEX 20.1.0 through its C API;
