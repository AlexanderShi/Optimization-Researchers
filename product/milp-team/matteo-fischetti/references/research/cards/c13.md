- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is a prescription that implementations dropped. FGL 2005 §3.2 proposed the objective cutoff (2) and, in the same section, warned that rounded points feasible for Ax ≥ b but not for the cutoff are lost; it prescribed a look at the rounded point every round and, on mixed problems, an LP with the integers fixed (both passages quoted from [12], p. 2): "Cutoff and recovery were thus designed as two parts of one scheme" (p. 2). Later work replaced the cutoff by a weighted objective [1], "mentioned it in passing" [14], or "dropped the objective altogether" [7, 9]; SCIP's pump keeps neither part (p. 2), which §4 confirms from the source (p. 9). The problem is stated as a measurement gap: what the black-box use of the cutoff costs and what the recovery is worth "has not been measured" (p. 2). The black-box use is presented as what a practitioner does: add the constraint to the model and run the pump on the restricted problem (p. 2).
  - Transferable: quote the original prescription verbatim, then show from the code that implementations dropped part of it; the gap becomes a measurable question rather than a new heuristic (pp. 2, 9).
- **D2 Core idea**: A geometric mechanism plus a restoration. When the cutoff binds, convexity puts every projection optimum on the hyperplane c^T x = U; the rounded point costs more by the rounding gap δ = c^T x̂ − c^T x̃, so a feasible rounding lies outside the polyhedron the next LP projects onto, and a pump that does not look at it never sees it again (pp. 3, 6). The cutoff removes support, whereas the weighted term (4) only discourages (p. 3). The restoration adds both FGL parts to SCIP's objective-weighted pump [1]: the cutoff (2), recomputed every round so as to follow the incumbent, with continuation after each solution (p. 8); and a recovery at two levels, the *test* (x̂ checked against the original constraints, cutoff excluded) and the *completion* (integers fixed at x̂, min c^T x over the model's constraints, cutoff removed) (p. 7). Left unchanged: the rounding, the perturbation and SCIP's weighting (4), identical in every cell (pp. 2, 12). What is his own is "only the placement": the completion runs on a second LP kept apart from the pump's, reoptimised by the dual simplex from its own last basis, since fixing the bounds inside the pump's dive would destroy the pump's warm start (p. 8).
  - Transferable: when an add-on helps a little, ask what the loop discards under it, and restore the companion step before designing a new heuristic (pp. 3, 19).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MILP min{c^T x : x ∈ P, x_j ∈ Z (j ∈ I)}, P = {Ax ≥ b, l ≤ x ≤ u}, with a nonempty binary set B; the distance (1) is taken on the binaries, general integers through an auxiliary variable as in [3], and "the instances of this paper are mostly binary" (pp. 1-2). Census: 37 pure-binary instances (16 OR-Library set covering, 11 MIPLIB 2003, 10 MIPLIB 2017), what is left of a stratified sample of 58 after 21 with no band to survey (p. 4). Factorial study: the 463 MIPLIB 2017 instances with at least 80% binary variables, 424 after 39 exclusions with reasons (37 first LPs over 600 s, 2 LP numerical failures inside SCIP); "We select no instance on solution quality" (p. 11). Target experiment: 154 instances with a best known value, no general integers and no quadratic, SOS or general constraints, first LP at most 5 s, split exogenously into 43 benchmark (primary), 20 tuning and 91 secondary; of the 134 evaluated, 70 are pure 0-1 and 64 mixed (p. 17). Structure that matters: on set covering, rounding up a fractional cover is feasible and never cheaper (median δ/(z_inc − z_LP) = +0.62, so criterion (3) fails at λ = 0.5), while on the MIPLIB instances the median is near zero (pp. 3-4); for the completion the split that matters is the presence of continuous variables after presolve, and general integers make no difference (p. 12). Correctness assumptions: soundness rests on the certificate LP alone (p. 8); the completion works on the presolved model, so "a failed completion is not proof that the fixing admits none" (p. 8); z_low = z_LP keeps U ≥ z_LP and the projection LP feasible, except under the fallback (p. 8). The cell none0.5 is only the one closest to the black-box use: the user's version has an outside incumbent and a fixed U, here neither holds (p. 12).
  - Transferable: write down an a priori criterion that sorts problem classes before any run (here δ ≤ (1 − λ)(z_inc − z_low)) and say that it is heuristic (p. 3).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: (a) Code read at a pinned commit, SCIP heur_feaspump.c at dba4b2a (24 August 2026, version string 11.0.0; SoPlex at f0dbc81): it declares itself "objective feasibility pump 2.0"; it has exactly one SCIPtrySol call (line 1271), on the LP iterate when it is integral; the rounded point is built every round and never tested; SCIPgetCutoffbound is consulted once (line 795); SCIPchgVarObjDive (line 1104) resets the diving LP's cutoff bound, so the objective enters only through α; usefp20 and stage3 are off by default and in every run (p. 9). (b) Additions as parameters, all off by default (cutlam, cutfallback, tryrounded, lpfix on a clone LP, restartonsol, stopafter, a treatment-blind delivery, completion counters), "so that every variant is the same binary, differing only in parameter values" (p. 9). (c) Design invariant: two decoupled LPs. The projection LP may carry constraints that are invalid for the problem; the second LP carries only the model and its objective and is the certificate; soundness (no infeasible point accepted, whatever the first LP contains) is kept apart from effectiveness (nothing guaranteed); each LP warm-starts from its own basis (p. 8). (d) Arguments: convexity places the iterate on the cutoff hyperplane whenever the unconstrained projection lies above U, which fails at the first round (p. 3); criterion (3) (p. 3); constraint versus penalty: the equivalence holds for the LP, but the pump composes an LP with a rounding, "where no duality operates", so the cutoff has no Lagrangian counterpart (p. 6); zero-distance face: with α = 0 a feasible rounding is never lost, nor below an instance-dependent threshold on α, and whether SCIP's schedule (α = 0.1, divided by ten each round) is below that threshold "we neither bound nor verify" (p. 7). (e) Numerical safeguards: every reported solution is written to a file and re-checked by a separate SCIP process on the original, unpresolved model: 10,218 solutions, none infeasible, and six runs with a better recomputed value, from integer variables at 1 − 10^-15 (p. 11); in E3 a re-check at Gurobi's absolute tolerances fails one success in 2,891, which is counted as a failure (p. 17). (f) Confounds removed from the instrument: no cut is ever separated (the pump runs before the first separation round; the separators log zero calls), because SCIP's aggregation separator starts from the objective row with the cutoff bound and root reduced-cost fixing uses the incumbent, so a feasible but non-improving point could be excluded (pp. 9-10); the fallback (cutoff anchored to c^T x̂ before an incumbent exists) is a factor of its own, never bundled (p. 8). An implementation flaw is reported rather than hidden: the completion LP does not check the time limit, and 12 of 2,640 timed runs overshoot it by more than one second (p. 15).
  - Transferable: put an invalid guidance constraint only in the search LP and accept through a second LP that carries only the model's constraints, each LP warm-started from its own basis (p. 8).
  - Transferable: cite the solver's implementation by file, function and line at a pinned commit before saying what it does (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Three experiments, side by side in Table 2 (p. 10). Census: a from-scratch FGL pump on Gurobi 13.0.3, 37 instances, three seeds per level, λ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, z_inc fixed at the first solution of the no-cutoff run on the same seed, 2,000 rounds or 120 s, one thread (p. 4). Two thirds of the feasible roundings lie above the cutoff on covering at every level (Table 1, p. 6), 23% when the perturbed roundings are left out (p. 6), and the lost improving points sit at a median band position of 0.051 at λ = 0.5 (16,613 points; p. 5). Factorial study: SCIP's pump run standalone (limits/nodes = 1, other heuristics off, its own stopping rules lifted, stopafter) (p. 9); ten of fifteen grid cells plus two fallback variants, 424 instances, five seeds, 25,440 runs (pp. 11-12); a budget of 20× the instance's own first LP clamped to [20, 300] s (221 instances at the floor, 122 by the rule, 81 at the cap), wall clock, two blade types with hyper-threading off, no instance straddling them, its 60 runs back to back on an exclusive blade with the variants in random order (pp. 10-11). Subsets E1 (176) and E2 (248) are defined a priori by one pilot run of the plain pump at half budget on one seed, "not a filter" (p. 11); the design was fixed before any result, with twelve declared comparisons and Holm within each subset, and two later comparisons labelled post hoc (pp. 11-13); per-instance median over the five seeds, then a paired sign test on the non-tied instances (tie below 10^-6) (p. 12); primary endpoint the final gap to the LP bound, 100 without a solution; secondary endpoints time to first solution, rounds, LP iterations and completion counters; in E2, found or not as 2×2 tables (p. 13). The baseline none– is SCIP's pump under the common settings, which is "not the stock pump as shipped" (p. 11). Robustness: the twelve comparisons rerun on all 424 instances give the same signs (p. 13); the single family of 24 changes no sign (p. 12); an α = 0 rerun of check– against none– gives 17–158–1 (p. 14); the completion step is timed in a rerun with a clock that leaves the pump's statistics unchanged (median share of wall clock 22%, 7% and 13.5% on the 56 mixed instances, p. 15). Results: FGL0.5 beats none– 116–51–9 (post hoc), none0.5 87–75–14 (post hoc) and check– 114–52–10 (declared) (p. 13); the completion beats the test at mid-gap 27–142–7, Holm p = 0.0033, with all 27 wins on the 56 mixed instances (27–23–6) (pp. 13-15). Target experiment E3: the from-scratch pump on Gurobi with the perturbation and restart rule of [12] as published, three policies (naive, test, completion), the moat w chosen on 20 disjoint tuning instances by a declared rule, 134 instances × 5 seeds = 4,020 runs, the three policies of a seed run simultaneously on one blade, Holm over twelve (pp. 17-18; Table 9, p. 23). Failures and losses printed: nine losses in the headline and 16 instances on which the test costs (p. 14), nothing detected in E2 (p. 16), the fallback harmful (p. 16), naive a strong baseline in E3 with nothing surviving Holm (p. 18), the adaptive and |δ|-scaled moats (p. 18), and the cutoff-sweep perturbation, 147 against 158 solved (pp. 19-20).
  - Transferable: define the analysis subsets a priori by one pilot run of the challenged baseline, then rerun every declared comparison on the full testbed to check that no sign depends on the subset (pp. 11, 13).
  - Transferable: give each instance a budget of k times its own first-LP time, clamped, and timed on the machine it runs on, so that instances of different size get comparable numbers of iterations rather than comparable seconds (p. 10).
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper restates and restores rather than attacks. It credits the pump's progeny (projections [6, 2], randomization [11], weighted objective [1, 20], the alternating-direction reading [15], the survey [4], the book [5]) (p. 2) and names the works that did not follow the prescription, his own among them: FP 2.0 [14] (with Salvagnin) "mentioned it in passing", and Boosting FP [7] (which he co-authored) and [9] "dropped the objective altogether" (p. 2). Achterberg and Berthold's objective pump is the base, not the rival: "the two designs are additive rather than alternatives" (p. 12). Other invalid constraints in the projection LP are credited: the variable neighbourhood pump [17, 19], and his own local branching [13], which "is the inequality reversed" (p. 8). The claim about implementations rests on SCIP alone: the abstract speaks of "Commonly used implementations" (p. 1), but only SCIP's code is read (p. 9). For covering-like structure he names the Lagrangian heuristic of Caprara, Fischetti and Toth [10] as the right reference and does not measure against it (p. 20).
  - Transferable: take the implementers' later variant as the base and add the old parts to it, so that the comparison measures the parts rather than old against new (p. 12).

- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is a computational study that restores a 2005 scheme inside an open solver, with a public artefact: SCIP patches, job scripts, instance lists, the solution files with their independent validation, both from-scratch pumps and the raw logs (p. 25); "Each table carries in the source the exact command that regenerates it" (p. 20). The limits are specific: the pump is measured standalone; inside branch-and-cut it runs once at the root, so "the ceiling on what a modification of the pump can contribute there is set by how often it runs, not by how well", and the embedded experiment was not run (p. 20); E1 is the ground most favourable to the plain pump and its instances are smaller (median about 72,000 nonzeros against 110,000) (p. 20); the seeds enter only through the median, whose spread within an instance is large (p. 20); no good cutoff level is searched for, and the four levels are read as interchangeable, none recommended (pp. 15, 20); the weighting is at SCIP's default only, its threshold unmeasured; on covering-like structure the Lagrangian heuristic [10] is the right reference and is not compared; E3 tunes on 20 noisy instances, reports test at a = 0.5 at a width the rule did not pick, and its sample is the easier third of MIPLIB 2017 (p. 20). Future work: the two parts inside a full branch-and-cut, and a good cutoff level (p. 20); the lead in time of the first improving rounding, "a property worth measuring on its own" (p. 4); whether a cutoff can help find a first solution rather than improve one (p. 16). Reader's note: the headline (FGL0.5 against none–, 116–9) is post hoc, but the declared comparison against check–, an arm never worse than none– on the median final gap (22–0), is 114–52–10 with Holm p < 10^-4 (pp. 13-14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Read the solver's implementation at a pinned commit and cite file, function and line for every statement about what it does (p. 9).
- Implement every arm as parameters of one binary, all additions off by default, so that the arms differ only in parameter values (p. 9).
- Make the bookkeeping treatment-blind: hand every integral iterate to the solver at the top of the loop, before any variant-specific code runs, in every cell alike (p. 12).
- Fix the analysis subsets a priori from one pilot run of the challenged baseline, then rerun all declared comparisons on the full testbed to check every sign (pp. 11, 13).
- Give each instance 20× its own first-LP time, clamped to [20, 300] s and timed on the blade it runs on; keep all runs of an instance on one exclusive blade, variants in random order within each seed (pp. 10-11).
- Declare the comparison family before the data; take the per-instance median over seeds, a sign test on the non-tied instances and Holm within each subset; label post hoc rows and show that they survive when folded into the family (pp. 12-13).
- Re-check every reported solution in a separate solver process on the original, unpresolved model, and count a failed re-check as a failure (pp. 11, 17).
- Before building a fix, instrument a from-scratch reimplementation to count what the heuristic discards and where those points sit relative to the cutoff and the incumbent (pp. 4-6).
- For a null result, report the discordant counts, an exact interval on their proportion and the effect sizes still compatible with the data (9 against 6, [0.32, 0.84], odds ratios 0.5 to 5; p. 16).
- Put an invalid guidance constraint only in the search LP and decide acceptance by a second LP that carries only the model's constraints, each warm-started from its own basis (p. 8).
- Time an add-on with a clock that leaves the heuristic's statistics unchanged, and report its median share of wall clock with interquartile range and maximum, per instance class (p. 15).
- Tune a single knob on a random tuning set disjoint from the evaluation, with a declared rule (most successful (instance, seed) pairs, ties broken by the median censored time), and flag any arm run at a value the rule did not pick as exploratory (p. 17).
- Treat a parameter as the knob it is: measure a range of levels and, if they cannot be told apart, recommend none of them (pp. 8, 15).
