- new-pattern candidate: "Additive bounding via residual instances" (bounds from different substructures chained through reduced-cost residual instances, validity from two conditions, pp. 4, 22; the same approach applied to the ATSP, MDVSP and STSP per p. 22).

- **D3 Problem class and data model**: A 2-D table with marginal equations (1)–(3), nonnegative suppression weights, lower and upper protection levels per sensitive cell, and attacker bounds [a − lb, a + ub], typically [0, 0] for zero cells and [0, +∞] otherwise (pp. 3–4); MW-BSP is a relaxation valid for inclusion-minimal solutions and coincides with the problem for general tables (p. 8).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Padberg–Rinaldi branch-and-cut (p. 13); preprocessing drops the protection levels already met by the primary suppressions, with a parametric max-flow (p. 13); reduced-cost fixing and pricing (p. 14); cut management: capacity cuts first, stop at the first family with violations, at most 100 cuts per round, a separator switched off after 5 consecutive failures, no 2-edge cover or path separation when NR or NC exceeds 100, two minimum cuts per max-flow (p. 14); matching cuts separated as {0, 1/2}-Chvátal–Gomory cuts by a minimum odd cut (p. 15); covers by the MT1R knapsack code after variable fixing (p. 16); a shortest-path variant of the Kelly–Golden–Assad heuristic with LP-guided weights (1 − x*) w at every node (pp. 18–19); strong branching on 5 candidates after Applegate, Bixby, Chvátal and Cook, best bound first (p. 19). Validity is proved by Chvátal–Gomory derivations with multipliers 1/2 and a parity argument (pp. 9, 12).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: ANSI C on a PC Pentium/200 with CPLEX 3.0 as LP solver (p. 19); 49 SDC Library and 9 statistical-office instances plus three random generators, 10 instances per row, a 3,600 s limit, per-family cut counts (pp. 19–21); every SDC Library instance in under one second against Geurts' 2445–4244 CPU seconds on a SUN Sparc 1+ (p. 21); 45 of 550 Kelly–Golden–Assad instances unsolved, mean gap 0.27%, explained by marginal sensitive cells with abnormally large protection levels (pp. 21–22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Exact solution of tables with 250,000 cells and about 10,000 sensitive cells (pp. 1, 29–30); larger tables of the Benedetti class were not tried because they likely need no secondary suppression (p. 21); future: new inequality classes, the polyhedral structure of the bridgeless subgraph problem, multidimensional tables (p. 30).

### S139 · On the Separation of Maximally Violated mod-k Cuts (IPCO 1999, DOI 10.1007/3-540-48777-8_7)
**Read level**: full (all 12 file pages of the author copy, which has no printed page numbers; figure debris skipped). First-page check: title and authors Caprara, Fischetti, Letchford match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Alberto Caprara, Adam N. Letchford · **Position in trajectory**: the Chvátal rank-1 separation line after Caprara and Fischetti's {0, 1/2}-cuts (p. 2), before the MIP separation over the first Chvátal closure that the skill lists under Method 6.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Polynomial exact separation is known for few TSP facet classes and for no comb class (p. 2); Applegate, Bixby, Chvátal and Cook proposed looking only at maximally violated combs, and Fleischer and Tardos made this exact for planar support (p. 2); general {0, 1/2}-cut separation is NP-hard (p. 2). Entry: carry the maximally violated idea from mod 2 to mod k and to general ILPs (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea**: A mod-k cut is a Chvátal–Gomory cut with multipliers in {0, 1/k, …, (k − 1)/k}; no such cut is violated by more than (k − 1)/k, and a maximally violated one is any solution of a congruence system on the tight rows with θ = k − 1 (pp. 3–4), found by Gaussian elimination in GF(k) for prime k (Theorem 1, p. 4); Theorems 2 and 3 justify concentrating on prime k (pp. 4–5). For the TSP, only beads and dominoes of O(|V|) necklaces are needed (Theorem 4, pp. 6–7), which gives O(|E*||V|²) separation (Theorem 5, p. 7); one domino per necklace suffices (Theorem 6, p. 8); extended combs are facet-defining mod-2 cuts (Theorem 7, p. 9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: Any ILP min{cᵀx : Ax ≤ b, x integer} with integer A and b and P_I ≠ P, and a given x* ∈ P (pp. 1, 3); a known prime factorization of k when k is not prime (Theorem 1, p. 4); for the TSP, degree equations, subtour elimination constraints and nonnegativity (p. 5).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation (brief)**: Proof devices: scaling the multipliers by w ∈ {2, …, k − 1} in GF(k) (Theorem 2, p. 4); reduction modulo a prime factor (Theorem 3, p. 5); replacing a tight SEC by a combination of tight constraints whose coefficients agree mod k (Theorem 4, pp. 6–7); the cactus and necklace structure of tight sets (pp. 5–6). The proofs of Theorems 6 and 7 and the ATSP results are left to the full paper (pp. 8–10). Nothing is implemented.
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: none (p. 10).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Separation theory and new facet-defining mod-k TSP cuts, including a mod-2 facet with nesting level above 2 for |V| ≥ 12 (pp. 9–10) and mod-3 cuts such as NEW1 (p. 10); future: undominated mod-k TSP cuts, other problems, and the practical use of the separation (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: the separation sub-decision is written as an integer program in the multipliers ((1)–(5), pp. 3–4) but solved exactly only in its maximally violated special case by linear algebra in GF(k) (p. 4), not by a general-purpose solver; nothing is implemented, a counter-case to the skill's taste warning against an algorithm without implementation and test (practical use left open, p. 10).
- new-pattern candidate: "Restrict separation to maximally violated cuts" (an NP-hard separation becomes a linear congruence system once only cuts violated by (k − 1)/k are sought, pp. 2, 4).
- new-pattern candidate: "Chvátal–Gomory derivation as validity proof and separation handle" (mod-k derivations define the family, Theorem 4 swaps derivation rows mod k, extended combs shown to be mod-2 cuts, pp. 3, 6–7, 9; see S069).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When the separation problem of a Chvátal–Gomory family is NP-hard, look for maximally violated members only: drop every row with positive slack, fix the right-hand-side residue to k − 1 and solve the congruence system by Gaussian elimination modulo a prime (p. 4).
- Shrink the row set before solving by using the combinatorial structure of the tight constraints (here necklaces of tight sets: O(|V|) rows instead of O(|V|²), pp. 5–8).
- To show that a known inequality family belongs to a cut class, exhibit its derivation with explicit multipliers (p. 9).
