### S022 · {0, 1/2}-Chvátal-Gomory cuts (Mathematical Programming 1996, DOI 10.1007/bf02592196)
**Read level**: full. The whole 18-page author preprint ("November 1993, revised February 1995", p. 1) and its references were read. The prose is complete; many formulas lost their symbols in the extraction (braces, inequality signs, multipliers, floor brackets), so statements are taken from the surrounding prose and the theorem texts; Figure 1 (two Möbius ladders, p. 15) is unreadable. The journal version (pp. 221-235) was not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. Caprara (DEIS, Bologna); Fischetti's affiliation is printed as DIE, University of L'Aquila (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the polyhedral side of the skill's 1989-2006 strand (branch-and-cut for routing), and the only theory paper of the batch. Its framing, separation as an optimisation over Chvátal-Gomory multipliers, is the one the later MIP separation over the first Chvátal closure hands to a general-purpose solver (Fischetti and Lodi, listed under Method 6 in SKILL.md; per card D003 in batch c03, pp. 3-4, that separation MIP is written over the multipliers).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: A Chvátal-Gomory (CG) cut needs multipliers in [0,1)^m only to be undominated (p. 1); the paper restricts them to {0, 1/2} and studies the closure P_{1/2} (p. 1). The entry is a structural difference: the rank-1 closure P_1 depends only on the polyhedron P, whereas P_{1/2} depends on the system (A, b) describing it, and P_{1/2} = P can hold with P ≠ P_I, e.g. when b/2 is integral (p. 2). Motivation is twofold: for a 0-1 matrix Q with the bounds x ≥ 0 appended, P = P_{1/2} for every integer right-hand side characterises total unimodularity, and for every right-hand side in {1, +∞}^r balancedness (Theorems 1-2, p. 2), and {0,1/2}-cuts contain several facet classes of value in cutting-plane algorithms (p. 3). The problem is stated as a separation problem, {0,1/2}-SEP, in recognition form; by the Grötschel-Lovász-Schrijver equivalence a polynomial separation would give polynomial optimisation over P_{1/2} (p. 3).
  - Transferable technique: when a cut family is defined by restricting multipliers, ask first whether its closure depends on the polyhedron or on the rows describing it; that decides whether rewriting the rows can strengthen it (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The violation of a {0,1/2}-cut depends only on the parities of (A, b) and on the slack s* = b − Ax*: a violated cut exists if and only if min{s*^T u : u ∈ F(A, b)} < 1, where F(A, b) holds the 0-1 vectors u with b^T u odd and A^T u even (pp. 5-6). This makes {0,1/2}-SEP equivalent to the minimum-weight binary clutter problem (Theorem 3, p. 6), hence NP-complete in recognition form (Corollary 1, p. 6), and polynomial when A^T is the edge-path incidence matrix of a tree (EPT), as a minimum-weight odd cycle (Theorem 4, p. 7), or when A = [M; I] with M EPT, as a minimum-weight odd cut (Theorem 5, p. 8). For a general system, each row with three or more odd coefficients is weakened with the variable bounds until at most two remain (L-, U- and LU-weakening, pp. 8-10), and one can optimise in polynomial time over P ∩ P'_{1/2} (Theorem 6, p. 10). What changes relative to class-by-class separation: the object separated is the derivation (which rows enter with weight 1/2), not a combinatorial template; the cut is still a rank-1 CG cut (pp. 5-6, 16).
  - Transferable technique: write the separation of a fixed-denominator CG family as a minimum-slack parity problem over the multipliers (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Problem class & data model**: Pure integer systems, P_I = conv{x ∈ Z^n : Ax ≤ b} with A and b integer and P_I ≠ P, each row of (A, b) assumed to carry an odd coefficient (p. 1); for the weakening, the bounds 0 ≤ x ≤ d are assumed to be rows of the system, with d_j = +∞ allowed (p. 9). Structures exploited: rows with at most two odd coefficients, EPT matrices and their duplications and identity extensions (pp. 4, 7-8). The applications are polytopes, not instance libraries: clique partitioning, asymmetric TSP, uncapacitated plant location, acyclic subgraph and linear ordering (§5, pp. 10-15). No continuous variables; no test set.
- **D4 Algorithm & implementation design**: Proof devices: (i) Theorem 1 uses Camion's theorem: a square submatrix with even row and column sums whose entries do not sum to a multiple of four yields a half-integral point and a {0,1/2} multiplier vector that separate P from P_{1/2}; Theorem 2 repeats the construction on an odd-order submatrix with row and column sums two (p. 2); (ii) Theorem 3 by two reductions, the reverse one building a system and a point whose slack vector equals the clutter weights (p. 6); (iii) binary-clutter representations through the fundamental cycles and cuts of a graph and a tree, so that the separation becomes a minimum-weight odd cycle or a minimum-weight odd cut solved by the Padberg-Rao algorithm (pp. 4-5, 7-8). Reductions of a separation instance: rows with slack at least 1 are dropped, identical rows keep their least-slack copy, the problem splits along the components of the row intersection graph, and tight unit rows such as active bounds are eliminated (pp. 6-7). Sizes: L-weakening gives O(mn^2) rows, of which only O(n^2) need be considered for a given x* (p. 9); LU-weakening gives exponentially many rows, but for each triple (i, h, k) only two are worth considering, the minimum-slack ones with even and odd right-hand side, found by an O(n) dynamic programme (pp. 9-10). For the acyclic subgraph polytope the polynomial number of base inequalities is a heuristic restriction, e.g. cycles of length at most t = 4 (p. 14).
  - Transferable technique: before solving a separation problem, shrink it by slack, duplicate, component and tight-bound reductions (pp. 6-7).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: n/a: no implementation and no experiments. The only computational statement points to Fischetti and Toth (1994), where weak odd closed-alternating-trail (CAT) inequalities sped up an ATSP branch-and-cut on hard real-world instances (p. 12; see Quotes).
- **D6 Positioning**: Theorems 4 and 5 are presented as generalising two known polynomial cases: Gerards and Schrijver (1986), for integer matrices with Σ_j |a_ij| ≤ 2 per row, and Padberg and Rao (1982), for the capacitated b-matching polytope (pp. 7-8, 16). Known facet classes are re-derived rather than criticised: Grötschel and Wakabayashi's 2-chorded odd cycle inequalities, for which "no separation algorithm for these constraints has been proposed in the literature" (p. 10), odd wheel inequalities (Chopra and Rao; Deza, Grötschel and Laurent; p. 11), Balas's weak odd CAT inequalities (p. 12), odd cycle inequalities for plant location (pp. 12-13) and Grötschel, Jünger and Reinelt's Möbius ladders (pp. 14-15); one class for the acyclic subgraph polytope is claimed new (p. 14). Bert Gerards is thanked for discussions on the binary-clutter link (p. 16).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: A six-section theorem-proof paper with the contribution summarised in the abstract (p. 1) and a road map (p. 3). Hedging is light and explicit: "To our knowledge" twice (pp. 10, 14), and the relaxation "hopefully gives a tight approximation" (p. 10). The conclusions restate the results and end on one lesson sentence on how separation should be designed (p. 16; see Quotes).
  - Transferable technique: end a theory paper with one sentence on what the results imply for practice, not only with a list of theorems (p. 16).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Theory (complexity of {0,1/2}-SEP, two polynomial cases, a polynomially separable relaxation) and exact polynomial separation for large classes of inequalities, some new (p. 16). Limits stated: NP-hard in general (p. 6); the weakened family captures only a subclass, whose tightness is hoped for and not measured (p. 10); the separation complexity of lifted odd CAT and comb inequalities is left open (p. 12); the acyclic-subgraph routine needs a heuristic cap on cycle length (p. 14). No separation is implemented or tested, and there is no future-work section.

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: the critical sub-decision of a cut loop (which cut to add) is written as an optimisation problem over CG multipliers with the slack as cost (p. 6), the formulation that the later MIP separation over the first Chvátal closure hands to a general-purpose solver (SKILL.md, Method 6 practice; card D003, pp. 3-4); here it is attacked with complexity theory and combinatorial algorithms on a weakened system (pp. 6-10), and no solver appears.
- Heuristic 1 (invert the reflex) ⚠ variant: the closing lesson reverses the reflex of one separation routine per facet class, but as a conclusion drawn after the results, not as the study's starting hypothesis (p. 16).
- Heuristic 7 (close with a lesson) ✅ evidence, an early case: the conclusions end on an "An outcome of the research" sentence (p. 16).
- New-pattern candidate: *separate over the derivation, not over the class* (pp. 6, 10-16); S009 in this batch derives its 2-matching inequalities in the same way (S009 p. 6).
- New-pattern candidate: *weaken the source rows until the separation oracle is polynomial* (at most two odd coefficients per row, pp. 8-10).

**Transferable techniques**:
- [D1] Check whether a restricted-multiplier closure depends on the polyhedron or on its description before trying to strengthen it by reformulation (p. 2).
- [D2] For any CG family with a fixed denominator, work with (A, b) mod q and the slack vector: a violated cut exists iff the minimum slack over admissible parity vectors is below 1 (pp. 5-6).
- [D4] Shrink the separation instance by slack, duplicate-row, component and tight-bound reductions (pp. 6-7).
- [D4] If every row has at most two odd coefficients, separate by a minimum-weight odd cycle with slacks as weights and right-hand-side parities as labels; otherwise weaken rows with the bounds first and keep, per triple, only the two minimum-slack weakenings found by an O(n) dynamic programme (pp. 7-10).
- [D6] Re-derive known facet classes as {0,1/2}-combinations of weakened base inequalities: it explains them and yields a polynomial separation for a superclass (pp. 10-15).
- [D4] To show that a closure is strictly weaker than integrality, build a half-integral point and a {0,1/2} multiplier vector on a forbidden submatrix (Camion) (p. 2).

**Quotes**:
- "An outcome of the research is that separation sometimes becomes easier if one does not insist in detecting violated cuts belonging to a restricted (and sometimes complicated) class of inequalities, but concentrates on the way these cuts can be derived from the original formulation." (p. 16)
- "The computational experience reported in Fischetti and Toth (1994) has shown that these inequalities are useful to speed-up the convergence of a branch-and-cut algorithm for solving hard ATS real-world instances." (p. 12)

### S005 · A branch-and-cut algorithm for the symmetric generalized traveling salesman problem (Operations Research 1997, DOI 10.1287/opre.45.3.378)
**Read level**: full. The whole 36-page author preprint ("January 1994; Revised May 1995", p. 1) was read, including Tables 6.1-6.5 (pp. 29, 33-36) and the references. Figures 1.1, 2.1, 4.1-4.3, 6.1 and 6.2 are read from their captions and the text only. The journal version (pp. 378-394) was not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. J. Salazar González (DEIOC, La Laguna), P. Toth (DEIS, Bologna); Fischetti's affiliation is printed as DEI, University of Padova (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: a routing branch-and-cut of the skill's 1989-2006 strand and the algorithmic companion of the authors' polytope paper [5] (Networks 1995, p. 30), whose facets it turns into separation routines (pp. 1, 4-5). CPLEX 2.1 serves only as the LP solver (p. 25).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: GTSP: the nodes are partitioned into clusters and a minimum-cost simple cycle must visit at least one node of each cluster; E-GTSP asks for exactly one, and the two coincide under triangular costs (p. 2). Two decisions are entangled, a node subset and a Hamiltonian cycle on it (p. 2); applications are listed after Noon and Bean (p. 2). The entry is the weakness of the natural model: (1.1)-(1.6) "heavily relies on the integrality of the y variables", so its LP relaxation can be very poor (Figure 1.1, p. 4). The companion paper's facets (GSECs, generalized combs, fans, Basic GSECs) are the material (pp. 5-8); this paper supplies exact and heuristic separation, heuristics and the overall branch-and-cut (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: A Padberg-Rinaldi-style branch-and-cut (credited, p. 19) built from: max-flow separation of the generalized subtour elimination constraints (GSECs), exact when max_v y*_v = 1 (pp. 8-11); fast heuristic separations, of which GSEC_H2 is designed to return a nested family of violated cuts per round instead of the individually most violated ones (pp. 12-14); a Lagrangian phase on a simplified model that yields an incumbent and the initial LP rows (pp. 21-23); a core edge set with pricing (pp. 23-24); Lagrangian and LP-based upper bounds (pp. 22, 24); and branching on cuts (pp. 24-25). Relative to the TSP branch-and-cut it follows, what changes is the cluster-aware separation, strengthening and branching; the pool, pricing and flow machinery are kept (pp. 9, 19, 23). A by-product: RP2 finds the best E-GTSP tour for a fixed cluster sequence by |C_h1| shortest paths in a layered network, so E-GTSP is polynomial for a fixed number of clusters, O((m−1)! n^3) (p. 18).
  - Transferable technique: when a heuristic optimises one decision with the other fixed, check whether it yields a polynomial special case (fixed cluster sequence, layered shortest paths; p. 18).
- **D3 Problem class & data model**: Pure 0-1 model with edge variables x and node variables y; GSECs in cut form (2.1)-(2.3) and in the E(S) form (2.4)-(2.6), which has fewer nonzeros for |S| ≤ ⌊n/2⌋ and is "more suitable for a cutting plane approach" (p. 5). The polyhedral results assume m ≥ 5 (p. 5). The code is written for E-GTSP with integer costs, since all test instances have triangular costs (p. 19); intra-cluster edges are dropped (p. 7). Data: two geographic instances (Spain, Europe; data on request) and the TSPLIB instances with 48 ≤ n ≤ 442, clustered by a deterministic farthest-centre procedure with m = ⌈n/5⌉ and ties to the smallest index; natural (country) clusterings for five GR instances; and a grid clustering with an average of μ = 3, 5, 7 or 10 nodes per cluster (pp. 25-26, 28).
  - Transferable technique: derive test instances from a public library by a generator specified down to its tie-breaking rule, and add a second generator to test sensitivity to the derivation (pp. 25-26, 28).
- **D4 Algorithm & implementation design**: Separation: for (2.3) a node s of maximal y* can always play node i, so at most n−1 max-flows suffice, O(n^4) instead of O(n^5); (2.2) by shrinking a cluster into a sink; (2.1) over cluster pairs (pp. 8-9). When y*_s = 1, which occurs very often, n+m−2 max-flows give a most violated GSEC, by a dominance argument on degrees of violation (p. 10); a flow-reversal argument turns one max-flow into lower bounds on the max-flow from s to every other node (p. 10). Violated cuts are replaced by their facet-defining strengthening (GSEC_BUILD, O(n), pp. 11-12). GSEC_H2 adds the fractional edges in decreasing x* order (Kruskal) and tests the GSEC of each merged component, O(n^2 log n); the motivation is the spanning tree case, where the active subtour constraints form a nested family covering all nodes (pp. 12-14); a greedy merge rule by largest violation was outperformed (p. 14). Cascade: pool, fan inequalities, GSEC_H2, GSEC_H1, then lower thresholds 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, then the exact routine and the comb heuristics, each stage returning as soon as it finds cuts (pp. 19-20), with GSEC_H1 forced every tenth call (p. 21). Cut management: a global pool in compact form; a cut slack for 5 consecutive LPs leaves the LP; if an LP takes more than 5 minutes all slack cuts of earlier nodes go; a 4-byte hash per cut speeds the distinctness test (pp. 19, 21). Each node starts from the parent's optimal basis, stored in a scratch file (p. 19). The y variables are kept in the LP, since projecting them out would make it much denser (p. 19), but are projected out in the Lagrangian model (p. 21). Lagrangian: fans and the degree constraints for h ≠ 1 are dualised; the subproblem is a 1-tree on the shrunken cluster graph; two nested subgradient loops of at most 1000 and 50 iterations (pp. 21-22). Root LP: the fans with positive multipliers plus the Basic GSECs of the best 1-tree, so the first LP bound is at least the Lagrangian bound (pp. 22-23). Pricing: a core of the edges with zero Lagrangian reduced cost plus the 5 cheapest edges per node; at most 100 columns added per round; separation only after all columns price out; reduced costs also used for fixing and removal (p. 23); a row-by-row pricing scheme, O(|E| + nq + Σ_i |J_i|) instead of O(|E'| q) column by column, at the cost of O(n^2) memory (p. 24). Branching on a parity disjunction x(δ(S)) ≤ 2k or ≥ 2k+2, with S a union of consecutive clusters along the incumbent and 2 + ε ≤ x*(δ(S)) ≤ 4 − ε, ε = 0.2, chosen by a 2-exchange estimate L(S) of the bound increase; variable branching on x*_e nearest 0.5 as fallback; depth-first search (pp. 24-25).
  - Transferable technique: seed the root LP with the rows active in the best Lagrangian solution, which guarantees a first LP bound at least as good as the Lagrangian one (pp. 22-23).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: C code; HP 9000 Series 700 Apollo and DECstation 5000/240; CPLEX 2.1; times in HP 9000/700 CPU seconds (pp. 25-26). 53 instances (Tables 6.2-6.4, pp. 33-35) and 42 TSPLIB instances under four grid clusterings (Table 6.5, p. 36). Metrics: Lagrangian LB and UB, the LP bound of the simplified model (basic-LB), root LB and UB, all as percentages of the optimum; times (total, LP, separation); nodes; separation calls; cuts; LP rows and columns; cuts per family (pp. 26-28). Results stated: the Lagrangian UB equals the optimum on 30 instances (56.6%) and lies 0.5% above it on average, while the Lagrangian LB gap averages 11.7%; basic-LB barely improves on it, so the gap is the simplified model's slack, not the subgradient's error (p. 27). All 53 instances are solved to optimality, a significant part of the time is spent in the LP solver, and about 70% need no branching (37 of 53 with 0 nodes, p. 34); natural clustering gives easier instances (pp. 27-28). The exact GSEC routine, called last, found violated cuts only on 45TS225, 9 cuts, which the authors take as proof that the heuristic separations suffice (p. 28). Grid clustering gives harder instances, and TS225 with μ = 5 needed more than 100,000 CPU seconds, the only unsolved case (pp. 29, 36). A least-squares fit time = α n^β gives β = 3.99 (total), 4.23 (LP) and 4.70 (separation) (Table 6.1, p. 29). No rival algorithm is run or compared; one run per instance; parameters are presented as "the best parameter setting resulting from our computational experience", with no separate tuning set named (p. 19); the branching-on-cuts strategy "turned out to be superior" with no table shown (p. 25).
  - Transferable technique: next to a Lagrangian bound, report the LP bound of the same relaxed model; it separates the subgradient's error from the model's slack (p. 27).
  - Transferable technique: keep the exact separator as the last stage of the cascade and count how often it finds cuts the heuristics missed (pp. 20, 28).
- **D6 Positioning**: Branch-and-cut is credited to Padberg and Rinaldi and to Jünger, Reinelt and Rinaldi (p. 19); separation devices to Padberg and Grötschel, Gomory and Hu, and Padberg and Rao (pp. 9, 14); GSEC_H2 is called "an improved version" of Grötschel and Holland's component heuristic (p. 13); the nested subgradient loops are said to follow the Held-Karp approach (p. 22). Prior GTSP work (Laporte and Nobert; Noon and Bean; Salazar; Sepehri) is listed (pp. 2-3) but neither criticised nor compared against.
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Procedures in Pascal-like pseudocode (pp. 10-11, 13-14, 19-20, 26). Parameter values come with "(say)" (5 consecutive LPs, 5 minutes; p. 19). Design choices are hedged, as depth-first search is used "(although, in general, this is not the best strategy one can choose)" (p. 25), while table summaries are firm: the authors write that GSEC_SEP's silence proves the effectiveness of their heuristics (p. 28) and call the performance "quite satisfactory" (p. 29). No conclusions section: the text ends with the regression and the acknowledgements (p. 29).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Algorithm and implementation (separation, pricing, branching), heuristics, and computation on instances of up to 442 nodes (p. 1). Limits stated: the exact GSEC routine is exact only when max y* = 1 (p. 11); exact separation of generalized 2-matching inequalities is polynomial but too time-consuming, so only heuristics are used (p. 14); row-by-row pricing needs O(n^2) memory and may become impractical on very large instances (p. 24); depth-first is in general not the best strategy (p. 25); the Lagrangian LB is poor (p. 27); TS225 under grid clustering is unsolved (p. 36). No future directions.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: loop-level reasoning in 1995: a cut loop that adds the individually most violated cuts needs many iterations, so GSEC_H2 returns a nested family spanning the graph (pp. 12-14; see Quotes). The cure changes what the separator returns per round, not the point the loop separates.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: what lives in the LP is decided by density and bound, not by a variable's role: y is kept for sparsity (p. 19) and projected out only in the Lagrangian model, whose LP bound is measured and found slack (basic-LB, pp. 21, 27); most edge columns stay out of the LP through a core and pricing (pp. 23-24); intra-cluster edges are dropped for E-GTSP (p. 7).
- Method 4 ✗ contradiction (dated, 1995): a single code is judged against the optimum, with no rival; parameter settings come from computational experience with no named tuning set (p. 19); a branching strategy is called superior without a table (p. 25).
- Method 3: no repeated runs or variability anywhere (pp. 25-29); no link.
- Heuristic 7 (close with a lesson) ✗ contradiction (dated): no conclusions section, no lesson and no status label; the text ends with the running-time regression (p. 29).
- New-pattern candidate: *dual information steers the primal side*: Lagrangian tours refined by RP2 (p. 22), the LP's edges at 1 completed by insertion (p. 24), zero Lagrangian reduced costs seed the core (p. 23); also in S009 (pp. 14, 16) and S012 (pp. 10, 17).
- New-pattern candidate: *core plus pricing, never frozen* (pp. 23-24); also in S009 (pp. 15-16) and S012 (p. 11).
- New-pattern candidate: *separation cascade with early exit, the exact separator last as an audit* (pp. 19-21, 28); also in S009 (p. 16).

**Transferable techniques**:
- [D2] Look for a polynomial special case inside a heuristic that fixes one of two entangled decisions (layered network for a fixed cluster sequence, p. 18).
- [D3] Specify instance generators completely, ties included, and test sensitivity with a second generator (pp. 25-26, 28).
- [D4] Order separation from cheap to exact (pool, fans in O(mn + |E*|), spanning-forest heuristic, cluster max-flow heuristic, lower thresholds 0.1/0.01/0.001, exact max-flow last) and stop at the first stage that returns cuts (pp. 19-21).
- [D4] Return a nested family per separation round: add fractional edges in Kruskal order and test the cut of every merged component (pp. 12-14).
- [D4] Seed the root LP with the rows active in the best Lagrangian solution (here fans with positive multipliers and the Basic GSECs of the 1-tree) (pp. 22-23).
- [D4] Price columns row by row when rows are sparse and many columns must be priced (p. 24); hash each cut's compact form into 4 bytes for duplicate tests (p. 21).
- [D4] Branch on a parity cut x(δ(S)) ≤ 2k or ≥ 2k+2, with S built from consecutive clusters of the incumbent and chosen by a 2-exchange estimate of the cost increase (pp. 24-25).
- [D5] Report the relaxed model's LP bound beside the Lagrangian bound (p. 27), the exact separator's hit count after the heuristics (p. 28), and a fitted exponent per time component, time = α n^β (p. 29).

### S012 · Algorithms for railway crew management (Mathematical Programming 1997, DOI 10.1007/bf02614314)
**Read level**: partial. The prose of all 20 preprint pages was read ("Preprint submitted to Elsevier Science", June, year unreadable; p. 1). The text layer stores digits and most punctuation as control characters under at least two font encodings (body text and reference list); commas, parentheses, quotation marks and the ff and ffi ligatures can be recognised as such, but no number can be read. Not read, therefore: every figure in the paper (instance sizes, operational limits, time limits, both result tables on pp. 12 and 18, the competition outcomes, the reference years). The card states no number from the paper. Quotes are given without the lost punctuation. Flagged for re-OCR. The journal version (pp. 125-141) was not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. Caprara, P. Toth, D. Vigo (DEIS, Bologna) and P. L. Guida (Ferrovie dello Stato SpA); Fischetti's affiliation is printed as DMI, University of Udine (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the railway strand of the 1989-2006 period: an overview, co-signed by an FS author, of the two codes the authors built for FS, the set-covering heuristic CFT for crew scheduling and the crew-rostering relaxation and heuristic of Caprara, Fischetti, Toth and Vigo (pp. 9-11, 14-17), each tested in an FS competition (FASTER, p. 11; FARO, p. 18). No general-purpose MIP solver appears.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The application comes first: deregulation and privatisation push European railways towards optimisation (p. 1), and FS's planning problems are large (sizes unreadable, p. 3). The problem is decomposed, as in practice, into crew scheduling (duties covering the trips) and crew rostering (duties sequenced into cyclic rosters) (p. 3), for three stated reasons: crews return to their home depot, short and long work segments obey different rules, and a global approach is unlikely to be implemented because of its difficulty and the planners' "unwillingness to change their actual practice" (pp. 3-4). In urban transit the number of crews follows from the scheduling phase, whereas in railways a clever sequencing of the duties in the rostering phase yields considerable savings, so the scheduling costs should anticipate rostering; this "suggests the opportunity of feedback between the two phases" (p. 4). For crew scheduling the question came from FS, which promoted new techniques for very large pure set-covering instances, leading to a joint project with the authors (p. 9).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Both subproblems are cast as covering the nodes of a directed graph by circuits (pp. 4-5), with two model families: arc variables with exponentially many infeasible-arc-set constraints, also indexed by crew type (pp. 6-7), and set partitioning over feasible circuits with side constraints (pp. 7-8). The choice rule: set partitioning when feasible circuits cover few nodes and their feasibility depends on the whole node sequence (railway crew scheduling); the arc model when the main rules concern two consecutive items, which arc costs absorb (railway crew rostering) (p. 8). Scheduling: all feasible duties are generated beforehand ("pairing generation"); since crews may travel as passengers, partitioning is relaxed to covering, so only inclusion-maximal duties among those of equal cost are kept (pp. 8-9). CFT, relative to the Lagrangian SCP heuristics it follows (Balas and Ho onwards, p. 10): Lagrangian costs from near-optimal multipliers become scores for a greedy, the greedy is run over a sequence of near-optimal multiplier vectors, high-score duties are fixed and the three phases (subgradient, heuristic, fixing) are iterated, followed by refining (pp. 10-11); a core of low-Lagrangian-cost duties is updated by pricing inside the Lagrangian optimisation instead of being frozen (p. 11). Rostering: a relaxation RP on a multigraph with direct, simple-rest and double-rest arcs plus integer counts r and z of rests (pp. 14-16); its Lagrangian relaxation splits into an assignment problem and two scalar problems (pp. 16-17); a constructive heuristic builds one roster at a time with a score taken from the lower-bound increase, updating the bound parametrically, and a refining step rebuilds the last rosters with perturbed or retuned parameters (p. 17).
- **D3 Problem class & data model**: Binary covering of graph nodes by circuits. The arc model needs costs additive over arcs and its LP relaxation is weak when the operational constraints are tight (p. 6); the set-partitioning model allows any circuit cost and a typically much tighter LP, at the price of very many columns and possibly column generation, given an effective pricing procedure (p. 8). Scheduling is a pure set covering problem (p. 9); the exact SCP codes of the literature handle up to a few hundred trips and a few thousand duties, so FS instances need heuristics (p. 9). Rostering rules: limits on special duties per week, weekly rests that are simple or double, the share of double rests and the average rest time, limits over cyclic groups of days, and break rules around overnight duties (pp. 12-14; all numeric limits unreadable). Data: FS instances from the FASTER (scheduling) and FARO (rostering) competitions (pp. 11, 18).
  - Transferable technique: if the application tolerates over-covering (here deadheading), relax partitioning to covering and generate only inclusion-maximal columns among those of equal cost (p. 9).
- **D4 Algorithm & implementation design**: CFT: the Lagrangian subproblem is solved by the sign of the Lagrangian costs (p. 10); the greedy uses Lagrangian rather than original costs; it is applied for several near-optimal multiplier vectors because "almost equivalent nearoptimal Lagrangian multipliers can produce SCP solutions of substantially" different quality and the bound does not correlate strictly with solution quality (p. 10); an aggressive step-size policy speeds the subgradient phase (p. 11); fixing yields a smaller SCP on which the procedure is iterated (p. 11); the core is not frozen, since an optimal solution typically contains duties that are individually worse than others (p. 11). Rostering bound: v(RP), in minutes, always corresponds to an integer number of days, so r bounds the number of weeks of an optimal solution (p. 16); the Lagrangian subproblem is an assignment problem on the cheapest Lagrangian cost among the three arc types of each pair, plus scalar choices of r and z (pp. 16-17); a tight bound is obtained from only two assignment problems (p. 17). Heuristic: the initial duty is preceded by a rest; the successor is chosen by a score; the bound is updated parametrically; the roster is closed when possible; construction stops at a cap on weeks (p. 17). No proof beyond the bound argument.
  - Transferable technique: relax the counting side constraints of a sequencing relaxation in Lagrangian fashion so that the bound splits into an assignment problem plus scalar subproblems (pp. 16-17).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: FASTER: Table 1 gives per instance the trips, duties, density, the subgradient lower bound, CFT's value and the best value found by other methods, under time limits on a PC for three instances and on an HP workstation for the rest (p. 11); FARO: Table 2 gives the duties, the simple and the Lagrangian lower bounds, the heuristic's weeks and its time on a Pentium PC (p. 18). The stated conclusions are that CFT provides near-optimal solutions within limited time on very large instances, with an average lower-bound gap whose value is unreadable (p. 11), and that a number of rostering instances is solved to proven optimality within a number of minutes, both counts unreadable (p. 18); CFT is also said to outperform previously published methods on literature instances (counts unreadable, p. 10). Baselines: the other competitors' best solutions (p. 11) and the simple lower bound (p. 18). The refinement uses random perturbation (p. 17), but no seeds or repeated runs are reported. Not read: every number in Tables 1 and 2.
- **D6 Positioning**: A survey of crew and vehicle management (p. 2), of SCP methods, exact (Beasley; Beasley and Jörnsten; Balas and Carrera) and heuristic (Balas and Ho; Beasley; Fisher and Kedia; Ceria, Nobili and Sassano; Wedelin; Lorena and Lopes; Beasley and Chu; Jacobs and Brusco) (pp. 9-10), and of rostering (p. 12). The railway case is positioned against airline and urban transit (pp. 4, 9, 12); FS instances are said to be orders of magnitude larger than typical airline ones (p. 9; the factor is unreadable). Details are deferred to the authors' own reports (pp. 9, 14, 19).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: An overview ("we give an outline", abstract, p. 1) that defers the details to technical reports (pp. 9, 14, 17). The modelling advice is framed as experience ("According to our experience", p. 8). Results are claimed firmly (the rostering table is said to show the effectiveness of the approach, p. 18). No conclusions section: the paper ends with the rostering results (p. 18).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: A modelling guide and the description of two FS codes and their competition tests (pp. 1, 4, 11, 18). Limits stated: decomposition is a compromise forced by difficulty and practice (p. 4); feedback between the phases is suggested, not implemented here (p. 4); the core definition is critical (p. 11); exact methods are out of reach for scheduling at FS size (p. 9). No future work.

**Method links**:

### S009 · Solving the orienteering problem through branch-and-cut (INFORMS Journal on Computing 1998, DOI 10.1287/ijoc.10.2.133)
**Read level**: full. The whole 31-page author preprint (undated; references up to 1997; an early version was presented at EURO XIII, Glasgow, July 1994, p. 3) was read, including Tables 1-7 (pp. 25-31) and the references. Figures 2.1 and 2.2 are read from their captions and the text. The journal version (pp. 133-148) was not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. J. Salazar González (DEIOC, La Laguna), P. Toth (DEIS, Bologna); Fischetti's affiliation is printed as DEI, University of Padova (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the second routing branch-and-cut with the same co-authors and much of S005's machinery (pool, pricing, max-flow GSEC separation, an LP-guided heuristic), plus two devices driven by the incumbent, conditional cuts and branch cover cuts (pp. 9, 17). CPLEX 3.0 is the LP solver (p. 18).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The orienteering problem (OP): from a depot, a simple cycle whose total travel time is at most t0 and whose collected prize is maximal (p. 2). Prior work is listed: heuristics (Tsiligirides; Golden, Levy and Vohra; Golden, Wang and Liu; Chao, Golden and Wasil), exact enumeration (Laporte and Martello; Ramesh, Yoon and Karwan), an LP bound (Leifer and Rosenwein) and a branch-and-cut by Gendreau, Laporte and Semet (p. 2). The entry is computational rather than polyhedral: the TSP polytope and the OP polytope are disjoint whenever the time constraint is active, so known facet results do not transfer, and a facet analysis is judged difficult and left out; the inequalities are justified by their effect on the test bed (p. 5).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: A model with edge and node binaries, a knapsack-type time constraint, degree equations and GSECs (pp. 3-4), plus five families: logical constraints, which make the integrality of y redundant (p. 5); 2-matching inequalities (p. 6); knapsack covers, extended covers and a new cycle cover inequality using that the chosen edges form a cycle (p. 6); and new path inequalities that combine the cycle and knapsack relaxations (pp. 7-8). The new concept is the conditional cut: an inequality that may cut off feasible solutions but is satisfied by every feasible solution with value greater than the incumbent LB (p. 9); for x(T) − y(V(T)) ≤ −1 it suffices that no solution better than LB lies inside T, e.g. T = E(S) with prize p(S) ≤ LB (p. 9). At the root, when every separation fails, a branch cover cut excludes the current support graph G*, which is stored; when the root is fathomed, the union of the stored support graphs is solved by a second branch-and-cut with ordinary branching and no branch cover cuts (pp. 17-18). What changes relative to TSP branch-and-cut: knapsack-based and incumbent-based cuts and the two-stage root; the pool, pricing and branching framework is kept (pp. 15-18).
  - Transferable technique: when separation fails, cut off the current support with one conditional inequality, store the region and solve it later as a restricted instance (pp. 17-18).
- **D3 Problem class & data model**: Pure 0-1 model; integer nonnegative prizes, times and t0; at least one feasible solution assumed, checkable by a shortest cycle through the depot; cycles of length 2 excluded and handled by enumeration (pp. 3-4). The time constraint is a knapsack over the edges, exploited by cover separation (p. 6). Test classes: Class I, 15 OP and VRP instances from the literature (Tsiligirides's OP21, OP32, OP33 with times multiplied by 100; TSPLIB VRP instances; Christofides, Mingozzi and Toth), with customer demands read as prizes (p. 18); Class II, the TSPLIB TSP instances up to 400 nodes under three prize generations (unit prizes, pseudo-random prizes by a stated formula, and prizes growing with the distance from the depot, the hardest) (pp. 18-19); Class III, Laporte and Martello's random generator; Class IV, Gendreau, Laporte and Semet's generator (pp. 19, 21). The time bound is t0 = ⌈α v(TSP)⌉ with α a parameter and the v(TSP) values given (p. 19).
- **D4 Algorithm & implementation design**: Validity and correctness devices: the logical constraints leave only y ∈ {0, 1/2, 1} and exclude 1/2 (p. 5); path inequalities are proved valid by contradiction, following a violating solution edge by edge (pp. 7-8); simplifying overlapping teeth never decreases the violation (p. 11); a violated path inequality must lie in the support graph, which bounds the enumeration (p. 11); the two-stage scheme converges because branch cover cuts are inhibited in the second round (p. 17); fractional points are exhibited that violate a new inequality but none of the earlier ones (pp. 5, 7-8). Separation: GSECs by max-flow per node in decreasing y*, raising the capacity of edge [1, v] after a hit so the same cut is not regenerated, and two minimum cuts read from each max-flow, the source side reused for conditional cuts (pp. 9-10, 13); 2-matching inequalities with Kruskal components as handles and greedy teeth (pp. 10-11); covers by solving the knapsack separation problem with specialised codes after fixing the variables at 0 or 1, the set made minimal before extending (p. 12); cycle covers from fundamental cycles of a maximum-weight spanning tree (pp. 12-13); conditional cuts by enumerating OP on the support graph within a time limit TL = 5 TS, TS the time of the last separation round (pp. 13, 16). Loop: the first LP holds all y, the initial heuristic's edges and the 5 cheapest edges per node (p. 15); artificial variables if the LP is infeasible (p. 15); at most 100 columns priced in per round, until the LP value is a valid bound (pp. 15-16); purging by reduced cost and by slackness (slack in the last 5 iterations or above 0.01) and root fixing by reduced cost (p. 16); separation in the order pool, logical, GSEC, 2-matching, cover, path, conditional, leaving at the first family that yields cuts (p. 16); branching when the bound improves by less than 0.001 over 10 iterations (p. 16); the heuristic every fifth separation with edge weights w_e := x*_e (p. 16). Second stage: strong branching over the 15 variables closest to 0.5 with score 0.75 UB0 + 0.25 UB1 (pp. 17-18); in the first stage, branching after 5 branch cover cuts per node against tailing off (p. 18).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: C code; DECstation 5000/240 and HP Apollo 9000/720 (80 MHz; 59 SPECs, 58 MIPS, 18 MFlops); CPLEX 3.0; a time limit of 18,000 s, after which the best value is taken as optimal and %-UB becomes an upper bound on the error (pp. 18, 20). Metrics: root time, %-LB and %-UB at the root, nodes, cuts, optimum, share of nodes visited, total time; for Class I also LP and separation time and cuts per family (pp. 19-20, Table 2 p. 26). Coverage: Class I at α = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 (45 runs, one at the time limit: gil262 at α = 0.25, p. 25); Class II at α = 0.5 (44 instances under each of three generations, 10 of 132 runs at the time limit, pp. 27-29); α = 0.25 and 0.75 were run "with comparable results" but are not shown (p. 20); Class III, averages and maxima over 10 instances for 11 sizes up to n = 500 and four values of α, all solved except 5 instances (pp. 20-21, 30); Class IV, averages over 5 instances (p. 21, 31). Comparisons: Laporte and Martello's own FORTRAN code, supplied by its authors (p. 22), run on the Class III instances with n = 25: 0.1 s on average at α = 0.2, 77.2 s at 0.4, more than 2 hours at 0.6 and none solved within 5 hours at 0.8 (p. 20); Gendreau, Laporte and Semet through their published results, their 10,000 s SUN limit converted by Dongarra's speed ratio of 1.8 into 5,555 HP seconds, their solved counts set against the authors' counts at both limits, and averages taken over solved instances as in their paper; root LB and UB ratios of 0.02% and 0.28% against their 3.59% and 1.74% (pp. 21-22). The two-stage scheme is said to perform typically better than, though not to dominate, the classical one, with no table (p. 18; see Quotes).
