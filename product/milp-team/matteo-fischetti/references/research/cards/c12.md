### S150 · GPU-accelerated wind farm layout search with a distilled endogenous wake model (EndoWake) (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2610.07844)

**Read level**: full. All of pp. 1–34 (abstract, §§1–8, funding, acknowledgments, AI declaration, CRediT, data availability, appendices A–C); references pp. 34–40 skimmed. Version read: arXiv:2610.07844v3 (4 Nov 2026, p. 1); title and authors (Martina Fischetti, Matteo Fischetti) match on p. 1.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Martina Fischetti (University of Seville, IMUS; first author). CRediT: Martina Fischetti conceptualization, data curation, funding acquisition, investigation, methodology, validation, original draft; Matteo Fischetti conceptualization, formal analysis, methodology, software, review and editing (p. 28). · **Position in trajectory**: second paper of the 2026 EndoWake line, after the model paper [24] (arXiv:2609.40157, p. 35) and before S162, which cites this one as 2026b; it continues the Fischetti wind-farm line of refs. [11–13], carried into industrial practice in [2] (p. 4). An earlier version was submitted to Applied Energy, whose two reviewers' reports shaped the controls and comparisons of §§6–7 (p. 28).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: Wind farm layout optimization under the conditions of an award (cardinality, no-go areas, site costs, sheltering obstacles); the bottleneck is the wake computation: engineering wakes are fast but approximate, and the RANS reference of the paper costs 405 s per wind direction for one layout (p. 1). The entry is the authors' own model: EndoWake [24] makes the wind speed of every grid cell a continuous variable linked to its upwind cells by linear constraints, so the wake sits in the same MILP as the binary site decisions and the planner's constraints (p. 2). The question is what EndoWake is worth to a planner, framed by the optimizer's curse (a search finds and exploits small localized optimistic errors) and by a recent result in which the most accurate wake model did not give the best RANS-validated layouts (pp. 2–3). The two-part answer is announced on p. 3: a more faithful field does not by itself give better layouts; the throughput pays when the search is built for it.
- **D2 Core idea and design**: Three moves (pp. 3–4). (i) *Distillation*: the four numbers of EndoWake (α, β, γ and the source amplitude) are fitted once on the deficit field of one isolated turbine, from a RANS run or an analytical wake; since the single wake is a convolution power of the stencil, the best amplitude is in closed form and the fit is a three-parameter derivative-free search that takes seconds (eq. (7), p. 10). (ii) *Batch evaluation*: the maximal-field property inherited from [24] makes the evaluation of a layout one forward sweep with five multiply-adds per cell and no data-dependent branching, so batches of layouts run on a GPU at 1,093,103 layouts per second per direction (pp. 8, 12). (iii) *Screen-and-confirm*: EndoWake fitted on PyWake ranks a random sample of the neighbourhood (up to about 153 thousand moves); PyWake evaluates the head of the ranking on the current incumbent and accepts a move only if it improves the PyWake value; the list length is L = ⌈q rP/rE⌉ from the two evaluation rates measured inside the clock, so that confirming costs about as much time as ranking; a ranking serves a whole round, the sample doubles only after a round without acceptance, rejections are keyed to the incumbent at which they were observed, and a certified variant scans the whole final neighbourhood by PyWake (pp. 20–21; Figure 5, p. 23). Kept from [24]: the model, the MILP with its valid inequalities, the calibration recipe (p. 3). Changed: the role of EndoWake, from the model a search optimizes to the model that decides which moves the trusted model examines (p. 3).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: A binary site-selection MILP: x_c ∈ {0,1} on allocatable cells, continuous speed w and power z per wind scenario; propagation inequalities (3); a wake source (4) with the big-M term V_s(1 − x_c); the power curve by SOS2 interpolation with B = 8 breakpoints, used in the MILP only, while searches score with the exact curve; minimum separation as clique inequalities on the conflict graph and cardinality bounds (6) (pp. 7–8). Every block is an inequality that relaxes in the direction of the objective; Proposition 1 of [24] (a greatest feasible field, computed by one sweep in topological order) makes that relaxation cost nothing (p. 8). Size: |A| binaries and O(|S||C|) continuous variables and constraints, against Θ(|A|²) interaction terms of interference-matrix models; any condition written on x (zone caps, non-convex exclusions, site costs, integer counts) joins (6) without touching the field (p. 8). Physics assumptions named: a finite-support kernel widens as the square root of distance where wakes widen linearly, recovery decays exponentially where wakes decay polynomially, and blockage, shear, veer, meandering and added turbulence are absent (p. 10); the kernel depends on cell size, so a layout found at one spacing must be re-evaluated at another (p. 10), and it is sensitive to turbulence, so it must be fitted at the site's turbulence (p. 12). Data: Lillgrund (48 SWT-2.3-93, public SCADA deficits) and Horns Rev 1 (80 V80); an idealized cubic power curve to avoid proprietary data (pp. 9, 16, 26).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: One model, three solvers (pp. 13–14): Gurobi on the SOS2 model on square grids 8×8 to 20×20, which closes only some seeds of the smallest grid; a concave-envelope relaxation, itself a MILP, whose dual bound certifies every ladder layout within 8.75% of the exact optimum (proof in the technical note); a vanilla greedy plus one-exchange local search with the exact O(|C|) evaluator, within 0.169% of the MILP's layouts; Hexaly as the independent corroboration at sizes where no bound closes. Numerical safeguards: Gurobi's NumericFocus at its highest value, because in the big-M implication (4) a binary within the integrality tolerance leaves a slack that the constant multiplies and the substitution chains carry downstream; the setting costs 1.5× in time (pp. 31–32). Single precision only where EndoWake ranks: 0 of 496 checked pairwise comparisons change sign (p. 13). An improving move must gain more than 10⁻⁹ (p. 33); one seed's trajectory was re-evaluated with PyWake through an independent code path (p. 33). Proof devices are stated in the paper and proved in the replication package's technical note: the convolution-power closed form (7) (p. 10), the concave-envelope dual bound (p. 13); the maximal-field property is inherited (p. 8).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: (a) Side constraints: on 12 instances of a 20×20 grid with a noise-limit knapsack constraint the local search is better on average, but 2 instances lose more than 5% (worst −11.3%) because a one-exchange move cannot add a turbine against a binding side constraint; the MILP declares its gap (up to 59.4%), the local search does not (p. 14). (b) Industrial sizes 100×100 to 200×200 (40,000 cells), 3 seeds and 600 s per run, spreads over seeds reported instead of a best-of (Table 4, pp. 14–15). (c) A RANS-judged bench on Lillgrund: three pipelines (PyWake; EndoWake at ∆ = D and at D/3), five seeds, 1,800 s each, sharing start, budget, power curve, calibration data and the judge; the judge is a RANS run on each final layout at twelve directions drawn independently of every search (pp. 15–16, 31). Phase test: arm B gains +4.06% at the sector centers and −1.48% at the six directions 15° away (p. 16). At the random judge the paired margins are −0.78 [−2.36, 0.80] and −0.80 [−3.40, 1.80] points: five paired starts detect no difference (p. 19). (d) Main result: five development seeds used to design the search, twenty unseen seeds run only after the configuration and the list policy were frozen (p. 20). At 8 m/s screen-and-confirm gains +4.28% in 38 s against +4.33% in 294 s for PyWake alone (paired −0.05, standard error 0.07; 7.9×; certified 3.0×); on the annual energy 14.9× (certified 4.7×); a TOPFARM SLSQP baseline from the same starts; list ×0.5 to ×4 and one move per ranking; on Horns Rev 1 (10 seeds) the screen is 0.29 points below PyWake alone in every seed (Table 6, p. 22; pp. 24–27). The speed-up is decomposed on the development seeds: a full ranking after every move gives 1.0×, one ranking per round 4.5×, single precision 6.9×, sampling 9.3× (p. 24). The annual-energy protocol was fixed in writing, with a reserve-seed rule (twenty more seeds if the half-width of the paired interval exceeded 0.10 points; it was 0.08) (p. 33). Times are warmed wall times with the rate measurement on the clock; charging each run's setup gives 14.5×; two node groups gave 12.6× and 17.7× (pp. 21, 34). Hardware and software versions are logged (p. 31).
- **D6 Positioning**: Table 1 places the work among methods that approximate or accelerate an expensive evaluation (multifidelity correction, neural surrogates, closed-form rose averages, fast gradients, interference matrices) by what is approximated, what is kept exact and what cost is reduced (p. 5). Multi-fidelity preselection and the gap between fidelity and decision quality are credited as having precedents (Jasa et al.; TOPFARM's multi-fidelity origin; manifold mapping; pre-screening; a graph-network correction of the directional error), and the claim is limited to their combination in one search (pp. 3, 6). The tools other than PyWake and TOPFARM are positioned and not run, and no running-time claim is made about them (p. 6). The directional quadrature error is credited to practitioners, Martina Fischetti's 2017 thesis [32] among them (pp. 2, 16, 36); the spatially localized surrogate bias is claimed as not reported before, to the authors' knowledge (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Four roles are defined before any experiment: evaluator, guide, judge, arm, and an arm comparison is said to measure complete pipelines, not the wake model in isolation (p. 9). The reference is called the RANS reference and never the CFD, because it is one closure on one mesh (p. 10). A paragraph headed *What is inherited, and what is new* (p. 3). Hedges sit where the claim sits: the mechanism of the spatial bias is attributed as a hypothesis, not a measured mechanism (p. 17); the order of the means is descriptive (pp. 19, 27); Hexaly's agreement is corroboration, not a proof of optimality (p. 14). A colourful name used in an earlier version (*the sirens of the rose*) is reported as dropped (p. 15). The abstract gives every speed-up with and without the certificate (p. 1). AI declaration: Claude as programming assistant, adversarial reader of the mathematical statements and editor; Codex as adversarial reviewer (p. 28). The original draft is Martina Fischetti's (p. 28), so the prose is weak evidence of Matteo Fischetti's own voice.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contributions: a distillation recipe and a GPU evaluator; two diagnostics of a discretized objective against a RANS judge; screen-and-confirm; the code (pp. 3–4). Limits, stated concretely: one site except §7; RANS at one inflow and one speed; the judge's closure verified on one published single wake; margins in farm power at one speed, not annual energy (p. 27); at equal hardware the distillate is not faster than PyWake (27 against 58 layouts per second on one core, 1,152 against 1,317 on thirty processes), so the speed-ups are those of a GPU added to PyWake's processes (pp. 12–13); on Horns Rev 1 the screen's local optima fall short (p. 27). An implementation error of the previous submission (the fP factor left out of the production term of the ε equation, compensated by CR = 60) was corrected and the calibration, judgments and controls recomputed with CR = 4.5 (pp. 10, 30). The replication package is a Zenodo deposit to be released at publication, with a private link for reviewers (p. 29). Future work: a speed-dependent reduction factor, representable in the MILP by one more piecewise-linear block per site; a judge outside the RANS closure (TurbOPark, coarse LES) and the Lillgrund lidar transects (pp. 27–28).

**Method links**: Method 4 ✅ evidence: arms share start, budget, power curve, calibration data and judge; the judge is called by no search; development and unseen seeds are separated and the unseen ones run after freezing; the list length comes from measured rates and is not tuned; the equal-hardware comparison that goes against the method is printed (pp. 9, 12–13, 15–16, 20–21). Method 3 ✅ evidence: seeds in every table, paired t-intervals, spreads instead of best-of, a reserve-seed rule fixed in writing, and the verdict that five paired starts resolve nothing (pp. 14, 19, 33). Method 3 ⚠ variant: the content-free perturbation is applied to the judge, not to the search: two judges that differ only by a 15° shift expose an effect of phase alone (p. 16), and the annual-energy layouts are re-evaluated with the rose rotated by half a degree and the quadrature step halved (p. 26). Method 2 ⚠ variant: outside cut loops, the trusted oracle (PyWake) stays and the loop changes which moves it is asked to evaluate; the speed-up comes from the query policy (one ranking per round, sampling, single precision), not from a faster evaluator (pp. 12–13, 20–21, 24). Method 6 ⚠ variant: the stock MILP solver is run on the model as written first, then a relaxation for certificates; beyond 20×20 the certificate is given up and the tool is a heuristic checked only by a second heuristic (pp. 13–14). Method 5 ✅ evidence: the authors' own earlier implementation error is found, corrected and every dependent number recomputed (pp. 10, 30). Method 1 ✅ evidence (weak, inherited from [24]): a slim formulation, O(|S||C|) continuous variables against Θ(|A|²) interaction terms (p. 8). Heuristic 10 evidence: the independent code path, NumericFocus, and every final layout checked off the clock against its whole neighbourhood (pp. 31–34). Heuristic 7 evidence: status labels (hypothesis, descriptive, corroboration) (pp. 14, 17, 19). New-pattern candidates: **screen-and-confirm with a rate-balanced list** (pp. 20–21, 23); **fidelity is not decision quality, so judge with a model no search calls** (pp. 1–3, 15–16); **phase sensitivity of a discretized objective** (p. 16); **charge the certificate to the clock and report both speed-ups** (pp. 1, 21–22).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Name the roles before the experiments (evaluator, guide, judge, arm) and state that an arm comparison measures a whole pipeline (p. 9).
- Judge final solutions with a reference model that no search calls, at sample points drawn independently of every search (pp. 15–16, 31).
- Phase test for any objective discretized on a grid of directions (or scenarios): re-judge at shifted sample points and report half the difference as the method's phase sensitivity (p. 16).
- Size a screening list from rates measured inside the clock, L = ⌈q rP/rE⌉, so confirmation and screening take similar time; nothing tuned on results (p. 21).
- Key every rejected move to the incumbent at which it was rejected and re-evaluate it at later incumbents (p. 23).
- Report each speed-up twice: with and without the proof of local optimality charged to the timed search (Table 6, p. 22).
- Design on development seeds, freeze, then run the unseen seeds; fix in writing a reserve-seed rule tied to the interval half-width (pp. 20, 33).
- Print the equal-hardware comparison that goes against your method (p. 12).
- Decompose a speed-up into its sources on the development seeds (p. 24).
- When a big-M implication chains slacks downstream, raise the solver's numerical emphasis and report its time cost (pp. 31–32).

**Quotes**:
- "the fidelity of a wake model is not the quality of the layouts optimized under it" (p. 1)
- "The MILP fails for lack of time and says so (largest declared gap 59.4%), whereas the local search fails silently, at a local optimum indistinguishable from success." (p. 14)

### S161 · Just sort it! A note on the LP behind rank-one anti-loop edits (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2611.21432)

**Read level**: full. All of pp. 1–4 (abstract, §§1–4, Role of AI, data and code, references). Version read: arXiv:2611.21432v1 [cs.LG] (16 Nov 2026, p. 1); title and sole author (Matteo Fischetti, DEI Padova) match on p. 1.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none. The Role of AI section states the division of labor: the idea is the author's, who also supervised, decided what to run and what to publish and takes responsibility; everything else (reading the material, the choice of the sorting key and of the dose, both protocols, the code, the runs on the cluster, the statistics and the text, including that section) was done by Claude (Anthropic; Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 in Claude Code); the work took one day, 16 November 2026 (p. 4). · **Position in trajectory**: the last of the November 2026 notes; machine learning, not MILP; the only MILP-side content is reading an LP solution and replacing the solve.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: Weight-space programming (AHA, [1]) removes the thinking loops of a small reasoning model by a rank-one edit W′ = W + uv⊤ of an MLP output matrix; v is the solution of a sparse LP from a blog post [2] (abstract; p. 1). The question is whether the LP is needed (abstract). The entry is a measurement of the LP: per layer 12,000 constraint rows, 3,584 variables plus a slack per row and about 43 million nonzeros, solved twice for each of 20 layers, 3 to 11 s per layer with the GPU PDHG shipped with AHA, about 2.2 minutes in all; the first solve's optimum is very sparse (6 to 189 nonzeros out of 3,584) (p. 1). Then a reading of the solution: it looks like a separation model but does not separate (pushes of −0.79 and −0.70 on the two token classes at layer 15), and the public sweep of [2] leaves the accuracy unchanged when δ is multiplied by 2 or 8 while ε = 0.25 destroys it; hence the LP synthesizes a bias along u (pp. 1–2). The author's stated idea is that the LP looked like a knapsack in disguise (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: Replace the solve by a sort: compute the mean m_j and standard deviation σ_j of each neuron's activation on the fit rows (with the LP's class weights), take the k = 8 neurons with smallest σ_j/|m_j| and set v_j = c/(k m_j), where the dose c is the mean push of the LP solution on the same rows (sort_k8) (p. 2). The dose is borrowed from the LP on purpose so that the two edits differ in the neurons only (p. 2). Control knap_k8: the 8 neurons with largest |m_j|, the continuous knapsack rule with variance ignored (p. 2). The second protocol adds sortd_k8, with the dose −δ that AHA computes from u before any LP, so it takes nothing from the LP (p. 3). No solver is involved: two moments per neuron and a sort (p. 2).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MILP forms n/a. The object is an LP: minimize Σ|v_j| + C Σ s_t subject to v⊤h_t ≤ −δ + s_t on looping tokens, |v⊤h_t| ≤ ε + s_t on productive tokens, |v_j| ≤ 10, s ≥ 0, followed by a restriction to the 8 neurons with largest |v_j| std(h_j) and a second solve (p. 1). The structure exploited is a set of neurons that are almost always active with near-constant activation (p. 2). Model and data: Qwen3.5-0.8B with layers 15 and 19 fixed; MATH-500 and the first 500 GSM8K test problems; one sample per problem (p. 2). No correctness argument is offered; the note states that the sort does not solve the LP (abstract; p. 2).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: The baseline is AHA's own GPU PDHG (p. 1); the replacement needs one pass over the activations of the fit tokens (p. 1). The three vectors are compared on the fit rows by mean push per class, coefficient of variation of the push, hit rate on the loop constraint, mean hinge and shared support (Table 2, p. 3). The sort's hinge is about 5 times the LP's and at layer 19 it satisfies the loop constraint on 76% of the rows against 91%, yet the neurons it shares with the LP support carry 95% (layer 15) and 88% (layer 19) of the LP's mean push; the other LP neurons shape the hinge term, not the dose (p. 2). Proofs: n/a.
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: The unmodified AHA pipeline at commit e6e94ff5 on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell; protocol, arms and verdict rule committed before any number was produced; a non-inferiority margin of 5 points declared before the runs (abstract; p. 2). Seed 0 (Table 1, p. 2): unedited 9.5%, LP edit 67.4%, sort_k8 67.0% (paired −0.4 [−2.9, +2.1], interval from the discordant pairs), knap_k8 66.2% (−1.2 [−3.7, +1.3]); the sort and the LP disagree on 166 problems, 81 won by the sort and 85 by the LP. A second protocol, declared after seeing seed 0 and before its runs, rebuilds the edits from the stored u and v and evaluates them on three new sampling seeds, intervals clustered by problem: sort_k8 −0.6 [−2.0, +0.8], sortd_k8 −0.8 [−2.3, +0.6], knap_k8 −0.9 [−2.3, +0.5] (Table 3, p. 3). The note says the seeds measure sampling noise on fixed edits and do not replicate the pipeline that produces u (p. 2). A comparison outside the protocol is labelled as such (sortd_k8 splits by benchmark, 2.1 points below the LP on MATH-500) (p. 3). The control of a constant shift along u at the same dose was not run; the sweep of [2] on 128 problems is cited instead (57.8% against 62.5%) (p. 3).
- **D6 Positioning**: Only AHA [1] and Barry's LP [2], both blog posts with code, are discussed; the public sweep of [2] is used as evidence for the bias reading (pp. 1–3). No literature on activation steering or on sparse LP beyond these is cited (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: An imperative title; the abstract carries the numbers and intervals. The rhetorical turn is a falsified first reading: at first glance a separation model, but the solutions do not separate (pp. 1–2). The wording is calibrated to the missing control: *nearly constant* is defended as the right word because the steering arm was not run (p. 3); one model and one run of the pipeline are admitted (p. 3); the closing claim is given as the author's view (p. 3). The prose, by the note's own statement, was written by the AI assistant (p. 4), so D7 is no evidence of Fischetti's own voice.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: on this model a sort of the neurons by coefficient of variation matches the LP within the declared margin over three seeds, and the dose can come from AHA's δ without any LP; the informative part of the edit is the direction u (p. 3). Limits named: one model and one run of the pipeline that produces u (p. 3); the seeds measure sampling only (p. 2); the steering control is missing and named the natural next control (p. 3). Protocols, code, vectors and per-problem results are on Zenodo (p. 4).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence: subtraction applied to someone else's pipeline: the LP solve is removed, the edit kept, and the removal is tested against a non-inferiority margin (abstract; pp. 1–3). Method 4 ✅ evidence, with a caveat: the dose is matched so that only the neurons differ, the pipeline runs unmodified at a pinned commit, the protocol precedes the numbers, and a knapsack-rule control is added (pp. 2–3); per p. 4 these design choices were made by the AI assistant and accepted by the author. Method 3 ⚠ variant: seeds come in as a second declared protocol, but they measure sampling noise on fixed edits, and the content-free control (a constant steering along u at equal dose) is named as missing (pp. 2–3). Method 6 ⚠ variant: the reverse question, whether the solver is needed at all, answered by reading what its optimal solutions do and replacing the solve by that rule; the simple version is kept because it works (pp. 1–2, 4). Method 5 ⚠ variant: the re-test apparatus (pinned commit, declared protocol, a negative result for the LP published) aimed at another author's fresh claim, not his own (pp. 2, 4). Heuristic 1 evidence: the field's reading of the LP (a separation model) is tested and inverted (pp. 1–2). New-pattern candidates: **read the optimum, then replace the solve by the rule it implements** (pp. 1–2); **non-inferiority margin declared before the runs for a simpler replacement** (abstract; p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before keeping an optimization step inside a pipeline, look at what its optimal solutions do (here near-equal pushes on both classes) and test the simplest rule that does the same thing (pp. 1–2).
- Match the dose: give the simple rule the scalar the old method realizes, so the two arms differ in one component only (p. 2).
- When the claim is that a simpler component is as good, declare a non-inferiority margin before the runs and report paired differences with intervals from the discordant pairs (abstract; Table 1, p. 2).
- Add a second cheap rule as a control (mean only, variance ignored) to learn which feature of the simple rule matters (pp. 2–3).
- Report structural measures of the solutions (hinge, hit rate, the share of the effect carried by the common support) next to the task metric (Table 2, p. 3).
- Label every comparison that was not in the declared protocol (p. 3).

**Quotes**:
- "The idea is the author’s: the LP of [2] looked like a knapsack in disguise, hence its solution might be replaced by a sort." (p. 4)
- "Note that the dose is borrowed from the LP on purpose, so that the two edits differ in the neurons and not in the dose" (p. 2)

### S162 · A Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation of the EndoWake model for optimal wind farm layouts (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2610.39503)

**Read level**: full. All of pp. 1–23 (abstract, §§1–8, appendices A–B, data availability, CRediT, acknowledgments, AI declaration); references pp. 23–25 skimmed. Version read: arXiv:2610.39503v1 [math.OC] (29 Oct 2026, p. 1), dated October 2026; title and authors (Martina Fischetti, Matteo Fischetti) match on p. 1.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Martina Fischetti (University of Seville, IMUS; corresponding author). CRediT: Martina Fischetti conceptualization, data curation, investigation, methodology, validation, original draft; Matteo Fischetti conceptualization, formal analysis, methodology, software, review and editing (p. 22). · **Position in trajectory**: third paper of the 2026 EndoWake line: certification of the model of 2026a (arXiv:2609.40157), with the GPU batch evaluation of 2026b (S150) serving the incumbent (pp. 2–3, 13, 23–24).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: Certifying EndoWake layouts turned out to be hard: the LP relaxation of the model as written carries no information and the band inequalities repair it only in part; the published branch-and-check (hypograph variables, an exact linear-time oracle, upwind optimality cuts) certifies the 10×10 grid in 77 s and leaves 12×12, 14×14 and 16×16 at 7.98%, 8.49% and 9.74% after one hour (p. 2). Good layouts are not the issue (greedy plus local search finds them in seconds); the dual bound is: with the optimum as cutoff the branch-and-check still closes no 16×16 seed in three hours and its bound stays 7.3% above the optimum (p. 2). The problem is stated as how far a certificate can be pushed, and at what cost, when the relaxation is tightened by a convexification precomputed on tiles and the master is shrunk before the tree (p. 2). Kept: the model, the instances, the exact oracle and the frame of the algorithm; replaced: the relaxation and the search; the earlier branch-and-check is the baseline, run as published (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: (i) *Tiles*: strips of b = 4 rows or columns at stride k = 2 (design V4s2+H4s2); every pattern of a tile that respects separation and budget is enumerated, and the power of each cell under each pattern is tabulated by one batched propagation; the patterns enter the master as Dantzig–Wolfe columns, with the site variables x as linking variables in the arrowhead form (eq. (9), pp. 8–9). The block is a relaxation of the tile, valid by antitonicity (turbines outside a tile can only lower the power of its cells), and exactness needs a cut at integer points (Proposition 3, pp. 9–10). (ii) *The cube coordinate* q = (w/V)³: the power becomes linear and the SOS2 interpolant disappears, the flow inequality becomes a weighted power mean of order 1/3, concave (Lemma 1, p. 6), and the field projected on the layout becomes concave (Proposition 2, p. 7), so the optimality cut of generalized Benders decomposition (8), with a supergradient from one reverse sweep, keeps the master exact at integer layouts (p. 7). (iii) *Shrinking the master*: reduced-cost fixing at the root against an incumbent (a column with c̄ < −(v − L) cannot enter a better layout), a pairwise-consistency propagation between overlapping tiles to a fixed point, and refixing at every improving incumbent through lazy λ ≤ 0 (pp. 11–12). The band inequalities of the earlier paper turn out to be a relaxation of the one-dimensional case of the tiles (p. 10). Sparse loss coordinates (10) give the same LP with 37.4% fewer nonzeros at 14×14 (p. 10).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: n×n grid with ∆ = D = 112 m, separation 3D through clique constraints on 3×3 windows, at most n + 2 turbines at no cost, four axial wind directions at V = 10 m/s (below rated), the ideal cubic power curve, and the calibrated kernel ρ = 0.293, (α, β, γ) = (0.05324, 0, 0.11859) (pp. 4–5). Binaries x, continuous w and z; a big-M wake inequality (2) (p. 5). The ladder runs from 10×10 to 16×16; the 20×20 and 24×24 grids of the original ladder are not attempted (p. 5). Assumptions of the correctness argument: Proposition 1 (maximal field, antitonicity) restated from 2026a with its proof (p. 5); the cube coordinate drops the cut-in jump, so a certificate transfers to the true curve only if no installed cell lies below cut-in, which was checked on the 153 stored layouts (lowest speed 3.91 m/s against 3 m/s) and is called evidence, not a proof (pp. 6–7); the shift ε = 10⁻⁶ that keeps the supergradient finite is a relaxation, a parameter of the implementation and not a tolerance (p. 6). Scope of validity: any model whose cell rewards are non-decreasing in a field that is antitone in the layout; exactness needs in addition a cut tight at integer layouts (p. 18).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Proof devices: induction along the propagation planes (Proposition 1, p. 5; Proposition 2, p. 7); quasi-concavity plus positive homogeneity of degree one to obtain concavity of the power mean (Lemma 1, p. 6); a concavity-against-antitonicity sandwich showing that the support of the supergradient yields an exact upwind cut (Lemma 2, p. 8); LP duality for column fixing with a moving incumbent, plus validity of the propagation (Proposition 4, Appendix A, p. 21). Why the cube: r = 3 is the smallest exponent for which both the propagation and the power are concave, and the only one with linear power (p. 7). Why the cubic program is not solved directly: its continuous relaxation is vacuous at the uniform point, so Benders cuts at fractional points cannot beat the trivial bound (p. 7). Design choices: three tile designs compared on 14×14 with the same incumbent (the certified optimum), on deterministic work units (Table 1, pp. 10–11); width 4 at every offset stalls the dual simplex on degenerate pivots (p. 11); one design for every grid and rose, with no selection per instance (p. 11). The incumbent enters as a complete MIP start including z at its exact powers, without which the lazy Benders cuts reject it (p. 11); an LP dive with barrier re-solves, greedy completion and 1-opt polishing serves where no GPU is available (p. 11). Tolerances: a fixing tolerance of 10⁻⁶ of the root value protects an optimal incumbent's patterns from rounding (pp. 11–12); the separation threshold is 10⁻⁶ of rated power (p. 14). The GPU tile heuristic scores every compatible pattern of a tile in single precision and re-evaluates the best eight exactly in double precision on the CPU, so a ranking error can only lose a candidate (p. 13); CPU and GPU runs follow the same trajectory, the GPU 65–66 times faster (p. 13).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: Baseline: the frozen branch-and-check code of 2026a, run as published (no MIP start, no cutoff, its band inequalities, the same seeds) on the same four-core Xeon E3-1220 v2 computers with the same gurobipy 13.0.3; at 16×16 also on the cold-start node type and with the optimum as cutoff (pp. 13–14). Five seeds per grid; 3,600 s on each code's tree (10,800 s at 16×16); medians; censored runs enter no ratio; work units where the CPUs differ (pp. 14–15). Ladder: the pipeline certifies every seed, 12×12 in 48 s and 14×14 in 395 s wall time, against 0/5 for the branch-and-check above 10×10 (Table 2, p. 15); its gap is a bound effect before an incumbent effect (p. 15). Six non-uniform roses: 78 of 78 runs close (Table 3, p. 16). 16×16: the single job (GPU heuristic, then the same arm) certifies five seeds in 1,174–1,613 s, the cold start from the LP dive 5/5, the branch-and-check 0/5 even with the optimum as cutoff; a rerun at gap zero gives the same width, nodes and work units (Table 4, p. 16). On that test set the codes ran on two partitions with CPUs of similar clock, disclosed with each arm (p. 17). Failures printed: twelve directions (12×12 and 14×14 open, Table 7, pp. 19–20) and the 17×17 grid (p. 19). Reproducibility: every number is read from the campaign archives by one script per campaign, and no layout, root or verdict is carried between runs except in two declared arms (p. 18).
- **D6 Positioning**: Each ingredient's precedent is credited and the novelty placed in how the structure of EndoWake makes them work together (p. 3): cutting-stock columns (Gilmore–Gomory) and Dantzig–Wolfe; the arrowhead form (Bergner et al., with Lübbecke) and Lagrangean decomposition (Guignard–Kim); local cuts (Chvátal–Cook–Espinoza; Buchheim et al.); pattern enumeration in harvest scheduling (Goycoolea et al.); route enumeration in exact vehicle routing (Baldacci, Mingozzi and coauthors); reduced-cost elimination and filtering (Irnich et al.; Focacci–Lodi–Milano; Yang 2025); consistency cuts (Clausen–Lusby–Ropke); generalized Benders (Geoffrion), outer approximation (Duran–Grossmann) and logic-based Benders (Hooker–Ottosson) for the earlier upwind cut (pp. 3–4). The one novelty claimed to the authors' knowledge is the use of the cube of the speed to obtain a concave extension (p. 3). What the tiles add over route enumeration is propagation between overlapping blocks (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The abstract defines *certified* (closed at the solver's gap of 10⁻⁴, plus a residual of at most 0.00042%) and says that times are medians (p. 1). Plain admissions: certification turned out to be hard (p. 2); the paper is a certification tool for the model, not a design tool for a site (p. 20). The Benders cut is presented as the cleaner equivalent of the upwind cut and not as a speed-up (p. 8). A working hypothesis is labelled as one (the fine rose is the open case, not a limit of the device) (p. 19). The certificates are called those of a floating-point solver (p. 14). AI declaration: Claude and Codex assisted in drafting and editing, in the code and scripts, and reviewed successive drafts as a simulated referee (p. 23); the original draft is Martina Fischetti's (p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contributions: the Dantzig–Wolfe reformulation on tiles, the cube coordinate, the shrinking of the master, certification of every ladder grid up to 16×16 and of two non-uniform roses at 16×16, and confirmation on a pre-specified unseen test set (pp. 4, 20). Limits named: tiles are tight only on axial wakes (with twelve directions the 10×10 root gap goes from 0.090% to 4.67% and from 12×12 on the fixing keeps every column) (pp. 19–20); the 17×17 grid stays open (49.5 million patterns, a 160 GB root, 0.3508% after three hours of tree) (p. 19); memory (out of memory at 14.3 GB on a ladder computer) (p. 19); floating-point certificates with no safe bound (p. 14); one instance family (p. 3). Future work: a root generated by column generation (p. 20); a coupling between direction families stronger than the shared site variables, e.g. the full consistency equations, for fine roses (p. 19); pricing columns in the tree rather than enumerating them at the root (p. 20).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ evidence: their own branch-and-check of weeks earlier is the baseline, frozen code run as published on its own computers and seeds, plus a cutoff run that isolates the proof effort; the failure is diagnosed as a bound effect (pp. 2, 13–16). Method 4 ✅ evidence: same CPUs, threads, solver version and seeds as the baseline; one design for all instances; heuristic parameters written before the first run; designs compared with the same incumbent on deterministic work units; censored runs out of every ratio; a held-out test set fixed before any run (pp. 10–11, 13–15, 17–18). Method 4 ⚠ variant: on the test set the two codes ran on two partitions of similar clock, disclosed rather than equalized (p. 17). Method 1 ✅ evidence: the cube coordinate removes the SOS2 power curve, so there is no branching on the curve (pp. 2, 7); fixing and propagation reduce 18.8 million columns to 290,489 before the tree (pp. 3, 12); levers added one at a time (p. 17). Method 2 ⚠ variant: the stall is located in the relaxation, not in the optimality cut, which is kept at parity (pp. 8, 15, 17). Method 6 ⚠ variant: the stock MIP solver closes the reduced master, while the critical sub-decision (the layout inside one tile) is solved by exhaustive enumeration on a GPU rather than by an auxiliary MIP (pp. 12–13). Heuristic 10 ✅ evidence: every energy recomputed by the exact oracle, every incumbent re-evaluated, the certificate width measured over 221 runs, stored layouts checked for cut-in, final layouts checked against masks and budgets (pp. 5, 7, 12, 14, 18). Heuristic 3 evidence: route enumeration borrowed from vehicle routing, pairwise consistency from constraint programming (pp. 3, 12). New-pattern candidates: **convexification computed rather than derived** (pp. 1–3, 8–10); **change the coordinate until the second stage is concave** (pp. 2–3, 6–7); **certificate-width accounting** (pp. 14, 16); **consistency propagation between overlapping blocks after reduced-cost fixing** (p. 12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When the relaxation is weak, enumerate the patterns of small overlapping substructures and add them as Dantzig–Wolfe columns; validity needs only a monotonicity (antitone) property, exactness a cut at integer points (pp. 8–10).
- Look for the coordinate in which the value function becomes concave (here the cube of the speed), so a generalized Benders cut with a supergradient from one reverse sweep becomes available (pp. 6–7).
- With an incumbent at or near the optimum, fix columns by root reduced costs (v + c̄ < L), propagate pairwise consistency between overlapping blocks, and refix at every improving incumbent with lazy λ ≤ 0 where bounds cannot change in a callback (pp. 11–12, 21).
- Pass the incumbent as a complete MIP start, continuous variables included at their exact values, or lazy cuts reject it (p. 11).
- Compare design variants with the same incumbent and on deterministic work units when machines differ (pp. 10, 14).
- Report a certificate's width as solver gap plus modelling residual, and rerun at gap zero to separate them (pp. 14, 16).
- Run the published baseline frozen and as published, then once more with the optimum as cutoff to measure the proof effort alone (pp. 14–16).
- Generate a held-out test set from a seed fixed before any run, varying what the development ladder kept fixed (masks, budgets, roses), and run each code once (p. 17).
- Score candidates in low precision on a GPU but accept only after an exact double-precision re-evaluation of the top few (p. 13).

### S163 · Minibatch persistency, eight years later: what batch reuse costs in steps and joules, and what it saves in data (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2609.13922)

**Read level**: full. All of pp. 1–29 (abstract, §§1–8, acknowledgments) and appendices A–C (pp. 31–36); references pp. 29–31 skimmed; the figures are present only as captions. Version read: arXiv:2609.13922v1 [cs.LG] (12 Sep 2026, p. 1); title and sole author (Matteo Fischetti, DEI Padova) match on p. 1.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (the 2018 paper under re-test is Fischetti, Mandatelli and Salvagnin, arXiv:1806.07353, p. 30). The text carries no statement on AI assistance (none found); an adversarial review of the harness is reported without saying who carried it out (pp. 5, 10). · **Position in trajectory**: the main case of SKILL.md Method 5: the re-test of his 2018 preprint (S133) eight years later, in the line of machine learning seen from the MILP side.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: The entry is a standing objection to his own idea. Choi et al. (2019) generalized minibatch persistency into data echoing and wrote in the same paragraph that the 2018 study tuned neither the baseline nor the method (pp. 1–2). He concedes the objection as methodological rather than conceptual and sharpens it: K steps at rate η resemble one step at about Kη, so an untuned comparison may measure nothing but a learning rate (p. 2). A second gap: nobody had reported what batch reuse saves in joules (p. 2). Aim: not to propose the idea but to measure it under the conditions the objection demands (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: Two knobs, K (optimizer steps between refills) and M (pool size in batches) (p. 4); three arms: the tuned baseline (K = 1), persistency (K = 4, M = 1, repeat) and an A/A null (K = 4, M = 4, resample), distributionally identical to the baseline if the stream is exchangeable (pp. 4–5). The null arm was first conceived as a locality control and was re-designated as an A/A calibration before any confirmatory run, after an adversarial review of the harness (p. 5). Two claims are kept separable: an optimization claim (steps and fresh tokens to a fixed loss) and an energy claim (joules) (p. 2). The quantity under test is the paired step-cost ratio R(B, s), with penalty g = R − 1 and H1: g(512) < g(32), a one-sided paired t-test on log differences and a registered three-outcome rule: supported if p < 0.05 and ĝ(512) ≤ ĝ(32)/2, refuted if the interval excludes any halving, inconclusive otherwise (p. 6). ρ(B) = η*(K=4)/η*(K=1) is a descriptive diagnostic: a value near 1/K would mean that reuse is a larger learning rate in disguise (pp. 2, 8).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MILP items n/a; the workload stands in. A 49 M-parameter decoder-only Transformer (6 layers, 6 heads, width 384, sequence 1024), AdamW (β 0.9/0.95, weight decay 0.1, clipping 1.0), a cosine schedule with 2% warmup and a floor of 0.1, only the peak rate tuned (pp. 5, 7). FineWeb-Edu sample-10BT, pre-tokenized because streaming would inject the very input-pipeline overhead to be measured; a fixed evaluation set of 160 sequences whose sampling error cancels to first order in every ratio (p. 7); 100 Mtok of fresh tokens per run and 16 evaluations at fixed fresh-token boundaries (p. 7). Assumptions named: exchangeability of the stream at sequence level for the A/A identity, not verified (p. 5); each arm anneals over its own planned steps, so the arms are read at different points of their schedules (pp. 5, 7). The model was cut from 162 M to 49 M parameters by Amendment 1 because the first design needed about 1,725 GPU-hours against a free tier of 30 hours a week (p. 7).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: No proofs; the design work is in the harness. Resumable runs with exact restore of optimizer, scheduler, data-iterator and RNG state, tested against uninterrupted trajectories; 294 passing tests (p. 10). Fresh examples are counted when the pool is filled, with regression tests on the accounting (p. 6); evaluation is excluded from time and energy because it is identical across arms and would dilute every ratio toward one (p. 6). An energy provenance rule: no joule without the mechanism that produced it, and a zero is never a measurement, guarded by a regression test against a silently zeroed counter (p. 12). A hardware rule: both legs of every paired ratio on one chip, absolute seconds and joules never pooled across machines (p. 10). Analytic devices: the identity fresh-token ratio = R/K at M = 1 (p. 6); the evaluation-grid offset (K − 1)/t verified seed by seed to machine precision (pp. 23–24); a bound that places the host-inclusive energy ratio between the device-joule ratio and the seconds ratio under any constant host power (p. 12).
