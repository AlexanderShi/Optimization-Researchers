### S166 · An improved compact formulation for the assortment optimization problem with small consideration sets (Journal of the Operational Research Society 76(10) 2025, DOI 10.1080/01605682.2025.2451738)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json; source EconPapers per abstract-sources.json) · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roberto Roberti (first author) and Domenico Salvagnin, all three at DEI Padova (Crossref) · **Position in trajectory**: one of the 2025-2026 Padova papers with Roberti and Salvagnin (S126, S144, S172; works.json). Open access under CC BY 4.0 at the publisher (Crossref license; Unpaywall "hybrid"), but not fetchable by script.

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: assortment optimization with small consideration sets: customers fall into classes and choose by the k-product non-parametric ranking-based model, each preference list holding at most k products; the problem is NP-hard already for k = 2, and the best approximation method stays 0.3-0.5% from optimal at k = 4 with 100 products and 10,000 classes (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: start from a compact MILP proposed for the full non-parametric ranking-based model and improve it into a compact model with a very tight continuous relaxation that a general-purpose solver handles (abstract).
- **D3 (problem class and data model)**: customer classes, preference lists of at most k products, purchase of the most preferred product offered (abstract).
- **D5 (computational evaluation)**: an extensive set of experiments: provably optimal assortments for up to 200 products, 100,000 customer classes and k = 5 in a few minutes; the abstract names no solver, hardware or baseline run (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: a formulation contribution that gives provable optima at sizes where the literature offered approximations; no limitation stated (abstract).
- Transferable technique: before building an approximation scheme for a choice-model assortment problem, tighten the compact MILP of the general model to the restricted preference structure and measure whether a stock solver proves optimality at the instance sizes the approximation papers report, and beyond (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (step 1, joint, 2025): the answer is a model handed to a general-purpose solver, not a dedicated algorithm (abstract).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (step 2, joint): the model is fitted to the problem class at hand (small consideration sets rather than the full ranking model), but by tightening the continuous relaxation, not by removing variables (abstract).

**Quotes**: "we propose an improved compact model that features a very tight continuous relaxation and can be easily solved with a general-purpose solver" (abstract); "This problem is known to be NP-hard even when k is equal to 2." (abstract)

### S196 · Solving Congested Facility Location by Branch-and-Cut (ROADEF 2016, 17th ROADEF conference, Compiègne, session SS1-1, 10 February 2016; abstract #235; no DOI)
**Read**: abstract (the conference's abstract page in its Wayback copy of 2018-08-09, recorded in abstracts-chase-a2-03.json; session SS1, Challenging Mixed-Integer Problems in Network Optimization) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ivana Ljubić, Markus Sinnl (conference page) · **Position in trajectory**: the conference counterpart of the congested case of S023 (EJOR 2016, card c07), inferred from authors, topic and date.

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: congested facility location extends the capacitated (or p-median) problem: a higher load at a facility means longer waiting times, so the diseconomies of scale are convex cost functions of the facility load (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: first a perspective reformulation of the standard MINLP model, which greatly improves the lower bounds of its continuous relaxation; then generalized Benders cuts obtained by projecting the allocation variables out of the perspective reformulation, embedded in a branch-and-cut framework (abstract).
- **D3 (problem class and data model)**: a capacitated or p-median location model with convex, load-dependent congestion costs, whose standard model is an MINLP (abstract).
- **D4 (algorithm and implementation design)**: generalized Benders cuts inside branch-and-cut, with a simplified practical implementation announced for the talk (abstract).
- **D5 (computational evaluation)**: none in the abstract; results were to be shown at the conference (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: a reformulation-plus-decomposition recipe for CFL; no results or limits stated (abstract).
- Transferable technique: when a location model carries convex load-dependent costs, take the perspective reformulation first and derive the generalized Benders cuts from it rather than from the standard MINLP (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (steps 3-4, joint): the allocation variables are projected out (Benders) and the master is solved by branch-and-cut, but only after the model has been strengthened by a perspective reformulation, the reverse of the order in the method's one-line rule (abstract).

**Quotes**: "We finally derive generalized Benders cuts by projecting out allocation variables from the perspective reformulation." (abstract); "This reformulation greatly improves the quality of lower bounds of the underlying continuous relaxation." (abstract)
