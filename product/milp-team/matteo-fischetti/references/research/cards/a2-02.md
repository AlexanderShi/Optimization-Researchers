### S107 · On the monotonization of polyhedra (Mathematical Programming 78(1) 1997, DOI 10.1007/bf02614506)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: E. Balas (first author) · **Role**: supplement

- **D1 problem/entry**: Implicit or explicit equations make it hard to analyse the facets of a polyhedron that is not full-dimensional. Such a polytope is therefore studied through its full-dimensional monotonizations, the submissive and the dominant. When a facet of these is also a facet of P had been posed in the literature as an important research problem (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: A generalized monotonization g-mon(P) that subsumes both the submissive and the dominant, with a sufficient condition for a facet of g-mon(P) to define a facet of P. For the symmetric and asymmetric traveling salesman polytopes and the linear ordering polytope the conditions are "trivially easy to verify" (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: Polyhedral theory with a measured claim: the paper "goes a long way" towards the problem and does not claim to solve it fully (abstract). The IPCO 1993 conference version is D001 (works.json note).
  - Transferable: When the polytope of interest has equations, prove facets on a full-dimensional monotonization and give a sufficient condition, easy to check, that carries each facet back to the original polytope (abstract).

**Method links**: new-pattern candidate: Prove in the full-dimensional relaxation, then transfer back. The candidate already exists from S102; this abstract states the device in its general form (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "It is therefore customary to approach the study of such a polytope P through the study of one of its (full dimensional) relaxations (monotonizations) known as the submissive and the dominant of P." / "Our paper goes a long way towards solving this problem."

### S120 · Lifted cycle inequalities for the asymmetric traveling salesman problem (Mathematics of Operations Research 24(2) 1999, DOI 10.1287/moor.24.2.273)

### S091 · Combinatorial Benders’ cuts (IPCO 2004 (LNCS), pp. 178-195, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-25960-2_14)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: G. Codato (first author) · **Role**: supplement · The conference version of S006 (Operations Research 2006, carded in c03), judged by title and authors (inferred)

- **D1 problem/entry**: MIPs whose logical implications are modelled with big-M coefficients are "notoriously among the hardest to solve" (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: An automatic reformulation that removes the model's dependence on the big-M coefficients (abstract):
  - a master ILP with no continuous variables holds the combinatorial information on the feasible integer combinations;
  - a slave LP validates each master solution or returns combinatorial inequalities, associated with minimal (irreducible) infeasible subsystems, that can be separated efficiently when the master solution is integer;
  - the master's LP relaxation "can be considerably tighter" than that of the original MIP.
- **D8 contribution**: On two specific classes of hard-to-solve MIPs, the reformulation is solved "some orders of magnitude faster" than the original model (abstract).
  - Transferable: Project out continuous variables that only carry big-M logic: keep an integer-only master and let an LP slave return cuts read from minimal infeasible subsystems (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (evidence ✅): step 3. The continuous variables, and with them the big-M coefficients, are projected out into a slave LP, as in the S006 card (abstract).
- New-pattern candidate: a separator exact at integer points suffices for correctness. The candidate already exists from S006; the cuts "can be separated efficiently in case the master solution is integer" (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "Our solution scheme defines a master Integer Linear Problem (ILP) with no continuous variables, which contains combinatorial information on the integer-variable feasible combinations that can be “distilled” from the original MIP model." / "The inequalities are associated to minimal (or irreducible) infeasible subsystems of a certain linear system, and can be separated efficiently in case the master solution is integer."

### S045 · Cutting plane versus compact formulations for uncertain (integer) linear programs (Mathematical Programming Computation 4(3) 2012, DOI 10.1007/s12532-012-0039-y)
**Read**: abstract (chased from the Springer landing page) · **Coauthors**: M. Monaci · **Role**: supplement · The round-1 card (s07) found that the file on disk was another work, now H006

- **D1 problem/entry**: Robustness cuts "risky" solutions away. Both usual approaches have pros and cons, and "it is not clear which is the best one" for a given problem (abstract):
  - the popular compact robust counterpart, with polynomially many extra variables and constraints;
  - an exponential family of cutting planes generated at run time.
- **D2 key idea**: Compare the two options computationally on prototype problems (abstract):
  - Bertsimas-Sim robust LPs;
  - in a probabilistic setting, a new uncertain set covering problem, where columns may disappear and rows must be covered with high probability, and an uncertain graph connectivity problem, where edges may fail; each gets compact ILP models and cutting-plane schemes;
  - where no compact ILP exists, a restart strategy.
