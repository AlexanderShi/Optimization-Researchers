### D001 · Linear Programming Using Limited-Precision Oracles (IPCO 2019, LNCS 11480, pp. 399-412, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-17953-3_30)
**Read**: abstract, the IPCO chapter's own (Springer landing page; recorded in abstracts-chase-a2-01.json). abstracts.json holds the differently worded abstract of the journal version S029 (ZIB-Report 19-57 = arXiv 1912.12820, carded in full in c09). The two share the main-result sentence. · **Coauthors**: Daniel E. Steffy; Gleixner is first of two (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: solving LPs exactly over the rational numbers through an oracle that returns limited-precision LP solutions. Earlier work assumed oracles of arbitrary specified precision. That gives polynomial-time algorithms, but at a precision the abstract calls often unrealistic for practical computation (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: treat the practical solver as a limited-precision oracle. Under mild assumptions, polynomially many oracle calls plus polynomially many bit operations suffice to compute an exact LP solution (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: complexity theory meant as a foundation for the methods that currently work best in practice for exact LP. The abstract does not name those methods. By the S029 card they are iterative refinement with basis verification or rational reconstruction (inferred) (abstract).
  - Transferable: to analyse a practical numerical method, give the floating-point solver a precision contract (a limited-precision oracle). Then count oracle calls plus exact bit operations, instead of assuming arbitrary precision (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ evidence (abstract). The limited-precision producer is taken as given and only the exact work is counted: the producing/deciding split becomes a complexity question. New-pattern candidate: oracle model of the production solver (existing, from S029; here at abstract level).

**Quotes** (abstract): "Under mild assumptions, it is shown that a polynomial number of calls to such an oracle and a polynomial number of bit operations, is sufficient to compute an exact solution to an LP." / "a foundation for understanding and analyzing the behavior of the methods that are currently most effective in practice for solving LPs exactly"

### S051 · On generalized surrogate duality in mixed-integer nonlinear programming (IPCO 2020, LNCS 12125, pp. 322-337, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-45771-6_25)
**Read**: abstract, the IPCO chapter's own (Springer landing page; in the chase file). abstracts.json holds the abstract of ZIB-Report 19-55 = arXiv 1912.00356, the text of the journal version S063 (carded partial in s04). That abstract has one more opening sentence. · **Coauthors**: Benjamin Müller (first), Gonzalo Muñoz, Maxime Gasse, Andrea Lodi, Felipe Serrano; Gleixner is fourth of six (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: MINLP relaxations are usually required to be convex. Current solvers can handle a moderate presence of nonconvexities, and that opens the door to tighter nonconvex relaxations (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: bring the surrogate relaxation (an aggregation of constraints) into MINLP. It was studied for linear integer programs in the 1970s and 1980s and has scarcely been considered since. The paper generalizes it to several simultaneous aggregations and gives the first algorithm that computes the best set of aggregations, with computational enhancements (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: an algorithm, with extensive experiments on dual-bound strength that show both benefits and challenges (abstract). The test set and the numbers are not in the abstract. This is a MINLP paper, outside this team's MILP field (team.json gives MINLP to minlp-team) (metadata).
  - Transferable: when solvers gain a capability (here, handling moderate nonconvexity), look again at classical relaxations that need it, even if they have been dormant for decades (abstract).

**Method links**: new-pattern candidate: revive a dormant classical relaxation once solvers can handle it (abstract). The nonconvex surrogate relaxation of the 1970s and 1980s is revisited because solvers now tolerate moderate nonconvexity. The abstract does not clearly support any SKILL.md method.

**Quotes** (abstract): "These relaxations were actively studied for linear integer programs in the 70s and 80s, but they have been scarcely considered since." / "present the first algorithm that is capable of computing the best set of aggregations"

### S057 · Accelerating domain propagation: An efficient GPU-parallel algorithm over sparse matrices (Parallel Computing 109, article 102874, 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.parco.2021.102874)
**Read**: abstract of the arXiv preprint 2009.07785 (abstracts.json). The work list attaches that preprint to the IA3 2020 version S067, which is carded in full in s03 from arXiv v5. The journal's own abstract could not be reached: Crossref and OpenAlex have none, and ScienceDirect refused the request (HTTP 403). The ZIB OPUS record of the journal article gives five bullet points (by their form, the article's highlights) that repeat the abstract's claims: propagation is single-threaded in current solvers, the rounds run entirely on the GPU, and speed-ups are 10x to 20x, up to 180x (metadata). · **Coauthors**: Boro Sofranac (first), Sebastian Pokutta; Gleixner is second of three (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: domain propagation of linear constraints is a crucial component of MIP and pseudo-Boolean solvers. - **D5 evaluation**: on state-of-the-art GPUs, geometric-mean speed-ups of about 10x to 20x for reasonably large instances and up to 180x on favourably large ones. The baseline, test set and hardware are not in the abstract (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: an algorithm and its GPU implementation (abstract). The later progress-measure paper S077 reports that the parallel version is even more competitive in a real-world setting than first reported (S077 abstract).
  - Transferable: design a parallel propagation so that complete rounds run on the GPU, with no CPU synchronization or communication (abstract).

### S077 · An algorithm-independent measure of progress for linear constraint propagation (Constraints 27(4), 432-455, 2022, DOI 10.1007/s10601-022-09338-9)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json, the arXiv 2106.07573 text). The Springer page of the journal article shows the same text, with em dashes where arXiv has "--". The CP 2021 version D003 is carded in full in c10. · **Coauthors**: Boro Sofranac (first), Sebastian Pokutta; Gleixner is second of three (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: iterative propagation codes stop by tolerance-based criteria, which makes fair efficiency comparisons between implementations difficult. Unbounded variable domains make the relative size of reductions hard to quantify (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: a measure of the progress an iterative propagation procedure has made at a given point of its run, independent of the algorithmic design (abstract).
- **D5 evaluation**: two applications on real-world MIP instances. (i) How far do heuristic stopping criteria end propagation prematurely? (ii) A GPU-parallel propagator is compared with a sequential state-of-the-art one, and the parallel one comes out even more competitive than originally reported (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: a measurement instrument for propagation research, used at once to re-evaluate an earlier GPU result. That the earlier result is S057/S067 is inferred from the description (abstract).
  - Transferable: before comparing iterative codes that stop at different tolerances, define a progress measure that depends on neither algorithm, and compare them on that scale (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence (abstract). A comparison problem that the field handles with tolerances becomes a defined measure, and two practical questions are answered with it. SKILL.md cites the CP version [CP21] for this. New-pattern candidate: algorithm-independent reference points (existing, from D003; here at abstract level).

**Quotes** (abstract): "However, these heuristic stopping criteria can pose difficulties for fairly comparing the efficiency of different implementations of iterative propagation algorithms in a real-world setting." / "(ii) We compare a GPU-parallel propagation algorithm against a sequential state-of-the-art implementation and show that the parallel version is even more competitive in a real-world setting than originally reported."

### S022 · A computational status update for exact rational mixed integer programming (Mathematical Programming 197(2), 793-812, 2023, DOI 10.1007/s10107-021-01749-5)
**Read**: abstract, the journal's own (Crossref and the Springer page agree; in the chase file). abstracts.json holds the abstract of the IPCO 2021 version D002 (arXiv 2101.09141, carded in full in c10). The two are identical except for the numbers in the last sentence: 6.6x and 2.8 there, 10.7x and 2.9 here. The article is open access (CC BY 4.0), and its PDF is now in papers/ for the next round. Only its first page was checked: title, Leon Eifler and Ambros Gleixner, ZIB and HTW Berlin. · **Coauthors**: Leon Eifler (first); Gleixner is second of two (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: the last milestone in roundoff-error-free solving of general MIPs over the rationals was the hybrid-precision branch and bound of Cook, Koch, Steffy and Wolter (2013). The paper starts from that framework (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: revise and extend the 2013 framework with four additions: symbolic presolving, an exact repair step for solutions from primal heuristics, a faster rational LP solver based on LP iterative refinement, and independently verifiable certificates of optimality (abstract).
- **D5 evaluation**: on the MIPLIB 2017 benchmark set, a 10.7x average speedup over the original framework and 2.9 times as many instances solved within two hours (journal abstract). The conference abstract gives 6.6x and 2.8 for the same comparison. Neither abstract explains the change; SKILL.md's anatomy offers the hardware and CPLEX differences between the versions as an inferred cause. The floating-point baselines are not in the abstract; SKILL.md's Method 2 takes them from the journal body, which was not read here.
- **D8 contribution**: a computational status report, with insight into how the new components behave (abstract).
  - Transferable: give an exact solver an exact repair step for the solutions that its inexact primal heuristics find (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (abstract): the predecessor milestone is named, the missing components are added to it, and the gain is measured against it.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (abstract): independently verifiable certificates of optimality.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (abstract): exact repair of heuristic solutions, and a rational LP solver built on iterative refinement.

**Quotes** (abstract): "The last milestone achievement for the roundoff-error-free solution of general mixed integer programs over the rational numbers was a hybrid-precision branch-and-bound algorithm published by Cook, Koch, Steffy, and Wolter in 2013." / "We describe a substantial revision and extension of this framework that integrates symbolic presolving, features an exact repair step for solutions from primal heuristics, employs a faster rational LP solver based on LP iterative refinement, and is able to produce independently verifiable certificates of optimality."

### S056 · Efficient separation of RLT cuts for implicit and explicit bilinear products (IPCO 2023, LNCS 13904, pp. 14-28, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-32726-1_2)
**Read**: abstract, the IPCO chapter's own (Springer landing page = arXiv 2211.13545v1; in the chase file). abstracts.json holds the arXiv v2 abstract (May 2024). That version adds one sentence: the separation algorithm applies to RLT cuts for all types of bilinear terms. The extended journal version S070 is carded in full in c17. · **Coauthors**: Ksenia Bestuzheva (first), Tobias Achterberg; Gleixner is second of three (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: RLT builds tight linear relaxations of nonconvex continuous and mixed-integer problems. The paper widens RLT's reach for bilinear products and makes it faster, because the high cost of separating RLT cuts is one of the major difficulties of using RLT in practice (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: two contributions. (1) Detect bilinear product relations hidden in MILPs by analysing linear constraints with binary variables, which brings bilinear RLT to a new class of problems. (2) A separation algorithm that picks the combinations of linear constraints and bound factors expected to give an inequality violated by the current relaxation solution (abstract).
- **D5 evaluation**: a detailed computational study based on implementations in two solvers. The abstract names neither the solvers nor any numbers; by the S070 card the solvers are SCIP and Gurobi (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: two algorithmic components, detection and separation, for nonconvex and mixed-integer linear problems (abstract).
  - Transferable: before generating cuts from a large family, select only the factor combinations predicted to give an inequality violated by the current relaxation point (abstract).
  - Transferable: evaluate a new solver component through implementations in two different solvers (abstract).

**Method links**: new-pattern candidate: independent re-implementation in a second solver as replication (existing, from S070; the abstract states implementations in two solvers). The abstract does not clearly support any SKILL.md method.

**Quotes** (abstract): "First, a method for detecting bilinear product relations implicitly contained in mixed-integer linear programs is developed based on analyzing linear constraints with binary variables, thus enabling the application of bilinear RLT to a new class of problems." / "We propose a new RLT cutting plane separation algorithm which identifies combinations of linear constraints and bound factors that are expected to yield an inequality that is violated by the current relaxation solution."

### S081 · Investigating Round-Up Properties for the Length-Constrained Cycle Partition Problem (Operations Research Proceedings 2024, LNOR, pp. 193-198, 2025, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-92575-7_27)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json, taken from the Springer page; the page text was the same on 2026-11-28) · **Coauthors**: Kilian Runnwerth (first), Mohammed Ghannam; Gleixner is last of three (Crossref).

- **D1 problem/entry**: LCCP asks for a partition of a graph's nodes into as few cycles as possible, where no cycle may be longer than the critical time of any of its nodes. Its set-partitioning formulation, solved by branch and price, consistently has the integer round-up property (IRUP) on the standard benchmark instances. The paper studies when that property holds (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: construct counterexamples with small coefficients in which the formulation lacks IRUP, and give simple conditions under which IRUP holds (abstract).
- **D5 evaluation**: the conditions explain IRUP on 30 of the 51 standard test instances that have it, so 21 remain unexplained (abstract; the 21 is my subtraction).
- **D8 contribution**: a six-page proceedings paper that partly explains an empirical regularity of a branch-and-price formulation; the abstract states no future work. By topic it belongs to the LCCP line that SKILL.md's Method 3 traces ([LCCP24] to [LCCP26]); this link is inferred (abstract).
  - Transferable: when a formulation shows a property on every benchmark instance, build small counterexamples to show that it is not guaranteed. Then derive simple sufficient conditions and report how many benchmark instances they explain (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 1 ⚠ variant (abstract). An observed regularity is checked per instance and counted (30 of 51). But the belief concerns a formulation's property rather than solver behaviour, and the check is a sufficient condition, not a per-decision check of a solver run. New-pattern candidate: counterexample plus sufficient condition, coverage counted on the benchmark (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract): "On standard benchmark instances from the literature, this formulation consistently satisfies the integer round-up property (IRUP), i.e., the objective value of the root node relaxation rounded up is equal to the optimal objective value." / "Our computational tests reveal that 30 out of 51 standard test instances with IRUP can be explained by these conditions."
