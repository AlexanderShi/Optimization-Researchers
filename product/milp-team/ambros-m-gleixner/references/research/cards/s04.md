### S098 · Realisierung von Beschleunigungsstrategien der anwendungsorientierten Mathematik und Informatik für optimierende Energiesystemmodelle (BEAM-ME-Abschlussbericht, DLR 2020, DOI 10.2314/kxp:1700331485)
**Read level**: full. Alle 45 Seiten gelesen: Titelblatt, Autorenliste, Inhaltsverzeichnis, Kapitel 1–8 mit den Tabellen 1–11 sowie die Publikations- und Vortragsliste (S. 1–45). Der Best Practice Guide, laut S. 42 als Anhang beigefügt, ist nicht in der Datei.
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Konsortialbericht von DLR (Fuchs, Borggrefe, Cao, Gils, Wetzel, Scholz, von Krbek), ZIB (Rehfeldt, Gleixner), TU Berlin (Koch), GAMS (Fiand, Bussieck), FZ Jülich (Breuer, Rohe) und HLRS (Khabi); Kontakt Fuchs, DLR (S. 2). Der Bericht ordnet keinen Abschnitt einem Autor zu. Gleixner erscheint in der Publikationsliste als Erstautor von „First Experiments with Structure-Aware Presolving for a Parallel Interior-Point Method“ (S. 43, Nr. 9) und eines Workshopvortrags von 2016 über Potenzial und Grenzen paralleler Rechnung für gemischt-ganzzahlige Energiesystemmodelle (S. 45, Nr. 39). Dass die Presolving-Passage (S. 19–20) sein Anteil ist, ist eine Schlussfolgerung daraus, nicht belegt. · **Position in research trajectory**: angewandte Energiesystem-Linie seiner ZIB-Gruppenleiterjahre (SKILL.md Research Trajectory, 2015–2023); BMWi-Projekt FKZ 03ET4023A-F, Laufzeit 01.11.2016–30.06.2019 (S. 1); die Publikationsliste kündigt den EJOR-Artikel S027 als Einreichung an (S. 43, Nr. 8).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Optimierende Energiesystemmodelle (ESOM) in GAMS brauchten auf Einzelservern Wochen bis zu einem Monat und 100 GB und mehr, an der Grenze von CPLEX, Gurobi, Xpress und SCIP (S. 7). Projektziel: Lösungszeiten deutlich senken und bisher unlösbare LPs lösen (S. 5). Der Bericht stellt fünf nummerierte Leitfragen I–V voran (S. 7). Zwei Säulen: modellbasierte Methoden (Aggregation, heuristische und exakte Dekomposition) und löserbasierte Methoden (PIPS-IPM++) (S. 5).
- **D2 Key idea**: Den quelloffenen parallelen Innere-Punkte-Löser PIPS-IPM, gebaut für zweistufige stochastische Probleme und ohne Linking Constraints, so erweitern, dass er Blockstrukturen mit vielen Linking Variables und Linking Constraints verteilt verarbeitet (S. 18–19). Die Blockstruktur liefert der Modellierer per Annotation in GAMS, weil eine automatische Zerlegung nicht ausreichte und monolithische Modellgenerierung verlangt (S. 8, 22). Generisch statt modellspezifisch: ein einfacher generischer Präkonditionierer, weil viele Problemklassen von sieben Instituten zu bedienen waren (S. 21).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: reine LPs aus ESOM, blockdiagonal mit verknüpfenden Zeilen und Spalten (S. 8, 18). Keine MILP-Komponente; MIP erscheint nur als Einsatzfall exakter Dekomposition (S. 17). Testmodelle: SIMPLE (skalierbares Testmodell, S. 8), REMix (DLR), ELMOD (TU Dresden), EUSTEM (PSI) und die sechs Modelle des Modellexperiments MEXT (S. 30–35). Hauptschwierigkeit sind numerisch schlecht skalierte Instanzen, deren Werte über mehrere Dekaden variieren (S. 19, 38).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Mehrfach-Korrektor-Schritte und getrennte primale und duale Schrittweiten, im Mittel Faktor 2 (S. 19); parallele Matrixskalierung, geometrische Skalierung als Standard; BiCGStab modifiziert, GMRES robuster, aber langsamer (S. 19); paralleles strukturerhaltendes Presolving, Gesamtlaufzeit oft um mehr als 50% reduziert (S. 19–20); die Nonzero-Struktur des globalen Schur-Komplements ist a priori berechenbar, der Präkonditionierer wird verteilt aufgebaut, ohne das Schur-Komplement explizit zu bilden (S. 20–21). Ein hierarchischer Ansatz ist noch nicht vollständig implementiert (S. 21). Blockweise Modellgenerierung verkürzt die Generierung eines Modells mit 512 Blöcken von 1417 auf 32 s (S. 24).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: REMix auf dem DLR-Cluster mit 8 Knoten: 20 Instanzen, jeweils bester PIPS-IPM++-Lauf gegen CPLEX als Innere-Punkte-Verfahren ohne Crossover, beide auf Genauigkeit 1e-5 (S. 24–25); Reduktion zwischen 83,3% und −31,8%, drei Instanzen ohne Gewinn (Tabelle 3, S. 25–26); Spitzenspeicher 139 GB (CPLEX) gegen höchstens 6 GB pro Knoten (S. 25). Skalierung auf JUWELS und Hazel Hen: rund 4500 s mit einem MPI-Prozess, 45,9 s bzw. 50,5 s mit 1024 Kernen (S. 27). ELMOD 16- bis 34-fach (CWE, 19 Knoten) und 50- bis 70-fach (EU, 38 Knoten; drei von vier konventionellen Lösern ohne Lösung) (S. 31–32); EUSTEM 65% bzw. 80% schneller (S. 32). Seeds oder Leistungsvariabilität kommen nicht vor (nicht im Text).
- **D6–D7 (one line)**: Projektbericht ohne Literaturdiskussion; auf eine quantitative Gesamtbewertung der modellbasierten Strategien wird ausdrücklich verzichtet (S. 14–15). Die Zusammenfassung nennt Spitzenwerte (Faktor 26, 76%, 96%; S. 6), nicht die Instanzen ohne Gewinn (S. 26). Tabelle 6 führt den Faktor 26,6 (155472/5838 s) in der Spalte „Reduktion“ als 2563.1%, anders definiert als die Prozentspalte der Tabelle 3 (S. 25, 30; Rechnung des Kartenschreibers).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Beitrag: Software (PIPS-IPM++, GAMS-Annotation und Schnittstelle), Anwendung, Best Practice Guide (S. 5, 8, 41–42). Grenzen konkret benannt: Der neue Ansatz übertrifft die kommerziellen Löser noch nicht konsequent in allen relevanten Problemklassen (S. 21). Viele Linking Constraints verhindern bei einigen Modellen selbst kleine Instanzen (S. 39). Mit PIPS-IPM++ auf HPC gelöst wurden nur ELMOD und EUSTEM (S. 38; S. 37 nennt ELMOD, EUSTEM und BALMOREL mit erfolgreicher Beschleunigung). Größere EUSTEM-Instanzen wurden nicht gerechnet (S. 33). Ausblick: hierarchischer Ansatz, automatische Zerlegung, verteilte Modellgenerierung (S. 21, 33–34, 40).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (die Lücke wird mit Zahlen gedruckt und als nächstes Projekt benannt: kommerzielle Löser noch nicht konsequent übertroffen, hierarchischer Ansatz als nächster Schritt, S. 21; in S027 heißt er „currently being implemented“, S027 p. 12). Method 2 ⚠ (gleiche Genauigkeit 1e-5 und CPLEX ohne Crossover „um die Vergleichbarkeit der Rechenzeiten zu gewährleisten“, S. 25; aber der jeweils beste von mehreren PIPS-IPM++-Läufen gegen eine einzige CPLEX-Konfiguration, S. 25). New-pattern candidate: generic over tailored for a heterogeneous user base (generischer Präkonditionierer wegen vieler Problemklassen, S. 21; vgl. Taste 8). Nebenbei H6 (nummerierte Leitfragen, S. 7).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Für einen fairen Laufzeitvergleich beide Löser auf dieselbe Genauigkeit setzen und beim etablierten Löser die Komponente abschalten, die der neue nicht hat (hier Crossover) (S. 25).
- Lösungsaufwand in rechnerunabhängigen Einheiten (CPLEX-Ticks) messen, wenn Instanzen verschiedener Größe verglichen werden (S. 15).
- In der Ergebnistabelle pro Instanz auch die negativen Reduktionen stehen lassen (Tabelle 3, S. 25–26).
**Quotes**:
- „Der neue Ansatz reicht jedoch noch nicht aus, um die kommerziellen Löser in Bezug auf Lösbarkeit und Laufzeit in allen relevanten Problemklassen konsequent zu übertreffen.“ (S. 21)
- „Eine effiziente Präkonditionierung wiederum ist in der Regel stark problemabhängig und damit ungünstig für BEAM-ME, wo viele verschiedene Problemklassen von sieben Instituten bearbeitet wurden.“ (S. 21)

### D007 · Strengthening SONC Relaxations with Constraints Derived from Variable Bounds (Proceedings of HUGO 2022, pp. 41–44, 2022, arXiv 2211.05518)
**Read level**: full. All four pages (file pp. 1–4) read, including Table 1 and the two references.
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Ksenia Bestuzheva (first author), Helena Völker; all ZIB, Gleixner also HTW Berlin (p. 1) · **Position in research trajectory**: group-era MINLP relaxation work around SCIP, led by Bestuzheva (as is S044); the work list records a separate 2023 ZIB report with the same title (S083, not read here).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: SONC certificates give dual bounds for polynomial problems at a cost that depends on the number of terms, not on the degree, so they suit sparse problems (abstract, p. 1). Entry: a first step to SONC relaxations inside branch and bound, where a relaxation must use the tighter bounds the tree produces (abstract, p. 1; p. 3). Obstacle: a SONC decomposition exists only if all vertices of the Newton polytope are monomial squares, which fails for most instances (pp. 3, 4).
- **D2 Key idea**: derive "bound constraints" x_i^{a_i} ≤ max{|l_i|,|u_i|}^{a_i} from finite variable bounds, with even exponents a_i chosen so that these exponents plus the monomial squares cover every inner term; the vector with a_i in component i alone meets the cover condition (p. 3). Because the constraints are built from the current bounds, they tighten as branching shrinks the box (inferred from their form, p. 3).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: constrained polynomial optimization (1), with finite lower and upper bounds on every variable assumed (pp. 2–3) and α(0) = 0 w.l.o.g. (p. 3); no integer variables in the stated model. The test set is not named; Table 1's rows sum to 349 instances (card writer's sum, p. 4).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: lower bound from a SONC decomposition of the Lagrangian via geometric programming (p. 3); validity of the extended cover is asserted as "easy to check" from the barycentric cover condition (p. 3). The relaxator, its solver and its settings are not described (not in the text).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: one table, with against without bound constraints: optimal 330 / 9, infeasible 0 / 4, unsolvable 0 / 329, numerical error 19 / 7; a finite lower bound on 94.6% against 2.6% of instances (p. 4). No bound quality, times, hardware or versions.
- **D6–D7 (one line)**: cites only Iliman and de Wolff (pp. 2, 4); the one strong word, "drastically", stands next to its count (p. 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: a "first step" (abstract, p. 1): a modelling device plus an applicability count. Limits are not discussed: the numerical errors rise from 7 to 19 without comment (p. 4), and neither bound strength nor use inside the tree is evaluated (not in the text).

**Method links**: New-pattern candidate: relaxation fed by the tree's data (the relaxation is built so that it uses the bounds branch and bound tightens, p. 3; extends H9). Method 3 ⚠ (a limit is printed as a count, 19 numerical errors, but with no cause or follow-up in this text, p. 4).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When a relaxation family needs covers or certificates that the input lacks, synthesize valid constraints from the variable bounds (here x_i^{a_i} ≤ max{|l_i|,|u_i|}^{a_i}), so the relaxation also improves when the tree tightens the bounds (p. 3).
- Before any claim about bound quality, report the outcome distribution of the relaxation solve per variant: optimal, infeasible, unsolvable, numerical error (Table 1, p. 4).
**Quotes**:
- "a desired property in a relaxation that is solved as part of a branch-and-bound algorithm is its ability to utilise tighter variable bounds in order to obtain lower bounds of improved quality" (p. 3)
- "Adding the bound constraints increases the number of instances where a finite lower bound was found from 2.6% to 94.6%, as the bound constraints always provide a valid cover." (p. 4)

### S027 · A massively parallel interior-point solver for LPs with generalized arrowhead structure, and applications to energy system models (European Journal of Operational Research 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2021.06.063)
**Read level**: full. The accepted-version postprint, file pp. 1–13: introduction and contribution, §2, §3 (the matrix algebra (32)–(41) read for its statements; Propositions 1–2 and their proofs read), §4, §5 with Tables 1–3, §6 and acknowledgements; references skimmed. The appendix (ELMOD model, presolving and scaling, the distributed-memory model) is online supplementary material and not in the file (pp. 4, 5, 9, 12).
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Daniel Rehfeldt (first and corresponding author), Hannes Hobbie, David Schönheit, Thorsten Koch, Dominik Möst; Gleixner is last author (p. 2) · **Position in research trajectory**: the journal paper of the BEAM-ME solver line (BMWi funding, p. 12; announced as a submission in S098 p. 43); LP, not MILP; ZIB group-head years.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: high-resolution energy system LPs with hundreds of millions of rows and columns take the best commercial solvers from more than a day to several weeks, may not fit in memory, and general LP solvers cannot run on distributed memory (p. 3). Their arrowhead (doubly bordered block-diagonal) form often has more than 100 000 linking constraints and variables, which makes the Schur-complement decompositions of two-stage stochastic programming prohibitive; most linking rows link only two consecutive blocks ("local") (p. 3).
- **D2 Key idea**: after a symmetric permutation, local linking constraints give the global Schur complement a sparse, doubly bordered block-tridiagonal pattern that can be computed mostly locally and precomputed (pp. 3, 6–7). A sparsified Schur complement C̃ (drop |c_ij| ≤ t·max{|c_ii|,|c_jj|}, after Bomhof and van der Vorst's circuit-simulation preconditioner) is built in a distributed way and, by default, substituted for C in the decomposition, which makes it an implicit preconditioner for the whole augmented system under BiCGSTAB (pp. 7–9). Kept from PIPS-IPM: the Schur decomposition and its incomplete-factorization local Schur complements (pp. 5, 8). Added generic parts: presolving, scaling, multiple centrality correctors, separate primal and dual step lengths (pp. 3, 9).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: LPs in arrowhead form (1)–(6), written in standard form for presentation (pp. 3, 5); assumes few global linking constraints and variables and local linking rows of the form F_i (p. 6); K_i assumed of full rank, with singular K_i handled by perturbation and iterative refinement (p. 5). Data: ELMOD (nodal-pricing model of the European electricity market; CWE and EU configurations for 2014–2016, 8760 hours; pp. 4, 10) and SIMPLE instances from a generator the authors did not build (p. 4).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: block-Gaussian elimination, steps (18)–(23) (p. 5). Proposition 1 counts the nonzeros of the pattern (35): linear in the number of blocks while global linking stays fixed (p. 7). Proposition 2 ("folklore", proved) shows that an inexact Schur solve leaves residual only in the Schur block, whereas perturbed K_i factorizations do not; hence the implicit preconditioner as default (p. 8). MPI across blocks, OpenMP only inside PARDISO 6.0 (p. 9); BiCGSTAB stops at relative residual 10^-10 or 75 iterations; t starts at 10^-3 and is lowered on slow convergence, never below 10^-6; conservative parameters chosen for "robustness, even at the cost of some performance" (p. 9).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: JUWELS (2×24-core Xeon Platinum 8168, 96 or 192 GB), plus a ZIB 88-core, 2 TB machine for commercial runs that did not fit (pp. 9, 11); CPLEX 12.8, Gurobi 8.1, MOSEK 8.1, Xpress 8.4.7 (the extracted text glues footnote markers 6–9 to these numbers; reading inferred), barrier only, crossover off, 16 threads (p. 9); PIPS++ needs a complementarity tolerance of 10^-7 instead of 10^-8 on ELMOD (p. 10); MOSEK dropped after crashing on most large instances (p. 11). Speed-ups up to 220 on 438 processes (SIMPLE3, with an assumed ideal step from one to two processes; Table 1, pp. 10–11); sparsification removes about 95% of C's nonzeros and cuts run time, e.g. 487 → 165 s (Table 2, pp. 10–11); ELMOD_EU in 1 119–1 245 s, but only to a primal–dual gap of about 0.3%, against 66 105–83 715 s for the one commercial solver that solves it (Table 3, p. 11; p. 12). One run per configuration; no variability protocol.
- **D6–D7 (one line)**: positioned against the stochastic-programming IPMs OOPS and PIPS-IPM (p. 3); novelty claimed twice "to the best of the authors' knowledge" (pp. 3, 8); the paper prints the factor that goes against it: on one process the commercial solvers are faster by a factor of 5 or more (p. 11).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithm plus software (PIPS-IPM++), applied to energy LPs with up to four billion nonzeros (abstract, p. 2; p. 12). Limits printed: the relaxed complementarity tolerance (p. 10); the 0.3% gap on the EU instances (p. 12); weaker presolve than the commercial codes (up to 60% against 82% of rows removed, p. 10); different machines in the largest comparisons (p. 11). Future: substitution and dependent-row presolve, more aggressive scaling, a homogeneous self-dual IPM, a hierarchical Schur approach (being implemented), public release in the PIPS repository (p. 12).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (crossover off in the commercial solvers because PIPS++ lacks it, p. 9; a reduced PIPS++ with only the Schur decomposition as an internal baseline, p. 11; the remaining mismatches stated: different machines, p. 11, relaxed tolerance, p. 10; the sequential factor against the new code printed, p. 11). Method 3 ✅ (limits printed with numbers and turned into named next steps, pp. 10–12; the hierarchical approach answers the gap printed in S098 p. 21). New-pattern candidate: generic over tailored for a heterogeneous user base (the preconditioner is meant to serve other energy system models, not only ELMOD, p. 7).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When linking rows are mostly local, permute to expose a block-tridiagonal Schur complement, count its nonzeros a priori (Proposition 1) and use the known pattern to save memory and communication (pp. 6–7).
- Sparsify the Schur complement with a relative drop rule and use it inside the decomposition as an implicit preconditioner of the whole system; lower the drop threshold only when the Krylov solver stalls (pp. 7–9).
- Report a stripped version of your own solver (only the textbook decomposition) beside the full one, so readers see what the added techniques carry (p. 11).
- In scaling runs, use process counts that divide the number of blocks, and state any assumed ideal step for configurations that do not fit in memory (p. 11).
**Quotes**:
- "Also, crossover (which can considerably increase the run time) is turned off, as this feature is not implemented in our solver." (p. 9)
- "While the commercial solvers are sequentially considerably faster (which is unsurprising, considering the decades of development that has been invested into them), the key advantage of the new approach is its scalability." (p. 12)

### S063 · On generalized surrogate duality in mixed-integer nonlinear programming (Mathematical Programming 2022, DOI 10.1007/s10107-021-01691-6; arXiv 1912.00356)
**Read level**: partial. The ZIB-Report 19-55 / arXiv v1 version (December 2019), not the 2022 journal text: abstract and §1–§8 (file pp. 3–33) read, with the proofs of Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 followed in outline; references (pp. 33–39) not read; appendix Tables 4–5 (pp. 40–72, per-instance data) read only for captions and first rows.
**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Benjamin Müller, Gonzalo Muñoz, Maxime Gasse, Andrea Lodi, Felipe Serrano; the byline puts Müller and Muñoz first and the rest in alphabetical order (p. 3), so the first two read as lead authors (inference) · **Position in research trajectory**: group-era MINLP dual-bounding work in SCIP with ZIB PhDs and postdocs (Müller and Serrano are listed as mentees in SKILL.md Academic Lineage) and Polytechnique Montréal; the IPCO 2020 version is S051 (work list; not read here).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: spatial branch and bound depends on its relaxations, which are convex by convention; since solvers now handle moderate nonconvexity, tighter nonconvex relaxations become usable (abstract, p. 3; p. 4). Surrogate relaxations, studied for linear integer programs in the 1970s and 1980s and "scarcely considered since", are revisited (abstract, p. 3); to the authors' knowledge they had never been used in practice for general MINLPs (p. 6). S(λ) is quasi-concave, in general discontinuous, and flat over most of the multiplier space (p. 5).
- **D2 Key idea**: keep the Benders-type multiplier search of Banerjee, Karwan and Dyer for one aggregation (Algorithm 1: an LP master that maximizes the violation of earlier subproblem optima, pp. 9–10; SCIP as a black box for each subproblem, p. 13) and generalize it to K aggregations, where the master becomes a disjunctive problem written as a big-M MILP (pp. 17–18, Algorithm 2). Practical enhancements: SCIP's root LP relaxation with its cuts inside S(λ), early stopping of subproblems at a target bound, early stopping of the master (α = 0.2), simple symmetry-breaking rows, support and box trust-region stabilization (pp. 11–13, 21–23).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MINLP (1) with a compact mixed-integer linear set X and factorable continuous g_i, feasible region assumed nonempty (pp. 3–4); Proposition 1 needs continuous g_i and compact X (p. 14). Data: MINLPLib, 1683 instances at the time, of which 1671 are in OSiL and handled by SCIP (p. 25); ROOTGAP keeps 633 after dropping root-solved, no-primal and LP-numerics instances (p. 26); DUALBOUND keeps 209 by four criteria (p. 29).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: lower semicontinuity of S^K by compactness and convergent subsequences (Proposition 1, p. 14); a two-constraint LP showing that S^K is not quasi-concave for K = 2 (Example 2, p. 15); monotonicity in K and exactness at K = m via unit-vector aggregations (Theorem 2, p. 16); an NLP where K = 1 is unbounded and K = 2 proves optimality (Example 3, p. 16); convergence of Algorithm 2 via monotone Ψ_t and convergent subsequences (Theorem 3, pp. 19–20). M is bounded by min{max_i |g_i(x̄)|, Ψ_prev} (p. 18). A relaxator plug-in runs after the root node, with tolerances tighter than SCIP's defaults (dual feasibility 10^-8, feasibility 10^-7) and a gap limit of 10^-4 (p. 25).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: three numbered questions, ROOTGAP, BENDERS and DUALBOUND (p. 24); 1 h per K ∈ {1, 2, 3}, each K+1 run warm-started from the best K solution (p. 24); five settings for K = 3, DEFAULT, PLAIN, NOSTAB, NOSUPP and NOEARLY (p. 25); SCIP for 3 h, then Algorithm 2 for 3 h with a 20% target (p. 25); gap closed against MINLPLib's best primal bound, shifted geometric means with shifts of 10 s and 5% (pp. 25–26); Xeon X5672, one job per node, a SCIP development version with CPLEX 12.8.0.0, Ipopt 3.12.11 and bliss 0.73 (p. 26). Results: mean root gap closed 18.4%, 21.4% and 23.4% for K = 1, 2, 3 on 633 instances (p. 26), up to 46.9% on 469 affected ones (p. 27); DEFAULT closes more gap than PLAIN on 100 of 457 affected instances and less on 36 (p. 27); early termination of the master matters most (p. 28); SCIP's dual bound improved on 53 of 209 DUALBOUND instances (p. 29). No seeds or permutations.
- **D6–D7 (one line)**: a long, even-handed history from Glover to Kim and Kim (pp. 6–8), and techniques that did not help are reported (Remark 1, the dual cutoff, constraint filters, lexicographic rows, smoothing; pp. 10, 12, 21–23). The conclusion credits "our tree experiments" with an average gap reduction from 284.3% to 142.8% "on very difficult MINLPs" (p. 33), but §6 reports that figure for the DUALBOUND root runs, on the 53 of 209 instances where Algorithm 2 improved SCIP's bound (p. 29); the tree-search use (Algorithm 3) improved local bounds at seven nodes of one instance (pp. 31–32). §6 also points to "Table 5" for the DUALBOUND details, but Table 5 is the BENDERS table (pp. 29, 57).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory (generalization, properties, convergence), the first algorithm for the K-surrogate dual (p. 6) and a SCIP plug-in. Limits printed: too costly for every node (p. 30); on DUALBOUND most subproblems are not solved to optimality within the limit (p. 30); Algorithm 3 rarely helps (pp. 31–32); no good a-priori constraint filter was found (p. 22). Open questions: quadratic constraints with convex subproblems, and a purely surrogate-based spatial branch and bound with its own branching rule (p. 33).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (limits and negative results printed with counts, open questions named; pp. 12, 22, 30–33). Method 6 ⚠ (the paper's own test sets are cut by stated criteria with totals, 1683 → 1671, 633, 469 and 209, but without per-criterion counts; pp. 25–29). New-pattern candidates: relaxation fed by the tree's data (SCIP's root LP relaxation is "the most crucial ingredient", p. 12; the plug-in runs after the root node, p. 24; extends H9); re-derive as an instance, then generalize (the K = 1 Benders search becomes the K-aggregation method with a MILP master, pp. 9–10, 17–18). Also H6 ✅ (numbered questions, p. 24) and Workflow B step 6 ✅ (one job per node, p. 26); H8 ✗ in the conclusion's headline (p. 33 against p. 29).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Stop a subproblem as soon as it finds a point with objective at most the target bound: that point is the next cut for the master and also proves that this multiplier cannot reach the target (pp. 12–13).
- Bound the big-M of a disjunctive master by quantities already known (max_i |g_i(x̄)| and the previous Ψ) (p. 18).
- Stabilize a Benders-type multiplier search by fixing the support of the last improving multiplier and boxing it, and release both after 20 non-improving iterations (pp. 23, 25).

- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Theorem 1 is proved by substituting z = 0 and z = 1, with a strength comparison when φ is already valid for the off set (pp. 9–10); Theorem 2 by computing the coefficient of (1 − z) (p. 10); worked examples with a cubic (pp. 10–11, 14). Semi-continuity is detected from pairs of implied bounds with equal offsets, found in linear constraints or by probing, and propagated to auxiliary variables by interval arithmetic (p. 12); for nonconvex constraints the handler first probes z = 1 to tighten bounds, then estimates (p. 13).

### S062 · Improving conflict analysis in MIP solvers by pseudo-Boolean reasoning (CP 2023, LIPIcs 280, 27:1–27:19, DOI 10.4230/lipics.cp.2023.27; arXiv 2307.14166)

**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Gioni Mexi (first author, ZIB), Timo Berthold (FICO and TU Berlin), Jakob Nordström (Copenhagen and Lund); Gleixner is third author, at HTW and ZIB (p. 1) · **Position in research trajectory**: the pseudo-Boolean import line with Nordström (SKILL.md Research Trajectory, 2020–2025; after "Learn to relax", ref. - **D1 Problem and entry point**: MIP conflict analysis reasons with clauses extracted from linear constraints, which loses exponential reasoning power, whereas PB solvers reason natively with 0–1 linear inequalities (abstract, p. 1; p. 3); yet PB solvers do not deliver this in practice, and on larger benchmark sets SCIP tends to be clearly more effective on PB optimization problems (p. 3). - **D2 Key idea**: read PB conflict analysis in MIP terms, as a sequence of linear combinations and cuts: saturation is coefficient tightening and division is a Chvátal–Gomory cut (p. 3), so a stronger cut can be used; the new reduction uses mixed integer rounding and dominates division (Lemma 4, p. 8), while saturation and MIR appear incomparable (p. 3). - **D3 Problem class and data model**: pure 0–1 ILPs; the theory uses normalized PB constraints with integer data, the implementation general linear constraints with real coefficients in floating point (pp. 4, 9). - **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: invariant: the resolvent stays falsified; Example 1 shows that plain generalized resolution breaks it (p. 5). Slack arguments: Lemma 1 (saturation reaches slack 0 after weakening, pp. 6–7), Lemma 2 (division or MIR with d = a_r reaches slack at most 0, by integrality of the slack, pp. 7–8), Lemmas 3–4 (dominance by coefficient comparison: saturation implies clausal, MIR implies division, p. 8). - **D5 Computational evaluation**: SCIP 8.0.3, Xeon Gold 6338, 16 GB (p. 9); baselines SCIP without conflict analysis and a clausal analysis restricted for fairness (no upgrading, one conflict per call; p. 10); shifted geometric means (1 s, 100 nodes) on all (975), affected (295), [100, limit] (218) and all-optimal (374) instances, with nodes compared only on all-optimal (pp. 10–11). **Transferable techniques**:
- To import a rival method, restate each of its steps in your own field's terms (here: a reduction step is a cut rule), then use the strongest member of that family and prove dominance by comparing coefficients (pp. 3, 8).
