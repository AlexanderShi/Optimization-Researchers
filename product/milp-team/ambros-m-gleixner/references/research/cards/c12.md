- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A release report, not a research question: it "discusses enhancements and extensions contained in version 8.0" (abstract, p. 1) with two declared aims, to explain the changes in SCIP's solving process and "analyze the resulting improvements on MILP and MINLP instances, both in terms of performance and robustness", and to show practitioners the new interfaces and extensions (p. 3). Most components enter from a named limit of SCIP 7.0: symmetry handling for binary variables only, and orbitopes only when a whole component is orbitopal (p. 8); chance-constrained programs for mixing cuts (p. 11); PADM's penalty-only objective, which "can lead to arbitrarily bad solutions" (p. 13). The largest rework enters from a numerical defect: explicit reformulation in presolve let SCIP report an optimal solution with gap 0.00% while the original constraint is violated (reproduced log ending "best solution is not feasible in original problem", p. 20); this happened on 7% of MINLPLib instances (p. 20). The paper explains why a final feasibility check would not repair it: the link from original to presolved problem was lost, so it was unclear which operation would remedy a violation (p. 20). Gleixner's credited part enters from an integration need: PaPILO had to postsolve dual solutions and bases before SoPlex could use it as a presolver (pp. 61-62).
  - Transferable: open a reliability rework with the solver's own log of the failure and the failure's frequency on a public library, then say why the obvious patch (a final check) cannot repair it (p. 20).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: No single idea; three components matter for this field. (i) Nonlinear constraints (credited to BM, FeS, KB, SV, p. 82): keep the original constraints, apply only simplifications that "in most situations, do not relax the feasible space when taking tolerances into account", and move the reformulation needed for relaxations into annotations of the expression graph (pp. 20-21); two extended formulations, one for propagation whose auxiliary bounds are stored in expressions and never become SCIP variables, one for the LP whose auxiliary variables are added as relaxation-only (pp. 21-23); structure detection in "nonlinear handler" plugins managed by cons_nonlinear (p. 25); no branching on auxiliary variables by default (pp. 21, 39). Kept from 7.0: spatial branch and bound mixing branch-and-infer and branch-and-cut (p. 21), FBBT (p. 30), the branching-point rule (p. 42). Removed: the specialised quadratic, second-order cone, absolute power and bivariate handlers (p. 19). (ii) Symmetry (JvD, CH, MP; FW for nonlinear detection; p. 82): SST cuts for general variables from a leader, orbit and stabilizer chain after Salvagnin (p. 8); heuristic suborbitope detection that sieves permutations decomposing into 2-cycles (p. 9); a fixed order per component, (sub)orbitopes first, then SST cuts, then symresacks or orbital fixing (p. 10). (iii) PaPILO 2.0 dual and basis postsolve (credited to AH and AG, p. 83): every modification that affects duals is recorded on the postsolve stack, for example row-bound changes through complementary slackness (p. 62); in dual-postsolve mode bound tightenings are applied only when they fix a variable, because otherwise the reduced solution may map to "a non-vertex solution in the original space" that needs crossover (p. 62); unbounded variables receive a finite bound "slightly worse than the best possible bound so that the bound can not be tight in the reduced problem", for pure LPs only (p. 62); eight LP presolvers support dual postsolve, the others are MILP-only or must be disabled (p. 62, fns. 6-7). Each component states what changed against 7.0 (pp. 7-8, 17-19, 42-43, 62).
  - Transferable: when a presolver must hand back duals and a basis, allow only reductions whose postsolve keeps a vertex a vertex (fix-type bound changes, artificial bounds that cannot be tight) and make the rest switchable (p. 62).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: MILP (1) with bounds in R ∪ {±∞}, assuming variables are not fixed to ±∞ (p. 2); MINLP (2) with explicit algebraic functions built from base expressions known to SCIP (p. 2); constraint integer programs as the framework class (pp. 2-3). Structures exploited: variable bound relations with binary x and non-binary y, normalised by preprocessing steps (i)-(iv) into assumption (A) (pp. 11-12); user decompositions with linking variables only (PADM, p. 13) or linking constraints only (DPS, p. 14); quadratic, SOC, bilinear, convex/concave, quotient and semi-continuous (perspective) structures inside expressions (pp. 42-53); the formulation group of permutations that leave the constraint system invariant, since the full symmetry group is NP-hard to compute (p. 7). Correctness arguments state their conditions: implicit discreteness needs a non-binary variable with finite bounds, no objective coefficient, one polynomial constraint with even-power monomials of one sign and an infinite side (p. 29); integrality identification needs a_i/b and c/b integral and integral f_i (p. 29). Test material: MIPLIB 2003, 2010 and 2017 plus COR@L for 347 MILP instances and MINLPLib for 113 (p. 4); all 1678 MINLPLib instances in §4.14 with the caveat that MINLPLib "is not designed to be benchmark set" because some models are overrepresented (p. 59); the 240 MIPLIB 2017 benchmark instances for PaPILO (p. 64); 90 chance-constrained lot-sizing instances (p. 13); 73 strIPlib instances for GCG (pp. 73, 113); Gally et al.'s 194 mixed-integer SDPs (p. 80).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: For this skill the main content is floating-point safeguards without exact arithmetic (group; §4.2 credited to BM, FeS, KB, SV, p. 82). Propagation: the nonlinear code uses outward-rounded interval arithmetic but many other parts of SCIP do not, so bounds are relaxed on entry by 1e-9·max(1,|b|), at most 1e-3 of the domain width, never for integer variables and never past the next integer; sides are relaxed by 1e-9 absolute; a new interval within ε = 1e-9 of the domain fixes the variable at the nearest bound instead of declaring infeasibility (p. 31); redundancy checks relax bounds and sides by the feasibility tolerance (p. 31); in the backward pass, tightenings are usually applied only on fixing, ≥5% relative progress or a sign change (p. 32); bounds from backward propagation are kept apart so that a node's propagation depends only on bounds of SCIP variables, not on another node's backward pass (p. 31). Cut cleanup before a cut enters SCIP's storage (p. 34): coefficient ratio at most 1e7 by eliminating extreme terms with bounds (a global cut may become local), scaling by 2^p into [1e-4, 1e4], relaxing coefficients within 1e-9 of an integer and right-hand sides near zero, because the SCIP ROW would round them, "since that could make the cut invalid"; a "strong" cut must close at least 20% of the convexification gap (α = 0.2, eq. (17), p. 33); when enforcement admits weak cuts, a cut must still be violated by at least 2^-50·max(|b|, max_j|a_j x̂_j|), justified by the 52-bit mantissa (p. 36). Enforcement ladder for almost-feasible LP points (pp. 35-38): tighten the LP feasibility tolerance to max(ε, v/2) and resolve when bound violations exceed constraint violations or constraint violations fall below the LP tolerance (p. 35); weak cuts only for constraints with at least 0.5 of the maximal violation (p. 36); variables whose bounds relaxed a failed cut become branching candidates (p. 36); last resorts are branching on any unfixed variable and cutting off a node whose variables are all fixed, with a log message (pp. 37-38); counts of weak cuts, tolerance tightenings and such branchings are kept as "an indication that SCIP has numerical problems to solve this instance" (p. 38). The ex1252 case study shows the ladder at work (pp. 36-37). Other checks: an Ipopt claim of local infeasibility is accepted only if the point proves infeasibility (Berthold-Witzig Theorem 1), otherwise the status becomes "unknown" (p. 58); subnlp re-solves the NLP with a tightened feasibility tolerance when a solution is rejected although its objective is good enough and its maximal violation is close to the feasibility tolerance in a presolved copy (pp. 56-57); the ROWPREP structure is made public because changing a SCIP ROW "may round numbers that are very close to integral values, which may invalidate a previously valid cut" (p. 17, MP and SV). PaPILO (§6): transaction-based presolve whose reductions are applied in a deterministic sequential order, conflicting ones discarded (p. 62); in 1.0 single-thread runs deferred reductions to round end for thread-count-independent results, 2.0 applies them immediately (switchable) (p. 63); an analysis of conflicts led to new reduction types RHS_LESS_RESTRICTIVE and LHS_LESS_RESTRICTIVE and a new order (pp. 63-64); after postsolve PaPILO checks primal and dual feasibility and the KKT conditions, logs the result and does not abort, with an optional check after every postsolve step in debug builds (p. 63); a debug solution passed with -b must survive presolve and postsolve back to itself (p. 64). Proof-type content is short validity arguments, no theorems: preprocessing into assumption (A) (p. 12), separation in O(|N| log|N|) and O(|M||N|) (p. 13), linear-time symresack minimal-cover separation using path and cycle components (pp. 10-11), lock propagation through the expression graph by monotonicity (pp. 24-25), the move-to-bound argument for implicit discreteness (p. 29).
  - Transferable: the cut-cleanup recipe (pp. 33-34, 36) and the bound-relaxation rules (p. 31) can be copied into any separator or propagator that runs in floating point next to code that does not round outward.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Release-comparison protocol: identical compiler and third-party versions in both arms, one single-threaded run per node, five seeds, admission of instances solved by either version with at least one seed, shifted geometric means with shifts 1 s and 100 nodes, brackets [0,tl], [1,tl], [10,tl], [100,tl], [1000,tl], diff-timeouts and both-solved; compare both-solved with all before claiming a speed-up (pp. 4-5).
- Component-isolation run: branch the development line, replace one component, list what else changed, run the whole library with two extra permutations per instance, and classify each run as final solution infeasible in the original problem, failed (bounds contradict best known, fatal error, no stop at the time limit), solved or time limit; define "fastest" with a tolerance, brackets [t1, t2) as "at least one ran ≥ t1, both finished < t2", and add performance profiles for time and for gap at termination (pp. 59-61).
- Cut cleanup before adding a floating-point cut: coefficient ratio ≤ 1e7 by eliminating extreme terms with bounds; scale by a power of two into [1e-4, 1e4]; relax coefficients within 1e-9 of an integer and right-hand sides near zero with bounds; require a violation of at least 2^-50·max(|b|, max_j|a_j x̂_j|) and 20% closure of the convexification gap; discard what fails (pp. 33-34, 36).
- Bound relaxation before interval propagation when other code does not round outward: 1e-9·max(1,|b|), capped at 1e-3 of the domain width, none for integer variables, never past the next integer; snap to the nearest bound instead of declaring infeasibility within ε; relax by the feasibility tolerance for redundancy checks (p. 31).
- Enforcement ladder for almost-feasible points: tighten the LP feasibility tolerance to max(ε, v/2) and resolve; allow weak cuts only for strongly violated constraints; turn the variables whose bounds relaxed a failed cut into branching candidates; count every fallback in the statistics (pp. 35-38).
- Dual-postsolve design: record every reduction that affects duals; in LP dual-postsolve mode apply bound tightenings only when they fix; give unbounded variables finite bounds that cannot be tight; after postsolve check primal and dual feasibility and KKT, with a debug option to check after each step (pp. 62-63).
- Presolve debugging with a known solution: pass a feasible solution, check that it survives presolve and that the reduced solution postsolves back to it; switch off dual-reasoning presolvers, which may correctly cut off optimal solutions (p. 64).
- Estimate the memory of an external LP solver by a linear regression on rows, columns and nonzeros measured on LP relaxations (weights 8.5e-4, 7.6e-4, 3.5e-5; R^2 = 0.99 for SoPlex), doubled when NLPs are solved (p. 16).
