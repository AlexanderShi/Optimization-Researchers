### S083 · Strengthening SONC relaxations with constraints derived from variable bounds (arXiv preprint 2023, arXiv 2304.12145; ZIB-Report 23-03)
**Read level**: full. Text read: arXiv 2304.12145v1, titled "Strengthening SONC Relaxations with Constraints Derived from Variable Bounds"; pp. 1–20 read completely (abstract, §§1–7, acknowledgements, declarations); references (pp. 20–23) skimmed.

**Role**: supplement · **Co-authors**: Ksenia Bestuzheva (first author, ZIB), Helena Völker (ZIB) (p. 1) · **Place in the trajectory**: MINLP side; Gleixner is listed at ZIB and HTW Berlin (p. 1); funded by Research Campus MODAL (p. 20). The implementation is a Python relaxator on a development branch of PySCIPOpt (p. 13, fn. 1). The skill's trajectory notes list this line (this preprint and its HUGO 2022 version) among directions with no journal version found (06-trajectory.md §6, item 4).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: spatial branch and bound rests on linear or convex relaxations, and "conventional relaxations lack the means to efficiently capture polynomial nonlinearities"; certificate relaxations (SOS, SONC, SAGE) capture whole polynomials but need expensive hierarchies, and SOS scales poorly with the degree (abstract; pp. 2–3). A SONC certificate costs by the number of terms, not by the degree (abstract; p. 2). Seidler's first SONC branch and bound branches on variable signs and has no convergence guarantee (p. 3). Entry: a relaxation solved inside branch and bound must use the tighter variable bounds of the nodes (p. 10), but a bound term x_i − l_i does not help a SONC bound because x_i is not a monomial square (p. 10); on most instances the Lagrangian's Newton polytope lacks even vertices, so the standard SONC relaxation gives a finite root bound on only 9 of 349 instances (p. 16).
- **D2 Key idea and design**: add polynomial-bound constraints x_i^α ≤ max{|l_i|, |u_i|}^α with an even exponent α per variable: valid on the box, they add monomial squares and no new non-square terms to the Lagrangian (p. 10). With α ≥ (n + (n mod 2)) · max ‖β‖∞ over the non-square exponents β (the mark that tells this set apart from MoSq is lost in the extracted text, p. 4), every non-square term is covered (Theorem 6, p. 11), and the new exponents with the origin form a simplex (p. 14). Unchanged: the Lagrangian geometric-programming relaxation of Dressler, Iliman and de Wolff, restated in §3 (pp. 6–10); changed: only the exponent set fed to it. Why this lever: degree costs a SONC certificate nothing, so the paper buys structure with degree (p. 12). Second contribution: a SCIP relaxator that uses the SONC bound wherever it beats the LP bound (pp. 3, 12).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: polynomial programs min f(x) s.t. g_i(x) ≥ 0 over x ∈ Rⁿ (p. 3), every variable with finite bounds (assumed, p. 10; true in the test set apart from SCIP's objective variable, which the relaxator removes, p. 15). The GP relaxation needs an ST-polynomial Lagrangian whose inner terms are not monomial squares (such terms are dropped otherwise) and, for each vertex α ≠ 0, a coefficient with exactly one strictly positive term (Theorem 5) (pp. 8–9). The relaxation runs only if at least half of the constraints are polynomial; linear constraints are added, non-polynomial ones dropped (p. 13). Data: the 349-instance set of the RAPOSa paper, 180 random polynomial instances of Dalkiran and Sherali and 169 MINLPLib polynomial instances with continuous variables (p. 15). Integer variables and MILP structure play no part.
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design** (brief): a Python relaxator via PySCIPOpt inspects the problem, undoes SCIP's objective-variable reformulation (it adds a monomial y that no square covers), builds coefficient vectors and exponent matrices, adds the bound constraints, and calls POEM, which solves the GP after a logarithmic transformation with ECOS via CVXPY (pp. 12–14). If the Lagrangian is already ST, only bound constraints whose exponents are already in its support are added, so no new monomials appear; otherwise the Theorem 6 exponents are added and all other vertices discarded, because POEM cannot treat affinely dependent vertices in the constrained case and keeping the multipliers consistent across ST pieces is left undone; discarding is valid since square vertices can only raise the bound, but it weakens the bound (pp. 14–15). Proof device: explicit barycentric coordinates β_i/α_ii on the new axis exponents, the remainder on the origin, nonnegative by the exponent bound (pp. 11–12); the step from a cover to a finite bound is not written as a separate statement (my reading of pp. 10–12). Call rule: "in the root node and in every 10 nodes" (p. 13) against "depth levels that are multiples of 10" (p. 15); the two descriptions differ (my reading). No warm start (p. 18).
- **D5 Computational evaluation**: a development SCIP in three arms (standard SONC; SONC plus bound constraints, with three exponent choices; default SCIP), no other solver run; 3600 s, gap tolerance 0.01%, Xeon E5-2660 2.60 GHz with 128 GB per node, threads not stated, no seeds or permutations (p. 15); metrics: a census of root relaxator statuses (optimal, infeasible, unsolvable, interrupted, did not run; Tables 1–2), shifted geometric means of time (shift 1 s) and nodes (shift 100) (Table 4; Table 3 states no shift), a best-time rule t/t1 ≤ 1.01 and t/t2 ≤ 0.9, root-bound wins and gap closed (Tables 3–5) (pp. 15–19); failures printed (4 lost infeasibility detections, 12 extra timeouts, p. 16); the exponent default is picked on the same 349 instances (pp. 17–18).
- **D6 Positioning**: between spatial branch and bound with RLT (RAPOSa) and certificate hierarchies (Shor, Nesterov, Lasserre, Parrilo; Iliman and de Wolff; Chandrasekaran and Shah), continuing Seidler (pp. 2–3); §§2–3 summarize known theory (pp. 3, 6); RAPOSa's test set is used, but RAPOSa is not run (p. 15).
- **D7 Writing**: the headline is a count (9 → 330 of 349, abstract); the negative result is announced in the introduction before any table (p. 3); causes are hedged ("This might have been caused", p. 16; "most likely", p. 19).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory (Theorem 6), an experimental relaxator, and finite root bounds on 330 instead of 9 of 349 instances (pp. 11, 16). Limits printed with numbers: shifted geometric mean time 25.29 s against 123.04 s (factor 4.9, my arithmetic) and nodes 27.04 against 34.60; the LP root bound is better on 305 instances, the SONC bound on 6, equal on 38 (Table 4, p. 18); causes named: SONC cost, no warm start, no presolve, reductions or branching aimed at SONC bounds (p. 18); infeasibility no longer detected on 4 instances, with two untested causes (p. 16); discarded vertices weaken bounds (p. 14). My reading of Table 5: five of the six root gains are one-variable unconstrained problems of degree 4 or 6 (76.1–91.8% of the gap closed); the sixth, waterund01 (quadratic, 40 variables, 38 constraints), gets 11.3% (p. 19). The abstract's guarantee is Theorem 6's, but its 330 comes from the default exponent 2 · max β_i + 4, which equals A(n4) for n = 1, 2 and in general falls outside Theorem 6 for larger n (p. 17, second quote); the choices within Theorem 6, A(n0) and A(n4), give 308 and 314, losing instances to degenerate points (a known POEM issue), to numerical trouble from large powers of the bounds, and to more timeouts (23 and 21 against 14) (Table 2, p. 17), while §6.1 says the bound constraints "always provide a valid cover" (p. 16). Future work: a SONC-based branch and bound whose reductions and branching strengthen SONC relaxations, and better use of bounds and constraints (p. 20); the remaining data were not yet public (p. 20).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) Give an imported relaxation the structure it needs through redundant constraints derived from variable bounds: x_i^α ≤ max{|l_i|, |u_i|}^α with even α adds one monomial square per variable and tightens by itself as branching shrinks the box (pp. 2, 10–11).
- (D4) Prove a covering condition by writing the witness: barycentric weights β_i/α_ii on the axis exponents, the remainder on the origin, and an exponent bound chosen so that the remainder is nonnegative (Theorem 6, pp. 11–12).
- (D4) Check what the host solver's own reformulations do to a plug-in's structural requirement, and undo them inside the plug-in: SCIP's objective variable is removed and its constraint moved back into the objective (p. 14).
- (D5) Report a new relaxation first as a census of root termination statuses per arm (optimal, infeasible, unsolvable, interrupted, did not run), then speed (Tables 1–2, pp. 15–17).
- (D5) Read a slowdown against the node count: a time factor far above the node factor puts the cost in each node, not in a larger search (Table 4, p. 18).
- (D5) When the theorem's parameter is numerically fragile, run the guaranteed choice, a padded one and a cheap unguaranteed one side by side, report statuses for each, and say which one lies outside the theorem (Table 2, p. 17).
