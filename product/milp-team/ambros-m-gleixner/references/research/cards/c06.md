### S011 · Three Enhancements for Optimization-Based Bound Tightening (Journal of Global Optimization 2017, DOI 10.1007/s10898-016-0450-4)
**Read level**: full. Body, references and acknowledgements (file pp. 3–25) read in full. Appendix A (Tables 3–8, file pp. 26–72, per-instance data) skimmed: captions, column headers, the rows of every instance named in the text, and a script recount of Table 2 from Tables 7–8 (see D5).
**Role**: core · **Co-authors**: Timo Berthold (FICO), Benjamin Müller (ZIB), Stefan Weltge (OvGU Magdeburg); Gleixner is first author and the byline is not alphabetical (pp. 1, 3) · **Position in research trajectory**: the MINLP-components line in SCIP (SKILL.md Research Trajectory, 2006–2015), parallel to the exact-LP work; this report prints the same numbers SKILL.md cites from the thesis (11 INT timeouts solved 1 / 6 / 10, the 5.7% gain, five instances with factors 90 to 357; here pp. 19, 21; SKILL.md: [Th pp. 145–147]), so it appears to publish the thesis experiment (thesis not read here); funded by Research Campus MODAL (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: OBBT is among the most effective and the most expensive bound-tightening procedures for nonconvex MINLP, since it solves up to twice as many auxiliary LPs as there are variables (abstract, p. 3; 2n LPs, p. 6). The entry is a field belief: most solvers run a restricted OBBT, and OBBT is believed to pay off once its cost is under control (abstract, p. 3). The stated goal is algorithmic techniques for an efficient OBBT implementation (abstract, p. 3). The motivation is relaxation mechanics: the convex envelope of a nonconvex term tightens as the domain shrinks (Fig. 1, p. 4), and branching on a continuous variable leaves the relaxation optimum inside both children (p. 4), so bounds and relaxations tighten each other (p. 4). Existing solvers (αBB, ANTIGONE, Couenne, LaGO, SCIP) run OBBT mostly at the root, and in the tree only with limited frequency or by success rate (p. 6).

- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: keep the OBBT LP itself (minimize and maximize x_k over the relaxation plus an objective cut-off, (5), p. 6) and change what is done with its solutions and in which order. (i) The optimal dual of each OBBT LP gives a Lagrangian variable bound (LVB), a one-row relaxation. When created it reproduces the OBBT bound. It is re-propagated cheaply, by substituting current bounds, whenever a right-hand-side bound shrinks (globally or by branching) or the incumbent improves (Theorem 1, p. 7; §2.2, pp. 8–9). It can be weaker than re-running OBBT, because its dual may no longer be optimal and the LP may have gained rows (p. 9). (ii) Any feasible relaxation point that satisfies the cut-off certifies that the bounds tight at it cannot improve, so those LPs are skipped (filtering, pp. 11–12). (iii) LPs are ordered greedily by the distance between the current LP value and the target bound, to exploit simplex warm starts (Algorithm 2, p. 13). Compared with the nearest predecessors: Zamora and Grossmann filtered with the optimal relaxation solution only, while the paper uses any feasible point, including those met during OBBT, at no extra cost (p. 11). Tawarmalani and Sahinidis made the general observation that dual solutions give one-row relaxations; the paper restricts it to OBBT duals and propagates only toward the left-hand-side variable (p. 9). LVBs are redundant and are not added to the LP (Remark 3, p. 9).

- **D3 Problem class and data model**: the model is MINLP (1). The objective is linear without loss of generality (via an auxiliary variable), the constraints are factorable, and no smoothness is assumed (pp. 3–4). Theorem 1 assumes a linear relaxation {Ax ≥ b, x ∈ [l,u]}, a valid upper bound U and an optimal primal–dual pair of the OBBT LP (p. 7). It holds for every valid U, so LVBs stay valid as the incumbent improves (p. 9). LVBs can be absent or trivial: the OBBT LP is unbounded, the bound is implied by constraints, or nothing is tightened; dual degeneracy can still give nonzero duals in the last case, as seen in the experiments (Remark 2, p. 7). OBBT runs on all nonbinary nonlinear variables, also those only in convex constraints, because convex terms cannot always be enforced by outer approximation for numerical reasons (p. 11). Binaries are excluded: tentative fixing to 0 and 1 (equivalent to strong branching) gives the same result with dual-simplex warm starts, whereas OBBT LPs need the primal simplex (p. 11). Integer variables get rounded bounds (Algorithm 2, lines 13 and 18, p. 13). SOS, indicator or semi-continuous structure is not discussed (not in the text). Data: MINLPLib2 instances in OSiL, split after presolve into INT (integer variables remain, 605) and GO (pure NLP, 347) (p. 15). Filtering is exact only with exact tightness tests; a floating-point code needs a small nonzero tolerance (p. 12).

- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**:
  - Proofs use LP duality only. Theorem 1 aggregates the rows with the optimal duals plus the cut-off multiplier, and tightness follows from complementary slackness (p. 7); its validity is also an instance of the (PU-PR) range-reduction framework of Tawarmalani and Sahinidis (p. 7). A chain of minimizations, (15)–(17), shows that minimizing x_k over the one-row relaxation gives the OBBT bound, and evaluating it by substituting bounds, (18)–(19), is FBBT on the LVB (pp. 8–9). Example 1, with explicit duals and Fig. 2, shows a nontrivial LVB even where OBBT does not tighten, and that iterating OBBT with relaxation refinement converges only in the limit (pp. 7–8).
  - Propagation order: an LVB graph over lower- and upper-bound nodes, with strongly connected components shrunk and topologically sorted in linear time (Tarjan). Changed bounds label their out-neighbours as candidates, and each LVB is propagated once per call. Cycles can give non-finite convergence as in FBBT, and repeat limits are left to the framework (pp. 9–10).
  - Filtering (Algorithm 1, aggressive bound filtering): maximize a direction v over the relaxation with cut-off, zero the coefficients of filtered bounds, and repeat until everything is filtered, v becomes a unit direction, or the number filtered in the last round drops below a threshold δ; sign restrictions keep the LP bounded (pp. 11–12). A basis-status rule keeps filtering from losing LVBs: a variable basic at its bound (primal degeneracy) gets a short primal-simplex solve to harvest an LVB, and a nonbasic one is filtered at once (Remark 5, p. 12). With filtering off, greedy order picks the tight bounds first and solves them with almost no pivots, which makes trivial filtering almost superfluous except for nonbasic variables, which should be skipped (Remark 7, p. 19).
  - Orderings: greedy, reverse greedy, min–max, and random as a control (p. 13). A Prim-style minimum-spanning-tree order over stored bases failed: storing and restoring bases costs too much and invalidates the factorization (Remark 6, p. 14).
  - SCIP implementation: two propagator plug-ins. OBBT LPs use a dual feasibility tolerance of 10^-9, against SCIP's default 10^-7, because OBBT's validity rests on dual feasibility. OBBT has the lowest priority and runs only when other propagators found nothing, by default only at the root, with LP iterations capped at ten times the root LP iterations and each direction tried at most once per node. Root LVBs go to the LVB propagator and are sorted after root processing (p. 14).

- **D5 Computational evaluation**:
  - Design: two experiments, with the questions stated before the results (how many nontrivial LVBs, effect of filtering, effect of ordering) (p. 14). Root node: a pure OBBT round without working limits, for comparability, over six strategies (p. 14). Tree: SCIP without OBBT; with root OBBT (greedy, aggressive filtering, the limits of D4); and with OBBT plus LVB propagation; gap limit 10^-4, one hour per instance (p. 15).
  - Test set by counted exclusions: 1,357 MINLPLib2 instances; 1,275 in OSiL with expressions SCIP handles; minus 36 solved or linear after presolve; minus 287 where OBBT was never called; 952 left (INT 605, GO 347) (p. 15). Root runs drop instances with an LP error in any setting (28 INT, 5 GO); tree runs keep them on purpose, to measure the implementation as SCIP would run it (p. 15). Tree comparisons drop instances no arm solved (226 INT, 160 GO) and those with a memory abort, a solution violating feasibility by more than 10^-6, or bounds inconsistent with MINLPLib2's stated values (38 INT, 21 GO), leaving 341 and 166 (p. 19).
  - Hardware and software: Xeon X5672 at 3.2 GHz, 48 GB, one job per node; SCIP 3.1.0.1 with SoPlex 2.0.1, CppAD and Ipopt 3.12.4 (p. 15). Evaluation used Hendel's Ipet tool (p. 22).
  - Metrics: shifted geometric means with shifts of 10 (LP solves), 100 (LP iterations), 10 s and 100 nodes (pp. 15–16); a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at 5% (p. 16); LVB success-rate histograms (Figs. 3–4, pp. 16–17); bound-tightening profiles, per-instance step curves of tightenings against LP iterations normalized on both axes and averaged pointwise, so that a curve that stays higher wins under any iteration limit (pp. 17–18); difficulty brackets all solved, >1 s, >10 s and >100 s by the slowest setting, after Achterberg and Wunderling (p. 19).
  - Root results: only 15.6% (INT) and 13.0% (GO) of OBBT LPs tighten a bound, but 2.9 and 2.3 times as many nontrivial LVBs as tightenings are found (p. 17). Greedy needs the fewest LP iterations and dominates the profiles (pp. 17–18). Aggressive filtering solves fewer LPs with more iterations: OBBT is 16% faster on GO and unchanged on INT (p. 17).
  - Tree results: nodes drop by 6.2% to 41.9% (p. 19). OBBT alone slows the easier INT brackets slightly and cuts time by 5.7% on INT >100 s; OBBT plus LVBs cuts 17.1% on INT >100 s and 9.8%, 14.1%, 18.8% on the GO >1, >10, >100 s brackets (p. 19; Table 2, p. 20). Of the 20 instances solved by some arms but not all, plain SCIP solves 4 and OBBT plus LVBs 17: 16 gained and 3 lost against plain (p. 19).
  - Failures reported: the OBBT slowdowns on INT are significant while its 5.7% gain is not, and footnote 3 attributes the gain to five instances; the LVB gains are significant except on the two small GO brackets (20 and 11 instances), which is put down to sample size (p. 21). Ten constraint permutations back the three highlighted single-instance stories (p. 21).
  - Card writer's checks (script recount of the appendix): recomputing shift-10 s geometric means from Table 7 reproduces Table 2's INT rows (N = 330, 142 and 69 for all solved, >10 s and >100 s; >100 s: 353.5 / 333.5 / 293.0 s, as printed; the >1 s count differs by two instances, probably from rounding at the 1 s threshold) (pp. 20, 62–68). GO's >10 s and >100 s rows are reproduced too, but Table 8 lists 167 GO instances against 166 in the text (pp. 19, 69–72). Footnote 3's factors match, to rounding, shift-10 s time ratios of plain SCIP to SCIP+OBBT in Table 7. Two of its five instances, rsyn0805h and nvs09, show plain SCIP at the 3,600 s limit (pp. 64–65), so they lie outside the all-solved group and its >100 s subset as defined on p. 19. Inside the 69-instance group, three smallinvDAX instances carry the mean (shifted factors about 91, 164 and 309, p. 66); without them SCIP+OBBT is about 20% slower (ratio 1.195 on 66 instances). Tables 7–8 give one time per instance and arm (by inference, one run): on fin2bb that run has OBBT plus LVBs slower than plain SCIP (238.1 against 230.5 s, p. 63), while the ten-permutation means in the text show it faster (132.9 against 224.3 s, p. 21). The elec200 and space960 savings on p. 17 count OBBT LP iterations without the filtering LPs' iterations (Table 3, p. 34; Table 4, p. 38); on elec200, OBBT finds no tightening and no LVB in any setting (p. 38).

- **D6 Literature positioning**: OBBT is called folklore; its first mention in global optimization is credited to Quesada and Grossmann, and the cut-off variant to Zamora and Grossmann's branch-and-contract (p. 6); the theory of iterated OBBT to Caprara and Locatelli (p. 6). Priority for the dual one-row observation and for the validity argument is conceded to Tawarmalani and Sahinidis (pp. 7, 9), with a mild critique: applied unconditionally the idea looks too expensive and unclear in use (p. 9). The terminology is corrected: optimization-based, not optimality-based, bound tightening (Remark 1, p. 6). OBBT is placed above FBBT on the linear relaxation and reduced-cost propagation by a dominance argument (p. 6). The framing carries MIP-solver vocabulary into MINLP: reduced-cost fixing from Dantzig, Fulkerson and Johnson (p. 4), probing (p. 5), strong branching (p. 11), and the one-row relaxations behind Gomory mixed-integer, MIR and Chvátal–Gomory cuts (p. 7). Own-group citations are infrastructure: Achterberg's and Vigerske's theses, a Gleixner–Vigerske 2014 talk, MIPLIB 2010 for performance variability, and Hendel's thesis and Ipet for the statistics (pp. 4, 6, 16, 21, 22). The reference list (pp. 22–25) does not cite the 2013 CPAIOR paper by Gleixner and Weltge that SKILL.md lists as [LVB13], although §2 presents LVBs as the paper's main contribution (p. 6). Whether the journal version cites it was not checked.

- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: the title is a count. The abstract names the enhancements (filtering, ordering, LVBs), gives the headline as a number for a named subset (17% to 19% on hard instances) and then the solved-instance claim (p. 3). §2 opens by naming itself the main contribution (p. 6). One theorem with a four-line proof is followed at once by a worked two-variable example and figure (pp. 7–8). Seven numbered remarks hold definitions, caveats and a negative result (Remarks 1–7, pp. 6–19). Hedges sit in the interpretations: a common belief (p. 3), folklore (p. 6), we believe (p. 21), most likely (p. 21). The closing reframes the result from a mean speed-up to solved instances (p. 21). Two different threes coexist: the abstract's three enhancements (p. 3) and §3's three cost reducers, which are variable selection, filtering and ordering (p. 10). Slips: the conclusion assigns 17.1% to the continuous set and 18.8% to the integer set, the reverse of Table 2 and of the bullets (pp. 19–21); the bracket definition reads 1, 10 and 1000 seconds for the >1, >10, >100 s groups (p. 19); Example 1 is referred to as Example 13 (p. 8).

- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the contribution is algorithmic (LVBs, filtering, ordering), software (SCIP propagator plug-ins) and a computational study over a large heterogeneous test set (pp. 14, 21). Limits are stated concretely. OBBT alone gives no significant average speed-up, because its node savings are eaten by the LP overhead (pp. 21–22). Slowdowns on easier INT instances are called an acceptable price for solving more instances (p. 21). Three instances are lost (pp. 19, 22). LVBs act mostly at upper and middle tree levels, as local relaxations drift from the root relaxation (p. 21). OBBT stops when an OBBT LP fails (p. 15). The spanning-tree ordering failed (p. 14). Filtering costs 3.1% (INT) and 4.0% (GO) of the LVBs (p. 17). Running OBBT at every node had a large average overhead in preliminary runs, reported without numbers; local OBBT is left as a far harder challenge, with the root-tested ordering and filtering offered as a starting point (p. 15). Dynamic working limits are named as ideal but not studied (p. 17).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) From every auxiliary LP whose objective is one variable, keep the optimal dual as a one-row inequality (aggregate the rows with the duals, add the cut-off multiplier times U; Theorem 1, p. 7). Store only nontrivial ones, keep them out of the LP, and re-propagate by substituting the current bounds whenever a right-hand-side bound or the incumbent improves (pp. 8–9).
- (D4) Before a batch of bound LPs, test every feasible relaxation point you already hold (root optimum, earlier OBBT optima) and skip the bounds tight at it. If many remain, spend a few LPs on aggregated directions with a success-count stop (Algorithm 1, pp. 11–12).
- (D4) Use simplex basis status to decide where a few pivots pay: basic at the bound, run a short primal solve to obtain a dual certificate; nonbasic, skip (Remark 5, p. 12).
- (D4) Order a sequence of warm-started LPs greedily by the distance from the current LP value to the target bound (Algorithm 2, p. 13), and keep a random order as the control arm any ordering must beat (p. 13).
- (D4) Order many single-row propagations by a bound-dependency digraph: shrink strongly connected components, sort topologically in linear time, propagate once per call, and leave repeat limits to the framework (pp. 9–10).
- (D4) When a routine's validity rests on dual feasibility, tighten that tolerance for its LPs only (10^-9 against SCIP's 10^-7, p. 14).
- (D5) For a component that will run under working limits, compare strategies by per-instance success-versus-effort curves, normalized on both axes and averaged, after excluding instances where the component is trivial (fewer than 1,000 LP iterations or fewer than ten tightenings), so that the ranking holds for any limit (pp. 17–18).
- (D5) Report a solver-level change with nested arms (plain, +A, +A+B), brackets set by the slowest setting, a one-sided Wilcoxon test per bracket, counts of instances gained and lost, constraint permutations for single-instance stories, and per-instance appendix tables from which the aggregates can be recomputed (pp. 19–21; Tables 3–8, pp. 26–72).
