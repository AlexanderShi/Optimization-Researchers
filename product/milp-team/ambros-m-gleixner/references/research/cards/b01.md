- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Two entries, both from the solver rather than from a literature gap. (a) A pathology inside production solvers: in state-of-the-art MINLP solvers numerical instabilities frequently arise when solving the LP relaxations, so LP accuracy is fixed first (p. 14); multiscale ME models of bacterial growth make double-precision LP solvers fail to produce accurate solutions or even to decide feasibility (p. 14). (b) The gap between MIP and MINLP solving, read partly as a historical offset (global optimization computation began in the 1990s, Dantzig-Fulkerson-Johnson dates to 1954), so MIP-style component engineering should pay off (pp. 13-14). Exact LP is entered from the cost of the state of the art: QSopt_ex slows down sharply when it must boost precision or solve hard rational systems (p. 71). OBBT is entered from a field belief that it is worth it if its cost can be controlled (p. 133); branching from the observation that integer and spatial branching have been studied apart and their interaction hardly at all (pp. 161, 177). The aim is stated with the evaluation built in: implement in state-of-the-art solvers and test on heterogeneous public benchmark sets (p. 14).
  - Transferable: start from a failure you can observe inside the production solver (unstable LP relaxations) and repair the lowest layer first; write the aim together with where the method will live and how it will be tested (p. 14).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Iterative refinement is lifted from linear systems (Wilkinson) to whole LPs: shift the problem to the current primal-dual iterate, replace right-hand side, bounds and objective by the residuals scaled by ∆P and ∆D, re-solve in floating point and add the scaled corrector (Theorem 2.5, pp. 35-37; Algorithm 2.2, pp. 39-40); geometrically this zooms in on the iterate (p. 37). What changes against classical refinement is the scaling, which is essential because LP solvers work with absolute tolerances; without it the floating-point solver would accept the zero corrector (Remark 2.8, pp. 39-40). What is kept: the floating-point LP solver as a black-box oracle, which also makes the hot start of the simplex method the natural partner (pp. 40, 59). Exactness is reached in two ways: (i) the refined basis becomes optimal after finitely many rounds, so the stalled basis is factorized in rational arithmetic and checked (Algorithm 3.2, pp. 85-86); (ii) the rational limit point is reconstructed by continued fractions from a denominator bound guessed from the residuals and then verified exactly (Algorithm 3.3, pp. 88-92). An approximate Farkas ray is turned into a rigorous infeasibility box (pp. 52-54). In the MINLP half: dual solutions of the OBBT LPs become Lagrangian variable bounds, one-row relaxations propagated by FBBT through the tree (Theorem 6.2, pp. 138-142); OBBT is made cheaper by filtering bounds with any cut-off-feasible relaxation point and by greedy ordering for simplex warm starts (pp. 144-148). Branching: a minimum cover (variables whose fixing leaves a MIP) is a vertex cover of the Hessian co-occurrence graph (Theorem 7.4, p. 167); three rules add integrality or nonlinearity information to the default scores (pp. 177-180).
  - Transferable: when you port a classical numerical scheme into an optimization solver, check whether the solver's guarantee is relative or absolute, and add the scaling that turns its absolute tolerance back into relative progress (Remark 2.8, pp. 39-40).
- **D3 Problem class and data model**: LPs with rational data, standard form with equality constraints and lower bounds for the theory, general form with ranged rows and two-sided bounds for the implementation (pp. 28, 46-49); irrational data by rational approximation (pp. 35, 185). The correctness arguments rest on numbered oracle assumptions: absolute accuracy ε < 1 and a bounded duality gap (Assumption 2.9, p. 41), outputs of the form n/B^C with |n| ⩽ C (Assumption 2.11, p. 44), approximately basic solutions (Assumption 3.7, p. 83). He concedes that Assumption 2.9 will not hold in general (p. 59) and shows a violation on the instance world (p. 63). The LP test set includes the LP relaxations of MIPLIB, MIPLIB 2, MIPLIB 3, MIPLIB 2003, MIPLIB 2010 and COR@L (p. 25). QP: linear constraints, quadratic objective, KKT refinement without convexity (p. 125). MINLP: factorable programs (p. 114); MINLPlib2 instances after SCIP presolve, split into INT (605) and GO (347) for OBBT (p. 151) and into NLINT (368) and SPLIT for branching (pp. 171, 175, 180). Covers assume twice continuously differentiable constraint functions and depend on the formulation, hence computed after presolve (pp. 166, 169, 171). Link to MILP: fixing a cover turns the MINLP into a MIP, so a bounded integer cover gives finitely many MIPs (Lemma 7.8, p. 179).
  - Transferable: state what the inner solver must deliver as numbered assumptions, then report in the computational study where and why they fail (Assumption 2.9: pp. 41, 59, 63).
- **D4 Algorithm and implementation design**: Proof devices: (a) the affine shift-and-scale correspondence, under which primal and dual violations, complementary slackness, optimality and basis status all scale by 1/∆ (Theorem 2.5, pp. 35-37); (b) induction over refinement rounds for a linear rate max{ε, 1/α} and an iteration bound (Corollary 2.10, pp. 41-42); (c) control of encoding length by rounding the scaling factors to powers of the oracle's base, so iterates keep a common denominator, giving oracle-polynomial running time (Theorem 2.12, pp. 44-45); (d) a minimum-infeasibility threshold: any nonzero primal or dual violation of a basic solution is at least 2 to the minus a polynomial in the input size, so once refined iterates are closer than that the basis is optimal (Lemma 3.4, Theorem 3.5, pp. 78-80); (e) iterates become more and more basic under Assumption 3.7 (Theorem 3.8, pp. 83-84); (f) Diophantine approximation with a residual-based denominator guess inflated by β^k until it bounds the true error (Theorem 3.1, p. 72; Lemma 3.13, pp. 90-91); (g) LP duality aggregation for the validity of LVBs, tight by complementary slackness (Theorem 6.2, p. 139); (h) covers as vertex covers, NP-hardness by reduction from vertex cover, computed exactly by a binary program that a MIP solver solves in a fraction of a second on most instances (pp. 167-168). He labels the worst-case bound as underpinning, not as the reason for the method (Remark 3.9, p. 84). Implementation in SoPlex (from version 1.7, July 2012): rational LP storage on GMP, a C++ wrapper with an object pool and shortcuts for trivial operations, EGlib slab allocation (pp. 59-60); objective coefficients on implicit slacks in column representation (p. 60); hot start reusing the factorization; nonbasic correctors snapped exactly to bounds; primal scaling capped by dual scaling; a fallback series of solver settings on floating-point failure; sparse residual updates (pp. 61-62). Exact parts: rational LU obtained by replacing the data types of SoPlex's floating-point LU, Markowitz threshold fixed at 0.01; Steffy's DLCM reconstruction adapted; defaults β = 1.1, f = 1.2, L = 2 (p. 94). SCIP: OBBT and LVB propagator plug-ins in diving mode, dual feasibility tolerance 10^-9 for OBBT LPs (default 10^-7), optional condition-number limit off by default, lowest priority, root node only, iteration limit ten times the root LP iterations (p. 149); LVB graph ordered by strongly connected components after the root (pp. 142-143, 150); LVBs harvested from degenerate basic-at-bound variables by a few primal simplex pivots (Remark 6.7, p. 147). Branching scores combine with weight 1 (default score) and 0.1 (new score) as tie-breakers (pp. 178, 180).
  - Transferable: to keep rational iterates cheap, round step sizes (here the scaling factors) to powers of the floating-point base, so every correction shares a denominator; on momentum3 rational time per round stayed between 0.3 and 0.5 s, against almost 2 s for the last rounds of an earlier version without it (pp. 44-45, 63).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Build each refinement LP from the residuals and scale by the inverse maximum violations, capped per round by an incremental factor α, so the floating-point oracle sees violations of order one (Algorithm 2.2, lines 15-16, pp. 39-40; Remark 2.8).
- Round the scaling factors to powers of the oracle's base (formula 2.14) so rational iterates keep a common denominator and rational cost grows slowly with precision (pp. 44-45, 63).
- Verify a basis once it has stalled for L refinement rounds (default L = 2) instead of waiting for a worst-case bound; check the cheap residuals before the rational factorization (Algorithm 3.2, pp. 85-86, 94).
- Output-sensitive rational reconstruction: guess the denominator bound from the residuals times β^k (β = 1.1), call it at geometric frequency f = 1.2 so cumulative cost stays linear in the rounds, accept only after an exact feasibility and optimality check (pp. 89-92, 94).
- Measure the error of a floating-point LP solution as the exact infinity-norm distance to the optimal face: fix variables and inequalities by the sign of their reduced costs and duals, add one variable for the distance, solve exactly (p. 108).
- To compare numerics rather than simplex codes, warm start every arm from the same basis and parse all inputs at a precision every arm stores exactly (pp. 108-109).
- Compare strategies that will run under unknown working limits by per-instance profiles (success versus effort), normalized on both axes by the best strategy and averaged pointwise (p. 155).
- Remove instances with LP errors only in the comparability experiment and keep them in the production experiment, saying which is which (p. 151).
- Include a random order as a control that any ordering heuristic must beat (p. 148).
- Back single-instance success stories by ten runs with permuted constraints and report the mean (p. 159).
- Test consistency with a one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on log ratios of shifted values (SciPy, correction=False, zero_method pratt, 5% level), and remember that it ignores the size of the change (pp. 23-24).
