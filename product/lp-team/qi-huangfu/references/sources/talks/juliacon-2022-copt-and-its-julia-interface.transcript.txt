Hello everyone, in this talk I will introduce COPT and its Julia interface.

My name is Huangfu Qi, I am one of COPT developer. The full name of COPT is Cardinal Optimizer. It is developed by our company, which is called Cardinal Operations.

In the past three years, since 2019, we have introduced many mathematical solvers and functionalities. To help users to use our solvers, we provided many interfaces and most importantly, the Julia interface. We also

provided remote services such as COPT computer cluster, and the solver can be used on mainstream platforms. Here is an overview of COPT performance. It is based on results from _Benchmarks for Optimization Software_,

which is maintained by Professor Hans Mittelman.

The results are taken on June 26 of 2022, and if you want to see up-to-date results, you can always visit his website. According to the public benchmark, COPT is leading on many categories and we are catching up on the important MIP benchmark.

I'd like to give an overview of COPT solvers and its functionalities. To start with, I would like to firstly introduce the simplex solver.

Simplex algorithm is called "_the algorithm that runs the world_" in a New Scientist article from 2012\. And in our experience, the simplex solver is very suitable for large and sparse LP problems and it is necessary for solving LP relaxations

in MIP solver. Besides all the basic techniques, there are some highlights in COPT that I’d like to mention. For example, we have full parallelization in the simplex solver and we also support Quad-precision which can help to deal with

very numerically hard instances. And that we have this first order method which are very useful for some type of problems. The other technique for solving LP problems is the barrier solver. It is most suitable for denser problems, degenerate

problems and numerically hard problems. In COPT, we have parallel Cholesky which gives us deterministic and thread-independent results.

We also have parallel crossover which help us to get to a basic solution faster. And during our development, we also found that there is a technique to reduce the support of solutions and we published that on arXiv already. In the

past one year, we extended our barrier solver to solve more types of problems. For example, the second order cone problem (SOCP) and convex QP and QCP and finally the semidefinite problems (SDP). And with the semidefinite solver available,

there are already many users asking for Julia support. The MIP solver is often regarded as the most important component of a mathematical solver. As you can see in the past year, we have spent a lot of effort in improving over MIP

solver and achieved a very nice speedup.

However, according to public benchmarking, our MIP solver is still in a catching up position.

But if we can reproduce the same improvement as we've done in the past year, maybe by the mid of next year, 2023, we will be the best. Besides all the mathematical solvers, COPT also provided two very important

functionalities for resolving infeasible problems.

They are called IIS and FeasRelax. The IIS can help you to compute a smaller subsystem, which is also infeasible, and that can help you to debug the model more easily.

Compared to that, FeasRelax can relax an infeasible problem to become feasible. And meanwhile, it can tell you which of the variables and the constraints that are need to be relaxed.

Now let’s come to the COPT Julia interface.

It was introduced in 2022 and we have already noticed that there are many users who adopted it. It is maintained directly by the COPT team.

You can access its source code in our GitHub repository. Here I include an example of modeling using the COPT interface. Of course, the COPT have other interfaces, for example, the C interface, some of the object oriented

interface, and some third-party connections.

The COPT Julia interface is fairly new and there are future works to do. For example, we want to enable automatic download of Windows binary, which is already work with Linux and the MacOS.

This would allow for solving small instances without even applying for a COPT license for non-commercial purpose. We also want to add SDP support. Despite that we only released the COPT SDP solver last month, there

have been many requests for its Julia support.

It is possible that it’s already done when you see this talk. Going forward, we may need your help to get COPT Julia interface better. You can start by trying out our interface, report issues and bugs. If you feel interested, you can

always contribute to the open source COPT Julia interface code. I’d also like to mention that the Julia community is growing quickly in China.

and I just started to use Julia"_. If you feel interested in trying COPT and our Julia interface, you can always do so by following the three line commands I listed on the screen. To solve larger problems, you can always apply for a license from

our website. We give one year renewable license to academic users and we offer half year free trials for commercial users.

You are welcome to visit our GitHub repository and Blogspot for latest release news. Thank you!
