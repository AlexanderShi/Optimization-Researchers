## 1. Proof devices

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Projection identifies the bottom of a family | Eliminate the auxiliary and disaggregated variables by Fourier-Motzkin to show the coarsest member equals big-M with the smallest valid M; the same projection gives non-extended forms (constraints double per step) | [S040 pp. 5–8; S046 pp. 6–7, 9–11; S013 p. 5] |
| P2 | Order a family by aggregation, containment or refinement | Show the coarser member is the finer one with two blocks summed; for bounds, show monotonicity under refinement; for SDP families, order by principal minors. For P-split the order needs additive bounds (a box or interval arithmetic); with the tightest valid bounds it can fail, as the paper's own figure shows [S046 pp. 6–8] | [S046 pp. 7–8; S040 p. 8; S013 p. 5; S020 p. 12; S021 p. 7; S088 pp. 8, 11] |
| P3 | A negative result at the boundary of your construction | Before shipping, prove where it cannot help: a class no equation-wise cut can tighten, a ceiling no member reaches (midpoint of two extreme points on a hull facet plus Jensen), the smallest example where the hierarchy fails without its hypothesis, a point showing two relaxations are not nested | [S011 p. 9; S046 pp. 12–13; S040 pp. 8–9; S078 p. 12; S120 pp. 6–7; S049 pp. 25–28; S061 pp. 12, 17–18; S135 p. 6] |
| P4 | Exact-encoding proofs | For a combinatorial MIP encoding: necessary conditions first, then a bijection by induction on a natural quantity (shortest distance); for symmetry breaking, an explicit indexing algorithm whose output satisfies the constraints (completeness) | [S128 pp. 4–6, 11–13; S093 pp. 14–16; S067 pp. 18–19; S051 pp. 4–6; S017 p. 4] |
| P5 | Limits and convexity of a singular function | Remove a removable singularity with polar coordinates and L'Hôpital along the indeterminate line, ship the C² extension instead of an ε-shift, then prove Hessian minors positive after substituting w = e^n (also in the supervised MEng report) | [S024 pp. 9–14; S100 pp. 44–61] |
| P6 | Count before you build | Count simplices, leaf combinations, configurations removed per dependency, permutations or matrix entries before choosing a formulation; state the crossover where one encoding becomes smaller | [S012 p. 4; S020 p. 8; S005 p. 3; S076 p. 6; S120 p. 11; S040 p. 5] |
| P7 | Recognition tests from cited theorems | Turn a known theorem into a cheap test: a necessary and a cheap sufficient dominance test for edge-concave facets, a shift that makes a remainder edge-concave, interval-Hessian classes for term types | [S011 pp. 6–7; S025 pp. 23–24; S032 pp. 11–12; S001 p. 5] |
| P8 | Parametrise the complicating variable and track breakpoints | Keep the bilinear model's complicating variable as a parameter and characterise the optimal LP basis as a function of it; the breakpoints give the algorithm and the complexity map | [S049 pp. 7–12] |
| P9 | Approximation-ratio template | A lower bound, a ratio, tightness by a worst-case instance, a reduction for hardness; re-analyse a practitioner's published heuristic before replacing it | [S061 pp. 9, 12; S069 pp. 3–4; S135 pp. 4–5, 8] |
| P10 | Exchange arguments | Show an optimal or efficient solution can be modified pairwise without loss, then explain or certify by the absence of improving exchanges | [S017 p. 3; S055 p. 17; S135 p. 8; S058 pp. 7–8] |
| P11 | Duality-based robust counterparts | Reformulate the worst case over an uncertainty set by duality; rewrite to avoid inverse covariance matrices | [S048 pp. 6, 11; S055 pp. 7–8] |
| P12 | Keep only the direction that matters | Relax an equality the optimiser can only push one way (a variance) to a second-order-cone inequality | [S047 p. 6; S081 p. 28] |
| P13 | Linearise by powers-of-two weights or one-hot count indicators | Lexicographic constraints with powers-of-two coefficients; one-hot indicators over the range of an integer count to make a quadratic in it linear | [S051 pp. 8–9; S093 pp. 16–17; S067 p. 7; S128 pp. 10–15] |
| P14 | Degenerate-case check | Check that a new bound collapses to the known one, or a new notion contains the classical one, in the degenerate case | [S115 p. 5; S117 pp. 85–86] (S117: a supervised MEng thesis) |
| P15 | Replace an NP-hard certificate by checkable necessary conditions | When optimality cannot be explained in polynomial time, certify and explain feasibility and efficiency instead, and say why | [S017 pp. 3, 5] |
| P16 | Justify a restrictive assumption by hardness of its relaxations | Show that every relaxation of the assumption is NP-hard, so the tractable subclass is the frontier, not a convenience | [S049 pp. 22, 27–28, 31] |
| P17 | Reduce an in-tree bound to a root bound | Prove a node-level bound by applying the root result to a modified instance (reduced budget) | [S050 p. 13] |

## 2. Design moves

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Interpolating family by grouping | Group variables or terms into N blocks and take the hull over the blocks: P splits of a separable constraint, N partitions of a ReLU node's inputs, tree-ensemble partitions, an n-dimensional SDP family. For ReLU nodes, balance the blocks by weight: equal size (sort the weights, split evenly) or equal range (thresholds at the 5% and 95% quantiles, N ≥ 3), with random and snake-draft uneven partitions as controls; ordered indices suffice when the variables play symmetric roles | [S040 pp. 3–4; S013 pp. 3, 6–8; S020 pp. 11–13; S021 pp. 6–8; S046 p. 22] |
| D2 | Division of labour between complementary relaxations | Assign each term to one relaxation family, e.g. remove edges covered by edge-concave triplets from the graph that chooses variables to partition; keep a relaxation menu per term type | [S011 p. 13; S025 pp. 20–24] |
| D3 | Cheap gate before expensive strength | Give an expensive component a cheap gate: a necessary or cheap sufficient test, a coarse relaxation for OBBT and a fine one for nodes, the cheap convex part for strong branching, cuts learned in the top tree levels then frozen; report the gate's cost (Heuristic 11) | [S011 pp. 6–7; S025 p. 24; S004 p. 14; S020 pp. 10, 15–16; S087 p. 4] |
| D4 | One interface, many switchable parts | A polymorphic term class, plug-in detectors, class-specific plug-ins in a class-agnostic solver, switchable formulations behind one model block | [S001 pp. 4, 8; S068 pp. 9–10; S072 pp. 2, 6, 15–16; S006 p. 4; S087 pp. 3, 11] |
| D5 | Keep the optimiser near the data | Pick the device for the model class: a PCA-subspace penalty (fails on clustered data), a distance-to-data term with k-means centres, data-set bounds on GNN inputs, a learned failure boundary | [S020 pp. 17, 26; S023 pp. 10–12; S051 pp. 8, 25; S071 pp. 3–4] |
| D6 | Model only the unknown part | Keep known analytical objectives, kinetics or conservation laws exact and learn only the residual or the black-box part | [S095 pp. 3–4; S075 p. 4; S059 p. 9; S034 p. 5; S015 pp. 1–2] |
| D7 | Symmetry handling that keeps a representative | Lexicographic constraints on abstract nodes; auxiliaries generated for whole orbits so the relaxation keeps the symmetry; indicator constraints for inactive layers; an existential requirement moved to node 0 | [S051 pp. 3–4; S120 pp. 8–9; S047 p. 19; S038 pp. 18–20] |
| D8 | Separate the selection from the sequencing | Choose the batch statistically, then order it combinatorially (TSP over move costs); choose inputs and fidelities separately | [S039 p. 7; S031 pp. 10, 12–13] |
| D9 | Ship rival approaches behind one model object | Let users switch robust reformulation and cutting planes, or rival surrogate formulations, without rewriting the model | [S057 pp. 2, 6, 13; S006 p. 4] |
| D10 | An intermediate representation between file formats and formulations | Read ML models into one internal representation so each formulation is written once | [S006 p. 3] |
| D11 | Learn offline, decide online | Move an expensive per-node selection (which SDP cut, over which variables) into an estimator trained before deployment; fall back to the exact rule when it does worse than random | [S021 pp. 14–15, 24–25; S119 pp. 6–7] |
| D12 | Coarse to fine | Check whether the coarse level's solution, projected, already solves the problem before solving the next level; warm-start the next level from it | [S088 pp. 10–12, 19–20] |
| D13 | Keep a restriction a restriction | If a heuristic cannot handle a constraint, fail loudly instead of silently dropping it and returning points of a different model | [S072 p. 16] |
| D14 | Closed-form bounds cheap enough for every node | Derive a closed form for budgeted bounds (take the k most negative changes) so tightening can run at every B&B node; add branching-dependent bounds as local cuts | [S050 pp. 5, 7, 13] |

## 3. Experiment and evaluation protocols

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Re-check every returned answer | By script with a stated violation threshold, relabelling infeasible returns as failures; by hand; against the simulator; by sampling; by enumeration; by consensus across solvers | [S004 p. 19; S001 pp. 10–11; S032 p. 18; S020 p. 39; S071 pp. 4–5; S055 p. 20; S056 pp. 7, 15; S050 pp. 9, 13] |
| E2 | Strengthen the rival | Give the rival your components, time-counted and as an oracle; its own best solver flag; the best of several settings or penalty weights; a wall-clock-matched grid; withhold from your own method what the rival cannot get | [S072 pp. 17–18; S040 p. 11; S046 pp. 17, 21–22; S078 p. 11; S047 p. 8; S081 p. 17; S013 p. 8] |
| E3 | Matched controls that differ in one component | Swap only the component under test (the optimiser for random sampling, a refined model for the plain one, a random selector for the learned one) and keep seeds and initial designs shared | [S023 p. 22; S047 p. 8; S077 pp. 3, 9; S115 pp. 7, 22; S126 p. 18; S097 p. 7; S035 p. 24; S021 p. 25; S020 p. 20] |
| E4 | Ablation inside the whole algorithm | Cumulative (component added one at a time) per class with time and gap profiles and nodes; every component alone, in pairs and all together; every new knob in an appendix, keeping the simplest equal variant | [S011 pp. 17–19; S087 p. 16; S005 p. 7; S020 pp. 20–26; S059 pp. 28–30] |
| E5 | Calibrate difficulty | Build an instance ladder from one superstructure; generate at a phase transition; filter by portfolio thresholds; set perturbations or budgets so the baseline solves some instances | [S152 p. 99; S052 pp. 19, 44–45; S010 pp. 15–16; S113 p. 15; S013 p. 7; S046 p. 19] |
| E6 | Report what locates the result | Gap at the limit with a lower/upper-bound breakdown; time to match; incumbent trajectories; regret against iterations and against cost; win counts with a 10% tie rule; slices by structural class | [S072 p. 21; S078 pp. 11, 15; S061 p. 34; S039 p. 8; S113 p. 19; S009 pp. 28–29; S001 pp. 17–18] |
| E7 | Count solutions to measure symmetry removal | Use the solver's solution pool (or OEIS counts) to count feasible solutions before and after symmetry breaking | [S051 p. 9; S128 p. 16; S076 pp. 4–5] |
| E8 | Audit the inherited model or instance | Diff a predecessor's model against its primary source; correct a published model against its GAMS file with a change log; check whether a classic benchmark lies in an easy subclass; re-solve the classic instance before extending it | [S025 p. 3; S100 pp. 116–118; S049 p. 28; S080 p. 13; S024 p. 24; S152 p. 98] (S100: a supervised MEng report) |
| E9 | Harmonise settings and stopping rules before timing | Fix solver parameters across formulations, since automatic parameter selection can produce variation that has nothing to do with the formulations (e.g. Gurobi MIPFocus = 3, Cuts = 1, MIQCPMethod = 1 for every formulation, with PreMIQCPForm = 2 given to the hull only and said so); one feasibility tolerance; one stopping rule for warm and cold starts | [S040 p. 11; S046 pp. 21–22; S013 p. 16; S004 p. 19; S088 pp. 24–26; S052 pp. 20–21] |
| E10 | Count set-up and modelling-layer time separately | Report timings with and without your own construction or transformation code, in a column of their own | [S078 pp. 13–14; S057 p. 16; S071 p. 13] |
| E11 | Offline benchmarks when the experiment cannot be run | Build the benchmark from GP posterior samples fitted to the partner's data, or run retrospective BO over a measured candidate set, and say so | [S031 pp. 17–18; S082 p. 11; S081 p. 8; S034 pp. 7, 10] |
| E12 | Remove convenience bounds from library instances | Strip bounds others added to instances so bound inference stays part of the test | [S004 p. 22] |
| E13 | Freeze reproducibility metadata | Record library access date, tool Git tag and date, container and versions; publish the test suite as its own report with a freeze date and an exclusion rule | [S068 p. 10; S123 pp. 1–2; S001 pp. 8–10] |
| E14 | Remaining gap when time stops being informative | When more than half of a class times out, report the average remaining gap in the cell (in place of the node count, with the time shown as over the limit); keep unsolved large instances with their gap | [S046 p. 24; S152 p. 99] |
| E15 | Sweep the call frequency of an expensive component | Report the cost curve of an expensive callback over its frequency, and switch it off where it does not pay | [S005 pp. 6, 8] |
| E16 | Validate where the truth is computable first | Test an estimator or uncertainty method on cases with a closed-form answer or a synthetic generator, then on real data | [S066 pp. 7–11; S053 pp. 9–11; S055 pp. 20–23] |

## 4. Writing moves

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| WM1 | Losses and scope in the abstract and the conclusion | Write where the method loses and which class carries the result in the abstract, the results and the conclusion | [S155 pp. 1, 6; S072 pp. 1, 21, 23; S001 p. 17; S021 p. 27; S046 p. 26; S115 p. 8; S005 p. 7; S029 pp. 4–5] |
| WM2 | Reviews as maps | One framework figure with a table per algorithmic component and a solver × feature table; a taxonomy that states its excluded cells; numbered open questions at the end | [S003 pp. 12, 26, 45–55; S014 pp. 2, 7–18; S028 pp. 14–17; S145 p. 3; S069 pp. 22–23] |
| WM3 | The contribution claimed as integration | Say plainly when the contribution is how existing pieces are tied together | [S001 p. 2; S032 p. 2] |
| WM4 | A worked toy before each device | Show the device on a two- or three-variable example (or one tiny classic instance across all solvers) before the general statement; the toy explains, the benchmark proves | [S020 pp. 4, 12, 14, 17; S040 pp. 8–9; S013 pp. 6–7; S011 p. 8; S145 p. 2; S032 p. 8] |
| WM5 | Say what is not claimed | Name the claim a reader might infer and disclaim it | [S088 p. 26; S023 p. 28; S045 p. 31; S090 p. 4; S073 p. 2; S041 pp. 33–34] |
| WM6 | Disclose roles | Disclose own-solver authorship when it produced the result; add contribution statements | [H002 p. 1; S091 p. 10; S071 p. 13; S082 p. 35] |
| WM7 | State which parts repeat an earlier paper | In an extension, say which sections and tables repeat the conference version and which are new | [S041 p. 3] |
| WM8 | The lost guarantee next to the gain | When a speed-up costs a guarantee, write the lost guarantee in the same paragraph | [S029 p. 6] |
| WM9 | The do-not-use regime next to the win | State the regime where the method pays off (evaluation cost, problem size) and the fallback beyond it | [S145 p. 2; S081 p. 9; S095 p. 5; S003 p. 9] |
| WM10 | An appendix that answers the reviewers | Structure the appendix as titled answers to likely reviewer questions | [S067 pp. 20–23] |

General lesson from the reading notes (not about any one work): in long computational papers, re-check after each revision that summary counts, captions, tables and text agree.
