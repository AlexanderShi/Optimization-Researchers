### S145 · Deterministic Global optimisation at CPSE: Models, Algorithms, and Software (CPSE Newsletter, Issue 10, 2014, no DOI)

**Read level**: full (all 6 pages read, including the bibliography pp. 4-6 and Appendix A, p. 6).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (single author, Department of Chemical Engineering, Imperial College London, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: written the year GloMIQO 2.0 and ANTIGONE appeared (both cited as 2013/2014, p. 5), at the start of her Imperial career; an outreach/positioning piece, not a research paper.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Opens with a 1979 Grossmann-Sargent quotation that MINLP has no general efficient method, then answers it: global MINLP is NP-hard, "so a general, efficient solution method will probably never exist", yet the domain is broad (process networks, computational chemistry, finance), so research has three focal points: models of industrially relevant applications, algorithms exploiting special structure, solver software (p. 1). The four stated purposes (exciting domain; industrially relevant applications; complementarity between CPSE groups; reading list) are listed in the abstract (p. 1). A two-variable toy MINLP (Example (1), Figure 1) shows why a local method started at the "red star" cannot reach the global solution x1 = 2.37, x2 = 3 (p. 2).
  - Transferable: open an expository piece with a historical "no general method exists" quote from the field's founders and answer it with the complexity fact plus a reframing of what research can still do (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: The argument is the triad models / algorithms / software, with applications as the lens for finding structure: "researchers may be able to find and exploit special mathematical structure for particular problem classes", illustrated by Liberti-Pantelides' redundant constraints for process networks and her own automation of "an algorithmic variant in the software GloMIQO" (p. 3). Branch and bound is decomposed into four components (rigorous bounds, heuristic feasible solutions, domain branching, bound reduction), and CPSE's contributions are mapped onto each component (p. 3); bespoke class-specific methods (polynomial optimisation, feedstock blending, product scheduling) are a separate category (p. 3). Stochastic/non-deterministic CPSE work is positioned as supplying good incumbents that "expedite the branch and bound process" (p. 3) and as the tool of choice beyond the reach of deterministic methods (p. 2).
  - Transferable: map a survey of a group's work onto the four B&B components (bounding, heuristics, branching, bound tightening) so each contribution has a slot and the complementarity becomes visible (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Appendix A defines MINLP with two-sided constraint bounds and variable bounds and states three assumptions: finite bounds can be inferred on variables in nonlinear terms, the image of each f_i is finite, the LP relaxation is bounded (p. 6). Typical f_i (A.1) are linear + quadratic + signomial + exponential + logarithmic terms, i.e. the ANTIGONE term classes; special cases NLP, MIQCQP, mixed-integer signomial, MILP are named; bilevel, dynamic and multi-parametric uses are explicitly excluded from the article (p. 6). Note: Example (1) is written as a maximisation (p. 2), while Appendix A uses min form (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: n/a (no proofs; expository).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a (no experiments). The only computational claims are cited: ANTIGONE addresses benchmarks "up to O(10^4) variables and equations", and Khor et al. (2014) solved bilinear water-network synthesis problems (4657 continuous, 42 discrete variables, 5848 constraints, 2704 bilinear terms) to global optimality with ANTIGONE; "no other off-the-shelf optimisation software approached this efficacy" (p. 2). The comparison behind that last sentence is not given in the article.
- **D6 Positioning**: Places GloMIQO and ANTIGONE as descendants of the two early CPSE code bases, alphaBB (Adjiman et al. 1998) and Smith-Pantelides factorable programming (1997, 1999): her solvers "not only hybridise" them "but also incorporate a range of other cutting-edge algorithms" (p. 3). Generous citation of departmental colleagues across domains (multi-parametric, dynamic, bilevel global optimisation, guaranteed parameter estimation, p. 2; MC++ from Chachuat, p. 4). Frames the work through Grossmann-Sargent (CMU/Imperial) rather than through Floudas, although her own items are all with Floudas (p. 5). Availability through GAMS and Princeton, "used worldwide by optimisation practitioners and researchers" (pp. 3-4).
  - Transferable: when joining a new department, position your own software as a hybrid descendant of the department's earlier codes; it states lineage and invites collaboration in one sentence (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Short declarative lists separated by semicolons ("Deterministic global optimisation is an active research area integrating: engineering applications; mathematical algorithms; computational software", p. 1); a numbered purpose list in the abstract (p. 1); one figure-backed toy example before any definition, formal definitions pushed to an appendix (pp. 2, 6). Hedged scope claims: "will probably never exist", "may be most effective", "may be useful" (pp. 1-3). The cost-benefit sentence on when global optimisation is worth it is stated explicitly (p. 2).
  - Transferable: in an expository piece, put the intuitive picture (a 2-variable plot with a local trap) first and push the formal problem class to an appendix (pp. 2, 6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution is expository and positional: an introduction plus a cross-section bibliography of models, algorithms, software (p. 4). Limits are stated as scope, not as open problems: global MINLP is "computationally expensive" and suited to domains "where there is high reward for fractional improvements and sufficient time to explore the search space"; for very large problems "heuristics and stochastic methods may be most effective" (p. 2). Bilevel, dynamic and multi-parametric settings are left out (p. 6). No future directions named beyond "emerging opportunities" (biological/biomedical engineering, computational chemistry, project scheduling, p. 2).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information"): evidence ✅. Applications are the lens because they reveal "special mathematical structure for particular problem classes"; the example is redundant constraints for process networks automated in GloMIQO (p. 3). This is the earliest written-down form in this corpus of the redundant-equation story told later in T8.
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole solver): weak evidence ✅. B&B is presented as four interacting components and contributions are placed by component (p. 3); stochastic heuristics are valued for what they do for the whole B&B (p. 3). No ablation in this piece.
- Heuristic 7 (write the do-not-use conditions next to the method): evidence ✅. Global MINLP is recommended only with "high reward for fractional improvements and sufficient time", heuristics beyond the deterministic limit (p. 2).
- Research Taste mark 5 (ends in software others can extend): evidence ✅. Software is one of the three pillars, with availability channels named (GAMS, Princeton, COIN-OR MC++; pp. 3-4).
- new-pattern candidate: "models / algorithms / software triad" as a standing frame for describing a research area (title, abstract, p. 1, p. 4).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Decompose branch and bound into four components (bounds, heuristics, branching, bound reduction) and place each contribution of a survey into one slot (p. 3).
- State the economic condition under which an expensive exact method is worth it (high value of fractional improvement, time available) and name the fallback (heuristics) beyond the size limit (p. 2).
- Use a two-variable mixed-integer toy with a plotted local trap to motivate global methods before any formal definition (p. 2).

**Quotes**:

- "it is highly relevant to application domains where there is high reward for fractional improvements and sufficient time to explore the search space" (p. 2)
