# Batch c17 · Ruth Misener · core

Batch intent: see what Misener's 2026 machine-learning-side co-authorships (Bayesian optimisation with a BASF partner; variational inference for GP latent variable models) show about her group's research habits outside deterministic global optimisation: how a problem is entered from a broken modelling assumption, how the new method is tested against baselines (budgets, repetitions, statistics, stronger alternatives), and how limits and costs are written. Both works are multi-author papers where Misener is a middle author (7th of 9; 4th of 6), so they are read as evidence of the group's practice, not of her own voice.
Focus dimensions: D1-D8 (D4 is read as "derivation devices" since neither paper proves a theorem; D3 includes the modelling and data-generation assumptions).
Material roles: S113 core (arXiv preprint, BO for fed-batch processes, corresponding author C. Tsay, BASF co-authors); S126 core (arXiv preprint v2, structured SVI for GPLVMs, ML venue style with appendices A-I).

### S113 · Meta-learning for sample-efficient Bayesian optimisation of fed-batch processes (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2605.05382)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-21 read in full, including Algorithm 1 p. 12, Tables 1-4 pp. 13-15 and all figure captions; references pp. 21-24 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Becky Langdon, Gabriel D. Patrón, Chrysoula D. Kappatou (BASF), Robert M. Lee (BASF), Behrang Shafei (BASF), Jixiang Qing, Mark van der Wilk, Calvin Tsay (corresponding author) (p. 1); Misener is 7th of 9 authors · **Position in trajectory**: a journal-length application follow-up to the group's SANODEP proceedings paper (Qing et al., TMLR 2025, cited p. 2 and p. 8), in the BASF-funded data-driven optimisation line (BASF/RAEng Research Chair acknowledged, p. 21).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entered from an industrial setting where the standard tool's assumptions break: fed-batch recipes face "inherent, underlying, and unmeasurable fluctuations across batches" (abstract, p. 1), and GP surrogates "are static, forecast poorly, and lack generalisation across experiments" (p. 1). Standard BayesOpt "treats each new optimisation task as a new black-box function", so information "is effectively not retained from batch to batch" (p. 2). Each batch run with fixed unknown kinetic parameters is defined as a task (p. 1), and the decision problem is optimal initial conditions plus stopping time, eqs. (2)-(3) (p. 3). §2.1.1 argues formally why a GP cannot meta-learn: it "cannot represent two different valid outputs for the same (x0,t) pair" (p. 5), and its extrapolations "deteriorate quickly" (p. 5).
  - Transferable: before proposing a new surrogate, write a one-paragraph structural argument for why the incumbent model class cannot represent the phenomenon (here, one output per input vs several tasks per input), not just that it performs worse (p. 5).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Replace the GP in BayesOpt with SANODEP, a neural ODE process meta-trained offline on simulated trajectories drawn from a prior over kinetic parameters (pp. 8-11). Kept from NODEP: the latent split into initial-condition and control latents and a latent ODE, eq. (15) (p. 9). Changed: inputs include the initial conditions x0 as well as time (p. 9), and a "bi-scenario" episodic loss whose Bernoulli indicator switches between forecasting (a new trajectory with only x0 known) and interpolation (more samples on an existing trajectory), eqs. (18)-(21) and Algorithm 1 (pp. 10, 12). Prior knowledge enters as candidate models: "the task of stating the prior distribution becomes simply expressing candidate models and parametrisations" (p. 10). The acquisition is non-myopic: it jointly picks x0 and a measurement schedule, re-optimised after each in-batch measurement with a minimum delay Δt; it builds on batch hypervolume improvement, with details deferred to Qing et al. (p. 11).
  - Transferable: encode domain knowledge as a simulator-generated meta-training prior (sample parameters, solve the ODE, train episodically) rather than as a hand-designed kernel; the prior then becomes "which candidate models and parameter ranges", a question an industrial partner can answer (p. 10).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Dynamics are an ODE with task parameters k (eq. 1, p. 3); the true system is treated as a black box during optimisation (p. 3). Case study: Bajpai-Reuss penicillin model, eqs. (22)-(28), with Contois/Monod kinetics and fixed parameters from Cuthrell and Biegler (Table 1, p. 13); six stochastic parameters vary uniformly around nominal values (Table 3, p. 15); profit objective eq. (29) (p. 13). The meta-training data come from the true ODE with a ±5% parameter window, which the authors call "a strong assumption" (p. 15). Structural model mismatch is excluded ("Future work may study the effect of structural mismatches", p. 15; "structural mismatches are not considered here", p. 18). Cost model: the number of batches dominates and in-batch measurements are comparatively cheap (p. 17). As printed, the training prior is written U[(1−∆k)knom,(1−∆k)knom] with both bounds identical (pp. 14, 15), whereas the test prior uses (1−∆δ) and (1+∆δ) (eq. 30, p. 15): apparently a typo; not resolved in the text.
  - Transferable: say which mismatch you exclude (parametric vs structural) and test only the one you can generate, labelling the other as future work (pp. 15, 18).
- **D4 Analysis / derivation devices**: No theorems. Devices are derivational: the NP ELBO with KL correction, eq. (10) (p. 6); the SANODEP ELBO with two KL terms, one for the latent control signal and one for the latent initial state (p. 11); the factorised predictive distribution, eq. (17) (p. 9). The convergence argument is cited, not proved: GP-based BO "benefits from consistency guarantees" (p. 21). The EI formula, eq. (12), is printed with "gend" where the text defines gmax (p. 8), a notational slip.
  - n/a for proofs.
- **D5 Experiments**: Penicillin simulator only (pp. 11-15). Baselines: GP-Standard (zero-mean GP, RBF kernel, EI; explicitly a "problem-agnostic baseline", p. 14), GP-Exp, which gets extra intermediate trajectory samples "at no added cost" (p. 14), and random search (p. 17). Budgets differ: 10 black-box trajectories for SANODEP vs 20 for GP methods and random search, justified by SANODEP's higher per-step compute (p. 17). GP methods start from an LHS initial design; SANODEP starts with no task data (p. 16). Objective normalised per task by the best value from "a single run of GP-BO on the task with a large sample budget of 50" (p. 17). Five random repetitions; five on-task test tasks (p. 17). Distribution-shift protocol: test priors offset by δ ∈ {0, ±0.04, ±0.06, ±0.5} with window 0.01 (Table 4, p. 15), plus trajectory-MSE checks (Fig. 5, p. 16). Two cost metrics are reported, number of trajectories and total runtime (Fig. 10, p. 19). Results: SANODEP reaches in about 10 runs what the GPs reach in 20 (p. 17). Off-task, "it still significantly outperforms the GP-Based methods in all regimes" (p. 20), with no statistical test reported. At the full budget, GPs perform "on par" (Fig. 8 caption, p. 18). The Fig. 10 caption says SANODEP "underperforms in this setting" once the GP initial-data cost is counted (p. 19), which sits uneasily with the conclusion's "across all regimes, it outperforms GPs in the low-data scenario" (p. 21). One counter-intuitive finding is reported: performance is worse for central tasks than for edge tasks (p. 19).
  - Transferable: an "on / almost-off / slightly-off / very-off" grid of test distributions defined relative to the edges of the training prior (Table 4, p. 15) is a cheap, reusable stress test for any simulator-pretrained surrogate.
  - Transferable: plot the same runs against two cost axes (experiments run vs total runtime) and say which one the application pays for (p. 17, Fig. 10 p. 19).
- **D6 Positioning**: Placed in process systems engineering (fed-batch optimisation since Cuthrell and Biegler 1989, p. 11) and the meta-learning/NP line (Garnelo, Norcliffe NODEP, CNP/ANP/TNP, pp. 2, 7). Earlier models are judged by structure: NPs "are domain-agnostic and do not incorporate any of the structure physically inherent to the problem" (p. 2). The paper extends the group's own SANODEP (Qing et al. 2025) and is open about relying on it for acquisition and training details (pp. 8, 11). GP-BO is conceded long-run reliability (p. 21).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Long tutorial-style background (§2, pp. 3-8) for a PSE readership before the method. The contribution sentence is hedged at first appearance: "highlights the need for thoughtful design of the surrogate model and BayesOpt strategy, as well as the potential for meta-learning" (p. 3). Results prose is less hedged ("significantly outperforms ... in all regimes", p. 20) than the evidence (5 repetitions, no test). Ends with a usage recommendation, not a claim of dominance: start with SANODEP, then switch to GPs (p. 21). Typos survive ("sitll", "a a", p. 11).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Application/methodological contribution (a meta-learning BO framework for fed-batch recipes) on one simulated case study, without real plant data and without released code in the text (searched: no repository link). Stated limits: strong prior assumption (p. 15); no structural mismatch (pp. 15, 18); higher compute per step (p. 17); training cost grows with the complexity of the training distribution (p. 18); weaker off-task, with predictive accuracy dropping outside the training regime (p. 21). Future work: structural mismatch (p. 15) and experimental design (p. 21).

**Method links**:
- Heuristic 1 (choose by broken assumption) ✅ evidence: GP-BO's assumption that each task is a fresh black box collides with unmeasurable batch-to-batch fluctuations and costly experiments (pp. 1-2, 5).
- Heuristic 2 (industrial instance first, public stand-in) ⚠ variant: BASF co-authors and funding (pp. 1, 21), but the instance is a literature model (Bajpai-Reuss/Cuthrell-Biegler, p. 13), not partner data; no stand-in release is mentioned.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor) ✗ contradiction: problem-agnostic zero-mean GP as main baseline (p. 14), unequal budgets 10 vs 20 (p. 17), normalisation from a single GP run (p. 17), 5 repetitions with "significantly outperforms" and no test (pp. 17, 20). It partly keeps the method: a strengthened baseline (GP-Exp, p. 14), a second cost metric (Fig. 10, p. 19), and the loss written into the paper (Fig. 8 caption p. 18; conclusions p. 21).
- Heuristic 7 (write the do-not-use conditions) ✅ evidence: "SANODEP could be used as an initial strategy ... before switching to GPs in the high-data regime" (p. 21), and its per-step compute cost is stated (p. 17).
- Method 3 (the partner's model as the problem) ⚠ variant: here the partner's mechanistic knowledge enters as a simulated meta-training prior over candidate ODE models (p. 10), not as constraints in an optimisation model.
- new-pattern candidate: simulator-pretrained surrogate tested on a graded off-distribution ladder (Table 4, p. 15; pp. 18-20).

**Transferable techniques**:
- State the prior as "candidate models and parameter ranges", generate meta-training data by simulation, and train episodically with a mode indicator that matches the downstream use (forecast vs interpolate) (pp. 10, 12).
- Grade test distributions as on / almost-off / slightly-off / very-off relative to the training prior's edges, upper and lower separately (Table 4, p. 15; Fig. 12, p. 20).
- Report the same campaign against both cost axes the application might pay (experiments vs runtime), and count the baseline's initial-design cost (Fig. 10, p. 19).

**Quotes**:
- "While this is a strong assumption, we study the effect of the meta-learning dataset quality by testing on tasks outside of k, i.e., tasks the base model has never seen." (p. 15)
- "SANODEP excels in scarce-data regimes, while GP-based methods catch up as data accumulate." (p. 21)

### S126 · Amortized Structured Stochastic Variational Inference for Gaussian Process Latent Variable Models (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2610.03647)

**Read level**: full (main text pp. 1-10 and appendices A-H pp. 14-18 read in full; references pp. 11-13 skimmed; appendix I figure pages pp. 19-25 read from their captions, since the plots carry no text beyond axis labels).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Maksym Tretiakov, Sarah Filippi, Vincent Fortuin, Ruby Sedgwick, James Odgers (p. 1); Misener is 4th of 6 authors (Imperial College London) · **Position in trajectory**: continues the group's GPLVM line (Odgers, Sedgwick, Kappatou, Misener, Filippi, AISTATS 2025, cited p. 1 and p. 12) and points back to Sedgwick et al. 2025 transfer-learning BO (p. 10); method work in ML, not optimisation.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Enters from an assumption that, the authors say, all prior GPLVM work shares: "all approaches to the GPLVM share a shortcoming: a Mean Field (MF) approximation in the variational family" (p. 1). The consequence: the model underestimates the epistemic uncertainty of the GP mapping (p. 1), because every likely latent value is combined with every likely inducing value, and VI is mode-seeking (p. 3). The mathematical diagnosis: by eq. (6) the mean of f_p depends on both u_p and K_XZ, so after conditioning on Y the posterior over U and X "becomes conditionally dependent" (p. 4).
  - Transferable: name the independence assumption every predecessor makes, then show from the model's own conditional formulas (here eq. 6) that the exact posterior violates it (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Apply Hoffman-Blei structured SVI: mirror the posterior's conditional dependence in the variational family, q(x_n | U) instead of q(x_n), eq. (9) (p. 4). Kept: sparse inducing-point GP, Gaussian q(u_p) with constant parameters, amortisation by an encoder (p. 4). Changed: the encoder takes the sampled inducing state as an input, h(y_n, U) (p. 4). The ELBO splits into a global KL plus a sum of "local ELBOs", eqs. (10)-(11), estimated with K Monte Carlo samples of U, eq. (12) (p. 4). The direction q(X|U)q(U), not q(U|X)q(X), is chosen for computational reasons: it keeps the local/global split that SVI mini-batching needs, eq. (13) (p. 5). Implementation choices (App. C, pp. 15-16): separate MLPs for mean and log-variance; the encoder gets the reparameterisation noise Ξ_k instead of raw U_k because late in training the U samples "lie very close to one another" (p. 15); softplus variances; a β-annealed KL used only on MNIST/FMNIST, which "does not change the objective at the end of training" (p. 16).
  - Transferable: when one factor of a model is global and the other local, condition the local variational factor on samples of the global one; this keeps minibatching and adds only K-fold sampling (pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: feed a network the standardised noise behind a reparameterised sample rather than the sample itself, when samples concentrate late in training (p. 15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Gaussian observation noise, standard normal prior on latents, independent zero-mean GP per output dimension with an ARD kernel, shared inducing inputs Z across outputs (pp. 2-3). The variational family is still restricted: diagonal Gaussian q(x_n|U) and q(U) factorised over outputs, admitted as a limitation (p. 10). Closed-form ψ-statistics for the ARD kernel, eqs. (22)-(27) (pp. 15-16). Data are mean-centred (p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis / derivation devices**: No theorems. Derivation devices: the zero KL contribution of F because the variational and prior conditionals are equal (p. 3, p. 4); a per-output split of the local ELBO, eqs. (19)-(21) (p. 14); ψ-statistics (eq. 22, p. 15) give closed-form expectations, eqs. (23)-(27) (pp. 15-16); the Monte Carlo average over U samples, eq. (15) (p. 14); the reparameterisation u_pk = µ_p + L_p ξ_pk, eq. (28) (p. 15). Hoffman and Blei's implicit definition h = argmax of the local ELBO is noted and replaced by amortisation to avoid an inner optimisation per sample (p. 15).
  - Transferable: reuse the predecessor's closed-form expectations (ψ-statistics) by conditioning on one sample of the new dependency at a time, so the new family adds no new integrals (pp. 14-16).
- **D5 Experiments**: Four datasets: Oilflow, qPCR, and subsampled MNIST and FMNIST (1500 per class) (p. 8). Baselines: MF-GPLVM (small datasets only), A-MF-GPLVM, and an amortised importance-weighted IWVI-GPLVM, added as the rival way of tightening the bound (pp. 6-8). Same initialisation and hyperparameters for all models; 5 seeds (App. D, p. 16). Metrics: negative ELBO, RMSE, NLPD, CRPS, and 95% coverage on train and test splits (pp. 8-9). Tables 1-2 report mean ± sd (pp. 5, 7); two-sided Welch's t-tests A-MF vs A-S (Table 3, p. 17). Losses reported: IWVI has lower RMSE on Oilflow (p. 8) and slightly lower CRPS there (p. 9), coverage "do not show uniform dominance" (p. 9), the A-S RMSE gain on Oilflow is not significant (p = 0.12, 0.55; p. 17), and reconstructions are visually similar (p. 9). Overhead measured: wall-clock 5.03 → 8.36 s per 100 iterations and peak GPU memory 29.0 → 598.7 MB (Table 4, p. 17). Ablations on encoder width, number of inducing points and K (App. H, p. 18): encoder size has no strong effect, so the gain is not capacity; the margin shrinks with more inducing points, which they "suspect" comes from the encoder input growing (p. 18). Gap: the main-text and conclusion claims of significance against IWVI (pp. 8-10) have no p-value table in the text read; Table 3 covers only A-MF vs A-S (p. 17).
  - Transferable: add the strongest alternative route to the same goal as a baseline (here IWVI as "an alternative approach to tightening the variational bound", p. 8), and write where it wins.
  - Transferable: a capacity-control ablation (vary encoder width for both the baseline and the new method) to show the gain comes from the structure, not from extra parameters (p. 18).
  - Transferable: measure the cost in the resource that actually grows (here memory, not time) and say the numbers describe the implementation, not an algorithmic constant (pp. 17-18).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Titsias-Lawrence Bayesian GPLVM, Lalchand et al. generalised GPLVM, Hensman SVI-GP and Hoffman-Blei SSVI (pp. 1-5). Nearest neighbour: Ialongo et al. 2019, which removed mean field in GP state-space models; separated by setting, sequential vs latent-variable (pp. 5-6). Alternatives positioned as complementary: IWVI, RFLVM and AIS (pp. 6-7). Novelty is hedged: "As far as we are aware, no prior work has proposed a method for making the latent space conditional on the GP mapping as we do here." (p. 7).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Main contribution stated at p. 4 ("The main contribution of this paper is to allow the variational posterior to capture this dependence"). Mixed results are written out in the results and conclusion, dataset by dataset (pp. 8-10). There is a dedicated Limitations section (§6, p. 10). Claims are tied to statistics in an appendix written as an argument: the Oilflow NLPD-but-not-RMSE pattern is read as support for "improving the posterior approximation ... rather than merely shifting the predictive mean" (p. 17). The ablation conclusion is phrased as an interpretation that the results "support" (p. 18). Small inconsistencies: Table 2 promises bold best results that the extraction does not show (p. 7); App. D says "all three models" for four compared methods on MNIST/FMNIST (p. 16).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Methodological contribution (a structured variational family for GPLVMs plus amortised encoder design); code released for the Oilflow experiment only (p. 8). Limits written specifically: Monte Carlo cost with a large memory increase (pp. 10, 17-18); the restrictive diagonal-Gaussian, output-factorised family "still does not accurately match the exact posterior" (p. 10); it does not attain the exact epistemic uncertainty because of the GP approximation (p. 10). Future work: informative sampling of U, e.g. importance sampling (p. 10); using the improved manifold uncertainty in multiobjective BO (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor) ✅ evidence (partial): same initialisation and hyperparameters across models (p. 16), 5 seeds with Welch's t-tests (p. 17), the strongest rival route added as a baseline (IWVI, p. 8), losses and non-significant results written in (pp. 8-9, 17), cost measured (pp. 17-18). ⚠ variant: significance claims against IWVI are not backed by a reported test table (pp. 9-10 vs p. 17).
- Heuristic 7 (write the do-not-use conditions / limits next to the method) ✅ evidence: §6 Limitations with concrete costs and the remaining approximation gaps (p. 10).
- Heuristic 4 (prove the hierarchy, experiment for the rest, label it) ⚠ variant: no hierarchy is proved; the "better posterior" claim is explicitly an inference from metrics ("suggests", p. 9; "supports our claim", p. 17).
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information") ⚠ variant: the structure exploited is the conditional-independence structure of the exact posterior, mirrored in the variational family (p. 4); an analogue in inference, not detection in a solver.
- new-pattern candidate: capacity-control ablation to attribute a gain to structure rather than size (App. H, p. 18).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Derive the defect of the incumbent approximation from the model's own conditional formula (eq. 6), then fix exactly that dependence (p. 4).
- Choose the factorisation direction (q(X|U)q(U) vs q(U|X)q(X)) by which one keeps the local/global split needed for minibatching (p. 5).
- Pair a significance table with a sentence interpreting which metrics moved and which did not (NLPD vs RMSE) as evidence about the mechanism (p. 17).
- Run a capacity-matched ablation over encoder widths for both the baseline and the new method (p. 18).

**Quotes**:
- "Although the MF-GPLVM provides a tractable ELBO, which has been shown to achieve a good approximation of the latent space, the MF assumption is a strong and incorrect one." (p. 4)
- "This supports our claim that the main benefit of the structured variational family is in improving the posterior approximation and predictive uncertainty, rather than merely shifting the predictive mean." (p. 17)
