### S059 · Transition constrained Bayesian optimization via Markov decision processes (NeurIPS 37, 2024, doi:10.52202/079017-2799, arXiv:2402.08406)

**Read level**: full. Main text pp. 1-10 read closely; appendices A (p. 16), B (pp. 17-18), C.1-C.3 (pp. 18-21), the statement and proof set-up of Proposition C.1 (pp. 21-22), D opening and Lemma D.1 (pp. 23-24), E-G (pp. 26-30) and H (pp. 30-34) read; the algebra of the Proposition C.1 proof (pp. 22-23) and of D.1-D.2 (pp. 24-26) skimmed. Read in the arXiv v3 version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. P. Folch (first), C. Tsay, M. van der Wilk (Imperial); R. M. Lee, B. Shafei (BASF); W. Ormaniec, A. Krause, M. Mutný (ETH Zurich) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: follow-up to her group's SnAKe (NeurIPS 2022, ref. [11], p. 12) and truncated SnAKe (ref. [42], p. 13), now with the ETH group whose Markov-chain experiment-design framework [17] it extends; funded by the BASF/RAEng chair (p. 10).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Standard BO "focuses on the setting where you can arbitrarily query the search space"; in physical sciences the next query depends on previous ones: local movement constraints, monotonicity in some variables, transitions that change measurement accuracy (abstract, p. 1). The motivating application is the transient flow reactor, whose inputs must change "continuously and slowly" to keep quasi-steady state, so arbitrary jumps give infeasible measurement sequences (p. 2). Formal statement: find x* = argmax f with noisy Gaussian (possibly heteroscedastic) observations, GP prior, and the order of evaluations dictated by known, possibly stochastic Markov dynamics (p. 2).
  - Transferable: enter an established method through one assumption it makes silently (free choice of the next query) and name the concrete physical mechanisms that break it (p. 1, p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Lift the acquisition step from points to policies, π_{t+1} = argmax U(π|X_t) (Eq. 2, p. 2). The goal is maximiser identification, not regret: via hypothesis testing and a Gaussian tail bound, the utility becomes the largest posterior variance of pairwise differences among potential maximisers Z (Eqs. 3-4, p. 3), the XY-allocation objective of Fiez et al. (p. 3; App. F p. 29). With finite-rank (Nyström/Fourier) features the utility depends only on which states are visited, not their order (Eq. 6, p. 4), so the trajectory problem becomes a convex problem over the polytope of state-action visitations (Eq. 8, p. 5). It is solved with Frank-Wolfe, each linearisation being an RL problem with reward ∇U solved exactly by dynamic programming in the discrete case (Eq. 10, p. 7); receding-horizon re-planning on the empirical visitation (Eqs. 11-12, p. 7) yields a history-dependent, non-Markovian policy. For continuous spaces, linear dynamics x_{h+1} = Ax_h + Ba_h turn the subproblem into an MPC-like nonconvex problem solved heuristically (Eq. 13, p. 7; App. G p. 30). Kept from BO: the greedy (myopic) structure, "to control computational efficiency" (p. 5). Z comes from GP confidence bounds (Eq. 9, p. 6), approximated in continuous spaces by K Thompson samples or K UCB maximisers (p. 6; App. E.1 p. 26).
  - Transferable: when a sequential decision over trajectories is intractable, check whether the objective depends only on visitation counts; if so, optimise over the convex visitation polytope with Frank-Wolfe and recover a policy by marginalisation (pp. 4-7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: GP prior with known kernel and zero mean (p. 2); Gaussian noise with known, possibly state- and action-dependent variance (p. 4); transition constraints known a priori (p. 1) and encoded as zero transition probabilities (p. 5); finite-rank kernel approximation "While not necessary" makes objectives tractable (pp. 3-4; App. C.3 p. 21); GP hyperparameters fixed a priori and identical for all algorithms (p. 8; App. E.2 p. 27); the theory assumes episodic feedback, convexity, local Lipschitz continuity and smoothness of U, and a condition (⋆) on how fast Z_t shrinks, which is left open: "We leave the particular choice for Ct to make the above hold for future work" (p. 21, p. 22). Continuous case restricted to linear state-space dynamics, "not as general as it could be" (p. 7).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: (i) Gaussian tail bound on the probability of reporting a wrong maximiser, then expectation over the GP and a worst case over the composite alternative hypothesis, giving a log-Bayes-factor bound (App. C.1, pp. 18-20); (ii) eliminating the intractable distribution of f(x*) by bounding the gap term and keeping only the variance of differences (App. C.2, p. 20), with the remark that the result coincides with the frequentist lower bound of Fiez et al. (p. 20); (iii) additivity of the objective in state-action pairs via a Mercer decomposition (Lemma D.1, p. 24), with Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury and matrix-inversion lemmas (pp. 25); (iv) Proposition C.1: average suboptimality against a privileged algorithm that knows x*_f is O((1/T)ΣC_t + L log T/T + B/√T log(1/δ)) (p. 22), proved as "an extension of the Theorem 3 in [17]" (p. 22), with C_t ≈ √γ_t/t only conjectured (p. 21); (v) an approximate ODE kernel for the Knorr pyrazole reaction: linearise the kinetics at two equilibria, interpolate with a sigmoid, add a squared-exponential correction for model mismatch (p. 9; App. H pp. 30-34), with the closed-form solution checked against a numerical ODE solve (Fig. 18, p. 33).
  - Transferable: derive the acquisition from a decision rule (report argmax of the posterior mean) and a misidentification-probability bound, then show the tractable surrogate coincides with a known optimal design criterion from bandits (pp. 3, 18-20).
  - Transferable: build a physics-informed kernel from a linearised mechanistic ODE and add a nonparametric kernel term to absorb model mismatch; check the derivation numerically (p. 9; p. 33).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Discrete benchmarks with physical transitions: Knorr pyrazole synthesis in a transient flow reactor with non-decreasing residence time, 10 episodes of 10 steps (p. 9); Lake Ypacarai monitoring with obstacles and a start/end maintenance-port constraint, episodes of 50 (pp. 9-10; Fig. 12 p. 28); free-electron laser with movement-dependent noise σ²(x,a) = s(1 + w||x - a||²) (p. 10; App. E.4 p. 27). Continuous: Branin, Michalewicz, Hartmann 3D/6D, Levy, SnAr under local box constraints, synchronous and asynchronous (25-step delay) (p. 10; pp. 17-18). Baselines: Greedy-UCB (no planning), MDP-EI ("a weak version of planning"), truncated SnAKe and LSR (specialists for movement constraints) (p. 8). Metrics: share of runs identifying the true maximiser (discrete), inference regret (continuous); 25 runs; median with 10%/90% quantiles (p. 8, p. 9; App. B.5 p. 18). Solve times 1-15 s with state-space sizes and horizons tabulated (Table 1, p. 17); hardware given (p. 18). Ablations: Frank-Wolfe mixture components N = 1, 10, 25, where a single component is "better or at worst comparable", so only it is presented (p. 28); size K of the maximiser set, "fairly robust" (pp. 28-29); XY- vs G-allocation, G-allocation much worse on Branin2D (pp. 29-30). Losses reported: MDP-EI stronger early in high-noise Ypacarai (p. 17), comparable by the end in Knorr with immediate feedback (p. 17); LSR "comparable to MDP-BO-UCB in almost all benchmarks" (p. 9). Code promised "after public review" (p. 26).
  - Transferable: when the method generalises a specialist family, include the specialists (here TrSnAKe, LSR) as baselines and frame matching them in their own domain as the result (p. 10).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds directly on Mutný et al. (active exploration in Markov chains) and convex RL (Hazan et al., Zahavy et al.) (p. 4); the utility is taken from Fiez et al.'s transductive linear bandits, the novelty being "the added difficulty of transition constraints necessitating planning" (p. 5). Contrasts with look-ahead BO, whose cost "grows exponentially with the number of look-ahead steps (usually less than three steps)", versus "we comfortably plan for over a hundred steps" (p. 5). Movement-cost methods (including the group's own SnAKe) respect strict constraints only "under truncation heuristics" (p. 5). A visual abstract maps every component to its literature source and marks intractable routes not taken in red (Fig. 5, p. 16).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Three contribution bullets (p. 2); "Interestingly" and "Surprisingly" flag the non-Markovian policy and the coincidence with Fiez et al. (p. 2, p. 20); conjectures are labelled as such (p. 21, p. 28); a concession sentence on specialist baselines in the results (p. 10); a one-figure map of the method and its literature (p. 16).
  - Transferable: draw a visual abstract that shows the chain of reductions, the literature behind each, and the intractable alternatives you did not pursue (Fig. 5, p. 16).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic contribution with supporting theory and application benchmarks. Limits stated concretely: continuous case only for linear dynamics with heuristic subproblems (p. 7); condition (⋆) unproved (p. 21); fixed hyperparameters (p. 8). Future work: "the continuous variant of the framework to deal with more general transition dynamics, or explore the performance of new objective functions" (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole; say when special-purpose code beats yours): evidence ✅. LSR matches MDP-BO-UCB "in almost all benchmarks" (p. 9); SnAKe and LSR are acknowledged as specialists that are expected to be strong (p. 10); the mixture-policy option is dropped from the paper when the single component is as good (p. 28).
- Method 2 (parameterised family, show the parameter does not matter, remove it): variant ⚠. Not a family between two formulations, but the same parameter discipline: K is swept and found robust (pp. 28-29), and the mixture size N is reduced to its simplest value (p. 28).
- Method 1 (structure as insider information): variant ⚠. The structure exploited is in the objective (order-invariance of the utility, p. 4) and in the chemistry (known kinetics turned into a kernel, p. 9), not in an algebraic MINLP.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): partial evidence ✅. Shared fixed hyperparameters for all algorithms (p. 27), quantiles over 25 runs (p. 9), solve times and hardware (pp. 17-18), losses reported (pp. 9, 17); no independent verification step is needed or described.
- Also (heuristics, not methods): Heuristic 1, broken assumption of free querying (p. 1), and SKILL.md already cites this paper as a Heuristic 1 case; Heuristic 8, the SnAKe critic Krause is a coauthor (p. 1).
- new-pattern candidate: "relaxation mindset exported to BO": convexify an intractable sequential decision (trajectories → visitation polytope) and solve by linearisations (pp. 4-7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Reformulate an experiment-design objective over trajectories as a convex function of state-action visitations when it is additive in visited states, then use Frank-Wolfe with an RL/DP oracle and receding-horizon re-planning (pp. 4-7).
- Use maximiser identification (pairwise-difference variance over a confidence set of candidate maximisers) instead of regret when the experimenter needs a reported best point (pp. 3, 6).
- Model movement-dependent measurement noise directly in the design matrix rather than using a worst-case noise level; show the cost of the stateless alternative (p. 10; App. E.4 p. 27).
- Ablate every new knob (mixture size, candidate-set size, XY vs G allocation) in an appendix and present only the simplest variant that is as good (pp. 28-30).

**Quotes**:
- "This reveals an essential observation that the utility depends only on the visited states; not their order." (p. 4)
- "It is important to highlight that SnAKe and LSR are specialist heuristic algorithms for local box-constraints, and therefore it is not surprising they perform strongly." (p. 10)

### S077 · Dependence in constrained Bayesian optimization: When do we need it and how does it help? (Optimization Letters 18(6), 2024, doi:10.1007/s11590-023-02047-z)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-17, including references; the Online Resources 1-3 are not in the file and were not read).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Shiqiang Zhang (first, Imperial PhD student), Robert M. Lee, Behrang Shafei, David Walz (BASF SE) (p. 1, p. 17) · **Position in trajectory**: BASF/RAEng-chair era BO work on black-box constraints in chemistry, citing her ENTMOOT and tree-ensemble papers as motivation (refs 6, 7, 12, p. 15); funded by a Hans Rausing scholarship, EPSRC EP/P016871/1, the BASF/RAEng chair and BASF (p. 14).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Constrained BO "for simplicity" mostly assumes independent constraints (abstract, p. 1); a 14-row table of cBO methods shows independence wherever several GPs are used (Table 1, p. 3); the first cEI paper justified it by tractability (p. 2). The paper asks: "What happens if we do not assume independence between multiple surrogate models?" (p. 2), in the setting where objective and constraints are evaluated simultaneously, unlike Gelbart et al.'s decoupled case (p. 2). The subtitle states the question directly (p. 1).
  - Transferable: take a simplifying assumption that the whole literature makes for tractability, tabulate who makes it (Table 1, p. 3), and make "when does it matter?" the research question.
- **D2 Core idea**: Dep-PoF, the joint probability of feasibility under a multi-output GP for the constraints, computed with expectation propagation for multivariate Gaussian probabilities (EPMGP) (pp. 3, 6-7). It is a drop-in replacement for PoF "without disturbing other parts in classical cBO" (p. 3), inserted into a product-form acquisition (Dep-cEI) and a sum-form one (Dep-cAS, adapted from Ludl et al. with an optimisation term) (p. 7). Only acquisitions that use PoF directly are tested; broader comparisons are left to Eriksson and Poloczek (p. 5). Dep-cAS is included "neither to recommend nor criticize" it, only to check dependence in a second setting, while conceding its extra parameters are "not preferred in machine learning" (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Black-box objective and p > 1 black-box inequality constraints on a compact set rescaled to [0,1]^d, no gradients, expensive evaluations (p. 6); homotopic data (p. 4); dependence means dependence of the prior distributions over constraints, not of the constraints themselves (p. 6); the analysis assumes the ideal case where the predictive mean equals the true constraints and the posterior covariance tends to 0 as n → ∞ (citing Teckentrup) (p. 8); all benchmarks noise-free (p. 9); exact GP/MOGP inference for both models "to eliminate their possibility of influencing the comparison" (p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: Proposition 1: at non-boundary points Dep-PoF → PoF as n → ∞, via continuity of the multivariate Gaussian cdf and the bound (1-δ)^p (p. 8). Proposition 2: at a boundary point with q active constraints PoF → 1/2^q (pp. 8-9), a "decay" that slows the search for boundary optima; relaxing the feasible region would fix it but "requires more hyper-parameters and possibly finds an infeasible 'optimum'" (p. 9). Complexity: p independent GPs O(pn³) vs a MOGP O(p³n³); PoF O(p) vs EPMGP O(p³ + mp²) (p. 9). The analysis is "motivated by experimental results", i.e. written after the experiments to explain why the two coincide (p. 7).
  - Transferable: when an experiment shows a refinement makes no difference, prove a limit result explaining why (here the refined quantity converges to the simple one away from the boundary) and a second one locating where it can still matter (p. 8).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: cEI, Dep-cEI, cAS, Dep-cAS and random search; 10 Latin-hypercube initial points, budget 100, 50 replications, mean ± one standard error; infeasible points get the worst feasible objective value (pp. 9-10). A constructed extreme case: the 2D Gardner constraint repeated p = 2..5 times, where PoF = p0^p but Dep-PoF = p0, regarded "as an upper bound for the improvement that Dep-PoF may bring" (p. 9, p. 10). Synthetic: Gardner, Gramacy, Sasena, G4, G6-G10 with active sets and feasible ratio ρ estimated from 10^6 uniform samples (Table 2, p. 12); engineering: tension-compression spring, pressure vessel, welded beam, speed reducer (p. 12). Dep-PoF helps only on G7 and G10 (for cAS), whose feasible regions are tiny (G7: one feasible point in 10^6 samples) (p. 10); elsewhere indistinguishable, explained by large feasible regions, slight correlations or negative correlations (pp. 10, 12). Their cEI does better than in Eriksson and Poloczek, attributed to their practical treatments (pp. 12-13).
  - Transferable: construct a synthetic case where the refinement must help (duplicated constraints) and use it as an upper bound on the benefit before testing on real benchmarks (pp. 9-10).
  - Transferable: report the feasible-volume ratio and the active set at the optimum for every constrained benchmark, so results can be explained by problem features (Table 2, p. 12).
- **D6 Positioning**: Table 1 classifies 14 cBO methods by acquisition, PoF use, number of inequality/equality constraints, known/unknown status, dependence and model type (p. 3); the closest prior work (Gelbart et al., multi-task BO) is distinguished on two concrete points (p. 2); comparison with entropy-based methods is explicitly delegated to an existing study rather than repeated (p. 5).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The negative result is in the abstract: "Our results are largely negative" (p. 1); the discussion is organised as bold claim-sentences ("Dep-PoF is not worse than PoF", "Dep-PoF is very similar to PoF after sufficient samples", "Improvement versus Additional cost") (pp. 13-14); a plain-language conclusion for users: "a user will not feel guilty to assume independence" (p. 14). Hedges are frequent where explanations are conjectural ("probably", "may explain", pp. 10, 12).
  - Transferable: state a negative result in the abstract, then turn it into user guidance (when the extra cost is worth paying, when to keep the simple assumption) (p. 1, p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: an implemented relaxation of a standard assumption, two small propositions, and a negative empirical answer that licenses the assumption (p. 14). Scope where Dep-PoF may still pay: constraints and their dependence totally unknown, and experiments so expensive that any small gain counts (abstract p. 1; p. 14); it only helps early (p. 14). Limits stated: no general way to measure dependence ("we do not know how to measure it for general cases", p. 10); only two acquisitions tested (p. 5). No future-work agenda.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 (grade a component inside the whole algorithm; the default follows evidence): evidence ✅. Dep-PoF is judged inside complete BO runs across benchmark classes, the verdict is split by class (tiny feasible regions vs the rest, p. 10), and the simple default (independence) is kept (p. 14).
- Method 2 stop rule (report a failed idea as failed; distrust extra parameters): evidence ✅. Negative results published as such (p. 1, p. 14); extra parameters of Dep-cAS and of feasible-region relaxation are flagged as a cost (p. 7, p. 9).
- Method 5 step 3 (strengthen the baseline, add oracle runs): variant ⚠. Instead of an oracle solver, a constructed best case bounds the possible gain (pp. 9-10); exact inference for both arms removes a confounder (p. 9).
- Also (heuristic): Heuristic 7, do-not-use conditions written next to the method (abstract p. 1; p. 14).
- new-pattern candidate: "negative result as licence for a simplifying assumption" (p. 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace one modelling component (independent PoF) by its dependent version while holding everything else fixed, including exact inference on both sides, so that the comparison isolates the assumption (pp. 3, 9).
- Bound the best-case benefit with a duplicated-constraint construction before running general benchmarks (pp. 9-10).
- Characterise benchmarks by feasible ratio and active set to explain where a method helps (Table 2, p. 12).
- Pair asymptotic propositions (equivalence away from the boundary, 1/2^q decay at it) with complexity counts to argue the cost-benefit trade-off (pp. 8-9, 14).

**Quotes**:
- "Our results are largely negative: incorporating dependence between the constraints does not help much." (p. 1)
- "These (negative) results are useful because they allow us to make a simplifying assumption without concerns." (p. 14)

### S044 · BoFire: Bayesian optimization framework intended for real experiments (Journal of Machine Learning Research 2025, arXiv:2408.05040)

**Read level**: full (all 6 pages of the arXiv v1 text, including references pp. 5-6; the JMLR published version was not read).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. P. Dürholt (first and corresponding author) and five more from Evonik; J. Keupp (Boehringer Ingelheim); A. Osburg (Heidelberg); T. Boyne and R. Misener (Imperial); R. Eldred, W. Steuer Costa (Chemovator); C. Kappatou, R. M. Lee, D. Linzner, D. Walz, N. Wulkow, B. Shafei (BASF) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: industry-consortium software in the BASF/RAEng-chair era; the voice is the companies' ("Our companies actively deploy BoFire", p. 2); Misener's individual role is not stated in the text.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Industrial chemistry questions (yield and purity via temperature and pressure; formulations; choosing among thousands of candidate molecules) are still answered by intuition, trial and error or expensive mechanistic models (p. 1). Existing BO and active-learning software "would require substantial adaptation prior to deployment" and inconsistent data handling blocks integration into larger lab pipelines (p. 2).
  - Transferable: motivate a software paper by the deployment gap (what practitioners must rebuild before using existing tools), not by algorithmic novelty (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: One open-source package joining BO with classical design of experiments (p. 1). A Domain holds inputs (continuous, discrete, molecular, categorical), outputs and constraints, including practitioner constraints such as NChooseK and interpoint equality and learned black-box inequalities (p. 2); objectives are defined separately from outputs (minimise, maximise, close-to-target, sigmoid), with a priori weighting or a posteriori Pareto approximation via qParEGO and q(log)(N)EHVI (p. 3). Strategies are either classical designs (factorial, space-filling, D/E/A/G/K-optimal) over constrained mixed spaces or predictive BO strategies on BoTorch, extensible with custom strategies such as ENTMOOT; a StepwiseStrategy switches between them (p. 3). Data models (Pydantic, JSON-serialisable) are split from functional components, so a minimal install carries only data models for a process-orchestration layer (p. 3); problems, strategies and surrogates are mutually independent modules (pp. 3-4). A paint-formulation example (20 compounds, at most 5 per mixture, shared temperature per batch, target viscosity and maximal hydrophobicity) runs through the paper (pp. 2-4; Listing 1, p. 4).
  - Transferable: separate the serialisable problem specification (data models) from the solver code, so the same problem object can travel through REST APIs and lab orchestration layers (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Requirements rather than mathematical assumptions: self-driving labs and human-in-the-loop use, RESTful communication with data integrity, mixed-type constrained input spaces (pp. 2-3). The input space is a product X = x1 ⊗ ... ⊗ xD of typed features (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: n/a (software paper, no analysis).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a. No benchmarks; one illustrative example (pp. 2-4) and an unquantified deployment claim: "we deploy BoFire daily" (p. 4).
- **D6 Positioning**: Against Ax: adds chemoinformatics, classical DoE and Pydantic serialisation; against BayBE: adds DoE strategies, serialisation and "true multi-objective optimization compared to a pure scalarization based approach" (p. 2); against commercial DoE software (Modde, JMP): designs over constrained mixed-type spaces (p. 3). Builds on BoTorch to reuse its ecosystem (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Short (4 pages of text); each feature section ends with an indented paragraph continuing the paint example (pp. 2-3); the mission is stated as fast-tracking research into industrial use via "maintainable open-source software" (p. 1).
  - Transferable: in a software paper, carry one realistic user story through every feature section, with the code listing for it (pp. 2-4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Software contribution; no limitations stated. The stated aim is a channel from academic ML into practice: researchers can "translate new strategies and surrogates into practice" (p. 4), example given: practical multi-fidelity modelling (Folch et al. 2023) (p. 4). The decision to pool effort is stated as a choice: each company could have built its own bespoke package, but they joined forces to catalyse ML research (p. 4, paraphrase).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 (generalise behind one interface; reuse in BO): evidence ✅. Her group's ENTMOOT is plugged in as a custom strategy (p. 3); the package's modular split of problem, strategy and surrogate is the "one interface, switchable components" design (pp. 3-4). Caveat: the paper speaks for the companies, so this is evidence of where her tools land, not of her own design decisions.
- Also (heuristics/taste): Heuristic 8, collaborate through shared code (p. 4); Taste mark 5, software others can extend (p. 3); Heuristic 1, existing software's assumptions break in industrial deployment (p. 2).
- new-pattern candidate: "practitioner-constraint vocabulary as first-class objects" (NChooseK, interpoint equality, close-to-target objectives) (pp. 2-3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Define objectives separately from measured outputs so outputs stay physically meaningful and objectives can change without re-modelling (p. 3).
- Offer classical DoE and BO behind the same interface with a stepwise switch (space-filling start, then predictive strategy) (p. 3).
- Make every problem and strategy object JSON-serialisable to integrate with lab orchestration and databases (p. 3).

**Quotes**:
- "However, in an industrial chemistry setting, existing BO and active learning software would require substantial adaptation prior to deployment." (p. 2)
- "Through BoFire, we offer the possibility for researchers to use our platform to translate new strategies and surrogates into practice." (p. 4)

### S028 · Cross-disciplinary perspectives on the potential for artificial intelligence across chemistry (Chemical Society Reviews 54, 2025, doi:10.1039/d5cs00146c)

**Read level**: full (main text pp. 1-23 read, §8 pp. 14-17 closely; references pp. 23-37 skimmed for her group's citations).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: 18 authors led by A. M. Mroz and K. E. Jelfs (p. 1). Per the author-contributions statement, "R. S. and R. M. contributed Experimental Design for Discrete and Mixed Input Spaces" (§8); all authors edited the final version (pp. 22-23). Only §8 is treated as Misener's voice (jointly with R. Sedgwick); the other nine perspectives (quantum chemistry, force fields, generative AI, drug discovery, retrosynthesis, data-led experimentation, LLMs, robotics, data management) belong to other authors and are not used as evidence about her · **Position in trajectory**: an outreach/positioning piece from the EPSRC "AIchemy" hub (p. 23), summarising the BO-for-chemistry line in which her group works.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** (§8): Data acquisition in many chemistry applications is expensive, so ML-aided experimental design should reduce the number of experiments (p. 14). Uncertainty is easy on continuous spaces with GPs, but inputs are often discrete or mixed and heterogeneous, so "there is no one-size-fits-all solution" (p. 14).
- **D2 Core idea** (§8): A taxonomy first, methods second. Discrete variables are categorical (with dichotomous as a special case), ordinal, combinatorial or mixed (pp. 14-15). Surrogates are grouped as parallel surrogates, continuous relaxation, tree models, specialist kernels, latent-space methods and others (graph diffusion kernels, piecewise-affine surrogates that allow mixed-integer programming) (pp. 15-16; Fig. 4, p. 15). Decision policies split by whether discrete variables are mapped to continuous spaces (gradient-based acquisition optimisation possible) or not (evolutionary, random-walk, local search or global optimisation of the acquisition by mixed-integer programming for tree models) (p. 16).
  - Transferable: organise a methods survey as a matrix of problem-type taxonomy × model families × acquisition-optimisation strategies, with one panel per family (Fig. 4, p. 15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model** (§8): The framing is surrogate plus decision policy; BO optimises the black box, active learning learns the whole function, Bayesian quadrature an integral (p. 14, p. 16). Each surrogate family's assumption is named: parallel surrogates scale with the number of discrete values (p. 15); continuous relaxation needs an ordering and can resample the same points (p. 16); latent-space methods "generally assume smoothness" (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: n/a (review).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a; "No primary research results, software or code have been included" (p. 23).
- **D6 Positioning** (§8): Even-handed survey; her group's work appears as one option among many: tree-ensemble kernels including BARK (ref. 397), shortest-path kernels for graphs (BoGrape, ref. 401), mixed-integer optimisation of the acquisition over tree models (ref. 425), BoFire (ref. 427), system-aware neural ODE processes (ref. 433), and Sedgwick et al. on DNA molecules (ref. 373) (pp. 15-17; refs pp. 33-35); competing packages (BoTorch, BayBE, WebBO, Atlas, Anubis, Dragonfly, GAUCHE) are listed without ranking (p. 16, p. 17).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric** (§8): Each family gets its use cases and its limitation in the same paragraph (pp. 15-16); the outlook names the adoption problem directly (p. 17). The whole article is a collation of ten voices with a short joint outlook (pp. 21-22).
  - Transferable: give every surveyed approach its failure mode next to its use cases, so the reader can map a problem to a method (pp. 15-16).
- **D8 Contribution and limits** (§8): Contribution is a map for experimentalists. The stated limit is adoption: "uptake of such methods is slow" (p. 17), so software should include educational aspects that help experimentalists map their problem to methods and understand the assumptions (p. 17). Future directions: meta-learned acquisition functions that skip the surrogate, decisions that change with time, experimental design for causal relationships (p. 17).
