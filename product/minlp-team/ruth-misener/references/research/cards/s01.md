### S032 · A framework for globally optimizing mixed-integer signomial programs (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 2014, doi:10.1007/s10957-013-0396-3)

**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §§1-3 (pp. 1-16, except the algebra of the GMA and Hartree-Fock examples on pp. 9, 14), the test suite §4 (pp. 16-18), all of §5 results text and tables 3-5 (pp. 18-22), and the conclusions (p. 23). Not read: references pp. 23-30 and the appendix pp. 31-32.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Christodoulos A. Floudas (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: third step of the GloMIQO line (MP 2012, JOGO 2013, OMS 2014 cited as [1-3], p. 2). The framework ships inside ANTIGONE from GAMS 24.1 (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entered by widening the class: GloMIQO handles MIQCQP. Mixed-integer signomial programs (MISO) arise because convex posynomial GPs "could not model important engineering applications", with a list of process-systems domains (p. 2). The problem statement admits real powers on continuous variables only, assumes finite inferable bounds, nonnegative variables under noninteger powers, and absolute values (pp. 2-3).
  - Transferable: when extending your own solver, define the new class so that it collapses exactly to the old one when the new terms vanish ("GloMIQO and this framework are equivalent when there are no signomial terms", p. 3). This makes regressions and attribution checkable.
- **D2 Core idea**: The novelty is in the interconnections, not new relaxations. "The primary contribution is to integrate existing ideas into an algorithmic framework for globally optimizing MISO" (p. 2). Four components: (1) flatten the factorable-programming tree towards term-based data structures, so that specialised multivariable underestimators can be used. Products and nonconvex integer powers are expanded, convex terms are kept, and noninteger powers are distributed only in the nonconvex direction (pp. 5-6). (2) Add nonconvex terms until each term is either a product of existing terms or has a known convex hull, building "a web of interconnections" for RLT (p. 8). (3) Run dynamic RLT inside branch-and-cut (pp. 10, 14-15). (4) Term objects in a class hierarchy, where polymorphism applies "the most specific relaxation" for the current node bounds (p. 12). Auxiliary variables are chosen over variable-free underestimators "because of the importance it places on an adaptive RLT implementation" (p. 4).
  - Transferable: when combining two data-structure traditions (operator trees vs term lists), name the weakness the combination creates (lost interlinks, weak high-dimensional terms) and add a repair step for it. Here the repair is the added nonconvex terms (p. 8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Finite bounds inferable on variables in nonconvex terms; the relaxation is a bounded MILP (p. 3). Noninteger powers only on nonnegative variables, to simplify interval analysis (p. 3). The exponential transformation is used only on positive domains, with no domain shift for negative values (p. 12). Edge-concavity is detected only on single signomial terms, where interval arithmetic has no overestimation because each variable appears once (p. 12).
- **D4 Algorithmic devices**: (a) Edge-concavity test: the sign of the interval-Hessian diagonal classifies a term as always, sometimes or never edge-concave. "Sometimes" triggers locally valid cuts at the relevant nodes (pp. 11-12). (b) For terms of ≤4 variables, all (up to 28) convex-hull facets are added directly. Generalised Meyer-Floudas dominance tests screen candidate facets before the Rikun test (pp. 13-14). (c) RLT detection over all variable/term/equation products. Partitioning terms into disjoint interaction sets first makes this scale: 15,859 RLT equations initialised in about 9 CPU s on johnall (p. 10). (d) Cuts are added until the LP objective stops improving, are global or local by node, and are switched off at deeper levels (pp. 14-15). (e) The reliability-branching error rule is extended to signomial terms (p. 15). (f) NLP multistart from every MILP relaxation solution, with three safeguards: no starts at discontinuities, freezing variables at infinite-derivative points, and removing redundant RLT equalities before the local solve (p. 16).
  - Transferable: before a local NLP solve, strip the redundant equalities you added for the relaxation, because they are "unnecessarily difficult" for local solvers (p. 16). Keep two models: one for the bound, one for the primal heuristic.
- **D5 Experiments**: 257 MISO instances (60 minlp.org, 82 GLOBALLib, 100 MINLPLib+Bonmin, 19 other). Quadratic-only instances are excluded to showcase the new parts (pp. 16-17). Compared against BARON 12.3.3, Couenne 0.4, LINDO 8.0 and SCIP 3.0, the latest available versions as of July 2013, at defaults, with gap 1e-6 and 7200 s limits, run back to back (p. 18). A Perl script re-checks every returned point, and benign failures are separated out (p. 18). Time and remaining-gap-at-7200-s performance profiles are given per subset (pp. 19-22), with one worked instance each for three of the four subsets (Tables 3-5, pp. 20-22). A root-gap example (Goldstein-Price, about 50× tighter root) isolates the reformulation effect (p. 8).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Algorithm and software contribution (integrated in ANTIGONE). Limits are stated concretely. Other solvers "admit more functional forms" (p. 3). The strong minlp.org result is "largely an outcome of the MISO framework reducing to GloMIQO" on ruiz_concbased (p. 20). Validated interval arithmetic made the code slower on computational geometry but more reliable on scheduling and water networks, and "there is no way for a user to manually switch off" it (p. 15). No explicit future-work section (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: a recognition layer before relaxation. It covers convexity look-ahead before expansion (p. 6), edge-concavity detection via the interval Hessian (pp. 11-12), RLT detection (p. 10), and term classes in an inheritance hierarchy with polymorphic relaxation choice (p. 12). This is SKILL Method 1 step 3 in an early form.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: newest competitor versions with the date stated, harmonised tolerances, back-to-back runs, Perl feasibility re-check with relabelling to failures, and time plus remaining-gap profiles (pp. 18-19). Wins are attributed honestly to old GloMIQO components (p. 20).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: stability over speed is chosen (validated interval arithmetic, p. 15), but as a fixed default with no user switch. Elsewhere the method ships class-specific trade-offs as options.
- new-pattern candidate: integration-as-contribution. The paper claims novelty in the connective components between existing techniques (p. 2, p. 23).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Show a reformulation's effect on one tiny classic instance by root-node bound alone, across all solvers, before the full benchmark (Goldstein-Price, Table 1, p. 8).
- Partition variables into interaction classes before enumerating pairwise products (RLT). This avoids testing products that cannot occur (p. 10).
- Report a robustness/speed trade-off of a numerical-safety layer by naming the classes that lost and the classes that gained (p. 15).

**Quotes**:
- "The primary contribution is to integrate existing ideas into an algorithmic framework for globally optimizing MISO." (p. 2)
- "Instances where solvers returned infeasible points but claimed they were ε-global optimum points, local optimum points, or integer feasible points were automatically relabeled as failures (fl)." (p. 18)

### S045 · Global superstructure optimisation of red blood cell production in a parallelised hollow fibre bioreactor (Computers & Chemical Engineering 2014, doi:10.1016/j.compchemeng.2014.10.004)

**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract, introduction, literature review, design decisions and assumption Tables 1-2 (pp. 1-10), §4 opening (pp. 10-11), §5.6 (p. 29), §6 results, what-if and sensitivity (pp. 29-33), conclusions (p. 33). Skimmed by headings only: the parameter derivations §§4.1-4.6 and model equations §§5.1-5.5 (pp. 11-29). Not read: appendices pp. 41-48.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: María Fuentes-Garí, Maria Rende, Eirini Velliou, Nicki Panoskaltsis, Efstratios N. Pistikopoulos, Athanasios Mantalaris; Misener first and corresponding author, Department of Computing (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: full-length version of S085. It adds start-up via multiple quasi-steady states and drops the robust-optimisation part for length (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A partner's device (the Panoskaltsis et al. 2012 hollow-fibre bioreactor) works, but its best configuration is unknown, and experiments are "cost- and labour-intensive" (p. 2). Prior hollow-fibre optimisation varied single degrees of freedom and assumed steady state. "none exploit the known advantages of simultaneously integrating many design choices" (p. 5). The business question: can ex vivo RBC compete with rare-blood transfusion prices (pp. 2, 5-6)?
- **D2 Core idea**: Superstructure MINLP that couples topology (number of parallel reactors, number and type of hollow fibres, size/aspect ratio, inoculum) with operation (feed concentrations, flow rate) (p. 6). The cell-kinetics ODE model of Colijn-Mackey/Ma et al. is discretised into 4 quasi-steady states to get an MINLP (p. 5, p. 9), which ANTIGONE solves to global optimality (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Table 1 states and justifies every model assumption (pp. 7-9). Table 2 lists for each risky assumption the "Implication if Violated" and "How to Check / Mitigate". Examples: CFD shows 10-20% divergence from the Krogh model, so allow 10-20% uncertainty; the zero-order reaction assumption "is incorrect", so allow rate uncertainty (pp. 9-10). The model is nominal, "to carefully justify all design decisions" (p. 6).
- **D4 Modelling devices**: (a) An order-of-magnitude estimate of the number of reactors (NR ≈ 250) is made before solving, and the optimum (NR = 339) is checked against it (pp. 29-30). (b) Start-up cost is included only to stop the optimiser exploiting the inoculum ("otherwise the optimization model will drive the inoculum to have as many HSC as it needs", p. 30). (c) Careful scaling of quantities spanning 1e-15 to 1e12 is called "the key for solving this model quickly" (p. 30). (d) The solve is stopped at a 10% gap, justified by parameter uncertainty (p. 30).
- **D5 Experiments**: GAMS 24.1 / ANTIGONE 1.1. 261 variables (2 integer) and 205 equations, reformulated to 185 bilinear terms, 3 log terms and 50 RLT equations. Solved to a 10% gap within 300 CPU s (pp. 29-30). The study adds what-if scenarios at physiological glucose/O2 (p. 32) and a one-at-a-time sensitivity on 29 parameters at 50/90/110/150% or known error bars, where 11 parameters shift the optimum by ≥10% (p. 32).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Three contributions are named: mathematical, engineering and industrial (p. 5, p. 33). Limits are concrete. Cost excludes nutrients, materials, operator time and transport, so no claim against mainstream transfusion (p. 31). The cost of bioreactor malfunction is not modelled (p. 31). Full uncertainty analysis is "the missing step", excluded for space (p. 33).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the partner's model is the problem, but here it is a mechanistic device-plus-kinetics model from the partner lab (pp. 2-3), not a trained ML model. Partner data is used directly, with no public stand-in.
- new-pattern candidate: tolerance matched to model uncertainty. The global solve is stopped at a 10% gap because "a tight duality gap is meaningless" under uncertain parameters (p. 30).
- new-pattern candidate: assumption-violation table. Each risky modelling assumption is paired with its consequence and a check or mitigation (Table 2, pp. 9-10).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Write a two-column "implication if violated / how to check or mitigate" table for every risky modelling assumption (pp. 9-10).
- Predict the optimum's order of magnitude by hand before solving, then compare (NR ≈ 250 vs 339, pp. 29-30).
- Add a small cost term solely to block an unphysical exploitation by the optimiser, and say so (p. 30).
- Read design recommendations off which bounds the optimum hits: fibres at the physical limit, flow at its maximum, reactor at maximum length (pp. 30-31).

**Quotes**:
- "a tight duality gap is meaningless for these uncertain parameters." (p. 30)
- "We do not claim that the bioreactor is competitive with mainstream transfusion (US $ 225.42 / unit)" (p. 31)

### S085 · Robust superstructure optimisation of a bioreactor that produces red blood cells (Computer Aided Chemical Engineering 33 / ESCAPE-24 2014, doi:10.1016/b978-0-444-63456-6.50016-8)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-6; the equations are garbled in extraction, pp. 2-4, and were read from the surrounding text only).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jonathan Chin, Min Lai, María Fuentes Garí, Eirini Velliou, Nicki Panoskaltsis, Efstratios N. Pistikopoulos, Athanasios Mantalaris; Misener first and corresponding author (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the short, robust-optimisation version that S045 later expands (S045 p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Same bioreactor and cost gap ($8,330 per lab unit vs $225.42 typical and $1,150-3,025 rare blood, p. 1). The new angle is that prior work optimised individual degrees of freedom without uncertainty. This paper "considers multiple design choices and explicitly incorporates uncertainty into the framework for the first time" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Robust superstructure MINLP. Mass-transfer uncertainty (diffusivity in lumen ±20% per Young et al.; scaffold diffusivity via porosity uncertainty) is treated as bounded, model-inherent uncertainty (Pistikopoulos 1995) and handled as in Lin, Janak and Floudas (2004). The uncertain equality (Eq. 9) becomes an inequality once one of Cmin/Cmax is known to be inactive (pp. 2, 4-5).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Cost is growth factors only. Nutrients, materials and transport are neglected (p. 2). Quasi-steady state replaces delay-differential kinetics (p. 4). Membranes pose no mass-transfer barrier (p. 5). Uncertainty sources left out are listed: reaction rates, proliferation/differentiation rates, exit rate (p. 5). Robust optimisation is chosen because each reactor must work with high certainty (p. 4).
- **D4**: Modelling device: an axial × radial discretisation (10 × 10 grid) turns the PDE-like mass transfer into an MINLP (p. 5). No proofs.
- **D5 Experiments**: GAMS 24.1, 694 continuous and 2 binary variables, 1,069 constraints, with 41 bilinear, 31 signomial and 10 logarithmic terms. ANTIGONE 1.1 solves it in under 60 CPU s. Uncertainty leads to "a smaller bioreactor with more hollow fibres" (p. 5).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Claimed as the first superstructure optimisation of a hollow-fibre bioreactor, priced competitively with rare blood (p. 5). Limits are the listed omitted uncertainty sources (p. 5). The future direction is generic: bioprocess optimisation (p. 5).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the partner's (Mantalaris/Panoskaltsis lab) device model is the optimisation problem (pp. 1-2), but it is mechanistic, not learned.
- new-pattern candidate: robust counterpart of a superstructure MINLP, solved globally. Floudas-lineage bounded-uncertainty reformulation is applied to a bioprocess design (pp. 2, 5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- State why the conservative (robust) uncertainty model fits the decision ("require a high degree of certainty that each individual bioreactor will operate", p. 4). List the uncertainty sources you did not include (p. 5).
- Report the qualitative design shift that uncertainty causes, not only the cost (smaller reactor, more fibres, p. 5).

**Quotes**:
- "Observe here that robust optimisation is, as a conservative method for representing uncertainty, an appropriate choice for optimising a bioreactor where we have to prioritise consistent, effective cellular expansion and require a high degree of certainty that each individual bioreactor will operate." (p. 4)
- "uncertainty in mass transfer leads us to design a smaller bioreactor with more hollow fibres." (p. 5)

### S037 · Towards unravelling the kinetics of an acute myeloid leukaemia model system under oxidative and starvation stress: a comparison between two- and three-dimensional cultures (Bioprocess and Biosystems Engineering 2015, doi:10.1007/s00449-015-1401-z)

**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and introduction (pp. 2-7), materials-and-methods opening and statistical analysis (pp. 7, 12-13), the diffusion-modelling paragraph in Results (pp. 18-19), and the conclusion (pp. 19-20). Not read: detailed protocol pp. 7-12, most results pp. 13-18, references and figures pp. 21-32.

- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Experimental contribution. Future work: the mechanisms of cell-scaffold interaction, and stress combined with chemotherapy in 3D (p. 20).
