### S061 · Heuristics with performance guarantees for the minimum number of matches problem in heat recovery network design (Computers & Chemical Engineering 113, 2018, DOI 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2018.03.002; arXiv 1709.04688)
**Read level**: full for the parts listed: abstract and introduction (pp. 1-3), complexity and approximation framework (pp. 8-10), single-interval MILP and greedy algorithms (pp. 10-12), big-M computation MHG (pp. 13-14), LHM-LP statement (p. 26), numerical results (pp. 28-35), contributions and conclusion (pp. 35-37), proof of Theorem 7 opening (pp. 49-50). Sections 6-8 bodies and most appendix proofs skimmed only through their theorem statements.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Dimitrios Letsios, Georgia Kouyialis (Imperial) · **Place in trajectory**: start of the group's approximation-algorithms line (with S138/S069 and the bounded-job-start scheduling papers); dedicated to Floudas (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The minimum number of matches is the bottleneck MILP of the sequential HENS method, strongly NP-hard, and exact solvers fail on moderate instances; symmetry hurts tree search (pp. 2-3). Entry: Furman and Sahinidis (2004) already gave approximation algorithms, and all standard MILPs carry big-M constraints whose weak relaxations affect both heuristics and exact methods (pp. 2-3). Since the downstream NLP is easy, the goal is "many good candidate solutions" for Floudas's network-design step (p. 37; p. 1 dedication).
  - Transferable technique: when a sequential decomposition feeds an easy downstream solve, aim the upstream method at producing several provably good solutions rather than one optimum (pp. 3, 37).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Read the problem as packing. A new NP-hardness reduction from bin packing (Thm 1, p. 9) motivates greedy packing heuristics (p. 9). For one temperature interval, an optimal solution is a forest with v = n + m − ℓ, so minimising matches = maximising trees; this gives a big-M-free bin MILP (Eqs. 13-19) and an improved 1.5-approximate greedy IG that first matches equal heat loads (pp. 10-12). For multiple intervals, Algorithm MHG computes the maximum heat between two streams, giving tighter big-M values than Gundersen et al. (pp. 13-14). Nine heuristics in three families: relaxation rounding (FLPR, LRR, CRR), water filling (WFG, WFM), greedy packing (LHM-LP, LHM, LFM, SS) (pp. 3, 10).
  - Transferable technique: before designing heuristics, find a structural property of some optimal solution (here acyclicity) that turns the objective into a count you can formulate without big-M (pp. 10-11).
  - Transferable technique: reuse an existing heuristic family unchanged but feed it tighter big-M parameters, and measure the gain separately (pp. 31-33).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Standard transportation/transshipment MILP with temperature intervals (§2, pp. 3-8). Ratios are worst-case over all instances; the equal-heat-load case is admitted to be "largely a thought experiment" (p. 12). Theorem 7's ratio depends on a precision ε (p. 26).
- **D4 Analysis and proof techniques (brief)**: Lower-bound-then-ratio template made explicit with a figure (Fig. 2, p. 9). Tightness of Furman-Sahinidis's ratio 2 and of the new 1.5 by worst-case instances (Thms 2-3, p. 12; App. B pp. 42-44). Negative result: FLPR is Ω(n)-approximate; positive result tied to the filling ratio, so tighter big-M improves the guarantee (Thms 4-5, pp. 17-18). Water filling is Θ(k) (Thm 6, p. 23; p. 46). MHG correctness from max-flow/min-cut (p. 13). LHM-LP's O(log n + log(hmax/ε)) ratio by a greedy charging argument with unit costs κℓ = 1/Eℓ (Thm 7, pp. 26, 49-50).
- **D5 Experiments**: 51 instances: 26 Furman test cases, digitised and released for the first time, 10 Chen et al., 12 Grossmann, 3 random 160-stream instances; CPLEX 12.6.3 and Gurobi 6.5.2 on both MILPs with 1800-14400 s limits; heuristics vs Furman-Sahinidis's reported results; performance-ratio box plots, normalised CPU-time box plots; Table G.10 compares three big-M computations (pp. 28-34). Exact solvers still give the best solutions on literature instances (p. 34); greedy packing wins over 90% of cases among heuristics (p. 34); on 160-stream instances SS reaches in 10 minutes what CPLEX needs hours for, but CPLEX wins large-scale0 (p. 34). CPLEX parameters deliberately not tuned (pp. 34-35).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Theory (new reduction, tight ratios, negative and positive guarantees), algorithms (nine heuristics, big-M-free MILP, MHG) and a released data set and code (pp. 29, 35-37). Limits stated: LHM-LP is slow in practice despite polynomial bounds (p. 31); rounding and water filling trade quality for speed (pp. 36-37). Future direction: big-M reduction and fast primal solutions for exact methods (p. 37).

**Method links**: Method 1 (✅ evidence): the contribution is reading the problem's structure (packing nature, acyclic optimal solutions, temperature-interval residual capacities) and exploiting it (pp. 9-13). Method 5 (⚠ variant): new public test set and both exact solvers reported, but the commercial baseline is explicitly not tuned ("we could additionally study approaches to improve the heuristic performance of CPLEX", p. 34), and the paper concedes the exact solvers win on literature instances (p. 34). New-pattern candidate: "approximation-algorithm lens on PSE problems" (prove tight worst-case ratios for practitioners' heuristics, then improve them; pp. 9-12, 35-36).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Re-analyse a published heuristic before replacing it: prove its ratio tight, then add one structural step (equal-load matching) and prove the improved ratio tight too (p. 12).
- Pair a negative worst-case result with a positive parametrised one (FLPR is Ω(n), yet 1/φ(M)-approximate in the filling ratio), so the analysis points to the fix (tighter big-M) (pp. 17-18, 35).
- Report the exact solver's incumbent trajectory over time against a heuristic's 10-minute answer, not just final values (p. 34).
**Quotes**:
- "In practice, hot and cold process streams are unlikely to have equal supplies and demands of heat, so discussing equal heat loads is largely a thought experiment." (p. 12)
- "the point of this paper is to develop a deep understanding of a very important problem that consistently arises in process systems engineering" (p. 35)

### S075 · Design of Experiments for Model Discrimination Hybridising Analytical and Data-Driven Approaches (ICML 2018, PMLR 80; arXiv 1802.04170)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-8: introduction, background §2, method §3, results §4, discussion and conclusions §5-6; supplementary material not in the file, not read).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Simon Olofsson (PhD student), Marc Peter Deisenroth (Imperial / PROWLER.io) · **Place in trajectory**: first GP-surrogate design-of-experiments paper; extended into GPdoemd (S041), which says its §4 repeats this paper (S041 p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Regulators require interpretable mechanistic models (pp. 1-2); several rival models and too little data to discriminate (p. 1). Classical analytical design criteria need parameter gradients, unavailable for legacy ODE/PDE code (p. 3, "Limitations"); Monte Carlo methods handle black boxes but cost days (six days of wall-clock time for one Vanlier et al. case, p. 4) while a pharmacokinetic experiment takes about 1-4 days (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Replace each rival (non-analytical) model by a GP surrogate learned from many noise-free simulations over design and parameter space, then reuse the classical analytical machinery (Gaussian parameter posterior, design criteria of Box-Hill, Buzzi-Ferraris, Akaike weights) on the surrogate's differentiable mean (§3, pp. 4-5). Kept: the classical criteria and discrimination tests; changed: where gradients come from.
  - Transferable technique: when one assumption of a classical method fails (gradients available), substitute only that ingredient with a learned surrogate and keep the rest of the method, so you can test against the classical method on its home ground (pp. 4-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: i.i.d. zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise with known covariance (pp. 2, 4-5); Laplace-type Gaussian approximation of p(θ | D_exp) around the best fit (p. 5); RBF kernel motivated by smooth ODE solutions, other kernels possible (p. 4); point-estimate GP hyperparameters because simulated data are plentiful (p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: no theorems. Devices: first- and second-order Taylor expansions of GP mean and variance in θ around θ̂ give closed-form marginal predictive moments (Eqs. 7-16, p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**: Case 1 (Buzzi-Ferraris et al. 1984, 4 analytical kinetic models): 500 tests, GP surrogate vs analytical method vs uniform random design, metrics A, SE, success, failure, inconclusive rates (pp. 5-7, Tables 1-3). Case 2 (Vanlier et al. ODE models): indiscriminable pair M1/M2 detected; removing M2 restores discrimination (p. 7, Table 4). Design time minutes vs days (p. 8).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Method plus open-source GPdoemd (MIT, GitHub, p. 2). Limits: depends on the Gaussian parameter approximation, with a mixture extension suggested (p. 8); accuracy traded for tractability (p. 8). Future: dual control, other biological models, links to implicit generative models (p. 8).

**Method links**: Method 3 (⚠ variant): the model is again the object of optimisation, but here a black-box mechanistic model is converted into a differentiable surrogate so that analytical design methods apply, rather than encoded exactly as constraints (pp. 4-5). Method 5 (✅ evidence, partial): failure and inconclusive rates are reported next to successes, with a random-design control (pp. 5-7). New-pattern candidate: "hybridise analytical and data-driven: surrogate only the broken ingredient" (pp. 2, 4, 8).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Report success, failure (correct model discarded) and inconclusive rates separately for a sequential decision procedure, plus a uniform-random baseline (pp. 5-7).
- When results look poor, test the indiscriminability hypothesis directly by removing the suspect rival and re-running (p. 7).
**Quotes**:
- "For model discrimination, we wish to be agnostic with regards to the software implementation or model type" (p. 3)
- "In practice, researchers faced with apparent model indiscriminability have to rethink their experimental set-up to lower measurement noise or add measured states." (p. 7)

### S034 · Bayesian Multiobjective Optimisation With Mixed Analytical and Black-Box Functions: Application to Tissue Engineering (IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering 66(3), 2019, DOI 10.1109/TBME.2018.2855404)
**Read level**: partial (abstract and introduction pp. 1-2, application §II-A pp. 2-3, method §III opening and EHVI pp. 5-6, results §IV pp. 7-11, discussion and conclusions pp. 11-12; the EMmI derivation pp. 6-7 and the GP/BO background pp. 3-5 skimmed; table contents are images and were not read beyond captions).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Simon Olofsson, Mohammad Mehrian, Roberto Calandra, Liesbet Geris, Marc Peter Deisenroth; Misener is corresponding author (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: journal extension of a conference paper on one acquisition function ([26], p. 2); part of the 2015-2019 biomedical BO line.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Optimise a perfusion bioreactor for bone neotissue: maximise scaffold filling (an expensive PDE simulation that runs on one machine only, no parallel evaluations) and minimise medium cost (analytical) (pp. 1-3). Entry: existing Bayesian MOO treats every objective as a black box, while here one objective is known in closed form (p. 2). Re-implementing the legacy model was costed at six person-months, so it is reused as is (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Model only the filling with a GP and keep the cost deterministic, so the joint density is a Dirac delta times a Gaussian; derive closed-form EHVI and EMmI acquisition functions for this mixed case (§III, pp. 5-7). Kept: standard GP-based BO; changed: the acquisition integrals collapse one dimension.
  - Transferable technique: when only some objectives are expensive black boxes, keep the cheap known ones exact inside the acquisition function instead of modelling them (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Two objectives, one deterministic; GP surrogate with smoothness-encoding kernel, which the discussion flags as a choice that matters (p. 12); working assumption that evaluating the black box dominates GP training and acquisition cost (p. 11); cost model c(r, a) ignoring the constant initial medium cost (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: no theorems. Device: decompose the region dominated by the current Pareto approximation into rectangles C_ij and integrate the improvement per rectangle in closed form (Eqs. 1-2, pp. 5-6).
- **D5 Experiments**: Reduced-order ODE model plus five two-objective test problems chosen for concave, convex, mixed and discontinuous frontiers (pp. 8-9); 12 genetic algorithms from Platypus at default parameters and random search; 10 initial + 50 iterations; 25 repeats with standard errors; metrics GD, MPFE and a log volume ratio (pp. 8-9); scaling study D up to 30 with 100 repeats, where on Fonseca-Fleming all methods are "equally poor" (pp. 9-10); full PDE run: 8 historical points + 14 EMmI points over about two months (pp. 10-11).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Algorithmic (closed-form mixed acquisition functions), applied (a cost-filling trade-off that suggests which culture regimes to test, p. 11), code on GitHub (p. 12). Limits written: when the surrogate is costlier than the black box, GAs and random search win in wall-clock time (p. 11); GPs scale poorly with dimension (p. 11).

**Method links**: Method 1 (✅ evidence): exploits the known analytical part of the problem rather than treating it as a black box (pp. 2, 5). Method 5 (⚠ variant): broad competitor set and repeated runs with standard errors, but competitors at default parameters (p. 9) and no strengthened baseline; the do-not-use regime is written into the discussion (p. 11).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Use a cheap reduced-order model of the expensive simulator to benchmark algorithms, then spend the expensive budget once with the chosen variant (pp. 7, 10).
- Put a logarithm on the hypervolume ratio so that differences near 100% are visible (p. 8).
**Quotes**:
- "We could replicate the existing model, but we estimate six person-months for recreating and validating the new code." (p. 2)
- "However, for problems where training the surrogate model and/or evaluating the posterior predictive distribution is more expensive than evaluating the black-box function, genetic algorithms and random search will be able to explore the variable space much quicker (in terms of wall clock time) than Bayesian optimisation, giving them an advantage." (p. 11)

### S041 · GPdoemd: A Python package for design of experiments for model discrimination (Computers & Chemical Engineering, 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.compchemeng.2019.03.010; arXiv 1810.02561)
**Read level**: partial (abstract and introduction pp. 1-3, the Jensen-Rényi criterion §3 pp. 9-12, software §5 pp. 18-22, results §6.1-6.2 opening and §6.3 pp. 22-25, 30-32, discussion and conclusions pp. 32-36; §2 background and §4 GP derivations pp. 3-18 skimmed; appendices not read).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Simon Olofsson, Lukas Hebing and Sebastian Niedenführ (Bayer AG), Marc Peter Deisenroth · **Place in trajectory**: journal and software extension of S075 (p. 3); an industrial (Bayer) coauthorship.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Rival mechanistic models, too little data; classical criteria need gradients; automatic differentiation fails on non-smooth models with switches or inner optimisation (p. 2); Monte Carlo design can take "days or weeks" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: (i) a new design criterion D_JR, the quadratic Jensen-Rényi divergence of the rival predictive distributions, chosen because the Shannon version has no closed form for Gaussians (pp. 9-10); it is agnostic to the model-weighting scheme (p. 10). (ii) The GP-surrogate method of S075, now with sparse GPs (pp. 3, 15). (iii) GPdoemd: seven modules (model type, parameter estimation, kernels, parameter covariance, marginalisation, design criterion, discrimination method), each with switchable options (Fig. 8, pp. 19-21), plus bundled case studies (Table 2, p. 19).
  - Transferable technique: when an entropy-based criterion is intractable, switch to an entropy family member (Rényi order 2) that has a closed form for your distribution class (pp. 9-10).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Gaussian noise with known or upper-bounded covariance (p. 3); Gaussian approximation of the parameter posterior (p. 21); fixed 99.9% "winning" thresholds in the tests, admitted to be high (pp. 34-35).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: no theorems. Devices: closed-form Gaussian-mixture quadratic Rényi entropy (Eqs. 12-15, pp. 9-10); qualitative comparison of criteria on constructed predictive distributions with constant, decreasing and increasing variance (Figs 3-5, pp. 10-12).
- **D5 Experiments**: Four case studies (ammonia synthesis, chemical kinetics, a new mixing study, biochemical networks), 100 random initial sets each; D_JR vs classical criteria vs uniform random design as a "sanity check" (pp. 22-25); GP-T1/GP-T2 vs analytical method, framed as "not significantly worse" (p. 25); biochemical networks re-run without the indiscriminable model (pp. 30-32).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Criterion, method extension and open-source package (pp. 1, 18). Limits written: GP scaling to large training sets (pp. 33, 35); no guideline for generating training data (p. 33); tests not fully representative of practice (p. 34). Future: other information criteria, global parameter estimation, batch (parallel) designs, state-space models (pp. 34-35).

**Method links**: Method 3 (⚠ variant): the "rival options become switches" toolkit design appears here in 2019 for design criteria and discrimination methods ("GPdoemd allows switching between these discrimination methods", p. 34; Fig. 8, p. 20), though the object is a set of mechanistic models, not a trained ML model. Method 5 (✅ evidence, partial): random-design control, failure and inconclusive rates, and a written scope paragraph (pp. 24, 34-35).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Ship a method with its benchmark case studies inside the package so others can compare against it directly (pp. 19, 36).
- When extending your own conference paper, state exactly which sections and tables repeat it and which are new (p. 3).
**Quotes**:
- "we do not attempt to provide general guidelines for how best to generate training data." (p. 33)
- "The Section 6 experiments are not in every way representative of model discrimination in a real setting." (p. 34)

### S088 · A multilevel analysis of the Lasserre hierarchy (European Journal of Operational Research 277(1), 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2019.02.016)
**Read level**: partial (abstract and introduction pp. 1-4, Lemma 3.1 p. 8, prolongation operators §4 pp. 10-15 with the statements of Theorems 4.1-4.3 and the proof of 4.3, numerical experiments §5 pp. 15-27, conclusions pp. 26-28; §3 background pp. 5-9 and the proofs of 4.1-4.2 skimmed).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Juan S. Campos (corresponding author), Panos Parpas · **Place in trajectory**: a moment-SOS side paper building on Campos and Parpas's multigrid SDP work (p. 2); a precursor in topic to the partial Lasserre work cited under Method 2.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Lasserre SDP relaxations grow exponentially with the order, and some POPs need order 2 or higher (p. 1); the information in the lower-order solution is usually discarded (p. 26). Entry: "The links between the solutions of relaxations at different hierarchies are studied here for the first time" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Prolongation operators from order w to w+1. Primal: project the first-order moments onto K and lift to all monomials (Eq. 12, pp. 10-11). Dual: embed the coarse matrices as leading principal submatrices padded with zeros (Eqs. 13-14, p. 12). Used as a warm start for an infeasible IPM (SDPT3), after lifting small eigenvalues to 1e-3 to move off the cone boundary (Algorithm 1, pp. 21-22).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 1.1: compact feasible set with tractable projection, needed in practice, not in theory (p. 3); uniqueness of the POP solution for Theorem 4.3 (p. 14), which the test problems lack, so objectives are perturbed by a small linear term (p. 18).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Lemma 3.1: the order-w data matrices are leading principal submatrices of the order-(w+1) matrices, and new monomials have zero blocks (p. 8). Theorem 4.1: primal prolongation of any point is feasible at the fine level (p. 11). Theorem 4.2: dual residuals carry over unchanged, and are zero on the new monomials (p. 12). Theorem 4.3: the duality gap of prolongated optimal points tends to 0 as w → ∞, by combining Lasserre's convergence with the identity that the dual objective is unchanged by padding (pp. 14-15).
- **D5 Experiments**: 200 quadratic {0,1}, 400 MAX-CUT and 200 integer-partition instances (n = 10-20) via SparsePOP; SDPT3, SeDuMi, Mosek at 1e-7; counts of POPs solved by each order and by projecting the coarse solution (Table 1, p. 20); infeasibility and gap of prolongated points (Table 2, p. 24); wins per solver (Table 3, p. 25) and time ratios under normalised stopping criteria (Table 4, pp. 26-27). Multilevel beats plain SDPT3 by 1.02-1.76×, but Mosek is up to 10× faster than multilevel on larger problems (p. 26).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Theoretical insight into the relation between levels plus a cheap warm start (pp. 3, 26). Limits written: bounded by SDP size limits and by IPMs' poor use of initial points (pp. 2-3); prolongated points are off the central path (p. 21); a preliminary SDPNAL+ test showed no gain (p. 21, footnote 2); Mosek offers no warm start, so it could not be tested (p. 26). Future: apply the idea to newer hierarchies such as BSOS (pp. 2, 28).

**Method links**: Method 2 (⚠ variant): works between levels of a hierarchy, but instead of building a family between two extremes it exploits the nesting of consecutive levels (Lemma 3.1, p. 8) to reuse a coarse solution (pp. 10-12). Method 5 (✅ evidence): stopping criteria normalised across solvers before timing (pp. 24-26) and the loss to Mosek written in, with "we do not claim" scoping (p. 26). New-pattern candidate: "coarse-to-fine reuse of a cheaper relaxation level's solution as a warm start" (pp. 10-12, 20-22).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Before solving the next relaxation level, check whether projecting the current level's solution already solves the original problem (Table 1, pp. 19-20).
- Compare warm-started and cold solvers on time ratios under one normalised stopping rule, not on each solver's own stopping criteria (pp. 24-26).
- Report the negative preliminary result with another base solver in a footnote with a candidate explanation (p. 21).
**Quotes**:
- "Unfortunately, exploiting initial points is a challenging and open problem for interior point algorithms, and this issue sets an upper bound on the performance of the prolongation operators developed in this paper." (p. 3)
- "we do not claim that the warm start method (which we have referred as the multilevel approach) is better than any other IPM or any other alternative to solve POPs" (p. 26)
