- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entered from an application with a policy hook (Climate Change Act 2008, waste heat, pp. 11-12), then narrowed to one function: LMTD is "often approximated and overlooked in practice" (abstract, p. 3), indeterminate (0/0) at ΔT1 = ΔT2 (p. 37), and usually handled by the Chen approximation or an ε-perturbation, which "introduce errors into the model" (p. 12; Huang et al. variants p. 38-39). The stated objective is a MILP approximation solved iteratively because MILP solvers "can guarantee global convergence" while MINLP solvers only give local optima (p. 12). Problem form: the Yee-Grossmann stage-wise nonconvex MINLP minimising total annual cost (pp. 36-37, 111).
  - Transferable technique: pick one "overlooked" nonlinear term that everyone approximates away, and make removing the approximation the whole project.
- **D2 Core idea**: Approximate the reciprocal m = ln(x/y)/(x − y) rather than LMTD itself, because the area constraint divides by LMTD and an approximation error in a denominator is "cascaded into the model by an additional layer" (p. 42); m is then proved strictly convex (Prop. 3.5, pp. 61-62), so tangent planes give a binary-free outer approximation, and the MILP is refined by adding tangents at the active exchangers' solution points and at their mirror images (Alg. 2, p. 80). Kept from the textbook toolkit: McCormick with nf4r partitioning for bilinear terms (pp. 72-75), a uniform PLF for the concave A^β cost (pp. 71-72). Changed: LMTD is neither replaced by the Chen formula nor perturbed by ε; it is exploited.
  - Transferable technique: before relaxing a nonconvex constraint, ask which algebraically equivalent function (here the reciprocal) is convex and sits in the "minimised" direction of the model; then the relaxation is a pure cutting-plane OA with no extra binaries (pp. 42, 69).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Domain x, y > 0 with box bounds [ΔTmin, T_i^in − T_j^in] per exchanger (p. 77); the model's structure pushes area, hence m, downward, so ≥ tangent constraints are tight at the optimum (pp. 42, 66); bilinear variables have known bounds (p. 41); the convergence argument assumes "a MILP solver that always gives us the global solution of any MILP" and a given objective tolerance ξ_TAC (p. 89); a priori partition counts Nq, NA, Nβ fixed for the whole run (pp. 76, 79). The report itself does not separate assumptions made for proof convenience from practical ones.
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) polar coordinates collapse the whole indeterminate set {x = y} to the single angle θ = π/4, giving a separable p(r)·q1(θ)/q2(θ) to which L'Hôpital and a product-of-limits theorem apply, so lim m = 1/c (pp. 44-48); (ii) the limits of m, ∇m, ∇²m define a piecewise C2 extension with m(t,t) = 1/t (Def. 3.1, pp. 48-49); (iii) a symmetry lemma m(x,y) = m(y,x) halves every later argument (Lemma 3.1, p. 50); (iv) directional derivative along (−1, 1) and monotonicity in each argument give box bounds attained on the diagonal, min 1/u and max 1/l (Prop. 3.1-3.3, pp. 51-56); (v) strict convexity by leading principal minors of the Hessian in w = x/y, with the substitution w = e^n turning the minors into functions of n (sinh/cosh) whose sign is settled by vanishing values at n = 0 and the sign of a higher derivative (Prop. 3.4, pp. 56-61), and a separate check on the diagonal (Prop. 3.5, pp. 61-62); (vi) equivalence of the nf4r constraints to McCormick on the active subinterval by direct substitution (p. 75); (vii) a counterexample sequence c + 1/i showing the tangent loop need not terminate finitely, motivating an ε-termination (p. 80); (viii) an error-cascade graph (Fig. 5.2, p. 82) and a convex-combination split of ξ_TAC across stream-to-stream, cold-utility and hot-utility groups to derive the partition counts N*_β, N*_A, N*_q (pp. 89-93). Caveats visible in the text: the statement of Prop. 3.5 prints m as (x − y)/ln(x/y), i.e. the LMTD expression, while the proof concerns the reciprocal (p. 61); the step for the OA tangents is informal ("In the worst case the algorithm will add tangents in different neighbourhoods but this reduces the overall error", p. 94). Results partly duplicate earlier proofs by Zavala-Río et al., acknowledged as "an alternative proof" derived before finding theirs (p. 41).
  - Transferable technique: to remove a removable singularity on a line, change coordinates so the line becomes a point in one coordinate, prove the limit there, and ship the C2 extension instead of an ε-shift (pp. 44-49).
  - Transferable technique: to prove a 2x2 Hessian definite for a function of a ratio, reduce the minors to univariate functions of w = x/y, substitute w = e^n, and argue by derivatives at n = 0 (pp. 56-61).
  - Transferable technique: for a fixed-precision MILP relaxation of a multi-term model, draw the error-propagation graph and split the objective tolerance across term groups to back out how many pieces each relaxation needs (pp. 82, 89-93).
- **D5 Experiments**: Gurobi 6.0.3 on one stated desktop machine (i7-4770, 16 GB, Ubuntu 14.04) (p. 97). Test set: three MINLPLib2 instances heatexch_gen1-3 from Escobar and Grossmann, chosen because best-known solutions and solver lower bounds (LINDO, BARON, SCIP on MINLP World) exist (Table 6.1, p. 97; data Table 6.2, pp. 98-99). Pre-study: two PLF and two OA variants of m on a McCormick-simplified model, bracketed against the global solutions; the two-triangle PLF is infeasible on Model 2 (Table 4.1, p. 66). Main runs: a single N = Nq = NA = Nβ swept over {1, 10, ..., 50}, ε = 1e-6; solution, iterations and time reported (Table 6.3, p. 100), objective-versus-iteration plots with the best-known solution dashed (Fig. 6.1, pp. 102-103). Findings: at most 10 iterations (p. 100); Model 3 takes 3807 s at N = 10 and 32224 s at N = 50 (p. 100); with N = 1 the bounds already exceed the solvers' lower bounds on Models 1 and 2 (120669.59 vs 100501.00; 604958.96 vs 585255.58) (pp. 97, 100-101); on Model 2 the "lower bound" exceeds the best-known solution, which the report puts "down to numerical error" (pp. 101, 104); on Model 3 it converges to the approximated model's optimum below the global one, blamed on equal-width partitions of the concave cost (p. 104). A heuristic Σ_k z_ijk ≤ 1 cuts time by 71% and 82% at N = 50 on Models 1 and 2 but not on Model 3, and is flagged as possibly excluding the global solution (pp. 104-105). No competitor solver was run on the same machine; no repeat runs.
  - Transferable technique: when an algorithm produces bounds, evaluate it on instances whose best-known solutions and published solver lower bounds are both available, and plot each run's objective by iteration against the best-known value (pp. 97, 102-103).
- **D6 Positioning**: Frames the field with Furman and Sahinidis's review (sequential versus simultaneous synthesis) and Escobar and Trierweiler's comparison favouring simultaneous MINLP models (pp. 37-38); criticises the Chen approximation and ε-shifts for introducing error while granting their convenience (pp. 38-39); adopts the Yee-Grossmann SYNHEAT model (pp. 36, 38) in the Escobar-Grossmann version and lists corrections to it found "when comparing with the GAMS specifications" (pp. 111, 116-118); credits Zavala-Río et al. for prior LMTD results (p. 41); takes nf4r from Gounaris, Misener and Floudas [20] with a presentation "similar to the presentation given by Misener, Thompson, and Floudas" (APOGEE) (p. 73). Not discussed: existing global MINLP solvers' own handling of LMTD beyond quoting their lower bounds (pp. 97, 107).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Student-report shape: a long textbook background chapter (pp. 15-31), then proofs written out in full algebra (pp. 56-62). Contributions as four bullets in §1.3 (pp. 12-13). The abstract overclaims ("The first of its kind.", p. 3) relative to what Ch. 6 shows (convergence to the approximated model's optimum, p. 104). Against that, the evaluation states plainly that "the algorithm fails to scale in the directions that we want it to scale" (p. 99), and the heuristic is labelled as such with its risk (p. 105). Figures carry the intuition before every proof (plots of m, p. 43; heat map, p. 45; PLF vs OA views, p. 64).
  - Transferable technique: put a plot of the troublesome function beside each property you are about to prove (symmetry, bounds, shape), and say which property each later modelling choice preserves (pp. 49, 68).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contributions: the reciprocal-of-LMTD argument, bounds and strict convexity of m and concavity of LMTD, a tangent-cut MILP algorithm with a convergence argument, a Python/Pyomo implementation (pp. 12-13). Limits stated: binaries from a priori partitioning drive run time and block scaling to realistic sizes such as a 41-stream ethylene plant (p. 99); equal-width partitions converge to a solution that is not the global one (p. 104); MILP re-solves lose the search tree (p. 107). Future work: adaptive refinement with error tolerances instead of partition counts, spatial branch and bound, and a convex (non-linearised) formulation for MINLP solvers using the convexity of m (pp. 107-108). Limits not stated: the convergence argument depends on a priori N* values and an exact MILP solver (pp. 89, 94), and the Model 2 bound anomaly is not resolved (p. 101).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information") ✅ evidence, student voice: plot the ugly function, see that it "looks convex", prove it, then exploit it with cuts (pp. 42, 56-62, 75-76); the talk title names "Special Structure Detection" (p. 13); the conclusion generalises "through rigorous analysis we can expose the structure of a model" (p. 108). This is the concrete case behind SKILL.md Method 1 step 2 ("Plot the ugly function before relaxing it").
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole solver) ✅ evidence, thin: PLF versus OA for m is decided by how each fits the other relaxations in the full model (all underestimators, so tightening moves the objective one way; no extra binaries; no re-formulation per iteration), not by approximation quality in isolation (pp. 68-69); tested on only three instances of a simplified model (p. 66).
- Method 2 (between formulations, parameter admitted) ⚠ variant: the partition count N interpolates from plain McCormick (N = 1) toward tighter relaxations and is swept with its time cost reported (pp. 99-100), but one N is used for all three relaxations, there is no rule for choosing it beyond the a priori error budget (pp. 89-94), and the parameter is not removed.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor) ✗ contradiction in part, student voice: an invalid bound on Model 2 (above the best-known feasible value) is put "down to numerical error" without re-runs (p. 101), against Method 5's stop rule that anomalies are re-run before blaming noise; no competitor runs on the same hardware (p. 97). In its favour, the published model was audited against the GAMS file and corrected equation by equation (pp. 111, 116-118), and losses are written in (p. 99).
- New-pattern candidate: reformulate-before-relaxing (approximate the reciprocal so errors do not cascade through a denominator) (p. 42).
- New-pattern candidate: repair an indeterminacy by a proved continuous extension instead of an ε-shift or a surrogate formula (pp. 12, 44-49).
- New-pattern candidate: audit and correct the published benchmark model before using it (pp. 111, 116-118).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Approximate the function whose error enters the model linearly (1/LMTD multiplies q; LMTD divides it), and check the model pushes that function in the direction where tangent cuts are tight (p. 42).
- Prove a removable singularity away with polar coordinates and L'Hôpital, then publish the C2 extension so solvers never see 0/0 (pp. 44-49).
- Seed an OA with tangents at the box corners on the diagonal so the cut model reproduces the function's exact bounds, and mirror every tangent point to keep the function's symmetry (pp. 76-78).
- Make all relaxations in a model point the same way (all under-estimators) so every refinement raises the bound monotonically; mixing an over-estimating PLF breaks this (p. 69).
- Show non-termination of a cut loop with a sequence converging to the optimum (c + 1/i), then switch to an ε-termination on the active terms only (pp. 80-81).
- Keep a change log when you correct a published model against its GAMS file (pp. 116-118).
