### S024 · Optimising heat exchanger network synthesis using convexity properties of the logarithmic mean temperature difference (Computers & Chemical Engineering 2016, doi:10.1016/j.compchemeng.2016.07.001)

**Read level**: full (preprint version "submitted to Elsevier July 2, 2016", p. 1; pp. 1-33 read in full; reference list pp. 33-36 skimmed). The online supplement with further proofs (Propositions 1, 5, Corollary 1, Section C.1) is cited but not in this file: not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Miten Mistry (first author; EPSRC CDT HiPEDS student, p. 33) · **Position in trajectory**: early Imperial Department of Computing period (affiliation p. 1, Royal Academy of Engineering Research Fellowship p. 33); the LMTD convexity result is the case the SKILL cites for "plot the ugly function before relaxing it" (Method 1 step 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry through an application with energy stakes (industry uses 73% of its energy on heating and cooling, p. 1) and a single troublesome function: LMTD "can cause numerical difficulties" (p. 2), and the usual remedies (Paterson or Chen approximations, bound-enforcing reformulations, perturbing by e.g. 10^-6) "add errors into the model and are not accounted for in the solution" (p. 2). The stated problem: approximate LMTD "while accounting for the error" (p. 2). A second gap: general convex-envelope methods (Tardella, Jach et al., Locatelli-Schoen, Khajavirad-Sahinidis) "cannot be directly applied to LMTD or its reciprocal" (p. 2). The model studied is the simultaneous SYNHEAT MINLP (Yee-Grossmann 1990) because simultaneous models can have better global optima than sequential ones (p. 2).
  - Transferable: open by naming the one function that causes numerical trouble, list the community's workarounds, and point out that each injects an error nobody accounts for; that makes "approximate with accounted error" the problem statement (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Work with the reciprocal RecLMTD = ln(x/y)/(x−y) instead of LMTD, because the area is A ∝ q · RecLMTD (Eq. (1), p. 9); after adding the limits on x = y, RecLMTD^β is strictly convex for β > 0 (Proposition 2, p. 11; Table 3, p. 11). This turns 8,267 of the 57,713 nonconvex terms of a 22×17×22 example into convex ones (pp. 7, 16). The MILP outer approximation then uses tangent cuts for RecLMTD (no binaries needed, p. 17), nf4r piecewise McCormick for bilinear terms and piecewise secants for the concave A^β (p. 16); Algorithm 1 re-solves the MILP, adding breakpoints/tangents where the relaxed solution "settled" (p. 19), and for the bivariate RecLMTD also adds a tangent at the reflection in y = x to model the function's symmetry (pp. 19-20, Fig. 6). A second variant distributes the exponent: A^β = q^β (h_i^-1 + h_j^-1)^β RecLMTD^β, so RecLMTD^β stays a single convex approximation and the concave estimate is taken before the McCormick product (Eq. (9), pp. 21-22). What did not change: the SYNHEAT model and standard relaxation pieces (McCormick, nf4r, secants) (p. 16).
  - Transferable: before relaxing a nasty bivariate function, check whether its reciprocal (or a power of it) is convex and appears in the model as a factor of a product; a variable substitution can turn a nonconvex constraint into an outer-approximable convex one (pp. 9, 16-17).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Domain R^2_+ (positive temperature differences), with the line x = y added by limits (pp. 9-11); in the model the RecLMTD domain is "a closed box with positive lower and upper bounds", which gives Lipschitz continuity for the convergence argument (p. 19); area exponent 0 < β ≤ 1 (p. 9, Table 1 p. 4); tangent cuts are valid only because the objective minimises a positive multiple of A^β, so RecLMTD is pushed down onto the topmost tangent (pp. 16-17); convergence "assuming a MILP solver that solves to global optimality" (p. 19). The algorithm needs only once-continuous differentiability, though C^2 is proved (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: (i) Limits on the indeterminate line x = y via a polar transformation that makes RecLMTD a product f(r)·g(θ), with l'Hôpital on g at π/4 (Proposition 1, pp. 9-10); the authors stress this replaces the series expansion of Zavala-Río et al. and is "shorter, simpler" (p. 3). (ii) Convexity of RecLMTD^β from the Hessian of a power composition (Eq. (4), p. 11): on the diagonal the limit Hessian is β/(12 x^{2+β}) times a 2×2 matrix whose leading minors are 5+3β and 24(1+β), so Sylvester's criterion settles it (p. 12). (iii) Off the diagonal, the first minor's factor p^[β] is linear and increasing in β, so it suffices to prove the extreme case β = −1 (p. 12); substitutions w = x/y and w = e^n reduce positivity to one-variable functions, proved by showing the function and its first derivatives vanish at 0 while a higher derivative is positive (r'' > 0, p. 13; h^(4) > 0 with h, h', h'', h''' = 0 at 0, p. 14). (iv) Convergence is "motivated" (p. 19) by a "funnel" of errors (Fig. 7, p. 21): each approximation's error tolerance propagates to TAC, finer partitions strictly reduce error, so a finite construction exists and the adaptive algorithm in the worst case reduces to it (pp. 19-21). (v) An order-of-operations error analysis: the McCormick maximum error |b|(x^u−x^l)(y^u−y^l)/4 (Androulakis et al., Eq. (10)) composed with the secant error of x^β (Eq. (11)), each with a fixed input error, gives bounds for both orders (pp. 30-32).
  - Transferable: to prove convexity of a bivariate function with a removable singularity on a line, change coordinates so the singular line becomes a single angle and the function separates (pp. 9-10); then prove the one-parameter family at its extreme parameter value using monotonicity in the parameter (p. 12).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Three small MINLPLib2 instances heatexch_gen1-3 (2/2/2, 5/1/2, 5/5/2 hot/cold streams/stages, p. 24; data Tables 5-7, p. 25). Pyomo + Gurobi 6.0.3, hardware and OS given (p. 23). The implemented algorithms drop line 4 of Algorithm 1 and so "do not necessarily converge to the global solution", because fresh solves discard previous knowledge (p. 23). Baselines are not re-run: the best primal value and best lower bound reported on MINLPLib2 by ANTIGONE, BARON, Couenne, LINDO, SCIP (and Alpha-ECP for one primal) are taken as reference (p. 24, Table 8 p. 28). Results: per-iteration TAC and cumulative time plots (Figs. 9-14, pp. 26-27); termination times 37 s to 29,964 s for the first algorithm and 22 s to 9,600 s for the second (Table 9, p. 28); "weak" variants without extra binaries reach lower bounds better than the reported solver bounds in at most 33 s (gap 23% vs 35% on Model 1; 2-5% vs 7% on Model 2, Tables 10-11, p. 29). For Model 3 they find a feasible solution ($64,138.16) below the library's reported global value $64,898.22, explained by the library instance perturbing LMTD (p. 24). Failure cases written in: Model 2 terminates slightly above the global value "from numerical error" (p. 28); Model 3 needs over 2 hours and a realistic problem "may not terminate" (p. 28). The error-bound comparison is numeric: second-algorithm bounds on average 24.2% lower (Table 12, pp. 31-32).
  - Transferable: when a benchmark library's instance perturbs a function to avoid numerical trouble, check the reported optimum against the unperturbed model; a feasible point below the "global" value exposes the perturbation error (p. 24).
- **D6 Positioning**: Presents its proofs as alternatives to Zavala-Río et al. (2005; C^1 and positivity) and Floudas-Ciric (1989; convexity of RecLMTD^β for 0 < β ≤ 1), and lists four reasons the new proofs are worth having: full shape for all β ≤ 1, a shorter proof, C^2 by the same transformation, validity on the whole of R^2_+ rather than half-planes (p. 3). Reinterprets prior heuristic success: the convexity result "justifies why" the Escobar-Grossmann (2010) reformulation works better with LMTD in the objective (p. 16). Credits that Najman and Mitsos (2016) used an early version (Mistry 2015) to exploit LMTD concavity in multivariate McCormick relaxations (p. 15), and points to where its linear approximation could be added to prior MILP approximations of HENS (Hasan et al., Nguyen et al.) as "a rigourous bound" (p. 17).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Numbered list of four reasons the alternative proofs matter (p. 3); a scale example counting nonconvex terms (57,713 for a 22×17×22 network, p. 7) and the 14% drop after reformulation, hedged at once: "This decrease does not change HENS complexity", only lowers the "(still exponential) complexity" (p. 16); a footnote "Proved in this manuscript" marks the one new property in the model description (p. 7). The theorem-to-intuition ratio leans on figures: heat map and slice of RecLMTD (Fig. 2, p. 10), tangent outer approximation (Fig. 3, p. 18), tightening diagrams (Figs. 5-6, p. 20), error funnels (Figs. 7-8, pp. 21-22). Convergence is stated as "motivated" rather than proved (p. 19).
  - Transferable: when an argument is a sketch, say so in the verb ("Algorithm convergence is motivated by showing that ...", p. 19) instead of calling it a theorem.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory (C^2, strict convexity of RecLMTD^β on R^2_+, full shape table) plus an algorithm (MILP outer approximation with adaptive refinement) and a bounding use: the method can "place a certificate on a MINLP solution" (p. 29). Limits are specific: the convergence-guaranteeing step was dropped in the implementation (p. 23); scalability fails because each iteration is a fresh MILP solve; the stated future direction is reusing "knowledge of a previous branching tree" (p. 28); the 2nd algorithm's speed-up on Model 2 "may be problem specific" (p. 28); only three small instances (p. 24).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information"): evidence ✅. The structure is an already-convex function hidden behind the LMTD form; the paper plots it (Fig. 2, p. 10), reads convexity from the plot ("Figure 2 suggests that RecLMTD is convex", p. 11), proves it (pp. 11-15) and exploits it with tangent cuts without binaries (p. 17). It also explains a prior heuristic's success from the structure (p. 16), matching SKILL Heuristic 6.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): variant ⚠. Competitors are not re-run; their MINLPLib2-reported bounds are the baseline (p. 24), on only three instances. But the auditor move of checking the reference answer is there: the library's "global" value for Model 3 is shown to rest on a perturbed LMTD (p. 24), and the paper's own numerical miss on Model 2 is reported (p. 28).
- Method 2 (between formulations): variant ⚠. Two variants (exponent outside vs distributed over the product) are compared, with the second preferred by an error-bound argument (pp. 29-32); not a parameterised family between two extremes.
- new-pattern candidate: "relaxation composition order as a design choice": applying the concave secant before the McCormick product rather than after gives provably smaller maximal error and better early bounds (Fig. 15, p. 30; Table 12, pp. 31-32).
- new-pattern candidate: "approximate with accounted error instead of perturbing": replace epsilon-perturbation of a singular function by an outer approximation whose error is tracked and refined (pp. 2, 24).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Polar-coordinate separation f(r)·g(θ) plus l'Hôpital to show a limit exists along a whole line of indeterminacy, and reuse the same transformation for gradient and Hessian limits (pp. 9-11).
- Prove positivity of a one-variable function via w = e^n: show the function and its first k derivatives vanish at 0 and the (k+1)-th derivative is positive elsewhere (pp. 13-14).
- For a bivariate convex function symmetric about y = x, add each new tangent at the incumbent and at its mirror image (Fig. 6, p. 20).
- Trace each approximation's error through the dependency graph to the objective ("funnel of errors") and use the levels to set graded termination tolerances (0.001 / 0.01 / 0.1, Figs. 7-8, pp. 21-23).
- Run a "weak" relaxation without added binaries as a cheap lower-bound certificate and compare its gap with the best bounds published for the instance (Tables 10-11, p. 29).

**Quotes**:
- "The convexity result in this manuscript justifies why the Escobar and Grossmann (2010) model works better with the LMTD term moved to the objective." (p. 16)
- "The solvers appear to be finding the global solutions however providing a certificate is not so easy as the lower bounds placed on the problems are not informative." (p. 33)

### S143 · In memoriam: Christodoulos Achilleus Floudas (SIAG/OPT Views and News 24(1) 2016, no DOI)

**Read level**: full (the essay pp. 12-14 read in full; its reference list [67]-[156], pp. 14-16, skimmed; the issue's other items, e.g. Scheinberg's article and the Borwein and Fletcher memorials, belong to other authors and were not read beyond the contents page, p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (single author, Department of Computing, Imperial College London, p. 14) · **Position in trajectory**: written in autumn 2016 (issue dated October 2016, p. 1), two years into her Imperial career; her own retrospective account of her PhD (GloMIQO, ANTIGONE) and of her advisor's research values. This is her voice about Floudas, so it is evidence of what she chooses to praise and remember, not a record of Floudas's own views.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The essay's thesis is stated up front: Floudas "fundamentally opened the field of deterministic global optimization", made it industrially relevant, and "pushed the tractability boundary of optimization theory, algorithms, and implementations using a multitude of applications" (p. 12). She scopes it to mathematical optimisation, "with a particular emphasis on deterministic global optimization", and says she will "mostly neglect" his process-design, chemistry and biology contributions (p. 12). Opening frame: "choosing an advisor is the most important decision a graduate student makes" (p. 12).
- **D2 Core idea**: The recurring idea she attributes to him is application-driven method development pushed to real scale: GOP for biconvex problems applied to pooling and phase equilibrium (p. 12); αBB arising from Lennard-Jones microclusters and generalised to functions with Lipschitz gradient, with the underestimator formula written out (pp. 12-13); αBB as a general MINLP solver used as his "major vehicle" across a dozen application classes (p. 13); de novo peptide design via a QAP connection (p. 13); energy systems at the "tractability boundary" (p. 13); pooling structure as a bipartite bilinear graph (p. 13).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: n/a (an obituary essay; no model of her own).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: n/a (no proofs; the αBB underestimator and its eigenvalue condition are quoted as background, p. 13).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a. The only scale numbers concern a Floudas-group model she objected to globally optimising: 15,439 continuous variables, 30 binaries, 15,406 equalities, 230 inequalities, 335 nonconvex terms (p. 13).
- **D6 Positioning**: Places the lineage explicitly: Floudas's PhD with Grossmann at CMU on heat exchanger network synthesis by MILP-generated superstructures and flexibility analysis, linked to modern robust optimisation (p. 12); her own work as an extension of Meyer-Floudas edge-concave facets to multiterm bilinear underestimators (p. 13); GloMIQO and ANTIGONE as incorporating αBB, generalised geometric programming, edge-concave underestimators and process-network transformations from his group (p. 14). She credits colleagues by name (Maranas, Liu, Adjiman, Androulakis, Meyer, Baliban, Elia, Boukouvala, Hasan, Pardalos, Pistikopoulos; pp. 12-14) and disclaims involvement where she had none ("although I had nothing to do with it", p. 14). She also cites Dey-Gupte's analysis of MILP techniques for pooling as the work that "further unpacked" the NLP-MIP link (p. 13).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: A personal-anecdote structure: her father's advice (p. 12); her objection to globally optimising a 15,439-variable model and Floudas's insistence (p. 13); his reply email of 12 March 2011, sent after the Tōhoku earthquake, quoted in full (p. 13); the 2008 exchange where he required a promise to "write and publicly release a global MINLP solver" and told her "you will not roll your eyes at your advisor!" (p. 14). Each anecdote carries a research value (scale, enthusiasm, belief in students, software release). Self-deprecation is used to credit the advisor ("Whatever mistakes I made as a Ph.D. student", p. 12; "He was right in the end", p. 14).
  - Transferable: in a tribute or retrospective, attach each research value to one dated, concrete episode rather than an adjective list (pp. 13-14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: a scholarly memorial with a bibliography of 90 items, [67]-[156] (pp. 14-16). The open question she carries forward is hers to answer: Floudas pushed "Why did these piecewise approximations work so well? How could we eliminate the computational dependence on parameters?", to which work with Baltean-Lugojan gives "a first answer" after eight years (p. 13). No limitations section (genre).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 (between formulations; remove the parameter): evidence ✅. The advisor's question about eliminating "the computational dependence on parameters" of piecewise-linear pooling relaxations is the origin she names for the parameter-skepticism and the Baltean-Lugojan parametric pooling work (p. 13; [127] p. 16).
- Method 1 (structure detection): evidence ✅. Pooling's structure described as a bipartite graph of bilinear terms (p. 13); GloMIQO/ANTIGONE incorporate "automatic transformations for process networks problems" (p. 14).
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): weak evidence ✅. The Floudas-Pardalos Handbook of Test Problems as "a standard test set for any global optimization solver" is singled out as a contribution to the field (p. 14), and the scale insistence (p. 13) matches the large-test-set habit; no benchmarking practice of her own is described.
- Research Taste mark 2 ("tested on real, application-sized instances"): this essay is the source of the SKILL quote "never interested in toy problems or proofs of concept" (p. 13). Taste mark 5 (software release): the PhD-stage promise to "publicly release a global MINLP solver" leading to GloMIQO and ANTIGONE (p. 14).
- new-pattern candidate: "software release as a PhD commitment": a public solver written into the early-stage PhD assessment (p. 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Keep a mentor's or reviewer's unanswered "why does it work?" question on file and report it as answered when a later structural result does so (p. 13).
- Test a new relaxation technique on a real application model with tens of thousands of variables before calling it relevant, as Floudas required for piecewise-linear bilinear underestimators (p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "But Chris was never interested in toy problems or proofs of concept." (p. 13)
- "Why did these piecewise approximations work so well? How could we eliminate the computational dependence on parameters?" (p. 13)

### S120 · Symmetry Detection for Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Programs Using Binary Layered Graphs (arXiv preprint 2017, arXiv:1712.05222)

**Read level**: full (arXiv v4 "20 Jan 2019", p. 1; pp. 1-19 read in full; bibliography pp. 20-21 skimmed).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Georgia Kouyialis (first author, PhD student, Imperial Department of Computing, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the symmetry-detection line (with the FOCAPO/CPC 2017 HENS symmetry paper [22], p. 20); the text still speaks of "The work introduced in this thesis" (p. 4), so it is close to thesis material. Per the work-list note, Spiral records v2 under another title and S091 (Processes 2019) has a similar title; not linked here. The SKILL's "Theorem 1,2 are incorrect" v3 comment is not in this v4 file; in v4 Theorems 1 and 2 are results attributed to Ramani-Markov and Liberti (p. 9).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Symmetry causes many equivalent optima and large B&B trees in nonconvex optimisation (abstract, p. 1; p. 5), and "Dealing with symmetry requires detecting and classifying it first" (abstract, p. 1). Entry through a circle-packing prototype: two circles in a unit square have four equivalent optimal placements related by variable permutations (Fig. 2, p. 5). Gap claimed: problem-specific symmetry handling "cannot be generalised to other problems" (p. 4), and detection is "the first phase of symmetry handling techniques", on which nauty-based steps depend (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Represent a QCQP by matrices and turn them into binary layered graphs for nauty: Method 1 is a tensor A^Q of (n+1)×(n+1)×(m+1) coefficient matrices, one per equation, so bilinear relations sit between variable rows/columns (p. 10); Method 2 is the 2-D coefficient matrix of the McCormick/RLT-linearised LQP (pp. 10-11). Coefficients are mapped to integer labels and encoded in binary across L = ⌈log2(ℓ+1)⌉ layers, so many distinct coefficients need only a logarithmic number of layers ("60 different coefficients in a graph with 6 layers", p. 17); multiplication becomes edges and loops instead of operator nodes (pp. 18-19). Changed relative to Liberti's DAGs: no expression-tree operator nodes; unchanged: nauty and colour-preserving automorphisms (pp. 9, 14, 17).
  - Transferable: when a graph-automorphism tool accepts only vertex colours, encode edge labels as binary digits over layers of vertex copies, giving log-many layers (pp. 14-15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: QCQP with box-bounded continuous variables (Definition 1, p. 1); symmetry defined over the feasible set (Definition 3, p. 5), but because that set is impractical to derive, the paper targets the formulation group (Definition 4, p. 7), a subgroup of the symmetry group by Liberti (p. 6). Matrix formulation groups defined in Definitions 5-6 (pp. 11-12). Loose statements: "Statement 1. Any quadratic program (QCQP) can be linearised by using the reformulation linearisation technique" (p. 2), although LQP is a relaxation; Definition 2 writes LQP as a max while QCQP is a min (pp. 1-2).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: No new theorem. The analytic content is counterexamples: Example 1 (integrality relaxed, the symmetry group of the original is not a subgroup of the relaxation's, p. 6), Example 2 (the converse, p. 6), Example 3 (a QP with trivial group whose McCormick linearisation gains symmetries, pp. 6-7), Example 5 (RLT applied to x1^2 only destroys the (x1, x2) symmetry; applied to both, the symmetry is kept, pp. 8-9). Size counting compares the two matrix methods: worst case (1+n)(1+n/2)(1+m+2n^2+2n) entries vs (1+n)(1+n)(1+m), Method 1 smaller except for dense cases with m > 3n^2+6n+3 (p. 11). Correctness rests on cited results (Ramani-Markov, Liberti, McKay-Piperno, Margot; pp. 9, 12, 14).
  - Transferable: before claiming a property of a problem carries over to its relaxation, test both inclusion directions on 2-3 variable examples (pp. 6-7).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: One worked example (QP1, 4 variables, 3 constraints) run through nauty's dreadnaut for both graph types, recovering the formulation group (x1 x4)(x2 x3) for QP1 and (x1 x4)(x2 x3)(X11 X44) for its linearisation (pp. 16-17, Figs. 12-13, p. 18). No test set, no timing, no comparison runs. The paper concedes that for this example the DAG representation is smaller and "generates the same formulation group" (p. 17).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Margot's ILP symmetry definition and Liberti's MINLP extension and DAG-based formulation-group detection (pp. 4, 7-9); surveys static (symmetry-breaking constraints, orbitopes) and dynamic (isomorphism pruning, orbital branching, orbital shrinking) handling (p. 4); positions itself only on the detection step (p. 4). The comparison is even-handed: orbital branching and DAGs are "easy to implement" and DAGs "can capture the structure of any class of mathematical programming problem"; BLG is an "alternative method that may be useful" with many distinct coefficients (p. 17).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Hedged throughout ("may capture", "may reduce", "may be useful", pp. 17-19). Long preliminaries (McCormick, group theory, notation table) take pp. 1-3 before the literature review. Draft-quality signs remain in v4: "Ussing" in the even-page running head (e.g. p. 2), "this thesis" (p. 4), "LPQ1" vs "LQP1" (p. 17). The conclusion is three sentences (p. 19).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: data structures and a graph encoding for formulation-symmetry detection in QCQP, demonstrated on one example (pp. 10-17). Limits stated by the authors: BLG may identify problems "that have the exact same symmetric structure but different formulation", it does not keep the exact formulation that DAGs keep, and the focus is detection, "not solving the problem itself" (p. 19). No computational validation and no future directions beyond the conclusion's general claim (p. 19).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information"): evidence ✅, with an instrument gap. Symmetry is the structure, a binary layered graph the detector (pp. 14-17), as SKILL step 3 lists ("a binary layered graph"); but step 5 (validate against a labelled library) is absent here: one example only (pp. 16-17). Compare SUSPECT's 1527-problem validation in the SKILL.
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole solver): contradiction ✗ for this preprint. The detector is not tested inside any B&B, and the paper explicitly limits itself to detection (p. 19); SKILL's stop rule "no full-algorithm result → no claim from the root alone" is not applied.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): not practised in this preprint (no runs), so no link beyond this note.
- new-pattern candidate: "check structure survives relaxation": symmetry of a problem and of its RLT/McCormick relaxation can differ in both directions, and partial relaxation can destroy symmetry; relax all symmetric terms uniformly (pp. 6-9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before using symmetry found in a relaxation to prune the original problem (or vice versa), construct small examples for both inclusion directions; Examples 1-3 give ready templates (pp. 6-7).
- When adding RLT/McCormick auxiliaries, generate them for every member of an orbit (x1^2 and x2^2 together) so the relaxation keeps the formulation's symmetry (Example 5, pp. 8-9).
- Compare two encodings of the same problem by counting worst-case matrix entries and state the crossover density (p. 11).

**Quotes**:
- "symmetry in the original problem does not imply the same symmetry in the relaxed problem and vice versa." (p. 6)
- "BLG may be associated with problems that have the exact same symmetric structure but different formulation." (p. 19)
