# Batch c14 · Ruth Misener · core

### S081 · BARK: A fully Bayesian tree kernel for black-box optimization (ICML 2025, PMLR 267; arXiv:2503.05574)

**Read level**: full (arXiv v2, pp. 1-28: main text pp. 1-9, references pp. 9-13 skimmed, appendices A-J pp. 14-28 read, including the posterior-sampling derivations and the regret-bound appendix).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Toby Boyne (first author, correspondence), Jose Pablo Folch (Imperial), Robert Matthew Lee and Behrang Shafei (BASF SE), Ruth Misener last author (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the fifth step of the trees → ENTMOOT → OMLT → tree-kernel BO line; it replaces the group's own 2022 two-step tree-kernel GP (LeafGP, Thebelt et al. 2022a) with a fully Bayesian one, keeping the MIP acquisition optimiser (pp. 1-2, 6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Tree models handle mixed inputs and non-stationarity but "lack explicit uncertainty quantification" for BO (p. 1). Existing tree kernels (Balog et al. 2016; the group's Thebelt et al. 2022a) use a two-step fit: a tree model is fitted first and the GP trained on the induced kernel; the paper calls this heuristic "not principled and hence potentially sub-optimal" (p. 1). Second gap: one-step methods keep a single MAP tree, a point estimate of an effectively infinite-dimensional hyperparameter that tends to overfit (p. 4). Entry: borrow BART's prior on tree structure and sample the kernel by MCMC (pp. 1, 4).
  - Transferable: frame your entry as "the previous method (including our own) fits component A with an objective different from component B's" and fix the mismatch by joint inference (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Read BART through its implicit GP: the expectation over leaf values of the BART posterior equals the forest kernel (Eq. 4, p. 3), so BARK samples tree structures by Metropolis-Hastings but marginalises leaf values, each sample being a GP rather than a single function (pp. 4-5). Changes vs BART: the leaf-variance tuning parameter k is removed (outputs standardised, σ0² = 1), the GP marginal likelihood replaces the leaf-conditional likelihood, splits are sampled uniformly over the domain region reaching the node instead of at data points, categorical splits over the power set of categories, noise sampled through a softplus random walk (p. 5). Unchanged: BART's depth prior, noise prior, grow/prune/change proposals (pp. 4-5). For BO, the integrated UCB over 16 kernel samples is optimised globally as a MIP over the tree encodings (Thebelt et al. 2022a; Mišić 2020) because the piecewise-constant prior has zero gradient almost everywhere (p. 6); uniform splits restore uncertainty growing away from data and avoid split clustering at exploited optima (p. 6, Fig. 3).
  - Transferable: when a surrogate is piecewise constant (trees), give up gradient-based acquisition optimisation and encode the surrogate as mixed-integer constraints; the gain is mixed spaces, nonlinear constraints and (within the gap) global optimality (pp. 6, 26).
  - Transferable: if a prior concentrates where the data are dense, move the proposal distribution to the domain, not the data, when the downstream task (BO) needs uncertainty far from the data (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Gaussian observation noise with BART's inverse-gamma prior, (ν, q) = (3, 0.9); m = 50 trees; depth prior (α, β) = (0.95, 2); σ0² = 1 on standardised outputs (pp. 4-5, Table 4 p. 16). The prior functions are discontinuous and non-stationary, which "violates the typical assumptions required for regret bounds" (p. 6). Sampling is approximate: 4 chains, 1000 burn-in, thinning 100, 16 kernel samples, burn-in only in the first BO iteration because one new point barely moves the posterior (p. 16). The acquisition MIP is solved to a 10% gap with a 100 s limit (pp. 26-27), so "global" means within that gap.
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: (i) Low-rank update: a tree proposal changes the kernel by two rank-L_t terms, so Sherman-Morrison/Woodbury and the matrix determinant lemma give O(N²L_t) likelihood updates instead of O(N³) (p. 5). (ii) Metropolis-Hastings ratio bookkeeping: for grow/prune the split-rule probability cancels between transition and prior ratios, "the acceptance probability is independent of the choice of splitting rule distribution" as long as the transition kernel uses the prior's split distribution (p. 22); Jacobian of the softplus-transformed noise proposal (pp. 22-23). (iii) The integrated UCB is convex in μ and σ, and the variance equality is relaxed to an inequality to give a second-order-cone constraint (pp. 6, 28). (iv) Appendix I is a reported failed proof: Srinivas-style bounds need Lipschitz/RKHS smoothness that fails at split discontinuities; bandit and combinatorial semi-bandit routes give bounds that are "far too loose" (pp. 23-24); the Laplace-kernel limit (a short theorem with proof under Poisson depth and independent splits, p. 24) does not hold for BART/BARK, and a "chopping process" derivation with the true depth prior shows the kernel diverges from Laplace as β grows (pp. 25-26, Fig. 14 p. 27).
  - Transferable: when a regret bound will not go through, publish the attempted routes and the computation showing why the standard approximation fails, rather than an unconditional bound under assumptions the method violates (pp. 23-26).
  - Transferable: in a sequential MCMC over ensemble members, express each move as a low-rank kernel update and reuse the previous inverse and log-determinant (p. 5).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Regression sanity check against BART on four UCI sets (NLPD, MSE; BARK equal or better except MSE on Auto MPG and Student, Table 1 p. 8) plus MCMC autocorrelation to justify thinning (Fig. 4, p. 7). BO: four mixed-space synthetic and four applied benchmarks (SVRBench, XGBoostMNIST, CCOBench, PestControl), min(2D, 30) initial points, 100 iterations, median and interquartile range over 20 runs (pp. 7-8, 17). Baselines: BART evaluated on a Sobol grid whose size "was chosen to match the wall clock time of the BARK optimization" (p. 17), GP-RBF with Hvarfner et al. priors in BoFire/BoTorch with alternating and (where possible) enumeration optimisers, LeafGP, SMAC, Entmoot, and an internal ablation BARKPrior that skips posterior MCMC (pp. 6-7, 16-17). Losses reported: DiscreteRosenbrock "shows a failure case of BARK" (p. 7); GP-RBF best on XGBoostMNIST when categories can be enumerated (p. 8); smooth continuous functions favour GP-RBF (p. 18). A finite-candidate MAX-phase materials benchmark ablates acquisition optimisation and shows BARK ≈ BART, both ahead of GP-RBF (p. 8, Fig. 7 p. 9). Wall time reported as the main limitation: about 50 s fitting plus up to 100 s acquisition optimisation (p. 9, Figs. 8 and 11). Hyperparameter-sensitivity grids for (α, β) and for chains × samples (pp. 20, 22-23). Hardware stated (AMD EPYC 7742, 16 GB per core, p. 17).
  - Transferable: when a baseline cannot use your optimiser, size its search budget to match your wall-clock time and say so (p. 17).
  - Transferable: add a "prior-only" ablation of your own method (BARKPrior) to separate the gain of the optimiser from the gain of the inference (p. 8).
- **D6 Positioning**: Continues and corrects the group's own LeafGP (Thebelt et al. 2022a) and Entmoot (Thebelt et al. 2021), using Mišić (2020) and Thebelt et al. (2022a) for the MIP encoding (pp. 2, 6). Builds on BART (Chipman et al. 2010) and Linero (2017), and notes that Lei et al. (2021) use BART for BO without exploiting the implicit GP (p. 2). A table compares 14 BO methods by surrogate, acquisition optimiser, GP, trees/partitions and mixed domain (Table 2, p. 15). BoFire (Dürholt et al. 2024, whose author list includes Boyne, Misener, Lee and Shafei) supplies GP-RBF and Entmoot implementations (p. 17, refs p. 10).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Three bulleted contributions after a summary figure (p. 2); a section roadmap (p. 2); the method is explained by "differences from the BART model" term by term through the acceptance probability (pp. 4-5). Weaknesses are written in the main text: a named failure case (p. 7) and a subsection opening "BARK's most significant limitation is its wall-time performance" (p. 9). Appendix B adds the hedge that tree-kernel properties "are not necessarily 'better'" (p. 14). The impact statement is the generic ICML template (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution is algorithmic plus software (code on GitHub, p. 6). Limits are specific: wall time (p. 9); O(N³) training cost growing to 951.91 s per 1000 samples at n = 400, against 20.81 s for BART (Table 8, p. 19); no regret bound (Appendix I); more kernel samples make the MIP harder (pp. 16, 20). Do-not-use regime stated: for cheap or fast evaluations "we recommend alternate methods" (p. 9, repeated p. 20). No future directions beyond these limits.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 (the partner's trained model is the problem): evidence ✅. Step 8 in practice: the tree-kernel surrogate is written as binary constraints and the integrated UCB is optimised globally as a MIP with an SOC constraint (pp. 6, 26-28); BASF co-authors and BASF funding (pp. 1, 9).
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole; re-grade your own work): evidence ✅. Re-grades the group's 2022 LeafGP as "not principled and hence potentially sub-optimal" (p. 1) and shows BARK beats LeafGP across problems (p. 8); BARKPrior and the MAX benchmark split the contributions of the optimiser and the inference (p. 8).
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): variant ⚠. BO-community form: 20 seeds with median/IQR, BART given a wall-clock-matched grid, failure case and losses to GP-RBF reported, hardware and versions given (pp. 7-9, 17). No independent re-check of returned points (not applicable to BO), and the MIP is stopped at a 10% gap (p. 26).
- Method 2 (parameter hygiene part, steps 4-7): variant ⚠. BART's k parameter is removed (p. 5); the remaining defaults are shown to be "largely insensitive" by an (α, β) grid (p. 20); the 16-sample choice is labelled a compromise (p. 20). There is no two-extreme family here.
- Heuristic 7 (write the do-not-use conditions next to the method): evidence ✅ (p. 9, p. 20).
- Heuristic 4 (prove what you can, label the rest experimental): evidence ✅. Appendix I says the regret analysis failed and why (pp. 23-26).
- new-pattern candidate: "publish the failed proof route": an appendix that records which standard regret arguments were tried and why they fail (pp. 23-26).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn a sampler over deterministic functions (BART) into a sampler over GPs by marginalising the leaf values analytically, so that every MCMC sample yields a mean and a variance usable by acquisition functions (pp. 3, 5).
- Use low-rank Woodbury and determinant-lemma updates for per-tree MCMC proposals in a forest kernel (p. 5).
- Optimise an integrated (sample-averaged) UCB over tree kernels as one MIP, relaxing the variance definition to a second-order-cone inequality (p. 28).
- Match a non-optimisable baseline's grid size to your method's wall-clock time (p. 17).
- State the evaluation-cost regime in which the method pays off, in minutes or money, beside the timing plot (p. 9).

**Quotes**:
- "While this heuristic gives good results in a variety of benchmarks, it is not principled and hence potentially sub-optimal." (p. 1)
- "We therefore recommend BARK for BO settings where the objective is expensive to evaluate, taking at least several minutes, or having a large associated financial cost." (p. 9)

### S082 · Transfer learning Bayesian optimization for competitor DNA molecule design for use in diagnostic assays (Biotechnology and Bioengineering 122(1) 2025, 10.1002/bit.28854; arXiv:2402.17704)

**Read level**: full (arXiv v2, pp. 1-36: main text pp. 1-16, references pp. 17-22 skimmed, appendices A-I pp. 23-36 read; nomenclature tables pp. 23-25 skimmed). Title check: the arXiv title differs in wording ("Transfer Learning Bayesian Optimization to Design Competitor DNA Molecules for Use in Diagnostic Assays", p. 1) but authors, topic and DOI match; Misener is among the authors (p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Ruby Sedgwick (first author), John P. Goertz, Molly M. Stevens (bioengineering), Mark van der Wilk (p. 1). Contributions statement: "R.M. and M.v.W. supervised the project, specifically giving guidance on the machine learning aspects" (p. 35); R.S. wrote code and manuscript, J.G. ran the lab experiments (p. 35) · **Position in trajectory**: application paper of the ML-for-experiments line, outside chemical engineering, tied to the Competitive Amplification Networks diagnostic (Goertz et al. 2023, whose author list includes Misener, p. 18).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Many similar biological sequences must be optimised, each with expensive lab runs; optimising each "from scratch discards useful information from previous tasks" (p. 2). Concrete case: competitor DNA molecules for a competitive-PCR diagnostic, each needing a target amplification rate and a drift below a threshold (p. 2). The paper states a belief that the relationship between competitors "may be non-linear" (p. 2), which motivates a non-linear transfer model.
  - Transferable: enter a BO application through the many-similar-tasks structure (one surface per sequence) rather than through a single black box (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Use the latent variable multi-output GP (LVMOGP, Dai et al. 2017) as the BO surrogate: each competitor gets a latent position (a distribution, not a point estimate), and distance in latent space sets the correlation between response surfaces (p. 5). Compared against AvgGP (pool everything), MOGP (share hyperparameters only) and LMC (linear coregionalisation) (pp. 4-6). Objective: target-vector expected improvement (Uhrenholt and Jensen 2019) for hitting a target rate (pp. 6-7), multiplied by the probability of feasibility for the drift threshold (p. 7). The combinatorial sequence design is reduced to two continuous inputs, number of base pairs and GC content, with NUPACK generating sequences for each pair (pp. 8-9).
  - Transferable: before BO, reduce a combinatorial design (a DNA sequence) to a few physically meaningful continuous descriptors and let a separate design tool realise each descriptor choice (pp. 8-9).
  - Transferable: for "hit a target, keep a nuisance below a threshold", combine a target-distance EI with a probability-of-feasibility factor (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Gaussian noise (p. 2); squared-exponential kernel and zero mean chosen because the function was believed smooth and no trend was known (p. 27); inputs and outputs standardised (p. 27); type-II maximum likelihood with random restarts and several initialisation schemes, with an explicit note that at low data this can overfit (pp. 27-29); latent dimension/rank 10 for cross-validation and 2 for BO (pp. 11-12, 29-31). Nesting: LMC recovers MOGP (Q = 1, B = I) and LVMOGP with a linear latent kernel recovers LMC, so negative transfer should vanish "only ... for large data sets" (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: No theorems. Model nesting is argued informally (p. 5). The LVMOGP variational lower bound is written out (Eq. 21, p. 26) and the latent-dimension "switch-off" via ARD lengthscales is used to explain why the LVMOGP is insensitive to latent dimension (p. 29). Mostly n/a.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: (i) Synthetic: three designed cases, uncorrelated (a negative-transfer check), linearly correlated, horizontally offset sigmoids (where LMC is expected to fail), 2 observed + 10 new surfaces, mean of 5 datasets (pp. 9-10). (ii) Cross-validation on 34 competitors, 592 points at 327 unique inputs, 70 repeats per training fraction; LVMOGP best on RMSE and NLPD for rate and drift, LMC poor NLPD (pp. 8, 10-11). (iii) Retrospective BO from a candidate set because a closed loop "was infeasible" for cost (p. 11); two scenarios (learning many; one at a time) × two first-point rules (centre point as a fair control; model's choice) (pp. 11-12); cumulative regret over 24 seeds (Fig. 6, p. 12). LVMOGP reaches the best point first 808 times vs 457/498/484 (p. 13); 36 vs 49 experiments for 16 competitors (rate only) and 37 vs 57 with the drift penalty (p. 13); the tolerance 0.05 is set to the lab measurement uncertainty (p. 13). Weak spots reported: penalised case gains smaller; LVMOGP "barely outperforms" AvgGP in one scenario; MOGP/LMC/LVMOGP miss the best point of one competitor because they overestimate drift (p. 13). Sensitivity to the choice of initial surfaces is checked (pp. 34-36). Observed inconsistencies in this version: Fig. 6 says 24 seeds (p. 12) while Tables 2-3 say 25 (p. 32); Appendix G says failed penalised runs were discarded from the averages (p. 31) whereas the Table 7 caption says they were set to the budget (p. 34).
  - Transferable: when closed-loop experiments are unaffordable, run retrospective BO restricted to the already-measured candidate set, and say so up front (p. 11).
  - Transferable: control for the advantage of models that can choose the first point by also running a fixed common first point (the design centre) for all models (p. 12).
  - Transferable: include a synthetic "no correlation" case purely to measure negative transfer (p. 9).
- **D6 Positioning**: Against coregionalisation, which assumes surfaces are linear combinations of a few latent functions (p. 4), and against latent-variable BO models that use point estimates of the latent variables (Zhang et al. 2020; Hutter et al. 2021), which "can cause poor uncertainty estimates" (p. 5). Situated in a long list of biological BO applications (pp. 1-2) and design-build-test-learn automation (p. 16).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Workflow figure first (Fig. 1, p. 3); model mechanics explained with a toy figure (Fig. 2, p. 6); results organised as cross-validation → single-objective BO → penalised BO (p. 10). Hedged: "we believe" (pp. 2, 16), "This may be due to" (p. 13). Discussion ends with generalisation claims to other bio-design tasks (pp. 15-16). The text is the first author's (p. 35).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Application/workflow contribution with released code split into an application-agnostic repository and a use-case repository (p. 31); raw data only "on request" (p. 31). Limits: seed variance from hard hyperparameter optimisation, worse for LVMOGP (p. 15); poor scaling to high-dimensional inputs (p. 16); retrospective rather than closed-loop validation (p. 11). Future: better hyperparameter optimisation (p. 16), integration into automated design-build-test pipelines (p. 16), other Bayesian function priors such as deep GPs or BNNs (p. 16).

**Method links**:
- Heuristic 1 (choose by broken assumption): evidence ✅. Standard BO assumes one task optimised from scratch; here many similar tasks, expensive experiments and an a priori unknown similarity collide with that assumption (pp. 2, 16).
- Heuristic 3 (calibrate; add weak controls): evidence ✅. AvgGP and MOGP serve as pooled and no-transfer controls; an uncorrelated synthetic case is built to show negative transfer (pp. 5, 9).
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole): variant ⚠. The surrogate is judged first by cross-validation and then inside the full BO loop with two scenarios and two start rules; the paper notes that the model ranking changes with the start rule (pp. 10-13).
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor): variant ⚠, partly kept. Retrospective design is stated openly and the tolerance is tied to measurement error (pp. 11, 13), but the seed counts and the averaging rule for failed runs are inconsistent between sections (pp. 12, 31-34).
- Heuristic 9 (release per paper): evidence ✅. Two GitHub repositories, one generic and one specific to the use case (p. 31).
- new-pattern candidate: "retrospective pool-based BO on an existing experimental dataset as validation when the closed loop is unaffordable" (p. 11); compare S081's finite-candidate MAX benchmark (S081 p. 8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace a combinatorial biological design space with two continuous descriptors (length, GC content) plus a sequence-design tool (pp. 8-9).
- Evaluate transfer-learning surrogates on three designed synthetic regimes: no correlation, linear correlation, nonlinear (offset) similarity (p. 9).
- Use a common centre first point as the fair-start control and the model's own choice as the realistic arm (p. 12).
- Report the number of experiments to reach the best point within the lab's measurement tolerance, translated into experiments saved (p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "Rather than tackling the difficult combinatorial problem of optimizing the sequence directly, we reduce the problem to two key input variables" (p. 8)
- "However, due to the cost of experiments, this was infeasible. Instead, we performed retrospective Bayesian optimization using the existing competitive DNA amplification dataset." (p. 11)

### S086 · System-aware neural ODE processes for few-shot Bayesian optimization (Transactions on Machine Learning Research 2025; arXiv:2406.02352)

**Read level**: full (arXiv v2, pp. 1-28: main text pp. 1-10, references pp. 11-13 skimmed, appendices A-F pp. 14-28 read; the figure-only pages 21-27 skimmed for captions). The TMLR version itself was not read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Jixiang Qing (first author), Becky D Langdon, Calvin Tsay (Imperial), Robert M Lee and Behrang Shafei (BASF SE), Mark van der Wilk (Oxford); Ruth Misener last author (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: part of the group's BO-for-experiments line with practical constraints (SnAKe and transition-constrained BO are cited as related, p. 7), here for dynamical systems where the experiment is a trajectory.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Optimise initial conditions and termination time of a system governed by an unknown ODE, where switching initial conditions is the main cost and states can only be measured with a delay (abstract; pp. 1, 6). Stated as a two-objective problem: maximise g(state) and −t, "maximize objective g early" (p. 3). The gap: standard GP kernels do not capture dynamics, Bayesian ODE models are too slow or approximate (p. 1), and "A meta-learned BO method specifically for dynamical systems is, to our best of knowledge, not revealed yet." (p. 7).
  - Transferable: define the decision problem from the experimental protocol (what is costly to switch, what delay a measurement imposes) and only then choose the surrogate (pp. 1, 6).
- **D2 Core idea**: Extend Neural ODE Processes (NODEP) to SANODEP: a set-based system representation pooled over context points from several trajectories, each point augmented with its trajectory's initial condition to avoid identifiability clashes between trajectories (pp. 4-5); a bi-scenario training loss mixing forecasting (only the initial condition known) and interpolation via a Bernoulli(λ) indicator (p. 5). On top, a two-stage BO framework: choose initial condition and a batch of observation times under the spacing constraint t_i − t_{i−1} ≥ Δt, then reschedule the next measurement time within the trajectory (pp. 6, 20, Algorithm 2). The framework is model-agnostic: GP and NP surrogates plug into the same loop (p. 6). Unchanged from NODEP: the rest of the architecture, except SiLU activations chosen for differentiability with respect to time and initial condition (pp. 5, 15-16).
  - Transferable: when context points from different runs can coincide, append the run's identifier (its initial condition) to each context element before pooling (pp. 4-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: f is Lipschitz (Picard-Lindelöf invoked) and a realisation of a stochastic family F from which meta-training tasks can be sampled, in practice parametric kinetic models with uniform parameter distributions (pp. 4-5, 7, 19-20); fixed, known measurement delay Δt (p. 6); known aggregation g (p. 3). Section 7 relaxes the prior: known kinetic form (PI-SANODEP) or a vector-valued GP prior over vector fields (pp. 9-10, 26-27). The meta-training distribution for the Selkov system is not given in Appendix D.1 of this version (pp. 19-20), although Selkov appears in all tables.
- **D4 Analysis and proof technique**: Proposition 1: for expected-utility batch acquisitions whose utility is monotone under set inclusion (qEHVI), the batch size search space {1, ..., N_max} can be reduced to {⌈N_max/2⌉, ..., N_max} without losing the maximiser (p. 6). Proof via Lemma C.1 (expectation preserves set-inclusion monotonicity), Corollary C.1.1 and the argument that a schedule with fewer than ⌈N_max/2⌉ points has a gap of at least 2Δt into which a point can be inserted (p. 19); the paper notes the property holds only "asymptotically" under Monte Carlo approximation (p. 19). Also an ELBO derivation (p. 17) and a chain-rule argument for why ReLU breaks gradients with respect to t and x0 (pp. 15-16, Fig. 7).
  - Transferable: shrink an integer "how many evaluations" variable in a batch acquisition by proving monotonicity under set inclusion plus an insertion argument (pp. 6, 19).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Modelling: six systems (Lotka-Volterra 2d/3d, Brusselator, Selkov, SIR, SIRD), models trained with 5 seeds, evaluated on 10^4 systems × 100 trajectories; the GP baseline only on 5,000 trajectories because of cost (p. 7). SANODEP beats NODEP on all six interpolation problems (Table 1, p. 8); against NP it is "either on-par or marginally better" with exceptions on Lotka-Volterra 2d/3d (p. 8); the no-augmentation ablation is less robust (p. 8). GPs overtake once context trajectories are many (p. 8). The comparison with Jiang et al. (2023) was not possible on irregular time series (p. 7). BO: one random initial trajectory plus 10 queried trajectories, 10 seeds, hypervolume against scaled experimental time, mean ± standard deviation (pp. 8-9, Table 4 p. 22); SANODEP competitive except Lotka-Volterra 3d (p. 9); bi-scenario loss gives "modest" gains, worse on Brusselator (p. 9). Costs: parameter counts and per-query times, SANODEP slowest (39.47 s per query vs 23.81 s for GP, Table 5 p. 23). Minor mismatch between Table 1 (p. 8) and Table 2 (p. 21) for SANODEP on SIR/SIRD (254.3 vs 257.5; 137.2 vs 139.5).
  - Transferable: report surrogate quality as a curve against the number of context trajectories, so the regime where meta-learning helps (few trajectories) and where a GP overtakes (many) are both visible (p. 8).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds directly on NODEP (Norcliffe et al. 2021) and Neural Processes (Garnelo et al. 2018b) (pp. 2-3); distinguishes itself from meta-learned dynamics models restricted to discrete time or regular grids (Singh et al. 2019; Jiang et al. 2023) and from few-shot BO surrogates designed as GP drop-ins (TNP, PFNs4BO) (pp. 6-7); relates to process-constrained BO (Vellanki et al. 2017) and the group's movement-constrained BO (Folch et al. 2022, 2024) (p. 7).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Four numbered contributions (p. 2); results organised under question headings ("Does System Awareness Improve Predictive Performance?", pp. 8-9); frequent typographical slips in the arXiv text ("Exept", "temperal", "Does Meta-learning Helps", p. 9). Limitations get their own paragraph (p. 10). The text is the first author's; Misener's role is not stated.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Model plus BO framework, open-source code (p. 7). Limits stated specifically: Neural-Process underfitting forces a trade-off between few-shot ability and fit (p. 10); without prior knowledge of the kinetic form the model "cannot be used out of the box" (p. 10); oscillatory systems are hard for the flexible GP-vector-field prior (p. 27); PI-SANODEP training needed parameter clipping to the known support for numerical stability (p. 26). Future: more adaptable conditional distributions (p. 10); meta-learned parameter estimation left as future work (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Heuristic 1 (choose by broken assumption): evidence ✅. Standard BO assumes free sampling and a static black box; here unknown dynamics, costly initial-condition switches and measurement delays collide with it (pp. 1, 6).
- Method 1 (structure as "insider information"): variant ⚠. The structure exploited is in the acquisition problem, not in an algebraic model: set-inclusion monotonicity halves the integer search range (p. 6), and the known ODE form is built into PI-SANODEP when available (p. 9).
- Method 3 (one interface, switchable formulations): variant ⚠. The BO framework is "agnostic to the model choice" and GP, NP, NODEP and SANODEP run in the same loop (pp. 6, 8); the surrogate here is meta-learned, not a partner's trained model.
- Method 2 (between two extremes): variant ⚠. Section 7 places SANODEP on a spectrum of prior strength from known kinetic form to a GP prior over vector fields and reports the trade-off between flexibility and fit (pp. 9-10); it is labelled "preliminary", with no proven ordering (p. 9).
- Heuristic 7 (write the do-not-use conditions next to the method): evidence ✅ (p. 10).
