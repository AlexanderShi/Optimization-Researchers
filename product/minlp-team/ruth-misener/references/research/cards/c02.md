### S011 · Global optimization of mixed-integer quadratically-constrained quadratic programs (MIQCQP) through piecewise-linear and edge-concave relaxations (Mathematical Programming 2012, DOI 10.1007/s10107-012-0555-6)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–20 text, the reference list pp. 20–25, result tables pp. 26–29; figures are images and their content was not read beyond the captions on pp. 18–19).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Christodoulos A. Floudas (Princeton, advisor) · **Place in trajectory**: The bridge from her pooling-specific work (APOGEE [65], piecewise-linear formulation studies [40, 61, 63, 64], p. 2, p. 4) to the generic MIQCQP solver GloMIQO (S004), which cites this paper as [99] for its edge-concave cuts and its piecewise-linear default (S004 pp. 12–13).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is her own pooling work. Large pooling problems were solved with piecewise disjunctive relaxations inside B&B (p. 2). The question is how to generalise to MIQCQP, which is "ubiquitous in process systems applications" (p. 1). The gap she names is that the sum of termwise McCormick hulls "does not necessarily generate the convex hull" of a whole constraint (p. 3). She claims this as, to the authors' knowledge, "the first effort towards integrating the edge-concave based relaxation methodology described by Meyer and Floudas [58] into a branch-and-bound" algorithm (p. 4).
  - Transferable technique: generalise from a solved application class to the parent class by asking which of your class-specific tools survive the generalisation and which need a new partner (here piecewise-linear survives, edge-concave cuts are added; pp. 2, 4).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Two complementary relaxations with a division of labour. Facets of low-dimensional (n ≤ 3) edge-concave aggregations EC-AGG that strictly dominate the termwise relaxation are added at every node (pp. 5–8). Bilinear terms that do not sit in an active edge-concave triplet, and concave multivariable terms, are handled by piecewise-linear relaxations (pp. 10–13). The division is enforced in preprocessing. Edges always covered by EC-AGG triplets are removed from the Hansen–Jaumard co-occurrence graph before greedy selection of the variables to partition: at most one partitioned variable per bilinear term, highest degree first, capped at 30 variables (p. 13). Concave terms are partitioned along the most negative eigenvectors, up to ten (pp. 12–13). Non-dominant facets are not thrown away. They feed feasibility-based bounds tightening (pp. 7, 14). What stays standard: McCormick hulls, RLT for bounding, reliability branching, OBBT (pp. 13–15).
  - Transferable technique: when two relaxation families are complementary, assign each term to exactly one family in a preprocessing pass (remove from the second family's selection graph whatever the first already covers), instead of stacking both everywhere (p. 13).
  - Transferable technique: reuse cuts that are redundant in the LP for interval-arithmetic bounds tightening. The worked example tightens [0.5, 2] towards [(1+√5)/2, 2] (p. 14).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Finite bounds can be inferred on variables in nonlinear terms, and Q_m is upper triangular (p. 2). Edge-concave aggregations are restricted to n ≤ 3 for tractability, since brute-force facet enumeration is "combinatorially complex" (p. 5). Each term goes into at most one aggregation "to avoid introducing too many extra cuts", and the aggregation is fixed in preprocessing (p. 7). Several numeric settings are fixed without a stated derivation: the 0.95 volume factor for FBBT and OBBT (pp. 14), the 30-variable and ten-eigenvector caps (p. 13), the branching point 0.15/0.85 with a 10% margin (p. 15), and N = 4 partitions "based on our previous studies [65]" (p. 17).
- **D4 Analysis and proof techniques**: No new theorem is proved in this file. The argument chains cited results: Tardella's edge-concavity test for C² functions on a box (p. 5), and Tardella's equivalence theorem for sum decomposability, which the paper states without proof (p. 6). From these she derives a sequence of Observations: affine parts are irrelevant (3.1.1.4); separable or two-variable aggregations give no dominant cut (3.1.1.5–3.1.1.6); a two-cross-term chain is sum decomposable (3.1.1.7). So all three cross-coefficients must be nonzero (necessary, 3.1.1.8). A facet that differs from all eight termwise underestimators is dominant (sufficient, 3.1.1.9) (pp. 6–7). A worked numeric example lists the eight McCormick cuts and the six facets and shows which four are new (Illustration 3.1.2.2, p. 8). Property 3.1.3.1 argues that the quality-tracking equations of process networks are sum decomposable. Hence no equation-by-equation cut, hers or Bao et al.'s multiterm cuts, can tighten them (p. 9). Sharpness of the piecewise schemes is cited to Wicaksono–Karimi and to "Appendix A of Misener and Floudas [62]" (p. 12). [62] is the Optimization Online version of this same paper (p. 24), and the appendix is not in this file (not read).
  - Transferable technique: before implementing a cut family, prove the negative result: for which equation structures can it never beat the termwise relaxation? Then route those classes to a different tool (Property 3.1.3.1 sends process networks to piecewise-linear and RLT, p. 9).
  - Transferable technique: give a dominance test that is cheap enough for every node. Compare each candidate facet to the finite list of termwise underestimators (p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: 43 instances: 14 point packing (n = 2–15, results checked against packomania.com), "twenty" pooling problems plus three GLOBALLib process-network models, and six GLOBALLib instances that are not sum decomposable (pp. 15–16). The counts do not quite match: the pooling tables list 19 pooling rows plus the three GLOBALLib models, 22 rows rather than the 23 of Fig. 2 (Tables 5–6, pp. 28–29; Fig. 2, p. 18). Each process-network and packing instance is run under four cumulative configurations: McC only → McC + EC → + piecewise linear N = 4 → + piecewise logarithmic N = 4. Packing is also run at N = 2 and 8, plotted but not tabulated (pp. 16–17). Setup: 7200 s limit, 1e-6 relative gap, one Core 2 Quad, CPLEX 11.1, SNOPT 5.3 (p. 15). The measures are Dolan–Moré performance profiles for time and for remaining gap (p. 15, Figs 1–2 p. 18), root gap closed (Table 2, p. 17) and mean nodes with standard-deviation error bars (Fig. 3, p. 19). No external solver is compared. Her own pooling solver APOGEE is the only outside reference, and it "regurally outperforms" this generic code (p. 19). Findings: of nine cases with ≥10% difference, seven favour McC + EC (p. 18). Packing gains come entirely from bounds tightening, since packing equations are sum decomposable (p. 18). Piecewise schemes cost time on packing but raise the solve probability and shrink the remaining gap on process networks (p. 19). Of ten cases with ≥10% difference, eight favour linear over logarithmic partitioning (pp. 19–20). Losses are visible in the tables: for example McC + EC on Meyer 4 (Table 5, p. 28). That row's gap entry (8.21e-01) does not match its own LB/UB (1.081e+06 / 1.090e+06), which imply about 8e-03, so it is probably a misprint. The six GLOBALLib instances have a duplicated name, "st iqpbk1" twice (p. 16, Table 2 p. 17).
  - Transferable technique: run a cumulative ablation inside the full B&B and report it per class with both a solved-time profile and a gap-at-limit profile, so that "solves faster when it solves" and "closes more gap on the hardest" can be told apart (pp. 18–19).
  - Transferable technique: when a component helps on a class where it adds no LP cut, isolate the mechanism. Here packing proves the gain comes from bounds tightening alone (p. 18).
- **D6 Positioning**: The literature is sorted into families: complicating-variable reductions, bilinear-term elimination, redundant constraints (automatic RLT/SDP vs model-specific), facet/eigenvector/KKT cuts, and alternative bilinear relaxations (pp. 3–4). Her group's piecewise work is placed among Meyer–Floudas, Karuppiah–Grossmann, Wicaksono–Karimi (15 formulations), Hasan–Karimi and Vielma–Nemhauser's logarithmic models (p. 4). Bao–Sahinidis–Tawarmalani multiterm cuts are not criticised but complemented: they are "more costly to generate" and fit the root node, while hers are cheap enough for every node, so "a hybrid algorithm could use" both (p. 10). The eigenvector treatment follows Saxena–Bonami–Lee, with two stated differences (p. 10). Prior art is restated, not attacked.
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The contribution sentence comes early and is scoped: "primary novelty ... rooted in the integration of edge-concave and piecewise-linear relaxations" (p. 4). Theory is written as numbered Observations and Properties with one worked numeric example each (pp. 6–9). The discussion names its own method: "This holistic view makes the comparisons fairly subtle" (p. 17). Hedging is moderate. The closing verdict is class-split ("strong evidence ... in any MIQCQP" vs "best applied to specific classes", p. 20). The conclusion is short and adds the claim that many instances "represent large-scale test cases" (p. 20).
  - Transferable technique: close the results with a one-sentence verdict per component that names the class where it pays off (p. 20).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contribution levels: relaxation theory applied (dominance conditions, the process-network negative result), algorithm (an integrated B&B), and evidence on which component helps where (pp. 4, 20). Limits written in the paper: no symmetry inference, so packing lacks Anstreicher's RLT cuts (p. 16); the generic code loses to APOGEE on pooling because it lacks topological knowledge and big-M updating (p. 19); the n ≤ 3 restriction (p. 5). No future-work section. The only forward idea is the root/node hybrid with Bao et al. cuts (p. 10).

**Method links**: Method 4 ✅ evidence: the holistic cumulative ablation and the class-split verdict (pp. 17–20) are the practice Method 4 step 2–3 quotes. Method 1 ✅ evidence: structure is analysed before relaxing (sum decomposability tests, aggregation detection in preprocessing, co-occurrence graph) (pp. 6–9, 13). Method 2 ⚠ variant: the piecewise family between McCormick (N = 1) and finer partitions is swept at N = 2/4/8 only on packing, and N = 4 for pooling is inherited from earlier work rather than tested here (p. 17). It shows one setting held across a class, but with no robustness sweep on the class where it matters. Method 5 ⚠ variant: rigorous internal profiles and an external correctness check (packomania), but no competitor solvers. The only outside comparison is a concession to her own APOGEE (p. 19). New-pattern candidates: "prove where a cut cannot help, then route that class elsewhere" (p. 9); "assign terms to complementary relaxations by exclusion" (p. 13); "reuse non-dominant facets for bounds tightening" (pp. 7, 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Derive a necessary condition and a cheap sufficient condition for a cut to dominate the default relaxation, and generate only cuts that pass, at every node (pp. 6–7).
- Prove a "no cut can help" property for an application class before testing on it, and use it to explain where the gains come from (p. 9, p. 18).
- Split terms between two relaxation families by deleting covered edges from the selection graph of the second family (p. 13).
- Report a cumulative component ablation with time and remaining-gap profiles plus nodes with error bars, per problem class (pp. 18–19).

**Quotes**:
- "This holistic view makes the comparisons fairly subtle but also allows us to draw better-informed conclusions." (p. 17)
- "When McC or McC + EC are sufficient to solve the process network problem, they tend to do so faster than the piecewise schemes, but the best strategies for closing the gap on the most difficult problems are the piecewise methods." (p. 19)

### S004 · GloMIQO: Global mixed-integer quadratic optimizer (Journal of Global Optimization 2013, DOI 10.1007/s10898-012-9874-7)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–27 text and acknowledgements, reference list pp. 27–34). Appendix A (performance-profile figures, pp. 35–40) was read from captions only, since the plots are images. Appendix B (test-suite tables 7–16, pp. 41–49) was skimmed. The Online Resource tables S2–S23 are not in the file (not read).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Christodoulos A. Floudas · **Place in trajectory**: The solver release that turns S011's relaxations into a general MIQCQP code. According to S001, GloMIQO was released publicly with the GAMS Development Corporation, first in GAMS 23.8 (S001 p. 2). ANTIGONE (S001) is its generalisation.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is an application spread: MIQCQP appears in process networks (pooling, water, crude-oil scheduling, natural gas), computational geometry (packing, polygons, chip layout) and finance (p. 2). The research claim is an engineering one: GloMIQO "represents an effort to carefully elucidate and exploit any special structure within user-defined MIQCQP" (p. 2). The gap named is that automatic reformulations, "while common in MIP solver software, are relatively rare in MIQCQP solver software" (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: A pipeline (Fig. 1, p. 4): reformulate the user model, detect structure, run B&B, and map the answer back to the user's variables (pp. 4–6). Reformulation: four variable-elimination rules for two-variable linear equalities and for bilinear-plus-linear equalities, plus two disaggregation rules (pp. 6–7). On Ahmetović–Grossmann water networks these rules turn modular mixer/treatment/splitter models into generalised pooling form, "because our computational experience is that pooling problems are easier" (p. 5). Detection: RLT products that add no new nonlinear term. Equality-variable products go straight into the model, and inequality and equation-equation products are stored and updated per node (pp. 8–9). Each equation is split into separable multivariable terms by graph components. This is justified by sum decomposability and is the unit for convexity (eigenvalues), eigenvectors and edge-concave aggregation (pp. 10–11). Relaxation: MILP relaxations with McCormick, EC facets, eigenvector projections, optional piecewise-linear, OA cuts on convex multivariable terms, and adaptive RLT (pp. 11–14). Adaptive RLT adds the 100 most violated stored cuts during the first three tree levels, then makes permanent every cut used in at least half of those nodes (p. 14). Branching: reliability branching on the LP, tempered by Algorithm 1 so that variables with narrow bounds or off-centre values are not chosen on stale pseudocosts (pp. 14–16). Bounding: FBBT extended with RLT/McCormick/EC facets and equation factoring, OBBT with parent-to-child switching and probabilistic restart, and reduced-cost tightening (pp. 16–18). Upper bounds come from CPLEX populate (p. 18).
  - Transferable technique: when a cut library is too big to add statically, learn which cuts matter in the first few tree levels and freeze the frequently used ones for the rest of the tree (p. 14).
  - Transferable technique: rewrite user models into the form your solver handles best (modular networks → generalised pooling) and reverse the transformation before reporting (pp. 5–6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Finite bounds inferable on variables in nonlinear terms, and quadratic matrices brought to upper triangular form (pp. 1–2). The reformulations "are specifically targeted at enhancing the performance of GloMIQO on process networks problems" though implemented generically (p. 5), and the disaggregation detection is "designed to target a specific collection of test problems" (p. 8). Convexity is detected per multivariable term rather than per equation, unlike SCIP 2.1.0 (p. 11). The piecewise-linear relaxations are off by default, and every reported run uses "exactly one choice of parameter settings" (p. 13).
- **D4 Analysis and proof techniques**: n/a (no theorems). Algorithmic devices with stated rationale: graph partition into disjoint vertex sets, justified by "the convex envelope of a sum of separable functions coincides with the sum of the convex envelopes" (pp. 10–11); a worked decomposition of ex8_1_4 into three multivariable terms (p. 10); equation factoring to stop interval overestimation when a variable repeats (p. 17); the reduced-cost bound formulas (p. 18); Algorithm 1 pseudocode with a halving gap threshold (p. 16).
- **D5 Experiments**: 399 instances in 11 groups. Process networks (61) and computational geometry (50) come from the literature and her own earlier work. Test libraries contribute 246: all QCQPs in GLOBALLib (187), all MIQCQPs in MINLPLib (39) and 20 convex MIQCQPs. Saxena-reformulated libraries contribute 42 (Table 2, pp. 20–22). The competitors are BARON 10.1.2, Couenne 0.4, LindoGLOBAL 6.1.1.588 and SCIP 2.1.0, "the latest version of each solver available to us as of January 2012" (p. 18). The vendors supplied licences, including a LindoGLOBAL build with an "undocumented quadratic mode option" (p. 18). The feasibility tolerance is set to 1e-6 for all solvers, and solvers run back to back on each instance (p. 19). A Perl script checks every returned point. Infeasible returns are relabelled failures, with "benign" (–) separated from "troublesome" (fl) failures (p. 19). Profiles use the 369 instances with well-defined bounds (p. 19). Competitors are given the benefit of the doubt: SCIP's gap at a memory crash is read from GAMS logs, and BARON's bounds count when it found no feasible point (p. 20). For the reformulated GLOBALLib cases, bounds added by Saxena et al. were removed so that bound inference stays part of the test (p. 22). For water systems, seven of eight models were modified by a piecewise-linear approximation of power-law terms (N = 4) to make them MIQCQP, so their optima differ from the sources (p. 21). Results by class: tied with BARON on pooling, with tighter gaps on Meyer10/15 (p. 23); 28 of 36 Kallrath packing instances solved, against 17 each for the commercial solvers (p. 24); worked instances (Li 6, congruent c72, ex5_2_5, waste) attribute each win to one component (pp. 23–26). Aggregate: fastest on ≈68%, ≈81% solved within the limit, SCIP second (p. 26). Losses written in: SCIP is faster on smaller instances (p. 26). Correctness findings about competitors: BARON reports a wrong optimum on waste, 623.4 against 598.9 (p. 25), and Couenne returns an infeasible point "in over half of the 399 test cases" (p. 26). No internal ablation of GloMIQO's components is reported. Wins are attributed through the worked instances (pp. 8, 24–25).
  - Transferable technique: verify every competitor's returned point with an independent script, and split failures into "did not return" and "returned something wrong" (p. 19).
  - Transferable technique: when a solver crashes, recover its gap from its logs instead of scoring it as a blank, and say so (p. 20).
  - Transferable technique: undo convenience bounds that earlier papers added to library instances, so that bound inference stays part of the benchmark (p. 22).
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper lists the codes it builds on (αBB, BARON, Audet et al.'s branch-and-cut, Couenne, GloptLAB, LindoGLOBAL, SCIP), each with the component most relevant to GloMIQO (pp. 2–3). SCIP is singled out as the most interesting comparison "because it helps clarify the trade-offs" (p. 3). Differences are stated factually: Couenne also uses MILP relaxations, but "GloMIQO employs many more advanced cuts" (p. 11); convexity detection is per term, not per equation, as in SCIP (p. 11). The adaptive RLT is "deeply inspired" by the literature on choosing RLT cuts a priori, but learns them dynamically instead (p. 14). Competitors are framed generously: "all of the excellent solvers that we compare ... are subject to active development" (p. 18).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Long, implementation-level description. Every component is tied to a later worked instance (e.g. "superior performance of GloMIQO on ex5 2 5 ... is a direct result of detecting and integrating this cut", p. 8). The paper also speaks to modellers: "a modeler will significantly improve the performance of GloMIQO by writing linear constraints that can be multiplied together", with horizontal over diagonal symmetry-breaking as the example (p. 9). The trade-off with SCIP is left as an open question rather than claimed away (p. 26). Promotional sentences exist ("GloMIQO is on the leading edge", p. 3; "strong advantage", p. 27), but the class-level results text is measured.
  - Transferable technique: in a solver paper, tell users how to write models the solver can exploit, with a concrete pair of equivalent formulations (p. 9).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Software plus algorithm-integration contribution (pp. 26–27). Stated limits: the slower small-instance performance against SCIP and the open question whether GloMIQO could "dynamically abandon its more elaborate approach" (p. 26); the dependence of the water-system results on the reformulations (p. 6); reformulations tuned to a collection (p. 8); no addition of extra bilinear terms à la Liberti–Pantelides "the current version of GloMIQO does not use this strategy" (p. 9). There is no future-work section beyond that open question (pp. 26–27).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence: reformulation and structure detection come first, and the detected structure is reused for relaxation and bounding (pp. 4–11). The modeller advice shows structure being designed into models (p. 9). Method 4 ✅ evidence for the default rule: piecewise-linear is off by default because it slows instances that would converge anyway, and users are told to switch it on for hard pooling/process-network models (p. 13). ⚠ Variant for the ablation step: this paper runs no component ablation and attributes gains through worked instances (pp. 8, 24–25). Method 5 ✅ evidence: newest versions with dates, vendor-supplied licences, harmonised tolerance, back-to-back runs, Perl check, benign/troublesome failures, gap profiles, log recovery for crashed competitors, bounds kept honest (pp. 18–22, 25–26). Method 2 ✅ evidence for "never re-tune per comparison": one parameter setting for all 399 runs (p. 13). New-pattern candidates: "learn-then-freeze cut selection in the top tree levels" (p. 14); "modelling advice as part of the solver contribution" (p. 9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Use one parameter setting for every benchmark run and state it next to the default decision (p. 13).
- Learn which stored cuts matter during the first n tree levels, then add those used in ≥ half the nodes everywhere (p. 14).
- Temper pseudocost branching by excluding variables with narrow or lopsided domains, and relax the threshold by halving until a candidate exists (Algorithm 1, pp. 15–16).
- Benchmark with an independent feasibility re-check, a benign/troublesome failure split and log-recovered gaps for crashed competitors (pp. 19–20).

**Quotes**:
- "Therefore, the default in GloMIQO is not to use the piecewise-linear relaxations." (p. 13)
- "We do not know if it would be possible for GloMIQO to dynamically abandon its more elaborate approach under circumstances where a SCIP-like algorithm is more effective or if a slower solution time on the smaller problems is a price we pay for addressing the exceptionally large problems." (p. 26)

### S001 · ANTIGONE: algorithms for continuous/integer global optimization of nonlinear equations (Journal of Global Optimization 2014, DOI 10.1007/s10898-014-0166-2)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–18 text, reference list pp. 18–26; the performance-profile figures pp. 12–16 and 27–31 were read from captions only, since the plots are images). The online supplement (Tables 9–18 and 24–43) is not in the file (not read).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Christodoulos A. Floudas · **Place in trajectory**: The general-MINLP successor of GloMIQO (S004) and of the MISO signomial framework [105]. It first appeared in GAMS 24.1 (p. 2). The preprint gives her present address as Imperial College London and acknowledges a Royal Academy of Engineering Research Fellowship (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is consolidation. The MIQCQP and MISO frameworks already existed and GloMIQO had users (crystal orientation, blend scheduling, network flow, water networks, ellipse cutting; p. 2). The paper's purpose "is to show how the extensible structure of ANTIGONE realizes" those frameworks, adding exponential and logarithmic functions (pp. 1–2). The contribution is declared to be architectural: "the intellectual contribution is the way ANTIGONE ties together its many constitutive algorithms" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Term-based underestimation, as in αBB and SGO (p. 3). Every equation is rewritten as a sum of term types (Table 1, p. 5), and auxiliary variables are introduced only for more complex terms (p. 4). The term base class is polymorphic, so each term type knows its variables, its convexity/edge-concavity regions and its derivatives. New term-specific underestimators can be added "easily", and "we anticipate further advances in the area of term-specific underestimators and have architected ANTIGONE accordingly" (p. 4). Three composite univariate terms (exponential, fractional, logarithmic) are new to the literature. Their convexity regions come from analytic second derivatives plus interval arithmetic (pp. 4–5). Reformulation flattens the expression tree (log of products → sum of logs; product of exponentials → exponential of sum), on top of all GloMIQO and MISO reformulations (pp. 6–7). Structure detection labels terms and multi-term expressions as always/sometimes/never convex or edge-concave. It also covers RLT products that introduce no new terms and αBB relaxations for bilinear aggregates (p. 7). The B&C phase talks only to the term base class, so it is "entirely equivalent" to the MISO framework (p. 8). For convex NLPs, a local solver's certificate at the root is accepted as global (p. 8).
  - Transferable technique: put the extension point at the term level (a base class every relaxation and bounding routine queries), so that new underestimators from the literature drop in without touching branch-and-cut (pp. 4, 8).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Finite bounds inferable on variables in nonlinear terms, and a finite image of f_m (p. 3). Functional forms are limited to Table 1 after preprocessing. Instances with trigonometric, min/max or erf terms, or with feasible points of objective −∞, are excluded from the test libraries (pp. 8–9). Convexity detection covers individual terms and quadratic aggregates only. It does not "at this writing, apply function composition rules or symbolic methods" (p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis and proof techniques**: n/a (no theorems). Devices: closed-form second derivatives of the three composite terms, used with interval arithmetic to find convexity regions (p. 5); the two tree-flattening identities (Eq. 1, p. 7); a worked reformulation of ex6_2_9 into two bilinear terms, one log term and one convex composite term (p. 14). The paper itself notes a missed opportunity in that example: it "could detect an additional composite term" because one linear expression is a constant multiple of another (p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: 2571 instances. 115 from minlp.org were compiled by the authors, because the site's "Test Set" was "unfortunately unusable as a test set for solver comparisons" (p. 10). 482 MIQCQPs come from the literature, and the rest from AMPL Book, Bonmin, GLOBALLib, LINLib, MacMOOPLib, MPLLib, MINLPLib and convex/nonconvex PrincetonLib (Table 2, p. 9). Libraries are frozen at "the latest versions as of 11 March 2013", and an explicit exclusion rule is stated (pp. 8–10). Competitors in GAMS 24.2.1: BARON 12.7.3, Couenne 0.4, LINDO 8.0 and SCIP 3.0, "the latest versions as of February 2014". Termination is by relative/absolute gap 1e-6, 7200 s or an iteration limit, and "No other parameters were altered from default" (p. 10). Solvers run back to back on one Xeon (p. 10). The Perl feasibility check relabels as fl any point violating a bound or constraint by max(1e-4, 1e-4·|V|) (p. 10). Profiles come from GAMS PTOOLS, both time and gap at 7200 s. The only processing is the infeasibility penalty, and "lower bounds based on guessed variable bounds are discarded" (p. 11). Results are sliced by library (Figs 4–9) and by structure: quadratic 1397, signomial 759, other 415; discrete 528 vs continuous 2048; nonconvex 1781 vs convex 790 (pp. 17–18, Figs 10–16). Some counts do not reconcile in the file: 528 + 2048 = 2576, not 2571, and the "2112 problems with a nonconvex component" of Fig. 3 (p. 11) differ from the 1781 of Fig. 15 (p. 18), with no explanation in the text. There is one worked instance per library (ngw-r1-53050, castro Ex16 M1, ex6_2_9, tls4, weapon, SLay10H; pp. 11–17). Small slips: ex6_2_9 is "19 seconds" in the text (p. 14) but 22 s in Table 5 (p. 15), and weapon's results are referred to as "Table 1" but sit in Table 7 (p. 16).
