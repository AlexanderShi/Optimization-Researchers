### S023 · ENTMOOT: A framework for optimization over ensemble tree models (Computers & Chemical Engineering 2021, doi:10.1016/j.compchemeng.2021.107343; read as arXiv:2003.04774v3)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-29 read in full incl. appendices A-D; reference list pp. 29-33 skimmed). Version read: arXiv v3 preprint dated May 19, 2021 (p. 1), not the journal typeset version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. Thebelt (corresponding), J. Kronqvist, M. Mistry, R. M. Lee and N. Sudermann-Merx (BASF SE), R. Misener (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: second step of the trees → ENTMOOT → OMLT line; extends the Mišić (2020) / Mistry et al. (2020) tree encoding into a Bayesian-optimisation (BO) loop (p. 6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Enters from a named weakness list in the BO literature: Shahriari et al. (2016) "identify unreliable uncertainty estimates and the lack of gradients when optimizing tree model-based acquisition functions as the main drawbacks" of tree-based BO (p. 2; again p. 4). Trees are attractive (robust, scale, categorical features, p. 2) but tree-based BO tools optimise the acquisition by sampling, so "non-optimal solutions to the acquisition function lead to poorly performing query points" (p. 6). Problem stated as constrained black-box min f(x) s.t. g(x)=0, h(x)≤0 (Equ. 1, p. 2).
  - Transferable: take a published "main drawbacks" list for a popular method family and answer each drawback with one design element (here: uncertainty → distance measure; no gradients → MIP encoding).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Two changes to tree-based BO, nothing else: (i) a distance-to-nearest-data-point uncertainty α(x)=min_d ||x−x_d||_p (Equ. 2, p. 5); (ii) solve the LCB acquisition μ̂ − κα to ε-global optimality as a MIP built on the Mišić tree encoding (Equ. 3-5, pp. 6-7). Squared Euclidean gives a nonconvex MIQP solved by Gurobi 9 (p. 8); Manhattan gives a MILP with SOS-implemented complementarity (Equ. 7, p. 9) at the price of one constraint per dimension per data point (p. 9). Sign flip (+κα, Equ. 8, p. 10) turns exploration into a penalty that keeps decisions near data ("decision-making under uncertainty", §4, pp. 9-10), with a big-M nearest-point selector and an explicit valid M (Equ. 9, p. 10). Scale-up: k-means cluster centres replace data points (Equ. 10, p. 11); for >2000 trees a tailored spatial B&B with strong branching, a bound split into a tree part (partition refinement, Mistry et al.) and an O(|K|·n) distance part from box projections (Equ. 11-12, Fig. 3, pp. 11-12), warm start at the best cluster centre (Equ. 13, p. 12). What is kept: the tree encoding and the LCB acquisition with SKOPT's κ default (p. 22).
  - Transferable: when the acquisition is a sum of a hard term and an easy term, bound them separately in B&B and use the cheap bound to fathom first (p. 12).
  - Transferable: one formulation, two signs: −κα for exploration, +κα for "stay near data" (pp. 6, 10).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumes "high values of α indicate large average model errors" (p. 5), tested empirically, not proved (§6.1, p. 15). Known constraints may be linear or (convex/nonconvex) quadratic (p. 2). Box bounds on x (Equ. 5c, p. 7). Data standardised (p. 8). α bounded by α_limit = ζ·Var(y_D), a choice that "has shown, in our preliminary studies, to be a good measure" (p. 8). Black-box evaluation assumed far costlier than an iteration of the optimiser (p. 28).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: No theorems. Devices: explicit sufficiently-large M for the nearest-point big-M (Equ. 9, p. 10); valid lower bound by projecting cluster centres onto the B&B box (Equ. 12, p. 12); feasible start from the fact that cluster centres have α=0 (Equ. 13, p. 12). Global optimality claim is ε-global through Gurobi's 0.01% gap (pp. 8, 22).
- **D5 Experiments**: Four parts announced (p. 14): (i) uncertainty-measure check by sweeping κ in the +κα model on 20D rover and plotting relative model error (Fig. 5, p. 15; κ ∈ {0.5,...,10}, 30 seeds, p. 24); (ii) ablation vs tree-based tools with a control ENTMOOT-RND that uses "exactly the same sampling points" as SKOPT-GBRT/SKOPT-RF (p. 22) so the acquisition optimiser is isolated (Fig. 6, pp. 16-17); (iii) vs SKOPT-GP, BOHAMIANN, CMA-ES, BFGS, Nelder-Mead and random search (DUMMY) on 60D rover, 80D fermentation, 200D Ackley, 14D robot push (Fig. 7, pp. 17-18); (iv) large-scale proof of concept on the UCI concrete data set, 4000 trees, ENTMOOT B&B vs Gurobi 9 over 4 h with both upper and lower bounds per hour and κ (Table 1, p. 19). Protocol: 50 shared random initial points, 300 evaluations, median and quartiles over seeds 101-130 (pp. 14, 24); hyperparameters "constant for each mode throughout all tests" and competitors at defaults (p. 14), CMA-ES given scaling per its manual, NM/BFGS tolerances lowered so they use the budget (pp. 22-23). Losses written in: BOHAMIANN slightly better on rover, SKOPT-GP and CMA-ES lead on fermentation, SMAC3 best on robot push, SMAC3 beats two ENTMOOT variants on Rosenbrock 40D (pp. 17-18). Run times reported in an appendix table, ENTMOOT-L1 up to 8.4 h vs seconds for CMA-ES (Table 2, p. 29). Hardware stated (p. 29).
  - Transferable: a same-seed, same-samples control (ENTMOOT-RND) that differs from the competitor in exactly one component (p. 22).
  - Transferable: for a B&B claim, tabulate ub and lb per hour for both solvers instead of a single time (Table 1, p. 19).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Mišić (2020) and Mistry et al. (2020) for the encoding and claims the extension "to a full-fledged BO framework with a reliable uncertainty measure" (p. 6). Critiques SMAC's empirical uncertainty via Shahriari et al. (narrow intervals in sparse regions, peaks where trees disagree, p. 4) rather than directly. Credits Mišić for further tree-optimisation improvements and a possible tree-based uncertainty alternative (pp. 16, 19). Chemical-engineering surrogate literature cited as the application community (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contributions numbered (i)-(iii) in the abstract (p. 1); "proving that the solutions are globally optimal, i.e. no better solution exists" (p. 1) is a strong phrasing for an ε-gap result. Concession sentence with scope ("still the best choice for small and moderate instances", p. 19) and a disclaimer that the large-model hyperparameters were not chosen to make sense for the data (p. 28). Code location given in the introduction (p. 3).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Layers: algorithm (uncertainty + global acquisition), software (pip package, p. 3; Fig. 4, p. 13), benchmark. Limits stated: Manhattan formulation's size and run time (p. 9; Table 2, p. 29); Gurobi struggles beyond 2000 trees (p. 11); large-scale part is a "proof of concept" (pp. 12, 18); run time can be cut only by loosening optimality (p. 29). Future: constraints for domain knowledge and safety, tree models in BO for chemical engineering (p. 20). Not addressed: proof that distance tracks error; choice of ζ beyond preliminary studies (p. 8).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: trained tree ensemble written as MIP constraints and solved globally (pp. 6-8); heuristic start + structure-exploiting B&B for large models (pp. 11-12); concession that Gurobi is the better choice at moderate size (p. 19); public stand-in data set (UCI concrete, p. 18) with BASF coauthors (p. 1); step 4 "keep the optimiser near the data" appears here as a distance-to-data penalty (Equ. 8, p. 10), not the PCA subspace named in SKILL.md.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (component isolation): ENTMOOT-RND separates the uncertainty measure from the global acquisition optimiser (pp. 16-17, 22).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: same seeds, shared initial design, fixed hyperparameters, losses reported (pp. 14, 17-18), but competitors run at defaults rather than strengthened, and the metric is best objective per evaluation with run time relegated to an appendix (p. 29).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: two hyperparameters (κ, ζ) admitted up front ("only introduces a maximum of two additonal hyperparameters", p. 2) and held fixed across all tests (pp. 14, 22), but κ is borrowed from SKOPT's default and ζ justified only by preliminary studies (pp. 8, 22): no robustness sweep in the BO comparisons.
- New-pattern candidate: "same-samples control": a variant that shares the competitor's random samples and differs in one component (p. 22).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Validate a heuristic uncertainty proxy by flipping its sign into a penalty and showing model error falls monotonically as its weight rises (Fig. 5, p. 15; Equ. 15, p. 24).
- Replace per-data-point constraints by k-means centres to keep a nearest-point MIP small, and use α=0 at centres for a free feasible start (pp. 11-12).
- Report ub/lb trajectories per hour for the tailored B&B and the commercial solver side by side (Table 1, p. 19).

**Quotes**:
- "While Gurobi 9 is still the best choice for small and moderate instances, i.e. instances that commonly occur in design of experiments, we want to highlight that algorithmic elements exploiting the underlying structure of tree-based models can help when scaling the optimization efforts." (p. 19)
- "We do not claim that these hyperparameters make sense for the particular dataset, the point is to show how tailored branch-and-bound algorithms can be used to make ENTMOOT more scalable." (p. 28)

### S040 · Between steps: Intermediate relaxations between big-M and convex hull formulations (CPAIOR 2021, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-78230-6_19; read as arXiv:2101.12708v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-14 read in full; reference list pp. 14-16 skimmed). Version read: arXiv v1 preprint (p. 1); the published CPAIOR pagination is not used here.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: J. Kronqvist (corresponding), C. Tsay (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: first P-split paper (Method 2's main case); later journal version and NeurIPS partition-based ReLU work not in this batch.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Enters from a trade-off every MIP modeller knows: convex hull is sharp but extended formulations copy each variable per disjunct; big-M is small but weak (p. 1); plus the "folklore observation" (credited to Anderson et al.) "that extended convex hull formulations tend to perform worse than expected" (p. 2). The goal is explicitly not the convex hull but "a family of relaxations that approximate the convex hull ... using a smaller problem formulation" (p. 2). Problem class: disjunctions of convex additively separable constraints over a convex compact X (Equ. 1, Assumptions 1-3, p. 2).
  - Transferable: open with the two textbook extremes and a folklore observation, then state that the target is the space between them, not either endpoint (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Split each disjunct constraint Σ_i h_i(x_i) ≤ b into P partial sums bounded by auxiliary α_s with Σ_s α_s ≤ b (Equ. 2, p. 3), treat the partial-sum constraints as global, keep only the α-constraints inside the disjunction (Equ. 4, p. 4), then take the extended convex hull of that small disjunction (P-split formulation, p. 4). Bounds on α come from min/max of the partial sums over X; weak bounds are valid but weaken the relaxation (Equ. 3, p. 3). Size: P·(|D|²+1) continuous and |D| binary variables, independent of n, versus |D|·n for the extended hull (Remark 1, p. 5). Non-extended realisation for two-term disjunctions (Theorem 3, p. 7).
  - Transferable: move most variables out of the disjunction by aggregating them into a few bounded auxiliaries, and disaggregate only those (pp. 2-4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 1 convex additively separable g_k with non-empty disjuncts; Assumption 2 boundedness on X; both "needed for the P-split formulation to be valid and result in a convex MIP" (p. 2). Assumption 3 (|C_l| << n) "characterizes problem structures favorable" for the method, a practical not a validity assumption (pp. 2-3). Assumption 1 "could easily be relaxed to partially additively separable functions" (p. 3). Hierarchy needs "independent bounds" (Definition 2, p. 8), true in general for linear disjunctions over a box (p. 8). Derivations use one constraint per disjunct for simplicity (p. 3).
  - Transferable: label each assumption as validity-critical versus favourable-structure (pp. 2-3).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Lemma 1 (projection of P-split representation equals the disjunction) by contradiction (p. 4); Proposition 1 correctness for integer λ (p. 5); Theorem 1 1-split ≡ big-M via Fourier-Motzkin elimination of disaggregated variables, recovering the smallest valid M = ᾱ − b (pp. 5-6); Theorem 2 n-split = convex hull for affine h via a bijective linear map Γ/Ψ between x and α̃ spaces (pp. 6-7), with the remark that it fails for nonlinear h (p. 6); Theorem 3 non-extended two-term realisation by repeated Fourier-Motzkin, each step doubling constraints (pp. 7-8); Corollary 1 (P+1 ⊇ P constraints under independent bounds) and Corollary 2 hierarchy big-M → hull for linear two-term disjunctions (p. 8). Necessity of the independent-bounds hypothesis shown by the worked example (ex-1), where the 2-split contains a weaker copy (2-s3) of the 1-split constraint and the relaxations "do not form a proper hierarchy" (p. 9, Fig. 1).
  - Transferable: prove endpoint equivalences by Fourier-Motzkin elimination, then show with a 4-variable example exactly which hypothesis breaks monotonicity (pp. 5-9).
- **D5 Experiments**: Three classes chosen to satisfy Assumptions 1-3 and "known to be challenging" (p. 10): K-means clustering (G2 and MNIST data, Equ. 23), P_ball assignment, ReLU NNs trained on Ackley/Rastrigin (pp. 10-11; Table 1, p. 11). Gurobi 9.0.3, 1800 s limit; Gurobi parameters fixed (MIPFocus=3, Cuts=1, MIQCPMethod=1) because default automatic selection "caused large variations"; PreMIQCPForm=2 given to the convex hull only, since it helped the hull and hurt the others (p. 11). Smallest valid M and tight α bounds for clustering and P_ball; interval-arithmetic bounds for NNs (p. 11). Partitions by ordered index only (p. 11). Results: time and nodes for P ∈ {2,...,32} (or 14-392 for MNIST) against both extremes, timeouts shown as ">" with node counts (Table 2, p. 13). P-split beats big-M on all clustering and P_ball instances and beats the hull on time for a wide range of P "in all but one" instance (p. 12). Losses reported: on NNs the reduction in nodes is small and non-monotone and big-M (1-split) wins on time for NN 2 and NN 3 (p. 12; Table 2, p. 13), explained by weak interval bounds and Gurobi's big-M handling (p. 12).
  - Transferable: fix solver parameters across formulations to remove strategy noise, and give the baseline any setting that helps only the baseline (p. 11).
- **D6 Positioning**: Distinguishes itself from the line of work on computationally efficient hull formulations and convex-hull derivation hierarchies (Balas, Lovász-Schrijver, Sherali-Adams, Ruiz-Grossmann): "our primary goal is not to generate the convex hull" (p. 2). Uses Anderson et al. as both motivation and corroboration (pp. 2, 12). Applications positioned via clustering, classification, NN optimisation, coverage (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Lemma openly labelled trivial ("The property is rather simple, but for completeness we have stated it as a lemma", p. 4). Negative statements kept: 1-split has "no clear advantages" over big-M (p. 6); Theorem 2 fails for nonlinear functions (p. 6). Results paragraph names the exception class before the conclusion (p. 12). Conclusion is one paragraph with no future-work list (p. 13).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: a parameterised formulation family with proved endpoints and a conditional hierarchy, plus computation. Limits stated: hierarchy only for two-term linear disjunctions with independent bounds (p. 8); nonlinear n-split is not the hull (p. 6, p. 9); weak bounds weaken everything (pp. 3, 12); NN case favours big-M (p. 12). Not stated: how to choose P or the partition (only ordered-index partitions tested, p. 11).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (core case): two extremes plus folklore (pp. 1-2); interpolating knob P (p. 3); endpoints proved (Theorems 1-2, pp. 5-6) and hierarchy under stated conditions (Corollaries 1-2, p. 8); sweep across P and classes against both extremes on the same instances (Table 2, p. 13); robustness claim "in general robust towards the choice of P" (p. 12). ⚠ Variant on step 5: this version uses ordered-index partitions only, with no random-partition or deliberately weak control (p. 11), and gives no rule for choosing P.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: strengthened baseline (PreMIQCPForm=2 for the hull only, p. 11), harmonised solver settings (p. 11), exact solver version and hardware (p. 11), losses on NN instances written in (p. 12).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: evaluated inside one commercial MIP solver on three classes, with the class-split verdict (clustering/P_ball vs NN, p. 12), but no default decision because this is a formulation, not a solver component.
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (weak): ReLU NN optimisation as one of the target classes (pp. 10-11).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Count added variables as a function of |D| and n to show when the new formulation is smaller than the extended hull (Remark 1, p. 5).
- Use a tiny 4-variable disjunction with a figure of the relaxed sets for P = 1, 2, 4 to show both the tightening and where the hierarchy fails (pp. 8-9, Fig. 1).
- Shade cells where the intermediate formulation strictly beats both extremes, and mark "NA" where P exceeds the terms per disjunction (Table 2 caption, p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "The default (automatic) parameter selection in Gurobi caused large variations in the results that were due to different solution strategies rather than differences between formulations." (p. 11)
- "Note that the P-split formulations are in general robust towards the choice of P." (p. 12)

### S006 · OMLT: Optimization & machine learning toolkit (Journal of Machine Learning Research 2022, arXiv:2202.02414)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-5 read in full; reference list pp. 5-8 read). Version read: arXiv v2 (15 Nov 2022) in JMLR 23 layout (p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: F. Ceccon and J. Jalving (equal first), J. Haddad (Sandia), A. Thebelt, C. Tsay, C. D. Laird (CMU; equal senior with Misener) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: toolkit stage of the trees → ENTMOOT → OMLT line; joint Imperial-Sandia-CMU software.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Translating trained NNs and GBTs into solver-ready formulations is "tedious and error-prone" (p. 2); use cases from both CS (neural acquisition functions, NN verification) and engineering (surrogates replacing constraints) (pp. 1-2). Entry is a tooling gap, not a mathematical one.
- **D2 Core idea and design**: ONNX (and Keras) as input interface to inherit Keras/PyTorch/TensorFlow models (p. 2); Pyomo blocks encapsulate the surrogate so users only link inputs/outputs (p. 3); OmltBlock delegates formulation generation to a PyomoFormulation object; users may supply scaling and variable bounds that ONNX/Keras lack but some formulations need (p. 3); for NNs an intermediate NetworkDefinition "acts as the gateway to several alternative mathematical optimization formulations" (p. 3). Formulations shipped: GBT big-M (Mišić 2020; Mistry et al. 2021), full-space vs reduced-space smooth NN (Schweidtmann-Mitsos), ReLU big-M (Anderson et al.), complementarity (Yang et al.), partition (Tsay et al., dense layers only) (pp. 3-4). Users override DEFAULT ACTIVATION CONSTRAINTS to add their own (p. 4).
  - Transferable: put an intermediate representation between the ML file format and the optimisation formulation so each new formulation is written once against it (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Supports ReLU, linear, softplus, sigmoid, tanh; dense and convolutional layers (pp. 3-4, footnote 3). Bounds and scaling are user-supplied and required by some formulations (p. 3). Depends on Pyomo's AD via the AMPL solver library for smooth activations (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a (software paper; no theorems).
- **D5 Experiments**: No benchmark. One quantitative illustration: the ReLU notebook switches "(in ≤ 1 line of code)" between complementarity, big-M and partition formulations with 248, 308 and 428 constraints (p. 2). Use cases are shipped as notebooks (MNIST dense/CNN verification, auto-thermal reformer from IDAES) (p. 2).
- **D6 Positioning**: Feature-coverage comparison with MeLOn, JANOS, reluMIP, OptiCL and gurobi-machinelearning: each covers a subset (one model type, one formulation, one solver) (p. 4). Concedes that dedicated verifiers such as Beta-CROWN could address the MNIST verification notebooks effectively, while related tasks (minimally distorted adversaries, lossless compression) cannot be handled by them (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Eight pages in the JMLR open-source format: use cases, library design, related work, outlook. Key rhetorical move: re-framing rival formulations, including the authors' own partition formulation competing with big-M, as user-selectable alternatives (p. 4). Funding and partner credit itemised (p. 5).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution layer: software. Limits visible in text: partition formulation dense-only in OMLT 1.0 (p. 4); no computational comparison of the formulations it offers; no guidance on which formulation to pick. Outlook: extend any Pyomo model with NNs/GBTs (p. 4); no explicit future-work list.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (step 7): one interface for NN and GBT models with switchable formulations (pp. 3-4); collaboration across Imperial, Sandia and CMU with equal-contribution senior authorship (p. 1), matching the stop rule about external co-maintainers; ENTMOOT simplified by reusing the GBT formulation (p. 3).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (step 8 "ship the family as a switch"): ReluPartitionFormulation offered next to big-M and complementarity (p. 4).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (default follows evidence, not authorship): the authors' own partition formulation is one alternative, not the default (p. 4).
- New-pattern candidate: "concede the specialised tool's turf, claim the adjacent tasks it cannot do" (Beta-CROWN vs adversary/compression tasks, p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- In a software paper, quantify the user-facing saving with one concrete count (formulation sizes switched in ≤ 1 line, p. 2) instead of a benchmark the paper cannot fairly run.
- Write the related-work section as a coverage comparison (model types × input formats × formulations × solvers) (p. 4).

**Quotes**:
- "In OMLT, competing optimization formulations become alternatives: users can switch between the Section 3 formulations and find the best for a specific application." (p. 4)
- "OMLT 1.0 saves the time needed to code/debug each optimization formulation." (p. 2)

### S014 · Maximizing information from chemical engineering data sets: Applications to machine learning (Chemical Engineering Science 2022, doi:10.1016/j.ces.2022.117469; read as arXiv:2201.10035v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-21 body read in full; reference list pp. 21-34 skimmed). Version read: arXiv v1 preprint (p. 1).
**Role**: core (review article; the authors' own voice, Misener corresponding) · **Coauthors**: A. Thebelt and J. Wiebe (co-first), J. Kronqvist, C. Tsay (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: a positioning review written from the ML-meets-PSE period; frames her group's tree, GP-robust and NN-verification work inside a data taxonomy.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Starts from a contrarian claim: AI's impact on chemical engineering is documented, "But classical machine learning approaches may be weak for many chemical engineering applications" (abstract, p. 1). Entry is a 2×2 variance-volume diagram: the high-variance/high-volume quadrant is where classical ML works and is excluded; the low/low quadrant is excluded as needing new data; the review covers the two off-diagonal quadrants plus noisy/corrupt/missing and restricted data (pp. 1-3, Fig. 1).
  - Transferable: organise a review by the property of the input that breaks the standard method (here, data characteristics), and say which cells you exclude and why (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Four data characteristics (high variance/low volume; low variance/high volume; noisy/corrupt/missing; restricted with physics-based limits) (p. 2-3). For each: why it arises in practice (§2: limited process understanding, operational limits, control, resources, hardware, pp. 4-6), applications (§x.1), and how literature addresses it (§x.2) (pp. 6-21). Qualifiers "high" and "low" are declared application-relative (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: n/a as theory; framing assumptions: data that seem "information-poor" can be enriched by engineering knowledge, classical AI and new interface research (p. 3, p. 21). Distinguishes a "data perspective" (fix the data) from an "uncertainty perspective" (decide despite it) for low-quality data (pp. 15-16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a (review; no proofs).
- **D5 Experiments**: none (review). Two short "real-world examples": PLS for powder composition (García Muñoz and Torres) and BASF catalyst optimisation over tree ensembles (Mistry et al.) (p. 6).
- **D6 Positioning**: Lists earlier reviews (AI, ML, optimisation, surrogate, hybrid, latent-variable) and claims to complement them "by showing how different data characteristics arise" (p. 1). Self-citations placed as examples in BO (Mistry et al., Thebelt et al.), robust optimisation with GPs (Wiebe et al.), NN verification (Botoeva et al.), and global solvers including ANTIGONE next to BARON and SCIP (pp. 9, 17, 20-21).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Each application paragraph ends with numbered challenges "(1) ... (2) ... (3)" (e.g. pp. 7, 8, 10, 11, 15, 18). Gaps admitted directly: one-class classification "could also be used ... but the authors are not yet aware of any such applications" (p. 19). Hedged scope statements on qualifiers (p. 3). Conclusion restates the thesis in one paragraph (p. 21).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: a taxonomy and challenge list for researchers "at the interface between chemical engineering and computer science" (p. 21). Limits: no quantitative synthesis or systematic search protocol stated; future challenges are the numbered lists per section rather than a separate agenda.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: §6.2 "Optimizing over ML models with constraints" states the programme (ReLU nets as MILPs, global optimisation of GBTs and sigmoidal nets, then pass the algebraic model to ANTIGONE/BARON/SCIP) (pp. 20-21); BASF tree-ensemble example (p. 6).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: "insider information" appears as domain knowledge entering data-driven models (hybrid modelling, physics-informed losses, chemical know-how in PLS) (pp. 6, 9, 19-20), not as automatic structure detection.
- Heuristic 10 evidence (not a Method): uncertainty perspective that decides despite uncertainty, incl. data-driven robust optimisation (pp. 16-17). Heuristic 1 evidence: the whole review is organised by where classical ML assumptions break (pp. 1-3, 21).
- New-pattern candidate: "taxonomy by broken input property with excluded cells" (Fig. 1, p. 2).

- For a review, end every application paragraph with a numbered list of open challenges so the paper doubles as a research agenda (pp. 7-18).
- Name both a "data" and an "uncertainty" perspective for the same defect and sort the literature by which one it takes (pp. 15-16).

**Quotes**:
- "We do not discuss the low variance, low volume quadrant: such data are likely not useful for machine learning, and engineering methods should be used to generate additional data." (p. 2)
