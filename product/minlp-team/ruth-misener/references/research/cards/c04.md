### S003 · Global optimization advances in mixed-integer nonlinear programming, MINLP, and constrained derivative-free optimization, CDFO (European Journal of Operational Research 2016, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2015.12.018)

**Read level**: full. Main text pp. 1-29 and all tables pp. 30-62 read in full; pp. 63-65 hold only figure captions (the figures have no text layer); the reference list pp. 66-105 was skimmed, mostly by searching for self-citations (Misener, Floudas, Boukouvala entries).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Fani Boukouvala (first author, Texas A&M), Christodoulos A. Floudas (corresponding author, Texas A&M) (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: written from Imperial Computing under a Royal Academy of Engineering Fellowship (pp. 1, 29), after GloMIQO (2012/13) and ANTIGONE (2014), before the tree-ensemble/ENTMOOT/OMLT line. Which sections are Misener's is not stated. The MINLP sections (§5, §8, Tables 6-8, 10-11, 13) cite her solvers throughout and the CDFO sections cite Boukouvala's own work (pp. 23, 38). Reading the MINLP part as mainly hers is an inference, unconfirmed.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The review is entered through two literatures that "apparently reference significantly different applications". The aim is to show that MINLP and CDFO "can benefit significantly from each other" or be used together on hybrid problems (p. 3). For MINLP the entry is historical: Floudas's group gave "the first, practically-applicable deterministic global optimization algorithms" ≈25 years earlier, and recent work on algorithms, frameworks and models has made global MINLP "promising for real-world use" (p. 2). For CDFO the entry is the three cases where derivatives are "available but deceptive", "prohibitively expensive" or "completely unavailable" (p. 2). The review declares an applications-first lens: "This manuscript also concentrates heavily on applications" (p. 3); "it is absolutely critical to examine deterministic global optimization of MINLP through the lens of applications" (p. 9).
  - Transferable technique: before surveying two neighbouring fields, list the concrete mechanisms by which each could feed the other: a decomposition with an MINLP lower level and a DFO upper level, shared penalty/barrier/filter devices, and surrogate fitting that is itself a global optimisation problem (p. 3). This turns "related fields" into a research agenda.
- **D2 Core idea and organisation**: The central claim for MINLP is that global MINLP "is tractable via many heterogeneous algorithms pieced together into a framework" (p. 2). So the review is organised by framework component, not by paper: reformulation and preprocessing (§5.2), convex underestimators (§5.3), cutting planes (§5.3.2), branching (§5.4), FBBT/OBBT (§5.5), heuristics (§5.6) and numerical issues (§5.7). Figure 2 places each contribution in a branch-and-cut framework (pp. 12, 63). The design stance is explicit: "Here we advocate an all-of-the-above approach" (p. 11). The tools it values most are hybrid algorithms that "opportunistically exploit the tightest relaxation at each node" (p. 5) and relaxations that "compete in real-time" (p. 13). ANTIGONE is placed as the implementation of this stance: its term classes "inherit from more general base classes of terms" (polymorphic programming, p. 13), and it "couples a hybrid relaxation strategy with polymorphic programming techniques to admit many possible relaxations" (p. 27). GloMIQO is placed as the structure-exploiting MIQCQP solver: it disaggregates bilinear terms, uses redundant linear constraints, and "may add bilinear terms to the model formulation to create tight reformulation-linearization technique relaxations" (p. 12). CDFO is organised on three axes: local vs global search, direct vs model-based, and deterministic vs stochastic (pp. 16-17). Figure 4 marks the two steps where MINLP could help CDFO: parameter estimation and optimisation of the fitted model (pp. 21, 65).
  - Transferable technique: organise a solver literature by its place in one algorithmic whole (a framework figure plus one table per component, Tables 6-8, pp. 45-51). Each contribution is then judged by the gap it fills in the pipeline, not in isolation.
  - Transferable technique: index a relaxation literature as term type × relaxation strategy × references (Table 6, pp. 45-49). The empty cells and the "Only in: <solver>" footnotes (Table 1, p. 30) show at once which terms only one code supports.
- **D3 Assumptions and models**: The paper defines MINLP with the standing assumption that "it is possible to infer finite bounds on the variables x and that the image of fi is finite on x" (p. 4). It then defines the nested subclasses MIQCQP, MIQCP, MIQP, box-constrained MIQP, BQP and QAP (p. 5) and MISO/mixed-integer polynomial (p. 6). For CDFO it separates known constraints from unknown (black-box) constraints and states that "no assumptions can be made about the form of the objective f0 and unknown constraints fi" (p. 8). It defines black-box and grey-box (grey-box = access to "a subset of the model equations", p. 10). Local DFO convergence holds "under certain smoothness and differentiability assumptions" (p. 17). Discrete local DFO needs "a user-defined criterion for local optimality with respect to the discrete variables" (p. 19).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: No new proofs; the devices are stated or cited only. (i) Sufficient convergence conditions for spatial B&B, after Horst and Tuy: every unfathomed node can be refined, and the relaxation converges to the original problem as refinement goes to infinity (p. 13). (ii) The convex hull is the tightest convex underestimator, and the convex relaxation of each term is generally not the tightest relaxation of the whole problem. This is the argument for multi-term and multivariable relaxations (p. 14, citing Westerlund et al. 2011). (iii) The cluster problem, met by "minding relaxation convergence rate" (p. 16). (iv) On the CDFO side: geometry measures (cosine measure of positive spanning sets, normalised simplex volume, poisedness), stationarity tied to the step/mesh size going to zero (p. 17), and asymptotic convergence of global surrogate methods "in the limit of infinite samples" (p. 21).
  - Transferable technique: when you write a new relaxation, check it against the two Horst-Tuy conditions as stated on p. 13 (refinability, convergence under refinement) before you claim it can sit inside a convergent B&B.
- **D5 Experiments / test suites**: No new computations. The MINLP test suite has 2571 problems (Table 13, pp. 60-61). It mixes minlp.org application classes with their counts and sources (for example Pooling 19, Crude Oil Scheduling 24, Water Treatment Network 18), MIQCQP classes (BoxQP 90, QAP 67, randomly generated QCQP 135) and full libraries (GLOBALLib 368, MINLPLib 249, convex/nonconvex PrincetonLib 459/657). The cases have "1 – 17,136 nonlinear terms". Full results on this set are referred to Misener and Floudas (2014a), i.e. ANTIGONE (p. 28). The CDFO suite (Table 14, p. 62) applies an explicit size filter: library problems are counted only if they do not exceed "a total of 300 in variables (continuous or discrete) or constraints", described as "a reasonable bound for the current capabilities" (p. 28). The review also says why no consistent CDFO benchmark exists: expensive-model methods are tested in low dimension and pattern-search methods in high dimension (p. 28).
  - Transferable technique: when you assemble a benchmark from public libraries, state the class-by-class provenance (source paper per class, Table 13) and an explicit inclusion cutoff tied to the current state of the art (≤300 variables/constraints, p. 28). Then a reader can rebuild the set.
- **D6 Positioning**: For software it extends the earlier surveys rather than competing with them: Bussieck and Vigerske (2010) is "most completely" the solver survey, and Belotti et al. (2013b) and D'Ambrosio and Lodi (2013) are acknowledged; "We augment their work with Table 11" (pp. 26-27). Its application table is set apart from Belotti et al. (2013b), whose list includes MIP formulations, local techniques and convex nonlinearities. Every entry in this review is a nonconvex NLP/MINLP "solved using deterministic global optimization" (p. 9). The DFO software side defers to Rios and Sahinidis (2013) (p. 27), and stochastic methods are explicitly not covered comprehensively (p. 23). It is generous to rival codes. BARON, Couenne, SCIP, αSGO and LINDO each get their own algorithmic rows in Table 11 (pp. 53-55), and SCIP's and BARON's ongoing extensions are named (p. 27). Self-placement is strong but factual. ANTIGONE and GloMIQO are listed as two of the four newest code bases (p. 27). A footnote states their relation: "ANTIGONE performs equivalently to GloMIQO when all nonlinearities are quadratic" (p. 55). The lineage is foregrounded on p. 2, where the Floudas-group algorithms of 1990-93 are called the "first, practically-applicable" ones.
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contribution sentences come in the first person plural and state a stance: "Here we advocate an all-of-the-above approach" (p. 11); "In our view, it is absolutely critical..." (p. 9). Firm scope advice is given twice, once mid-paper (p. 9) and again in the conclusion: "if a problem can be entirely mathematically formulated in any of the forms which are provided in the MINLP definitions, the use of an appropriate MINLP solver is suggested" (p. 29). Hedging is placed exactly where knowledge is missing: "it is currently unclear (for the general case) what are the best automatic reformulations" (p. 12). The applicability of most MINLP heuristics to global optimisation "has not been established" (p. 16). The connections section ends with three numbered open questions (p. 26). Tables carry compact type codes (nonlinearity Q/P/S/A/T, pp. 31-35; BC/GC/LC and DS/MS/SS, pp. 36-40) to work as a "quick-access guide" (p. 3). There are a few typos ("diverent", p. 16; "desribed", p. 19).
  - Transferable technique: close a survey-of-connections section with numbered, answerable research questions (p. 26), not a generic "future work" paragraph.
  - Transferable technique: tag every table row with short type codes (problem class, constraint type, search type) and define them in a table footnote (pp. 31, 36), so that a reader can filter by eye.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is organisational, not algorithmic: a component taxonomy of global MINLP (§5, Tables 6-8), a solver-feature table (Table 11), CDFO taxonomies and software list (§6, §9, Table 12), application tables (Tables 2-3) and two test suites (Tables 13-14). The stated limits are specific. The best general automatic reformulations are unknown (p. 12). Few works study heuristics *inside* deterministic global optimisation (pp. 15-16). Floating-point relaxations and sub-solver tolerance clashes can cause numerical trouble (p. 16). Global optimality for CDFO "cannot be mathematically guaranteed within a finite number of steps" (p. 29). Upper-level DFO in decompositions "cannot guarantee global convergence or uniqueness of results" (p. 24). The future directions are concrete. (a) Hybrid MINLP/CDFO solvers that fix the black-box variables, bound the rest with a global MINLP solver and so shrink the DFO search region (pp. 25-26). (b) Surrogates "with error bounding properties" to give estimators for hard MINLP terms such as trigonometric ones (p. 26). (c) Structure and sparsity detection for black boxes via machine learning, sensitivity analysis and statistics (p. 26). (d) Global optimisation of the fitted surrogates and of their parameter estimation, posed as three open questions (p. 26).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (structure detection as "insider information"), evidence ✅. GloMIQO is described as "a major effort to elucidate special structure via reformulations" applied generically (p. 12). Figure 1 shows MIQCQP classes (process networks, computational geometry, MIQP) as graph patterns of bilinear terms (pp. 6-7). The review proposes the same move for black boxes: "incorporation techniques to detect the structure of the problem and the model sparsity", via ML and sensitivity analysis (p. 26).
- Method 4 (judge a component inside the whole solver), evidence ✅. The framing is component-in-the-whole throughout. Effective heuristics for a deterministic framework "are not necessarily the same as effective stand-alone heuristics" (p. 15). The tightest relaxation of each term is not the tightest relaxation of the whole MINLP (p. 14). The ideal implementation "selects appropriate algorithms for any given problem" (p. 11). In a review this is stated, not tested.
- Method 5 (benchmark like an auditor), evidence ✅. The review carries the 2571-problem suite with per-class provenance and points to complete results across it (pp. 28, 60-61). It sets an explicit size cutoff for the CDFO suite (p. 28). No verification protocol is described here (not applicable to a review).
- Method 3 (the trained model is the problem), variant ⚠, stated agenda only. In 2016 the surrogate side is phrased as globally optimising fitted surrogate models and their parameter estimation with MINLP machinery (Fig. 4, p. 21; open questions 1-3, p. 26). The surrogate is still an approximation of a black box, not yet the partner's trained model taken as the problem. Joint text; which author wrote this section is not stated.
- Method 2 (between formulations), variant ⚠, weak. FBBT and OBBT are framed as two extremes, cheap but weak vs demanding but strong: "FBBT and OBBT represent extremes" (p. 15). No interpolating family is proposed.
- Heuristic 7 (do-not-use conditions next to the method), evidence: "when a problem can be sufficiently described by a well-posed, explicit MINLP formulation, then CDFO methods are not recommended" (p. 9; restated p. 29). Stochastic DFO is to be used "as a resort" (p. 23, citing Conn et al.).
- New-pattern candidate: **all-of-the-above heterogeneity, relaxations in competition**: implement many components and let them compete per node or per instance, rather than pick one (pp. 5, 11, 13, 27). This is related to OMLT's switchable formulations (Method 3 step 7), but here the selection is made by the solver, not the user.
- New-pattern candidate: **component-map review**: a literature organised as the parts of one algorithm, with a framework figure and per-component tables (pp. 12, 45-55, 63).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Survey layout: one framework figure (Fig. 2) plus one table per algorithmic component (Tables 6-8) plus a solver × feature table (Table 11), so that every cited contribution has a slot in the pipeline (pp. 12, 45-55).
- Relaxation index: rows by term type, columns by relaxation strategy, with "Only in: <solver>" footnotes recording which terms only one code supports (Tables 1 and 6, pp. 30, 45-49).
- Write a scope sentence that sends the reader to the neighbouring method family when the problem has an explicit formulation (pp. 9, 29).
- Benchmark assembly: per-class source references and counts, plus a stated inclusion cutoff justified by current solver capability (Tables 13-14; p. 28).
- End a connections section with numbered open questions that a thesis could take on directly (p. 26).
- Hybrid decomposition sketch for grey-box problems: fix the black-box variables, get upper and lower bounds from a global MINLP solver, and use them to shrink the DFO search bounds (pp. 25-26).
