## 3. The turns and what triggered them

Each turn gives when, the move, the trigger with its evidence, and what stayed constant. Trigger attributions are [inferred] unless a quotation states the reason.

**T1. 2003–2007 (Purdue): from primal cutting-plane algorithms (the 2005 talk, S16) to group relaxations that use two constraints.**
- Trigger: his advisor's programme, plus a gap he states himself: "All know group based cutting planes using a single constraint to derive cut." (S15, 2006) [stated · P].
- What stayed: lifting, the tool of Richard's thesis (§2.1).

**T2. 2007–2009 (CORE): from group facets to lifting integer non-basic variables into two-row lattice-free cuts.**
- Trigger: a question from his host. Wolsey's "main concern was how to deal with integer non-basic variables" (S26, p. 9) [observed · P].
- What stayed: facets and lifting.

**T3. 2010–2014: exit from the computational side of multi-row cuts.**
- Trigger: his own negative computation [stated · P]: "However such a goal was too ambitious and we have had to considerably restrict it." (S27, p. 2) and "the experiments in this paper suggest that type 2 triangle cuts are not competitive with split cuts." (S27, p. 25).
- In 2009 he had named two obstacles: "With so many cuts, an appropriate tool for cut selection is vital for a successful implementation. Another practical difficulty with this class of cutting planes is that they tend to be dense." (S26, p. 8). These became his next two programmes, sparsity (T7, 2014) and cut selection (T8, 2018). The statements are [stated 2009] and the programmes [practice 2014/2018]; the link is [inferred].
- What stayed: theory papers on the line continue to 2019, for example "Nonunique Lifting of Integer Variables in Minimal Inequalities", SIAM J. Discrete Math. 33 (2019), doi:10.1137/17M1117070.

**T4. 2010–2016: from MILP structure to convex-MINLP structure (closures, duals, integer hulls).**
- Trigger: a funded programme of transfer. The NSF 1030422 abstract says "there are no universally effective general purpose convex MINLP solvers such as those available for Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP)" and "The research objective of this project is to broaden the scope of the following key results known for MILP to the more general domain of convex MINLP" (S12) [stated · P].
- Papers [practice · P]: first doi:10.1007/978-3-642-13036-6_25 (IPCO 2010); then doi:10.1287/moor.1110.0488 (2011), doi:10.1137/110840868 (2012), doi:10.1007/s10107-012-0538-7 (2013), doi:10.1007/s10107-013-0649-9 (2014), doi:10.1137/140982210 (2016) and doi:10.1016/j.disopt.2016.11.001 (2017).
- What stayed: the list of MILP theorems to transfer (CG and split closures, subadditive duality).

**T5. 2011–2017: from pure MILP and convex MINLP to nonconvex MINLP (bilinear programs, pooling).**
- Trigger: industry funding and a student's internship: ExxonMobil support (S28) and Gupte's ExxonMobil internship (per note 04).
- His stated reason for the MILP route: "Since considerable progress has been made in algorithms and state-of-the-art solvers for MILP, these hard constraints can be better dealt with through a MILP solution procedure." (S28, p. 2) [stated · P].
- The analysis paper asks of an empirical success: "Why do these MILP relaxations work so well?" (S29, p. 39) [stated · P]. Papers: doi:10.1287/opre.2015.1357 (2015); doi:10.1007/s10898-016-0434-4 (2017).
- What stayed: MILP machinery as the instrument.

**T6. 2014–2018: into power systems (AC optimal power flow).**
- Trigger: a fresh exactness claim to test, taken up with a student and a power-systems colleague [stated · P]:
  - The 2014 abstract: "It has been recently proven that the semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxation of the optimal power flow problem over radial networks is exact in the absence of generation lower bounds." (S31, p. 1).
  - The introduction: "It was conjectured that under normal operating conditions the SDP relaxation is tight." (p. 2). It cites Lavaei and Low, "Zero Duality Gap in Optimal Power Flow Problem", IEEE Trans. Power Syst. 27 (2012), doi:10.1109/TPWRS.2011.2160974 (S55).

### 9. Turns, as the full texts show them

1. **From group-relaxation theory to closures of convex sets (2010–2014).** The MILP closure question is asked of strictly convex and then compact convex sets, each step naming the obstacle the next removes [S018 p. 2; S042 pp. 1–2; S025 pp. 2–3; D001 p. 2]. This confirms T4 on the texts.
2. **Exit from computational multi-row cuts (2014).** "However such a goal was too ambitious and we have had to considerably restrict it." [S052 p. 2]; type 2 triangle cuts not competitive with split cuts for typical instances [S052 p. 25]; density already named as the obstacle in 2009: "Another practical difficulty with this class of cutting planes is that they tend to be dense." [S079 p. 8]. Confirms T3.
3. **Pooling (2013–2017), back in 2020–2026 as rank-one and QCQP convexification.** [S002 pp. 2, 8–12; S014 pp. 1, 5–6; D002 pp. 2, 12–15] → [S051 pp. 1–9; S032 p. 2; S071 pp. 1–5; S077 pp. 1–2; S129 pp. 1–2]; the 2026 water-network paper continues the pooling line [S109 pp. 2, 10–14]. Links T5 and T9.
4. **Power systems twice (2016–2018, 2022–2026).** [S006 pp. 1–3; S001 pp. 1–4; S009 p. 3; S005 p. 2; S008 pp. 5–18] → [S062 pp. 2, 4–11; S058 pp. 1, 11; S047 pp. 3, 9–11; S080 pp. 2–3]. T6 ends in 2018 for OPF relaxations, but power-system work resumes in 2022.
5. **Worst case → random instances (2020–2026).** "In order to avoid worst-case lower bounds, a standard idea is to consider random instances." [S031 p. 2; D003 pp. 1–3]; carried to dual decomposition [S120 p. 1]. Refines T8.
6. **Cut theory → solver rules (2018, 2024–2025).** A feasibility-pump rule implemented and recommended in 2018 [S061 pp. 12–13]; branching rules meant as experts for imitation: "one of the motivations for this paper is to discover simple rules that can improve over FSB, which can then be used as expert for machine learning algorithms to imitate." [S130 p. 2]; the GMIC heuristic [S082 p. 6].
7. **Facets as yardstick → doubt about yardsticks (2010 → 2018–2026).** [S019 pp. 6, 20] → [S011 pp. 18–19; S117 p. 32; S122 p. 22].
8. **Re-solved operational instances (2020–2026).** A new line not in §3: the same model re-solved with new data, with a proved carry-over license and reuse aimed at the dual side [S044 pp. 1–3, 18; S098 pp. 1–2; S082 pp. 1–2, 6, 16–17; S067 pp. 2, 7, 40]. It became Method 6 of SKILL.md (08 §4).
9. **Duality and decomposition with a student (2024–2026).** [S097 pp. 2–5; S117 p. 2; S120 pp. 1–4; S125 pp. 1–4; D005 pp. 1–2; S112 pp. 1–5]. Confirms T12.
10. **Network design with Sahinidis (2024–2026).** Primal-side frameworks with the dual bound from a global solver [S059 pp. 9–15; S075 pp. 1, 11, 23; S109 pp. 2, 17–19].
