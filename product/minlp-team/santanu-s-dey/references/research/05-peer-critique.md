## 0. Summary for the synthesizer

- **No retractions, errata, or published comment–reply pairs** exist for any of Dey's papers in the records checked: Crossref for 97 DOIs, a Crossref author query, and zbMATH. The eight zbMATH reviews (2010–2016) are descriptive and contain no criticism. Peer critique instead comes through **follow-up papers**, which subsume, sharpen, re-benchmark or limit his results. Most of it comes from two rival groups: the AC optimal power flow (OPF) relaxation groups (Coffrin–Hijazi–Van Hentenryck and Los Alamos; Bienstock; Laird–Scott) and the infinite-group-relaxation school (Basu–Hildebrand–Köppe).
- **One supporting statement was shown incorrect.** Two Kocuk–Dey–Sun papers used an equivalence of the real and complex SDP relaxations of OPF, citing Taylor (2015). Bienstock and Villagra (2025) call the cited statement "incorrect", but in the same paper they prove the inequality direction that the comparisons need (§1.2). **One reformulation was proved only to be a relaxation.** The 2016 cycle-basis reformulation was "only proven to be a relaxation"; exactness (with added linear constraints) was proved later, in a Georgia Tech thesis whose committee included Dey (§1.4).
- **Limits that others named:**
  - SOC-based OPF relaxations are hard for solvers at large scale (§2.1).
  - The radial test library is "a significant departure" from real meshed networks (§2.2).
  - The OBBT in the spatial branch-and-cut is partial and not competitive at the root (§2.3).
  - The Dey–Gupte pooling heuristic only fits standard pooling networks (§2.5).
  - Partly strengthened two-row cuts, from lifting alone or from S-free sets alone, add almost nothing (§2.8).
  - The Morán–Dey–Vielma strong-duality condition excludes binaries and equalities (§1.5).
  - The NP-hardness of training a single ReLU neuron needs growing input dimension (§2.10).
- **Replications.** Imperial College groups (Misener) independently confirmed the Dey–Gupte pooling results twice (2016, 2021/22). Open-source software machine-checks the Dey–Richard–Li–Miller extremality claims on sample parameters, and its authors extended one family to irrational parameters and from extreme to facet. Rival OPF groups accepted the Kocuk–Dey–Sun counter-evidence to SDP exactness. Friction: one group could not obtain the Dey–Gupte random instances; one application found the integer L-shaped improvements ineffective (§4).
- **Predictions that did not hold.** The shared optimism about multi-row cuts in 2009 (Dey and Tramontani; Weismantel; Wolsey) was followed by negative verdicts: Dey's own (2014) and an independent one (2014/15). "SOCP relaxation is quite good!" (2015) was qualified by Dey's own 2017 paper and later contested by Bienstock and Villagra (2023) (§5).
- **How he answers critique** [practice; the reading is mine]. I found no rebuttals in print. He answers rival methods with head-to-head tables, qualifies his own earlier claims in the next paper, concedes promptly in live Q&A, and helps those who simplify his proofs. He also left a programme (the infinite group relaxation) after a result from the rival school, co-authored by his own collaborator Molinaro, undercut its premise. Much of the sharpest critique is in-house (§7).

### 1.2 An incorrect statement cited in two Kocuk–Dey–Sun papers (Bienstock and Villagra, 2025)

- **What the Dey papers said** [stated · primary]. 2015: "These two SDP relaxations produce the same bound, since the solution of one can be used to derive a solution to the other with the same objective function value. See e.g., Section 3.3 in Taylor (2015) for a formal proof." (Kocuk, Dey, Sun, "Strong SOCP Relaxations for the Optimal Power Flow Problem", author version, OPFCycle.pdf p. 9; doi:10.1287/opre.2016.1489). 2017: "Here, we used real matrices instead of complex matrices for convenience. It is proven in [48] that using real matrices is equivalent to complex matrices." ([48] = J. A. Taylor, *Convex Optimization of Power Systems*, Cambridge University Press 2015; OPFMinor.pdf p. 18; doi:10.1007/s12532-018-0150-9).
- **Critic** [observed · primary · external]. D. Bienstock and M. Villagra (Columbia), "Advanced Cutting-Plane Algorithms for ACOPF", arXiv:2510.21698v1 (24 Oct 2025; the page footer says PSCC 2026). They write that [22] (Taylor 2015) "claims that the matrix variable W in SDP-R is equivalent, up to an orthogonal similarity transformation, to the matrix variable L(X) in SDP-CR", and continue: "This statement has subsequently been cited in works such as [23], [7]. Unfortunately, this statement is incorrect (see Appendix A.2 for a proof). This commonly held yet unproven belief has played a central role in the literature on SDP relaxations, and, more broadly, on convex relaxations of the ACOPF problem." (p. 3; [23] = the 2016 Operations Research paper, [7] = the 2018 Math. Program. Comput. paper). And: "This distinction affects all works comparing convex SDP formulations for ACOPF, particularly those that rely on the presumed strength of SDP-R under the (unproven) assumption that it enforces fundamental inequalities such as the Jabr inequality." (p. 3).
- **Scope of the critique, from the same paper.** B&V immediately add: "However, we have a positive result: below we show that SDP-R is at least as strong as SDP-C." (p. 3; Theorem III.4). They also derive the Jabr inequality from W ⪰ 0 (Corollary III.3). The disproof in v1 is Proposition A.1, which rules out an orthogonal similarity between rank-one W and L(X) because rank L(X) = 2·rank X (Lemma A.2, p. 6). [observed · primary]
- **My reading** [inferred]. The incorrect step is the *cited justification*, a similarity-transformation equivalence. The strength ordering that the Kocuk–Dey–Sun comparisons need (SDP at least as strong as SOC) holds by B&V's own theorem for the real formulation. B&V v1 does not address the reverse direction that Kocuk–Dey–Sun also state ("produce the same bound"), and I did not check it.
- **A second remark in the same paper.** SDP solvers "often fail to fully scale or guarantee valid bounds (invalid bounds are reported in [7], [12])" (p. 1). Here [7], the Kocuk–Dey–Sun paper, is credited as a *reporter* of invalid SDP bounds. In the March 2017 author version I found only the Table 2 footnote "*: Numerical difficulties are encountered for the SDP relaxation, the resuts [sic] from SOCP relaxation is used instead." (OPFMinor.pdf p. 28). The published version was **not read**.
- **Answered?** I found no reply from Dey, Kocuk or Sun. The preprint appeared in October 2025, within the last 12 months.
- **Era and resources.** The rival's experiments ran on "a Apple M2 Pro machine with 16GB RAM" with "Gurobi 11.0.0" (p. 6). The 2015–2017 Kocuk–Dey–Sun papers relied on a then-new textbook for a foundational equivalence they did not prove themselves [inferred].

### 1.3 The Dey–Richard facet definition is a "weak facet" (Basu, Hildebrand and Köppe, 2016)

- **Critic** [observed · primary · external]. A. Basu, R. Hildebrand, M. Köppe, "Light on the infinite group relaxation I/II", 4OR 14 (2016), doi:10.1007/s10288-015-0292-9 and doi:10.1007/s10288-015-0293-8. Read in arXiv:1410.8584v2. "A similar facet definition, which we call a weak facet, is given in [26] and in fact was used in an erroneous proof of the so-called Facet Theorem in [39, Theorem 3]" (p. 8). Here [26] = Dey and Richard, "Facets of two-dimensional infinite group problems", Math. Oper. Res. 33 (2008), doi:10.1287/moor.1070.0283, and [39] = Gomory and Johnson, "T-space and cutting planes" (2003). The footnote: "Gomory and Johnson's original proof actually holds only for weak facets, and not for facets as claimed in [39]." (p. 10). The survey then leaves open: "Open question 2.9. Are the definitions of facets, weak facets, and extreme functions equivalent?" (p. 10).
- **What the critique says and does not say.** The erroneous proof is Gomory and Johnson's, not Dey and Richard's. The point that bears on Dey is definitional: his 2008 notion of "facet" is, in the rival terminology, the weaker notion, and whether it coincides with Gomory–Johnson facets is open [observed · primary].
- **Same survey, subsumption and non-essentiality.** On the 2008 paper: "In fact, their result is a special case of Proposition 3.8 and the simple observation that subadditive, genuinely k-dimensional functions have at least k + 1 slopes or gradient values" (p. 16). On Dey, Richard, Li and Miller (Math. Program. 121, 2010, doi:10.1007/s10107-008-0229-6): "The use of Theorem 6.3 does not seem to be essential, however; the extremality of all of these functions can also be established by following the algorithm of subsection 7.1." (p. 27). [observed · primary · external]
- **The same school, in software.** The `cutgeneratingfunctionology` docstrings (GitHub mkoeppe/cutgeneratingfunctionology, commit c54aa91, 2026-03-30, file `igp/extreme_functions_in_literature.sage`) say this about Dey–Richard–Li–Miller's backward 3-slope family: "In [40], they require that f, bkpt are rational numbers. The proof is based on interpolation of finite cyclic group extreme functions(cf. [8]), so it needs rational numbers. But in fact, by analysing covered intervals and using the condition f < bkpt <= (1+f)/4 < 1, one can prove that the function is extreme without assuming f, bkpt being rational numbers." They also say, for all three DRLM families: "(Although only extremality has been established in literature, the same proof shows that) drlm_… is a facet." [observed · primary · external]. Here a limit of the proof technique (rational data only) was removed by others, and the result was strengthened from extreme to facet.
- **Answered?** Not directly. Dey's group-relaxation papers taper after 2011 (note 01 §1.10). In 2018 Dey and Molinaro accepted that the rival school's density result casts doubt on the facet criterion itself: "However, interestingly, for the infinite group relaxation (which is infinite dimensional), it was recently proved that the facet-defining inequalities form a dense set within the set of minimal inequalities [48, 163]. This casts doubts on whether being a facet-defining inequality is really useful in this context." (Dey and Molinaro, doi:10.1007/s10107-018-1302-4, ThCutSelection.pdf p. 7) [stated · primary]. Here [48] = Basu, Hildebrand and Molinaro, "Minimal cut-generating functions are nearly extreme" (IPCO 2016, doi:10.1007/978-3-319-33461-5_17; Math. Program. 172, doi:10.1007/s10107-017-1153-4), with Dey's own collaborator Molinaro as co-author, and [163] = Lebair and Basu, arXiv:1708.04344.
- **Era.** The 2006–2010 extremality proofs rest on interpolation of finite cyclic-group extreme functions (per the docstring), and were presumably done by hand [inferred]. From 2014–2015 the rival school checked such claims with software [practice of others, primary].

### 1.5 The Morán–Dey–Vielma strong-duality condition has "two main drawbacks" (Kocuk and Morán, 2019; in-house)

- **Critics** [observed · primary · in-house]. B. Kocuk and D. A. Morán R., both former Dey PhD students (Kocuk 2016, co-advised with X. A. Sun; Morán 2014; note 04 R1). ## 2. Limits of applicability and blind spots identified by others

### 2.1 AC-OPF: SOC relaxations at scale (Bienstock and Villagra, 2023/2025)

- **Critic** [observed · primary · external]. D. Bienstock and M. Villagra, "Accurate linear cutting-plane relaxations for ACOPF", Math. Program. Comput. 18 (2025) 79–133, doi:10.1007/s12532-025-00287-x, read in arXiv:2312.04251v3. "Strong lower bounds are available through second-order cone (SOC) relaxations [46, 49]; however all solvers do struggle when handling such relaxations for large or even medium cases (see [31]; we will provide additional evidence for this point)." (p. 2; [49] = Kocuk–Dey–Sun 2016). "While on the one hand the SOC relaxation is strong, it also yields formulations that, in the case of large ACOPF instances, are very challenging even for the best solvers." (p. 7).
- **Against what Dey claimed** [stated · primary]. "the best one is within 99.96% of the SDP relaxation on average for all the IEEE test cases" (OPFCycle.pdf p. 1), and a 3,375-bus solution certified "within 0.13% of global optimality, all computed in 157.20 seconds on a modest personal computer" (p. 4).
- **Answered?** No reply found. A 2025 conference paper co-authored by Dey still builds on the 2016 arctangent envelopes: "arctangent envelopes proved the most effective in reducing the optimality gap, consistently outperforming the baseline and other cut types, an observation also supported by [10]" (An, Crozier, Dey, Grijalva, NAPS 2025, doi:10.1109/NAPS66256.2025.11272395; *context only*, full text not read) [practice · secondary].
- **Era and resources.** Dey's 2014–2017 tests used a single desktop (i5, 4 GB in 2014; note 01 §1.2) and IEEE/NESTA cases up to 3,375 buses. B&V's 2023–2025 tests use PGLib cases and multi-period instances on modern solvers. The disagreement may be one of scale and era rather than a contradiction in kind [inferred].

## 7. How Dey answers critique [practice unless marked]

1. **No rebuttals or errata in print.** I found no reply, comment, correction or erratum (§1.1).
2. **Head-to-head comparison against the rival.** The 2016 OPF paper measures its relaxations against the QC relaxation instance by instance. It reports where QC wins (3 or 4 of 19 small-angle cases) and attacks the rival's tight-angle-bound assumption as unrealistic (§3, row 1).
3. **Self-qualification in the next paper.** In 2016 he wrote of the group's own 2015 bound tightening: "this procedure can be expensive because we need to solve 4|L| SOCPs, each of the size of the classic SOCP relaxation" (OPFCycle.pdf p. 25) [stated · primary]. In 2017 he qualified the 2016 relaxation against SDP (§5.2). The 2014 multi-row verdict reversed his own 2009 hope (§5.1).
4. **Conceding a premise when rival results undercut it.** 2018: facet-definingness "casts doubts on whether … really useful in this context" (§1.3).
5. **Live concession with a boundary marked** (IPCO 2020, §1.6).
6. **Helping those who simplify or extend his proofs** (Braun–Pokutta acknowledgement, §3).
7. **Critique from his own circle.** The drawbacks of his strong-duality condition were published by two former students (§1.5). The relaxation-only proof and the uncompetitive OBBT were written up in a thesis he examined (§1.4). The density result that cast doubt on facets is co-authored by his main theory collaborator Molinaro (§1.3). Whether he encourages this is not documented [inferred].
8. **Leaving rather than defending.** After 2011 the infinite-group line tapers off (note 01 §1.10) while the rival school subsumes and re-proves its results. He never published a defence [inferred from absence].

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

- **C1. OPF practicality.** Kocuk–Dey–Sun 2015/16: certification "within 0.13% of global optimality, all computed in 157.20 seconds on a modest personal computer" (OPFCycle.pdf p. 4). Bienstock–Villagra 2023: "all solvers do struggle when handling such relaxations for large or even medium cases" (arXiv:2312.04251v3 p. 2).
- **C2. SOCP against SDP, Dey against Dey.** 2015/16: "within 99.96% of the SDP relaxation on average for all the IEEE test cases" (OPFCycle.pdf p. 1). 2017: the same relaxations are "not very competitive against the SDP relaxation, especially for easier instances" (OPFMinor.pdf p. 31). The test sets differ (IEEE/MATPOWER vs NESTA).
- **C3. Who dominates whom in OPF.** Kocuk–Dey–Sun 2016: their relaxations "consistently outperform" QC (OPFCycle.pdf p. 4). Coffrin et al. 2015/17: the Kocuk–Dey–Sun 2014 cut "is a special case of the cuts proposed here" (p. 14). Gopinath et al. 2020: SDP-BT closes 4 more NESTA instances than the Kocuk–Dey–Sun branch-and-cut (p. 5). These compare different objects (cuts, root relaxations, full branch-and-cut) on different hardware.
- **C4. The SDP equivalence.** Kocuk–Dey–Sun: the real and complex SDP relaxations "produce the same bound … See e.g., Section 3.3 in Taylor (2015) for a formal proof" (OPFCycle.pdf p. 9). Bienstock–Villagra: Taylor's statement "is incorrect", yet "SDP-R is at least as strong as SDP-C" (arXiv:2510.21698v1 p. 3). Not adjudicated here.
- **C5. Availability of the pooling instances.** Haugland 2019: "to which we do not have access" (p. 5). Ceccon–Kouyialis–Misener 2016 and Ceccon–Misener 2021 ran "the 70 large-scale standard pooling Dey and Gupte examples" (journal p. 3093) and "50 large scale dense instances (Dey and Gupte, 2015)" (arXiv:2105.01687v1 p. 22). The counts also differ: Ceccon–Kouyialis–Misener 2016 speak of 70 examples, Ceccon–Misener 2021 and Haugland of 50. Not reconciled.
- **C6. Are the gap instances realistic?** Coffrin et al. (p. 2): "realistic test cases can exhibit a non-zero optimality gap with this relaxation [11, 29]". Molzahn et al. (p. 7): the tree topologies of [30] "are a significant departure from the mesh networks used in the standard test cases from which they were derived". Both cite Kocuk–Dey–Sun 2014.
- **C7. Multi-row cuts.** 2009 hope (Dey–Tramontani p. 8; Weismantel p. 9). 2014/15: the negative verdicts by Dey's own team and by Louveaux–Poirrier–Salvagnin.
- **C8. Mixed reception in a single source.** Basu–Hildebrand–Köppe: "Dey and Richard [27] pioneered" (p. 12), against "their result is a special case of Proposition 3.8" (p. 16) and DRLM's Theorem 6.3 "does not seem to be essential" (p. 27).
