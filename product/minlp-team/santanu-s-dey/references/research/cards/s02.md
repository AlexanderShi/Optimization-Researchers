### S096 · Design and verify: a new scheme for generating cutting-planes (IPCO 2011, doi:10.1007/978-3-642-20807-2_12)
**Read level**: full (all of pp. 1-11 of the 2010 draft; the proof of Lemma 4.2, pp. 8-9, skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Sebastian Pokutta (MIT) · **Place in trajectory**: cutting-plane closure theory, before the sparse-cut line; journal version is the source of Heuristic 10.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry from solver practice: generic cuts are derived only to separate the incumbent fractional point, so they are generated "almost blindly" by a black box (p. 2). Question: how much stronger is a cut family if the black box only has to *verify* a proposed inequality cx <= d (show M(P ∩ {cx >= d+1}) is empty) rather than compute it (p. 2)?
  - Transferable: turn "compute" into "verify" and ask how much strength the asymmetry buys; the key inclusion M(P ∩ {cx >= d+1}) ⊆ M(P) ∩ {cx >= d+1} (eq. (1), p. 2) is the whole source of extra power.
- **D2 Key idea**: Define the V-closure ∂M(P) as the intersection of all integer inequalities whose reverse half-space is emptied by M (eq. (2), p. 2); Example 1 shows ∂SC(A3) is empty in one round while split rank is 3 (p. 3). What did not change: the underlying black box M (GC, split, N0, N, N+).
- **D3 Assumptions**: M "admissible" in the axiomatic sense of Pokutta-Schulz [19] (validity, inclusion preservation, homogeneity, coordinate rounding, flips/duplications, substitution independence, short verification; Def. 2.1, p. 4); rational polytopes, mostly in [0,1]^n.
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: (i) Axiomatic transfer: ∂M is "almost admissible" (all axioms except short verification; Thm 2.3, pp. 4-5), with strong homogeneity proved by tilting the cut with a multiple of the face inequality (Lemma 2.2, p. 5). (ii) Support-function comparison over integer directions only (Lemma 3.2, Thm 3.3, pp. 5-6). (iii) Coordinate rounding gives ∂M ⊆ GC and ∂M ⊆ N0 for any admissible M (Thm 3.4, p. 6). (iv) Lifting a separating example by one coordinate turns "M empties P, L does not" into non-refinement of ∂L by ∂M (Prop. 3.7, p. 7); the refinement map is Fig. 1 (p. 6). (v) Lower bounds inherited from M on the hard family A_n: rank >= floor((n-1)/3) (Thm 4.3, Cor 4.4, p. 9); TSP rank Θ(n) (Thm 5.5, p. 11). (vi) Positive: clique, odd hole/antihole/wheel inequalities have V-rank 1 (Thm 5.4, p. 10); in R^2, ∂GC(P) = P_I via maximal lattice-free triangles (Thm 5.6, p. 11). Many proofs omitted "due to lack of space" (pp. 4, 6).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory only (a new closure family and its place in the closure hierarchy). Conclusion claims strength and its limit together (p. 11). Not addressed: how to design candidate inequalities in practice; no algorithm or computation.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the positive "strength" results are paired with lower bounds showing the scheme is "not unrealistically strong" (Thm 4.3, Thm 5.5; pp. 9, 11); Lemma 4.5 gives the matching upper bound n-2 for A_n (p. 9).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: starts from a solver habit (blind black-box cuts, p. 2) but proposes a new paradigm instead of explaining why the habit works.
- Heuristic 10 (design the cut, verify later): this is the IPCO source of that heuristic; the design-then-verify sentence is on p. 2.
- New-pattern candidate: "axiomatic closure comparison" (state a closure as an operator satisfying admissibility axioms, then prove refinements for every admissible operator at once; pp. 4-6).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To compare a new closure with all known ones, prove it inside an axiom system (admissibility) so one theorem covers GC, split, N0, N and N+ (Thm 3.4, p. 6).
- To show a new operator is "strong but not unrealistically so", rerun the standard hard family (A_n, TSP relaxation) and inherit the base operator's lower bound (Thm 4.3, Thm 5.5; pp. 9, 11).

**Quotes**:
- "First we design a useful cutting-plane without considering its validity. Then, once the cutting-plane is designed, we verify whether it is valid." (p. 2)
- "On the other hand, we also show that the verification scheme is not unrealistically strong, as illustrated by Theorem 4.3 and Theorem 5.5." (p. 11)

### S036 · Two dimensional lattice-free cuts and asymmetric disjunctions for mixed-integer polyhedra (Mathematical Programming 2012, doi:10.1007/s10107-011-0455-1)
**Read level**: partial (abstract, §1 pp. 1-3, §3 statement and Lemmas 3.2-3.3 pp. 6-7, end of §3 and start of §4 p. 14, §5 overview pp. 17-18, Thm 5.8 and Cor 5.9 pp. 25-27, §6 conclusion p. 27; §2, rest of §3-4 and the proofs of §5 not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Sanjeeb Dash, Oktay Günlük (IBM Research) · **Place in trajectory**: multi-row (two-row) cut theory, 2008-2012.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Two strands of cut research meet: intersection cuts from maximal lattice-free sets in R^2 for the two-row continuous group relaxation (Andersen et al., Cornuéjols-Margot), and t-branch split disjunctions (Li-Richard; Balas 2009 for 2-branch) (pp. 1-2). Question: what is the exact relation between the two families, and is there an analogue of "split cut = MIR cut from a one-row relaxation" (Nemhauser-Wolsey) for two-branch disjunctions (p. 2)?
- **D2 Key idea**: Name the 2-branch split cuts "cross cuts", introduce asymmetric 2-branch disjunctions ("crooked cross cuts") (p. 2). For the canonical two-row set W: quadrilateral closure = unimodular cross closure, and crooked cross closure = Q ∩ T3 = conv(W) (Thm 3.1, p. 7); type 3 triangles are not contained in any cross set (Lemma 3.3, p. 7). For a general MIP: any (crooked) cross cut is, up to translation, a (crooked) cross cut of a three-row relaxation (Thm 5.8, p. 25), and of a two-row relaxation when A has full row rank, e.g. the corner relaxation (Cor 5.9, p. 26; p. 27).
- **D3 Assumptions**: P = {(x,y) ∈ Z^n1 × R^n2 : Ax + Gy = b, y >= 0} with rational data (p. 3); W is the two-row tableau relaxation with free integer basics and continuous nonbasics (p. 1).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: containment of lattice-free sets in unions of splits after a unimodular transformation to standard form (Lemma 3.2, p. 7); contradiction via integer points in the relative interior of triangle sides (Lemma 3.3, p. 7); reduction "a cross cut is a split cut for P ∩ {π1x <= γ1} and for P ∩ {π1x >= γ1+1}" combined with the one-row construction for split cuts, with the same three-case analysis as the split = MIR proof (p. 18).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory: a new disjunctive family and equivalences. Limits stated concretely as open questions: whether crooked cross cuts strictly dominate 2D lattice-free cuts (p. 27), polyhedrality of the (crooked) cross closure, and whether crooked cross cuts give the hull of sets with two integer variables ("We believe the answer to be affirmative", p. 27); strictness of the inclusions in Thm 3.1 believed but unproved (p. 14). Computational outlook: separating good lattice-free sets is hard, and choosing a good crooked cross disjunction is "probably hard" too; "Answers to such computational questions require extensive experimentation" (p. 27).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the MILP-internal precursor of the method: a known one-row equivalence (split = MIR) is lifted to two-branch disjunctions and multi-row relaxations (pp. 2, 18, 25); the substructure here is the k-row tableau relaxation that his later QCQP work copies.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: each equivalence comes with what fails (type 3 triangles are not cross cuts, Lemma 3.3 / Cor 3.4, pp. 7-8) and with explicitly listed unresolved strictness questions (pp. 14, 27).
- New-pattern candidate: "lift a known equivalence one dimension up" (split = 1D lattice-free cut → (crooked) cross cut = cut from a three-row relaxation; pp. 2, 18).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When generalizing a classical equivalence theorem, redo its case analysis literally (here: the three cases of the split = MIR proof) and order the new cases to match (p. 18).
- State the conjectured strictness as a belief and an open problem rather than leaving it unstated (pp. 14, 27).

**Quotes**:
- "Even though we believe this dominance to be strict, we are not able to establish it." (p. 27)
- "It seems hard to devise an effective algorithm to find good, maximal, lattice-free bodies such that the associated cuts are violated by the current LP solution." (p. 27)

### S037 · A strong dual for conic mixed-integer programs (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2012, doi:10.1137/110840868)
**Read level**: partial (abstract, §1-2 pp. 1-3, §4.1 pp. 4-6, start of §4.2 p. 6, §5 pp. 9-10, §6 pp. 12-13, §7 pp. 13-15; §3 proofs, the rest of §4 (pp. 6-8) and Cases 2-3 of Prop. 5.3 (pp. 10-12) not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Diego A. Morán R. (Georgia Tech), Juan Pablo Vielma (Pittsburgh) · **Place in trajectory**: early convex/conic MINLP theory (2010-2013), the "Dual ?" item of the MILP checklist.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The subadditive dual of MILP is well understood; can it be extended to mixed-integer conic programs inf c^T x s.t. Ax ⪰_K b, x_i ∈ Z for i ∈ I (pp. 1-2)?
- **D2 Key idea**: Define the dual over functions that are subadditive and nondecreasing with respect to the cone K, with the MILP equality conditions on integer columns and a lim-sup directional condition on continuous columns (D), p. 2; with K = R^m_+ it reduces to the MILP subadditive dual (pp. 2-3). Main result: strong duality holds if there is a mixed-integer point x̂ with Ax̂ ≻_K b, i.e. strictly inside (Thm 2.4, p. 3). That condition "plays the same role" as rational data in MILP (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions**: K closed, pointed, full-dimensional cone (p. 2); condition (∗) of an interior mixed-integer feasible point (p. 3). The dual is an infinite-dimensional function space problem; no claim of tractability.
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: proof split into three ingredients stated up front: finiteness of the primal iff finiteness of its continuous relaxation, conic strong duality, and a dual-feasible subadditive function matching the value function at b (p. 1). The first uses maximal lattice-free convex sets (Thm 4.3 and Cor 4.4, p. 5; Prop. 4.5). Prop. 5.1 builds a 0/1 feasibility indicator G that is subadditive, so g + λG shows the dual is unbounded when the primal is infeasible (p. 9). Prop. 5.3 builds g* from the value function by a case analysis on the signs of f(b) and f_R(b) (pp. 10-12, Case 1 read). Valid inequalities from dual-feasible functions follow as Cor 6.1 (p. 12).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory: duality for a nonlinear integer class. Limits shown by examples: property (4.1) fails for an irrational polyhedron and for a rational conic-quadratic set (Examples 4.1, 4.2, p. 4); the interior condition is "crucial" (p. 6); the converse of Prop. 4.5 is false (p. 6). Finite-dual-family results for pure IPs are "unlikely" to carry over, because the hull need not be polyhedral (Example 6.2, p. 12). No future-work section.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: a direct instance of step 1, "Dual ?" for the conic class: the MILP subadditive dual (Nemhauser-Wolsey II.3.3) is generalized and recovered as the special case K = R^m_+ (pp. 2-3).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: each hypothesis comes with a counterexample showing it cannot be dropped (Examples 4.1-4.2, pp. 4-6), and the converse of Prop. 4.5 is refuted (p. 6).
- New-pattern candidate: "find what replaces rationality" (in a nonlinear generalization of a MILP theorem, identify the geometric condition that plays the role rational data played; p. 3). Also seen in S029 (this batch).

**Transferable techniques**:
- List the proof ingredients of the classical theorem in the introduction (here: finiteness equivalence, LP/conic duality, value-function construction) and generalize each separately (p. 1).
- For each hypothesis of a generalized theorem, give a small (2D or 3D) counterexample where the conclusion fails without it (Examples 4.1, 4.2, p. 4).

**Quotes**:
- "Condition (∗) in the case of mixed-integer conic programs plays the same role as the assumption of rational data in the case of mixed-integer programs in the proof of the strong duality result." (p. 3)
- "The condition that there exists a mixed-integer feasible solution in the interior of the continuous relaxation is crucial for Proposition 4.5." (p. 6)

### S091 · Forbidding extreme points from the 0-1 hypercube (Optimization Online 2012, preprint 2012/05/3463)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-8, the whole note; the case computations of the Fourier-Motzkin claim, pp. 6-7, skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Gustavo Angulo, Shabbir Ahmed (Georgia Tech) · **Place in trajectory**: polyhedral side-product of the binary-expansion work for bilinear/pooling MIPs (ref. [2], Gupte-Ahmed-Cheon-Dey, p. 8).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Given a list V of m binary vectors, is there a description of conv({0,1}^n \ V) polynomial in n and m (p. 1)?
- **D2 Key idea**: Use the binary-expansion bijection to write {0,1}^n \ V as a union of m+1 "interval" sets K(a, b) = {x binary : a <= Σ 2^{i-1} x_i <= b} (p. 2); then the problem reduces to the hull of a two-sided knapsack with superincreasing weights, which is the intersection of the two one-sided hulls (minimal cover and minimal reverse cover inequalities; Thm 2, p. 3). Balas' disjunctive union then gives an extended formulation with O(mn) variables and constraints (pp. 2, 8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: V given explicitly as a list; weights superincreasing (Def. 1, p. 2).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: induction on n with three cases on w_n relative to a and b (pp. 3-4); in the mixed case, write the hull as the disjunctive hull of the x_n = 0 and x_n = 1 pieces and project out the auxiliary variables one at a time (Fourier-Motzkin), with a "Claim" fixing the form of every intermediate polyhedron P^k (pp. 5-7). Closed forms of the covers for w* = (2^{i-1}) from [2], [3] give the explicit inequalities (p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Short note: one compact extended formulation result plus a two-sided generalization of Laurent-Sassano. Priority caveat: Muldoon-Adams-Sherali independently gave the two-sided superincreasing hull (footnote, p. 1). No limits or future work stated.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (step 4, "prefer constructive proofs with an extended formulation, and state its size"): the result is an explicit extended formulation with its size O(mn) as the headline (pp. 1, 8).
- New-pattern candidate: "carry the induction through a projection" (prove a hull by disjunctive lifting plus variable-by-variable Fourier-Motzkin elimination, controlling each intermediate polyhedron with a claim; pp. 5-7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn "forbid a list of points" into "union of intervals in an encoding order" (binary expansion), then take the disjunctive hull of the pieces (p. 2).
- To prove a hull by induction when the last variable is free, take the disjunctive hull of the two fixings and eliminate the copies one coordinate at a time, stating the form of every intermediate projection as a claim (pp. 5-7).

**Quotes**:
- "Is it possible to give a description of conv(B \ V ) which is polynomial in n and m?" (p. 1)
- "Our approach relies on being able to give the convex hull of binary knapsack sets having a superincreasing weight." (p. 1)

### S029 · Some properties of convex hulls of integer points contained in general convex sets (Mathematical Programming 2013, doi:10.1007/s10107-012-0538-7)
**Read level**: partial (abstract, §1-2 pp. 1-4, Example 1 pp. 4-5, p. 10 (end of Thm 1 proof, Example 2), §4 intro and Prop. 1 statement pp. 17-18, Example 4 and §5 Remarks pp. 20-21; the proofs in §3 (pp. 6-16) and §4.1-4.2 not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Diego A. Morán R. (Georgia Tech) · **Place in trajectory**: convex integer programming theory 2010-2013, alongside S037.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Meyer's fundamental theorem (rational data ⇒ polyhedral integer hull) relies on Minkowski-Weyl and a rational recession cone, neither available for a general closed convex K (p. 2). Even closedness of conv(K ∩ Z^n) "is not well-understood" (p. 2). The paper splits the question into closedness and polyhedrality (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: Closedness iff the integer recession directions u(K) are the same at every integer point u, for line-free K (Thm 1, p. 3), with refinements for K with an interior integer point (Thm 2), strictly convex K (always closed, Thm 3), pointed cones (closed iff every extreme ray is rational scalable, Thm 4), and K with lines (Thm 5, p. 4). Polyhedrality: define "thin" sets (unbounded in direction c only along a recession direction; Def. 2, p. 4); thin + rational polyhedral recession cone ⇒ polyhedral hull, and the converse holds when int(K) contains an integer point (Thm 6, p. 4; generalizes Meyer, p. 17).
- **D3 Assumptions**: K closed convex; the necessity part needs int(K) ∩ Z^n non-empty (Thm 6, p. 4).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: intuition from planar examples before the theorems (K1 irrational cone not closed, K2 same cone but closed, parabola K3 closed; pp. 4-5); a Gordan-Dickson-type finiteness lemma for the sufficiency proof (Lemma 11, p. 17); maximal lattice-free sets for the line case (Thm 8, p. 7, not read in detail). The proof of Prop. 1 was suggested by a referee (Acknowledgements, p. 21).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory. Limits marked by examples: the "no line" condition in Thm 1 "is not artificial" (Example 2, p. 10); the interior condition in the necessity part is not artificial either (lattice-free K9, K10, Example 4, p. 20). Open questions stated concretely (pp. 20-21): necessary and sufficient conditions for local polyhedrality, and for solvability of min ⟨d, x⟩ over K ∩ Z^n. All results hold for a general lattice in place of Z^n (p. 20).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: generalizes the MILP structure theorem (Meyer) to convex integer sets, identifying which MILP proof ingredient (rational recession cone) survives and what must be added (thinness) (pp. 2, 17).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: every hypothesis is shown necessary by an explicit example (Example 2, p. 10; Example 4, p. 20), and the polyhedrality condition is shown to be also necessary under a mild assumption (Thm 6, p. 4).
- New-pattern candidate: "find what replaces rationality" (thin set + rational polyhedral recession cone replaces rational data; pp. 2, 17). Same move as S037, p. 3.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before the theorems, show a gallery of small planar examples that separate the candidate explanations (here: irrational recession cone alone does not decide closedness; K1 vs K2, pp. 4-5).
- Split a hard structural question into a weaker property first (closedness) and the strong one second (polyhedrality), since the first is necessary for the second (p. 2).

**Quotes**:
- "In the world of mixed integer linear programming (MILP) problems, these sufficient conditions for polyhedrality of the convex hull of feasible solutions are reasonable since we expect most instances to be described using rational data." (p. 2)
- "We note here that the condition that K contains no line in the statement of Theorem 1 is not artificial." (p. 10)

### S070 · Semi-continuous network flow problems (Mathematical Programming 2014, doi:10.1007/s10107-013-0675-7)
**Read level**: partial (abstract, §1 and §2 pp. 1-4, Prop. 1 statement and proof start p. 4, Thm 6 p. 8, Thm 15 statement p. 15, §5 pp. 19-21, Cor 22 and §6 computation pp. 25-29, §7 conclusion p. 29; the polyhedral proofs of §3-4 and the proof of Thm 21 not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Gustavo Angulo, Shabbir Ahmed (Georgia Tech) · **Place in trajectory**: polyhedral study of a substructure with computation, ExxonMobil-supported (p. 29).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Semi-continuous flows (x ∈ {0} ∪ [l, u]) arise in inventory, portfolio, petrochemical and supply-chain models (p. 1); modelling them with binaries enlarges the problem and adds branching, but binaries also enable solver presolve (pp. 1-2). Entry: a node substructure of a general semi-continuous network, the "semi-continuous inflow set with variable upper bounds" S(t, h), obtained as a relaxation of a node balance constraint (pp. 2-3).
- **D2 Key idea**: Show optimizing over S(t, h) is NP-hard (Prop. 1, p. 4), then give complete hull descriptions and extended formulations for the two special cases t = 0 (Thm 6, p. 8; Cor 9, p. 11) and h = 0 (Thm 15, p. 15; Prop. 17, p. 17). Then a transportation problem made of m such sets: NP-hard (Prop. 18, p. 19), but with h = 0 extreme points have at most m positive x (Thm 21, p. 24), so it is polynomial for fixed m (Cor 22, p. 25).
- **D3 Assumptions**: unbounded semi-continuous variables in the model; a big-M bound M = max{d̄, l̄, h̄} + t̄ for the binary formulation in computation (p. 25).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: knapsack reduction for NP-hardness (p. 4) and UFLP reduction (p. 19); hull completeness by the "for every objective, all optimal solutions lie on one listed facet" argument (Thm 6 proof, p. 8); extreme-point support counting via convex-combination splitting (Prop. 19, p. 21).
- **D5 Experiments**: random semi-continuous transportation instances, n, m ∈ {30, 50, 80}, 10 per size; CPLEX 12.2 default (C), own root cuts only (U), both (C+U), and the extended formulation (E, t = 0 only), each in semi-continuous and binary formulations, 1,800 s limit, single thread (pp. 25-26). Own cuts alone in the binary formulation is the fastest and the only way to solve the largest instances (p. 27; Table 7, p. 29); combining with CPLEX cuts over-generates cuts and slows down (pp. 28-29).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory (hulls, complexity) plus computation. Limits stated: the fixed-m algorithm is inapplicable in practice because the solver does not branch that many times (p. 25); the extended formulation for t > 0 is too large even for the root LP (p. 26); the binary formulation wins, "probably due to advanced bound tightening techniques in CPLEX" (p. 29). Future: finite upper bounds; simultaneous semi-continuous inflows and outflows (p. 29).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: a recurring substructure (one node's inflows) is cut out as a relaxation, convexified jointly, and returned to a testbed (pp. 2-3, 8, 25-29), the MILP form of what he later does for QCQP.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: NP-hardness of the substructure and of the transportation problem, with the boundary of tractability (fixed m) given and its practical irrelevance stated (pp. 4, 19, 25).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: the comparison is against CPLEX defaults on random instances, no exact ceiling; but the extended formulation E acts as the "all inflow cuts already present" reference (p. 26).
- Heuristic 3 (write dead ends in with the reason): the fixed-m branching tweak and the t > 0 extended formulation are reported as not useful, with reasons (pp. 25-26).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Test a cut family in both the "native" and the binary-reformulated model; the binary model may win because of solver presolve, and that is a finding to report (pp. 27, 29).
- When an extended formulation of the substructure exists, include it as an arm next to on-the-fly separation; it shows how much of the gain is reachable when all cuts are present (p. 26; Table 2).

**Quotes**:
- "However, our experimental experience indicates that a standard branch-and-cut solver does not need to branch that many times, rendering this approach inapplicable for practical purposes." (p. 25)
- "In fact, applying these cuts to a binary formulation of such problems turned out to be the most effective method." (p. 29)

### S045 · On the transportation problem with market choice (Discrete Applied Mathematics 2015, doi:10.1016/j.dam.2014.09.008)
**Read level**: partial (abstract, §1-2 pp. 1-3, §3 start with Props 3-4 pp. 3-5, Prop. 5 statement p. 7, Thm 1 statement and start of proof pp. 9-10, §4 computation pp. 23-26; the facet proofs (pp. 10-23) and the Appendix (pp. 26-33) not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Pelin Damcı-Kurt (Ohio State), Simge Küçükyavuz (Ohio State) · **Place in trajectory**: polyhedral MILP for a network design variant, a side line to his cutting-plane work.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A transportation problem where suppliers may reject markets (TPMC), a "complement" of capacitated facility location that is "less understood" (pp. 1-2); market-choice variants in the literature are uncapacitated or have soft capacities (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: Complexity boundary first: strongly NP-complete even with all demands 3 (Prop. 1, p. 3), polynomial when all demands are at most 2 via matching / b-matching, and then the Edmonds-Johnson blossom inequalities give the hull (Prop. 2, p. 3). For the general case: a TPMC inequality (5) (Prop. 3, p. 4) and two coefficient-update schemes on relaxed substructures, a mixed-integer cover set S1 (Prop. 4, pp. 4-5) and a mixed-integer knapsack with variable upper bounds S2 (Prop. 5, p. 7), with facet conditions (Thm 1, p. 9).
- **D3 Assumptions**: integral data; markets with d_j > s(V1) are pre-rejected, giving assumption (23) (p. 9).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: case split on whether a "large" market is rejected for validity (Prop. 3, p. 4); minimal-cover arguments for coefficient update (Prop. 4, pp. 4-5); necessary facet conditions each proved by showing the inequality is implied otherwise (Thm 1, pp. 9-10).
- **D5 Experiments**: CPLEX 12.4, 8 size settings (|V1| 200-400) × 3 instances made hard by rejection sampling (only instances needing at least 15 min under default CPLEX), 30 min limit; four arms: default and default plus each family of user cuts; metrics root gap, end gap, time, unsolved, nodes (pp. 23-25, Table 1 p. 24). Root gaps barely move; end gaps and unsolved counts drop, best with the ≥-type cuts alone (p. 25).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Complexity + polyhedral theory + preliminary computation. Limits stated plainly: no extensive computational study, separation heuristic "needs significant improvement" (p. 3); type (9) cuts were never generated on these instances, and the reason is given (p. 25).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: relax the problem to small mixed-integer substructures (cover set, knapsack with VUBs), derive inequalities there and lift them back (pp. 4, 7); notes that lifted flow cover and submodular inequalities from fixed-charge flow also apply (p. 7).

### S065 · Some lower bounds on sparse outer approximations of polytopes (Operations Research Letters 2015, arXiv:1412.3765)
**Read level**: partial (pp. 1-7 main text: abstract, §1, §2 definitions, §3 all theorem statements, §4 proof of Thm 5, start of the §5 proof of Thm 6, §6 idea of Thm 7, start of §7 p. 8; the details of the proofs of Thms 6-8 (pp. 5-9) and the appendix (pp. 10-11) not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Andres Iroume, Marco Molinaro · **Place in trajectory**: second paper of the sparse-cut line (after the 2014 IPCO / Math. Programming paper [2]), part of the Signature Work "Sparse cuts → cut selection → B&B theory".

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Solvers bias cut selection toward sparse inequalities (p. 1); the earlier paper [2] studied an "idealized" version (approximating a polytope by k-sparse cuts alone) (p. 1). This paper asks four "less idealized" questions: sparse cuts added to an LP relaxation, plus a budget of dense cuts, under any rotation, and along most objective directions (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Key idea**: Answer all four negatively on simple polytopes P_{t,n} = {x ∈ [0,1]^n : Σx_i <= t} and their symmetrized version, using the Hausdorff distance d(P, P^k) (equal to the worst directional gap, Lemma 1) as the measure (pp. 2-3). Theorems 5-8 (pp. 3-4): Ω(√n) error with an LP relaxation Q_n; error >= √n/6 even with exp(n/6002) dense cuts; Ω(√n) after any rotation; gap >= √n/20 in a random direction with probability >= 1 - 4/n.
- **D3 Assumptions**: polytopes in [0,1]^n or [-1,1]^n; k up to n/100, n/2003 or n/10 depending on the theorem; constants not optimized (pp. 3-4).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: an explicit IP formulation Q_n whose LP point (n/(n+2))e is far from the hull (Thm 5, p. 4); probabilistic method: a random ±1 point scaled by 2/3 satisfies each dense cut with probability > 1 - 1/d by Bernstein's inequality on the dense remainder after keeping the k largest coefficients, then a union bound (pp. 5-6); polar duality with a few-facets approximation of k-dimensional symmetric bodies to reduce rotations to the dense-cut theorem (Lemma 13, Thm 7, pp. 6-7); Gaussian concentration of Lipschitz functions for random directions (Thm 16, p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory: four negative results that bound the positive results of [2]. The authors call the outcome "perhaps not surprising" and name the open direction: when sparse inequalities are effective in these settings (p. 2). Not stated: the examples are specific symmetric polytopes, not solver instances.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: a solver habit (bias toward sparse cuts, p. 1) studied through a geometric proxy (distance of P from its k-sparse closure, p. 2), here pushed toward realism one feature at a time (pp. 1-2).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the whole paper is the demarcation of [2]'s positive results: worst-case, budgeted-dense, rotation and random-direction lower bounds (pp. 3-4).
- New-pattern candidate: "de-idealize the proxy one realism at a time" (list how the idealized model differs from solver practice, make each difference a numbered question, answer each; pp. 1-2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- After a positive result in an idealized model, write a follow-up whose numbered questions each restore one feature of practice (LP relaxation present, dense cuts allowed, reformulation by rotation, objective direction) (pp. 1-2).
- To show a budget of arbitrary cuts cannot help, pick a random bad point, split each cut into its k largest coefficients (a valid sparse cut) plus a dense remainder, bound the remainder by a concentration inequality, and union-bound over the budget (pp. 5-6).
