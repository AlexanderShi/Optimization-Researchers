### S100 · Beating the SDP bound for the floor layout problem: A simple combinatorial idea (INFOR: Information Systems and Operational Research 2018, doi:10.1080/03155986.2017.1363592; read as arXiv:1602.07802v2)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-14 main text read; appendices A-B on pp. 17-24 only located, proofs not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Joey Huchette (MIT), Juan Pablo Vielma (MIT).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Dual bounds for hard MIPs come either from a MIP solver with strong formulations or from SDP hierarchies, and the SDP route produces instances "too large to build in memory" (pp. 1-2). The floor layout problem (FLP) has mature heuristics but immature dual-bound technology; the most recent bounding work is the SDP of Takouda et al. 2005 (p. 4).
- **D2 Key idea**: exploit "combinatorial implosion": solve many small restricted subproblems on variable subsets S exactly (or bound them), get valid inequalities c_S^T x_S >= gamma(S, P_S), and aggregate them in a master LP/relaxation over R (Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2, pp. 2-3). Nested families S_k = {|S| <= k} give a polynomial hierarchy for fixed k that reaches the optimum at k = n (Prop. 2.3, p. 4). For FLP the subproblems are smaller FLPs on component subsets C, and the master is an LP in the distance variables d only (Prop. 3.1, p. 6); level 2 is in closed form (p. 7); components with zero weights let sets be trimmed (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Q any set with finite optimum; P_S any outer approximation of the projection (p. 3). FLP: unary MIP formulation (2) from their 2015 paper; 1D-FLP formulation (3) with widths fixed (pp. 4-6).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: (i) LP relaxations of (2)/(3) have value zero, shown by an explicit midpoint solution; simple bound inequalities (5)/(6) lift them exactly to omega_2 (pp. 7-8). (ii) Prop. 4.1: any family of valid inequalities with pairwise disjoint supports of size k cannot beat omega_k, proved by averaging two mirror-image feasible layouts and zeroing d-variables outside the supports (pp. 8-9). (iii) Props. 4.2-4.3: the lifted LP (Sherali-Adams-like) and lifted SDP (Lasserre/Lovasz-Schrijver-like) representations, and the ad-hoc SDP of Takouda et al., all give exactly omega_2 (pp. 9-10; proofs in appendices, not read).
- **D5 Experiments**: Julia/JuMP, Gurobi 6.0.4, Mosek 7.1, a 2.7 GHz laptop with 8 GB (p. 11); compare time to the same bound (CB vs lifted LP/SDP) and gap at equal time budget (CB levels k = 2..5 vs Gurobi), gap measured against the best feasible solution Gurobi finds (pp. 11-13, Tables 1-3). CB omega_2 takes < 1e-4 s; SDP times out at 10 min for 15+ components; CB beats Gurobi on most 1D-FLP and all but one FLP instances, e.g. A20M where Gurobi stays at 90.16% while CB reaches 70.75% (pp. 12-13).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: method + theory comparison + computation. Limits stated: only formulation (3) for 1D-FLP, no comparison against Amaral 2009 and Anjos et al. bounds "which offer promising alternative dual bounds" (p. 12); no parallel implementation (p. 11); gaps computed against the solver's incumbent, not the true optimum (p. 14). No future-work section (the arXiv text ends after §5, p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the deliverable is a dual bound ("A fundamental concept in optimization is the dual bound", p. 1), from the cheapest object that is strong enough, with the SDP shown to be no stronger (Props. 4.2-4.3, pp. 9-10) and reported on a laptop with stated hardware (p. 11).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: Prop. 4.1 is a negative result for the competing route (sparse disjoint-support cuts cannot beat omega_k, pp. 8-9), and the authors stress the restriction of their computational claim to formulation (3) (p. 12).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: no exact ceiling; the reference value is Gurobi's best feasible point, and the authors argue the true gaps are smaller (p. 14).
- New-pattern candidate: "rule out the heavier relaxation by an equivalence theorem" (lifted LP, lifted SDP and Takouda SDP all equal the closed-form omega_2, pp. 9-10); same move as S105 Prop. 1 in batch c16.
- New-pattern candidate: "bound by aggregating exact solutions of small restricted subproblems" (Theorem 2.2, p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To get a dual bound for a problem with many interchangeable components, solve the same problem exactly on every k-subset, write each optimum as a valid inequality on that subset's objective part, and solve the aggregated master; level k is polynomial for fixed k and parallel (Theorem 2.2, Prop. 2.3, pp. 3-4).
- To show sparse cuts cannot help, take two feasible solutions that are mirror images, average them, and zero all variables outside the cut supports: the resulting point satisfies every disjoint-support cut and has objective at most the bound you want to protect (Prop. 4.1, pp. 8-9).
- Two evaluation protocols in one paper: "same bound, compare time" when a theorem says the bounds are equal, and "same time, compare bound" when no ordering is known (p. 11).

**Quotes**:
- "slightly reducing the size of a problem can significantly reduce its solution time." (p. 2)
- "Our assessment is that the lifted representations, while theoretically interesting, do not provide an off-the-shelf practical approach for this problem as they are too large and not sufficiently strong." (p. 12)

### S107 · The strength of multi-row aggregation cuts for sign-pattern integer programs (Operations Research Letters 2018, doi:10.1016/j.orl.2018.11.001; read as arXiv:1711.06963v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-9 main text, statements and proofs of Theorems 1-3 read; appendix proof of Prop. 4 on pp. 10-11 skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Andres Iroume, Guanyi Wang (Georgia Tech, students at the time).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Aggregation cuts are "commonly used in practice by state-of-the-art solvers" (p. 1), and Bodur et al. [11] showed that for packing/covering IPs the aggregation closure is 2-approximated by the original 1-row closure, while for general IPs it can be arbitrarily stronger (p. 1). Conjecture to test: the reason is that packing/covering aggregations cannot "cancel" variables, and Marchand-Wolsey aggregation heuristics deliberately seek cancellations (p. 2). Instrument: sign-pattern IPs, where each column has one sign in all rows, so aggregation never cancels (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: compare closures through multiplicative alpha-approximation (P subset alpha Q, Definition 2, p. 3) for 1-row vs aggregation closure, aggregation vs 2-aggregation closure, and original 2-row vs 2-aggregation closure (Theorems 1-3, p. 4; summary Table 1, p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: (J+, J-) sign-pattern polyhedra with A_ij, b_i >= 0 and A_ij <= b_i for j in J+ (well-behavedness), rational data (Definition 1, p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1: a one-constraint sign-pattern polyhedron satisfies P subset 2P^I (Prop. 3, p. 6); intersect over all aggregations (scaling commutes with intersection, Observation 1, p. 6). Theorems 2-3: explicit two-variable and four-variable families indexed by M whose LP/IP ratio is M/2 and M/4 + 1 while A2 = P^I trivially (pp. 6-7), giving arbitrarily bad approximation; for packing/covering, bounded factors 3 and 1/2.5 (pp. 4, 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: pure theory. Result reads both ways: 1-row aggregation does little without cancellation (supports the conjecture), but multi-row aggregation can be arbitrarily stronger than original multi-row cuts (p. 2). Open cell left in Table 1: 2-A vs A2 for m = 3 is "?" (p. 5). No conclusion section; the second part of Theorem 3 is not proved ("very similar", p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: starts from a solver practice (aggregation cuts in solvers, p. 1) and a folklore explanation (cancellation), and isolates it in a class where the mechanism is switched off (p. 2).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: factor 2 is tight (p. 4); counterexample families for each comparison; an open case is shown as "?" in the summary table rather than omitted (p. 5).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (source side): this is the MILP closure toolbox (single-row, multi-row, aggregation) that Method 3 later exports to QCQP; the alpha-approximation yardstick is the same as in the later QCQP aggregation papers.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To test a mechanism conjectured to explain why a cut family works, find a problem class in which that mechanism is structurally impossible (here: no cancellation) and compare closures there (p. 2).
- Summarise a closure-comparison paper in one table with packing / covering / new class as columns and each containment as a row, including bounds from prior work and "?" for open cells (Table 1, p. 5).
- Counterexample families with a free parameter M that drives LP/IP to infinity while the stronger closure is exact (two rows give A2 = P^I trivially) (pp. 6-7).

**Quotes**:
- "Therefore for these classes of integer programs general aggregated multi-row cutting planes can perform significantly better than just looking at cuts from multiple original constraints." (p. 1)
- "supporting the conjecture that non-cancellation implies that aggregation is less effective." (p. 2)

### S041 · New SOCP relaxation and branching rule for bipartite bilinear programs (Optimization and Engineering 2019, doi:10.1007/s11081-018-9402-9; read as arXiv:1803.09266v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-5 results overview, §3.1 proof skeleton pp. 5-9, §3.2 opening p. 9, §4 pp. 11-12, §5 experiments pp. 15-22; detailed two-variable SOCP cases pp. 7-9, proof of Theorem 2 pp. 9-11 and branching heuristic details pp. 12-14 skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Asteroide Santana (ISyE student), Yang Wang (Georgia Tech Civil and Environmental Engineering).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: bipartite bilinear programs (BBP: fixing one side gives an LP; pooling, waste-water, supply chain, p. 2), and a new application, finite element model updating in structural engineering, "which motivated our work" (p. 4; model §5.1, pp. 15-16). Entry from MIP: "generate cutting-planes implied by single constraint relaxation" (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: (i) convex hull of a one-row BBP set in the extended (x, y, w) space is SOCP representable (Theorem 1, p. 2), because at every extreme point all but two variables (one edge) or one isolated variable are at 0/1 (Theorem 1(i), pp. 2, 6-7); S^SOCP = intersection of these one-row hulls (p. 3). (ii) S^SOCP is contained in SDP ∩ Boolean quadric polytope relaxation (Theorem 2, p. 3). (iii) A branching rule read off the construction: for each two-variable set involving the branching variable pick the split point minimising the sum of the two convex-hull volumes, then aggregate the candidate points (§2.2, p. 4; §4.2, pp. 12-14).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: box [0,1] after scaling, equality rows (slacks for inequalities) (p. 2). Computationally a "light" version: each row's hull only over that row's variables, then a tangential polyhedral outer approximation, because the full extended formulation is O(n1 n2 2^(n1+n2)) objects (Remark 1, p. 3; pp. 4-5, 17-18).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: extreme-point argument: if two x-variables are fractional, fixing the rest leaves a linear equation in them whose relative interior contains the point (Prop. 1, p. 7); then conv of a finite union of SOCP-representable pieces (Lemma 3, p. 6) and Tawarmalani-style splitting of an equation into <= and >= hulls (Lemma 1, p. 5). Remark 2: without w the hull is still SOCP but they are "unable to prove" Theorem 2 for it; the strength comes from w variables interacting across rows (p. 4).
- **D5 Experiments**: 10 FE-model-updating instances; MATLAB/CVX/Mosek, Gurobi 7.5.1, BARON 15.6.5, a 32 GB Windows PC (p. 17). Root: polyhedral SOCP beats Mc, SDP, SDP+Mc and is faster than SDP+Mc (Table 2, pp. 18-19); McCormick discretisation is stronger at the root (Table 3, p. 19). 10 h runs: Python B&B with SOCP relaxation + new rule (BB-SOCP-1) has gaps 1.5-27.9% vs BARON 40.8-90.7% (Table 6, p. 21); ablation BB-SOCP-Mc (new rule, McCormick relaxation) gives 52.6-99.2%, so the gain needs both parts (Table 5, pp. 20-21).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory + algorithm + new application. Limits stated: the light relaxation has no guarantee (p. 5); full S^SOCP extended formulation exponential (p. 4); McCormick discretisation wins at the root (p. 19). No conclusion or future-work section in this version (text ends at p. 22 with acknowledgments).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: single-row relaxation from integer programming copied into BBP (p. 2); convexify the whole one-row set rather than function envelopes ("does not necessarily yield the convex hull", p. 3); extended-formulation size stated (p. 3).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: SOCP relaxation proved at least as strong as SDP ∩ QBP (Theorem 2, p. 3), then replaced by an LP outer approximation that "produces similar bounds but solves much faster" (p. 5); deliverable is dual bounds vs BARON at 10 h (p. 21).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: exponential size and missing guarantee for the light version stated (pp. 4-5); Remark 2 states what they cannot prove (p. 4).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: naive Python B&B as instrument, with a two-arm ablation separating relaxation from branching rule (Table 5, pp. 20-21).
- Heuristic 2 ⚠ variant: BARON run as is on the same 10 h limit; no handicap or tuning discussion (pp. 17, 21).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To prove a one-row nonconvex set has a tractable hull, show that every extreme point has at most one or two "free" variables, then take the convex hull of the union of the low-dimensional pieces (Theorem 1, pp. 2, 5-7).
- Derive the branching rule from the relaxation's construction: choose the split point that minimises the total hull volume of the small sets the relaxation is built from (pp. 4, 12).
- Ablate a new B&B by crossing relaxation and branching rule (new rule + old relaxation), so the paper can say which component carries the gain (Table 5, pp. 20-21).

**Quotes**:
- "A common and successful approach in integer linear programing is to generate cutting-planes implied by single constraint relaxation, see for example [8, 24, 11, 3]. We take a similar approach here." (p. 2)
- "Unfortunately, there are no theoretical guarantees for the bounds of this light version of the relaxation." (p. 5)

### S099 · Lower bounds on the lattice-free rank for packing and covering integer programs (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2019, doi:10.1137/17m1149985; read as arXiv:1710.00031v2)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-7 definitions, all main-result statements and proof overviews, §4.1 proof of Prop. 1 pp. 7-8; detailed proofs of §§4.2-5 pp. 8-21 skimmed only). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Merve Bodur, Alberto Del Pia, Marco Molinaro.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: rank (rounds needed to reach the integer hull) is the standard yardstick for cut families; "only a few results" tie rank to the integrality gap (p. 1). Question: lower bounds on split, multi-branch split and lattice-free rank of packing (and split rank of covering) sets as functions of z_LP/z_I (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: two properties suffice: packing invariance (closure of a packing set is a packing set) and constant approximation (Q subset alpha_M M(Q)); then each round can shrink the LP/IP ratio by at most alpha_M, so rank >= log2(z_LP/z_I) / log2(alpha_M) (Prop. 1, p. 4). Tools: Theorem 1 reduces packing invariance to a closure-under-coordinate-restriction property of the lattice-free family (pp. 4-5); Theorem 2 lifts constant approximation from polyhedra to general packing sets because some closures are not known to be polyhedral (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: packing/covering with non-negative rational data; well-behavedness A_ij <= b_i (or e_j in Q), argued to be without loss (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: telescoping ratio z_LP/z_1 · z_1/z_2 ··· <= alpha^t (p. 7). alpha_S = 2 (tight), alpha_Sk = min{2^k, n} + 1, alpha_Lk = min{k, n} + 1 (Theorem 4, p. 5); split-rank bound tight up to a constant (Prop. 2, p. 6); covering: (1/2) P_C subset S(P_C), tight (Theorem 5, p. 6). Split closure results are not implied by the aggregation-closure results of [9] (examples in Appendix A, pp. 5, 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory. Stated limit: the covering result does not extend to k-lattice-free rank because well-behavedness of that closure is unknown (p. 7). No conclusion section.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: tight factor 2 for split, tightness of the rank bound up to a constant (Prop. 2, p. 6), and a named roadblock for the covering case (p. 7).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the proxy here is rank vs integrality gap, a classical theory measure, not a solver habit; it continues the packing/covering aggregation-closure line ([9], p. 5).
- New-pattern candidate: "rank lower bound = log of the integrality gap over log of a per-round approximation factor" (Prop. 1, pp. 4, 7), reusable for any closure with an invariance + constant-factor pair.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To lower-bound the rank of any cut closure, prove (a) the closure maps the class to itself and (b) one round improves the objective by at most a constant factor; the telescoping product then gives rank >= log(gap)/log(factor) (Prop. 1, pp. 4, 7).
- When a closure is not known to be polyhedral, prove the approximation property on polyhedra first and transfer it via inner polyhedral approximations that are arbitrarily close (Theorem 2, p. 5).

**Quotes**:
- "These results indicate that whenever the integrality gap is high, these classes of cutting planes must necessarily be applied for many rounds in order to obtain the integer hull." (p. 1)
- "We do not know if the k-lattice-free closure of covering polyhedron is well-behaved." (p. 7)

### S040 · Optimization-driven scenario grouping (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2020, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2019.0924; read as LLNL-JRNL-760222 submitted manuscript)
**Read level**: partial (abstract and introduction pp. 3-5, §2.2-2.3 model, proxy and complexity pp. 7-9, §3 algorithms pp. 12-13, §4 setup pp. 14-17 and PySP discussion pp. 26-29, conclusion pp. 33-34; most results tables pp. 17-33 and the MIP formulation §2.4-2.5 skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Kevin Ryan (lead), Shabbir Ahmed (Georgia Tech); Deepak Rajan, Amelia Musselman (LLNL); Jean-Paul Watson (Sandia).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: scenario decomposition dualises all non-anticipativity constraints; re-enforcing some of them ("groups") tightens the bound, but choosing groups by the true bound improvement would require solving the grouped programs (p. 4). Prior grouping is random, k-means on solutions (Crainic et al.) or needs enumeration of all groups (EGSO) (p. 5).
- **D2 Key idea**: a proxy theta_m = min over candidate solutions of the grouped evaluated cost minus scenario optima, an optimistic upper bound on the improvement (Prop. 1 and eq. (7), p. 8), and a partitioning problem maximising sum theta_m with group size <= P, solved as a MIP (problem (8), p. 8; MIP and matching formulations, pp. 10-11). Extension: Algorithm 2 alternates grouping, solving groups and increasing P, finitely convergent for finite first-stage feasible sets (pp. 13-14).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: two-stage stochastic programs with finite scenarios; experiments on 0-1 first-stage problems (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: P = 2 is maximum-weight matching, polynomial (Prop. 2, pp. 8-9); P = 3 NP-hard by reduction from Partition into Triangles (Prop. 3, p. 9); finite convergence by enumeration argument (Prop. 4, p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments**: SIPLIB SSCH/SSLP/SMKP on the LLNL Sierra cluster, CPLEX 12.5, 5 seeds, 3 h limit, against CPLEX on the extensive form, Asynch with no/random grouping (pp. 15-16); OSG with P = 4 solves all SSCH instances in all runs, including all four previously unsolved (pp. 17-18). PySP progressive hedging on stochastic unit commitment against no, random and k-means grouping: OSG gaps an order of magnitude smaller, but grouping lengthens PH runtime on one system and OSG preprocessing is longest (pp. 28-29).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: method + complexity + computation. Stated limits: preprocessing cost of OSG; subset of solutions to evaluate is chosen randomly (p. 28); extensive form preferable when it can be solved (p. 28). Future: risk-averse and chance-constrained decomposition (p. 34).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the target is a stronger relaxation bound, and the headline is proven optimality of previously unsolved SIPLIB instances (pp. 4, 18, 33).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: polynomial at P = 2, NP-hard at P = 3, a clean demarcation of the grouping subproblem (pp. 8-9).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: a proxy measure is built (theta_m), but as an algorithmic surrogate for an intractable objective, not as a theorem about a solver habit (pp. 4, 8).
- Taste 2 ⚠ variant: Dey et al.'s own non-anticipativity bounds are criticised as "entirely data-independent" (p. 5), the reverse of the taste mark that praises data independence; in this paper data dependence is the selling point.

**Transferable techniques**:
- When the quantity you want to optimise (bound improvement of a grouping) costs as much as solving the problem, upper-bound it with already-evaluated candidate solutions and optimise the bound; the proxy is exact whenever the group optimum is among the candidates (Prop. 1, pp. 8-9).
- Report the polynomial and NP-hard boundary of your preprocessing subproblem (matching at size 2, triangles at size 3) so readers know why a MIP is used (pp. 8-9).
- With randomised algorithms on a cluster, average 5 seeded runs, report times only over solved runs and gaps only over unsolved runs, and say so (p. 16).

**Quotes**:
- "To exactly evaluate the bound improvement corresponding a specific grouping would require solving the corresponding stochastic program to optimality" (p. 4)
- "However, these bounds are entirely data-independent." (p. 5)

### S049 · Exact augmented Lagrangian duality for mixed integer quadratic programming (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2020, doi:10.1137/19m1271695; read as arXiv:1907.00920v1)
**Read level**: partial (pp. 1-6 introduction, main results, proof flowcharts, Lemmas 12-15; §4 opening p. 6; §5 Theorems 21-22 statements and start of proofs pp. 11-12; conclusion p. 17; the long proofs pp. 7-16 skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xiaoyi Gu (student), Shabbir Ahmed.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: for convex QP the Lagrangian dual is exact, for MIQP integrality creates a gap; ALD adds a penalty rho·psi(b - Ax) (p. 1). Feizollahi-Ahmed-Sun [9] proved exactness for MILP; the size of the penalty weight "is rarely discussed" (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: three-level result (p. 2): asymptotic zero gap for proper, lsc, level-bounded psi (Theorem 10, p. 3); finite exact penalty for any norm, with rho* of polynomial size in the data (Theorem 11, p. 3). Proof assembled from a decomposition of a rational mixed-integer polyhedron into polytope + integer cone pieces with respect to Q (Lemma 14, p. 5) and a small-complexity bounding box (Lemma 15, p. 6), both taken from the MIQP-in-NP paper [7] (Del Pia, Dey, Molinaro).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Q rational PSD, rational data, MIQP feasible and bounded (Assumption 1, p. 2); dualise Ax = b, keep X = {Ex <= f} mixed-integer (p. 1).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: flowcharts show which lemmas feed which theorem (pp. 3-4). Exactness via a positive gap: if psi(b - Ax) >= delta > 0 on all infeasible points of X, a rho* = (gap)/delta works (Theorem 21, p. 11); for L-infinity, rewrite with a scalar w and bound x uniformly in rho (Theorem 22, Lemma 23, p. 12); small complexity via [7, Theorem 4] (p. 12).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory. Stated limits: the penalised problem may be harder to solve; ALD does not remove integrality so the subproblem is "still far from convex" (p. 17). Future: separable exact algorithms (ADMM-like) using the strong duality (p. 17).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: takes a MILP result (exact ALD, [9]) and asks the same of MIQP, in line with the "Dual ?" entry of the MILP list (pp. 1-2).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: graded hypotheses (general psi: only asymptotic; norm: finite; plus polynomial size), and the conclusion names what the result does not give (p. 17).
- New-pattern candidate: "reuse own complexity machinery as a lemma bank" (MIQP-in-NP decomposition and bounding lemmas re-used for a duality result, pp. 5-6, 12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For an exact-penalty theorem on a mixed-integer set, show a uniform positive lower bound delta on the penalty over infeasible points and set rho* = (feasible value - relaxation value)/delta (Theorem 21, p. 11).
- Put a flowchart of lemmas → theorems right after the main-results statement, one per main theorem (pp. 3-4).

**Quotes**:
- "On the other hand, the size (for example, in binary coding) of the penalty weight is rarely discussed." (p. 2)
- "However, by introducing a penalty term in the objective function, it might become more difficult to deal with." (p. 17)

### S068 · Subset selection in sparse matrices (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2020, doi:10.1137/18m1219266; read as arXiv:1810.02757v2)
**Read level**: partial (pp. 1-8 introduction, related work, overview, reduction Lemma 1, diagonal case Lemma 2 and Claim 1; §3.1 proximity Lemma 3 statement pp. 10-11; end of §3.2 p. 17; detailed block-diagonal proofs pp. 11-17 skimmed). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Alberto Del Pia, Robert Weismantel.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: future MIO models will be huge, so find "parameters in the input data that lead to polynomial-time algorithms"; sparsity is the natural one (p. 1). For integer LP with a block-diagonal-plus-k-columns matrix (1) the complexity is open unless ||M||_inf is constant (p. 2). Question: is Subset Selection (min ||Mx + c mu - b|| with |supp(x)| <= sigma) polynomial for this pattern? (pp. 2-3)
- **D2 Key idea**: reduce to 2^k problems where support constraints hit only block variables and the k coupling variables are a parameter lambda (Lemma 1, pp. 6-7); for fixed lambda, the diagonal case is solved by sorting |b'_i| (Lemma 2, p. 8), and the comparisons are linear in lambda, so a hyperplane arrangement with O(n^k) cells partitions lambda-space into regions with a fixed optimal support (Claim 1, p. 8; p. 6); block case needs a proximity lemma between optimal supports for s and s+1 (Lemma 3, p. 11). Finally solve one least-squares problem per candidate support (p. 6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: M of form (1) with blocks and k extra columns of constant width; 2-norm, extendable to any p-norm (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: count of least-squares solves O(2^k h^((k+1)^2 (2 theta-bar + 3))) (Theorem 1, p. 3); hardness if any one of k, n_i is part of the input (p. 3); explicit small matrices showing the class differs from prior tractable classes (eigenvalue condition [18], sparse covariance [1], restricted isometry) (pp. 3-5).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: complexity theory. Limits: exponents are large (Theorem 1, p. 3); no conclusion section, no implementation.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: exponent dependence on k and n_i justified by NP-hardness when either is free (p. 3); the new class is separated from three known tractable classes by explicit examples (pp. 3-5).
- Taste 5 ✅ evidence: "sparsity of the input data" as the parameter to exploit (p. 1).
- Workflow F1 ⚠ variant: prove the diagonal case first because it "features many key ideas" of the general case (p. 3), an instance of "prove the simple version first".
- New-pattern candidate: "separate your tractable class from prior ones with a tiny explicit matrix for each prior condition" (pp. 3-5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a combinatorial choice depends on a few continuous parameters only through pairwise linear comparisons, enumerate the cells of the hyperplane arrangement in parameter space (O(n^k) cells) to get polynomially many candidate discrete solutions (Claim 1, p. 8; p. 6).
- Prove the diagonal special case in its own section before the block case, so the reader sees the skeleton without the proximity technicalities (pp. 3, 7).
- For each competing tractability condition in related work, give one small matrix in your class that violates it (pp. 3-5).

**Quotes**:
- "One natural parameter in this context is the sparsity of the input data." (p. 1)
- "It is also easy to show that the M matrices we can provide a polynomial-time algorithm for, do not always satisfy the required restricted isometry condition." (p. 4)
