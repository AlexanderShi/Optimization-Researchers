### S020 · A theoretical and computational analysis of full strong-branching (Mathematical Programming 2024, doi:10.1007/s10107-023-01977-x; arXiv:2110.10754)
**Read level**: full. All 45 pages read: main text pp. 1–29, references pp. 29–32, appendix figures pp. 33–35 (captions only, the plots are not in the text layer) and appendix Tables 2–11 pp. 36–45 (rows checked where cited). Version read: arXiv v2 (9 Nov 2021), not the journal version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Yatharth Dubey, Marco Molinaro, Prachi Shah (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: follows Dey–Dubey–Molinaro on B&B for random binary IPs (SODA 2021) and lower bounds on general B&B trees (arXiv:2103.09807), both cited as [17], [18] (p. 30); the B&B theory step of the sparse-cut → cut-selection → B&B line.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is a solver practice nobody has measured in absolute terms. Strong branching is "well-known" to give the smallest trees and is "extremely expensive" (2K LPs per decision); the learning-to-branch literature imitates it (p. 3). The missing piece is stated as a question: how large is the strong-branching tree compared with the smallest possible tree, and for which classes is it close (p. 3–4). Node selection is fixed to worst bound, so only variable selection is studied (p. 2).
  - Transferable technique: take a rule everyone imitates and ask how far it is from the optimum, not from the next rule; the ceiling question is new even when the rule is old (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Two moves. (i) Theory: pick vertex cover, "the most well-studied problem in parameterized complexity", and bound the strong-branching tree by the additive integrality gap Γ (p. 5–6); add a negative class where any fractional-only branching tree is exponentially larger than a tree that branches on integer-valued variables (p. 9–10). (ii) Computation: a dynamic program over the 3^n faces of [0,1]^n computes the optimal tree, so every rule can be reported as a ratio to it (p. 11, §5.2 p. 21). Not changed: the standard score functions (linear with µ = 1/6, product) are taken as used in solvers (p. 3, p. 22).
  - Transferable technique: when a heuristic's quality is unknown, build an exact (exponential) oracle for small instances and report the heuristic as a ratio to it (p. 11, p. 21–22).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Theory uses the product score with ε = 0 and 0·∞ = 0, worst-bound node selection with ties broken by largest depth, and results "independent of the underlying LP solver" (p. 5–6, p. 14). The authors flag that tree size depends on the LP solver, so they speak of "some branch-and-bound tree" rather than "the" tree (p. 5–6). For the computations, Assumption 1 (if an integral optimal LP solution exists, the solver finds it) is introduced explicitly to make "optimal tree" well defined (p. 11); Observation 1 then shows nodes with LP value equal to the IP value are never branched (p. 11, proof p. 20). Theorem 3 assumes an adversarial LP solver that returns the most fractional optimal extreme point (p. 9, p. 17). Binary variables only (p. 2).
  - Transferable technique: when the object you want to measure depends on an implementation detail (here the LP solver's choice of optimal vertex), write that dependence as a numbered assumption and prove it removes the ambiguity (p. 10–11).
- **D4 Analysis & proof technique**:
  - Theorem 1, |T_S(I)| ≤ 2^{2Γ(I)+2} + O(n) (p. 6), proved by depth counting: every strong-branching child of a node below OPT gains at least 1/2 (Lemma 1, via half-integrality of vertex-cover LP vertices, p. 14), so at depth 2Γ+1 all nodes without an integral ancestor are pruned; Lemma 2 bounds the subtree under an integral node by O(n) using depth tie-breaking (p. 14–15).
  - Tightness: Remark 1, m disjoint triangles force 2^{2Γ+1} − 1 nodes for any tree (p. 6); the bound matches the bounded-search-tree FPT algorithm (Theorem 3.8 of [14]) (p. 6).
  - Persistency: Nemhauser–Trotter (Fact 1) and Picard–Queyranne's union lemma (Fact 2) give a "maximal set of integer variables" per node; Proposition 1 shows strong branching branches on such a variable only when the tree's dual bound already equals OPT; Theorem 2 shows the set is inherited by children (p. 6–8; proofs p. 15–16, with Lemma 3, a Hall-type neighbourhood inequality, p. 16).
  - Separation from a natural greedy rule: diamonds-plus-triangles instance; product score is 0 on diamond vertices and 1/4 on triangle vertices, so strong branching resolves triangles first; greedy's tree is bounded below by a binomial sum ≥ 2^{1.15m} (Theorem 3, p. 9; proof p. 17–18).
  - Negative example: the Bodur–Dash–Günlük extended formulation of any binary IP admits a ≤ 4n + 1 node tree that branches on integer-valued y variables (Proposition 2, p. 9; proof p. 19); applied to the cross-polytope, any fractional-only tree, hence strong branching, needs ≥ 2^{n+1} − 1 nodes (Corollary 1, p. 10, p. 19). The authors note this does not put binary IP in co-NP because the formulation may be exponential (p. 9–10).
  - DP: recurrence OPT(F) = 1 + min_j (OPT(F_{j,0}) + OPT(F_{j,1})), Phase 1 prunes faces by infeasibility or bound, total time poly(data)·3^{O(n)} (Theorem 4 p. 11; Algorithm 1 p. 22); "cascading 0-nodes" and grouping by the first ⌊n/2⌋ coordinates for parallelism and memory (p. 21–22).
  - Transferable technique: to separate two branching rules, fix an adversarial LP oracle and glue two gadget families whose scores rank in opposite order (p. 17–18).
  - Transferable technique: to show a restriction (branch only on fractional variables) is costly, lift the instance to an extended formulation where the cheap tree needs the forbidden moves (p. 19).
- **D5 Experiments**: Ten problem classes, 100 random instances each, all with at most 20 binaries because the DP does not scale further (p. 11): packing P5, covering C5, mixed G22 (n = 20, density 0.75) (p. 23); uncapacitated, capacitated and big-bucket lot-sizing (p. 23–25); vertex cover on Erdős–Rényi graphs (N = 20, p = 0.75) (p. 25); chance-constrained power planning and portfolio (p. 25–28); bipartite stable set with a knapsack side constraint (p. 28). Rules: SB-L, SB-P (ε = 10^-4), most infeasible, random (p. 22). Metrics: ratio of geometric means to the optimal tree (Fig. 2 p. 12), performance profiles (p. 29), per-instance tables (p. 36–45), and the share of integer-variable branchings in the optimal tree (Table 1 p. 13). Overall geometric means: optimal 42.65, SB-L 61.47, SB-P 61.09, most infeasible 90.19, random 145.37 (p. 29). The authors test their own hypothesis (more integer branchings in the optimal tree → worse strong branching) and reject it: stable set does well despite 47.7% integer branchings (p. 13). Word of caution about extrapolating beyond 20 binaries (p. 13). Not reported in the text read: which LP solver and B&B code produced the strong-branching trees (pp. 22–29 name none).
  - Transferable technique: next to the heuristic-vs-optimum ratio, tabulate one structural statistic of the optimal solution (here % integer branchings) and test a stated hypothesis against it (p. 13).
- **D6 Positioning**: Against the variable-selection literature, described as "intricate greedy rules" (p. 2), and the ML imitation line [28, 33, 3, 5, 22, 24, 35] (p. 3). Theorem 1 is claimed as "the first result of its kind that we are aware of" that uses a specific variable rule for an upper bound, in contrast to random-data results that do not depend on the rule [17, 10] (p. 8–9). Corollary 1 is claimed as "the first such explicit example" of the fractional-vs-integer branching separation (p. 10). Beyond-worst-case framing cites Roughgarden [42] (p. 5).
  - Transferable technique: position a theorem by what earlier upper bounds do not depend on (here, the branching rule) (p. 8–9).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: All results in a "Main results" section (§2) before any proof (p. 5); contributions split into "provably good", "provably bad" and "computational" bullets (p. 4). The scare quotes on "optimal" with a footnote explaining why (p. 4) are a hedging device. Aggregate claims are stronger than the tables: "Strong branching always performs the best" (p. 12) holds for the geometric means, while per-instance rows show most-infeasible beating both SB variants (Table 10 rows 4, 60, 65: 319 vs 282, 362 vs 254, 331 vs 273, p. 44). The text refers to "Tables 1 through 12" (p. 29), but the file contains Tables 1–11 only (p. 13, p. 36–45).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Theory (vertex cover upper bound, persistency, two separations) plus a computational protocol (DP optimal trees). Limits are written concretely: n ≤ 20 binaries and possible non-extrapolation (p. 13); room for better rules, e.g. CCP portfolio (p. 12, p. 29). Future direction: "use machine learning techniques to learn from the optimal branch-and-bound trees" (p. 12).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (strong branching as an unexplained solver practice; questions p. 3–4; proxy = ratio to optimal tree; parameterized instance model chosen because worst case is exponential, p. 5). Method 2 ✅ (DP ground truth up to 20 binaries, ratio reporting, word of caution, p. 11–13, p. 21–22). Method 4 ✅ (Remark 1 tightness and FPT match p. 6; positive class paired with a negative class p. 8–10; cost of the extended formulation stated p. 9–10). Observation for the skill: the SKILL's Full-strong-branching anatomy credits this DOI with non-monotonicity, a naive Python B&B and stripped SCIP; none of these appear in this arXiv v2 text (they belong to the IJOC 2024 paper or to a later version).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Exponential exact DP over the 3^n faces of the cube as a ground-truth oracle for B&B rules on ≤ 20 binaries, with cascading pruning and coordinate-group parallelism (p. 21–22).
- Make an ill-defined measure well defined by an explicit solver assumption plus a short observation proving it removes the ambiguity (p. 10–11, p. 20).
- Prove a parameterized (FPT-type) bound for a solver rule on the canonical FPT problem, then match it to the known FPT algorithm and a tight instance (p. 5–6).
- Report a structural statistic of the optimal object next to the performance ratio and state when your own hypothesis fails (p. 13).
**Quotes**:
- "However, to the best of our knowledge there is no understanding of how good strong-branching is in absolute terms." (p. 3)
- "A common way to meaningfully analyze an algorithm with exponential worst-case performance is to show its performance with respect to some parameter" (p. 5)

### S047 · Decomposable formulation of transmission constraints for decentralized power systems optimization (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2024, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2022.0326; arXiv:2001.07771)
**Read level**: full. All 22 pages read, including the appendix (zone-splitting MILP, p. 20) and references (p. 21–22). Version read: arXiv v2 (14 Mar 2021), not the journal version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Álinson S. Xavier, Feng Qiu (Argonne National Laboratory) (p. 1); Dey is the third author and the only Georgia Tech author; funding and computing are Argonne's (p. 20). The voice is largely that of the Argonne group; read as weak evidence for Dey's own methods. **Position in trajectory**: power-systems application line (after the AC-OPF SOCP arc), here DC power flow and unit commitment.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Application-driven. Centralized market clearing has scalability and data-access problems (MISO report; ISO-NE flows in the wrong direction about half of 2009) (p. 1–2). For decentralized optimization the two DC formulations each fail: phase-angle is local but cannot drop non-critical constraints and needs a copy of the angles per N-1 contingency; the ISF (PTDF) formulation scales and handles contingencies but is dense and unstructured (p. 2–3). The paper asks for a formulation with the good properties of both (p. 3).
  - Transferable technique: write the two incumbent formulations as a two-column list of strengths and defects and set the target as "both strengths" (p. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Theorem 1: for a two-zone partition, the ISF column of any bus in the other zone, restricted to lines of this zone, is a combination of the ISF columns of the boundary buses with weights summing to one (p. 9). With boundary variables w and equations (9a)–(9b), most of ∆ can be dropped, giving a block-diagonal formulation equivalent to ISF where only (9a)–(9b) link zones (p. 10–11; sparsity plot for case1888rte, Fig. 2 p. 11). ISFs are computed decentrally by |B| single-period DC power-flow solves, once per network (p. 7). N-1: internal outages via line outage distribution factors (p. 12); external outages via constants from the previous ADMM iteration, which collapse the contingency constraints into a robust bound (12) per line, so no new consensus variables are needed (p. 13–14). ADMM is the optimal-exchange form (Algorithm 1 p. 5), wrapped in a revised release-and-fix procedure that switches on objective or infeasibility stagnation and stops at the first globally feasible solution (p. 15–16, Fig. 3 p. 17).
  - Transferable technique: when a lag is acceptable, freeze the coupling terms at the previous iteration's values so that a family of coupled constraints becomes one robust bound per local constraint (p. 14).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: DC linearization (p. 2); zones are connected with no islands and boundary buses carry no injection, the latter made non-restrictive by artificial buses (p. 5); two zones, recursive subdivision possible (p. 7); different slack buses per zone allowed because ISF constraints are independent (p. 8); outages do not create islands and injections stay fixed (p. 12). ADMM converges only for convex problems; with binaries it "can still be used as a heuristic" (p. 4, p. 15–16). The external-outage scheme uses lagged constants and has no convergence or exactness statement (p. 14).
- **D4 Analysis & proof technique**: Theorem 1's proof (p. 9–10) works on the reduced Laplacian G = MᵀDM partitioned by zones: G12 = G21 = 0 because no line joins B1 and B2; Claim 1 changes only the phase angles of the far zone so that the 1 MW injection moves to the boundary buses, solvable because the block matrix Q is non-singular (p. 9–10); flows on near-zone lines are unchanged because their end angles are unchanged, and flow preservation makes the boundary injections sum to 1 (p. 10). Note: the statement gives γ ∈ [0, 1] and calls the result a convex combination (p. 3, p. 9), but the proof as read shows only (4a) and Σγ = 1; nonnegativity is not argued in the text (p. 9–10). Proposition 1 shows the contingency balance equations (10c) are redundant using Σγ = 1 (p. 13). Worked 8-bus numerical example before the theorem (Example 1, Fig. 1, p. 8).
  - Transferable technique: give a rounded numerical instance of a structural identity before proving it (p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: SCUC on seven MATPOWER European cases, 1,888–6,515 buses (Table 1 p. 15), missing generator data generated from real distributions and released in UnitCommitment.jl (p. 15); zones from an auxiliary MILP minimizing boundary buses with a balance tolerance (p. 15, p. 20). Julia 1.5/JuMP 0.21, CPLEX 12.9, one desktop (Ryzen 9 3950X, 64 GB), MPI with one process per zone, 3600 s wall clock (p. 15). Baseline: decentralized phase-angle with the same ADMM code, given relaxed barrier tolerance (BAREPCOMP 10^-3) because of numerical issues (p. 17–18). Metrics: time, primal residual, iterations, gap to the centrally computed optimum (Tables 2–3 p. 17, p. 19). Results: proposed formulation averages 600 s, 53 iterations, 0.13% gap; phase-angle finds no globally feasible solution on any instance within 1 h (p. 17). With N-1: 1170 s, 52 iterations, 0.08% gap; no phase-angle comparison because it would need one angle copy per contingency (p. 18). Infeasibility-over-time plots (Figs. 4–5 p. 18–19). One run per instance; no variance reported.
  - Transferable technique: measure a decentralized heuristic by its gap to the centrally solved optimum, which is available on benchmark instances even though it would not be in deployment (p. 17).
- **D6 Positioning**: Continues ADMM work in power systems (Boyd et al.; Feizollahi et al. 2015) and revises the Feizollahi release-and-fix switching rule (p. 4, p. 16). Corrects Biskas and Bakirtzis (2004), which handles internal contingencies only, claiming the first treatment of external outages (p. 3). Links sparsity to the authors' sparse-cut work: "Such sparse formulations can also have advantages" citing Dey et al. 2015, 2018 and Dey and Molinaro 2018 (p. 6). Industry practice of thresholding small ISF entries is criticized as losing accuracy without adding structure (p. 6).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Contribution stated through the structural fact at the end of the introduction (p. 3); one theorem, one proposition, the rest constructive. Claims of generality are hedged once: results are only for SCUC, "we stress that the formulation is applicable to any problem that uses the DC power flow equations" (p. 4, p. 19).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Formulation plus decentralized algorithm plus released instances. Limits named: only SCUC tested; how to choose zones "for improved computational performance" is open (p. 19). Not named: two-zone experiments only; no guarantee for the lagged external-outage scheme; heuristic ADMM with no dual bound.

**Method links**: Method 5 ⚠ variant (the deliverable is a primal decentralized solution, explicitly heuristic, with quality measured as gap to the central optimum, not a certificate from a relaxation; p. 4, p. 17). Method 2 ⚠ variant (compares against the exact central optimum, but in a full solver setting rather than a stripped instrument; p. 17). Also evidence for Heuristic 1 (augmented instances released, p. 15) and Heuristic 2 (baseline given relaxed tolerances and the same ADMM code, p. 15, p. 17–18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Partition the reduced Laplacian by zones and use the zero off-diagonal blocks to show that far-zone effects pass through boundary buses only (p. 9–10).
- Replace contingency copies by per-line robust bounds built from lagged coupling constants, sharing only small aggregated quantities between zones (p. 14).
- Split benchmark networks into zones with a small MILP (minimize boundary buses subject to a line-balance tolerance) and publish the rule in an appendix (p. 20).
**Quotes**:
- "The formulation is based on the fact, which we prove in Section 3, that the injection shift factors of external zones are a convex combination of the shift factors at boundary buses." (p. 3)
- "In comparison, the decentralized phase-angle formulation failed to solve even the smallest test cases, even when allowed very relaxed convergence and feasibility tolerances." (p. 4)

### S059 · A reformulation-enumeration MINLP algorithm for gas network design (Journal of Global Optimization 2024, doi:10.1007/s10898-024-01424-x; arXiv:2307.07648)
**Read level**: full. All 32 pages read, including Appendix A (proof of Theorem 1, p. 27–28), Appendix B (MISOC relaxation, p. 29–30) and references (p. 30–32). Version read: arXiv v3 (30 Jan 2024).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Yijiang Li (Georgia Tech student, first author), Nikolaos V. Sahinidis (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: energy-network application line (IDAES-funded, p. 27), a primal-side MINLP framework rather than a relaxation paper.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Application and scale. The "design-from-scratch" variant (choose discrete pipe diameters and on/off states of compressors, valves and control valves; minimize pipe cost) is a nonconvex MINLP (p. 1–2). The gap in the literature is stated as scale and scope: no prior design-from-scratch work has an instance over 500 nodes, and none combines active elements, discrete diameters and non-tree networks (p. 2).
  - Transferable technique: define the gap as the conjunction of features prior work never had together, and pick an instance (GasLib-582) that has all of them (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: A nested loop (p. 9–15). Inner "primal bound loop": a master problem (Pm) built from the convex energy-minimization program CVXNLP of Cherry/Collins/Raghunathan, extended with diameter and configuration binaries and a budget constraint (48)–(50), proposes a design; a nonconvex nomination-validation subproblem (Ps) with the binaries fixed checks it; infeasible designs get a no-good cut (58) (p. 11–13). Outer loop: binary search on the budget C (Algorithm 1 p. 14). Initialization: an integer program (Im) with only diameters and compressor states plus no-good cuts gives a lower bound and sometimes the optimum (p. 13–15). Theorem 1 justifies the master: under strict monotonicity of the potential-loss function, the network analysis equations have a solution iff CVXNLP has a KKT point, so the convex program can stand in for the nonconvex equations except for potential bounds (p. 11). The master need not be solved to optimality; a 60 s limit is used (p. 17).
  - Transferable technique: use a convex program whose optimality conditions are the physics (a "content" or energy function) as the master of a combinatorial decomposition, and check only the leftover bounds in a subproblem (p. 10–12).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Steady-state potential-loss law (3), q_max proportional to cross-section (2), compressor and control-valve models from [3], [4] with big-M or nonlinear on/off forms (p. 5–9). Theorem 1 needs φ strictly increasing with φ(0) = 0, "commonly satisfied by gas networks" (p. 11). Operational (compressor) cost excluded (p. 2). Four diameter choices per pipe from multipliers {0.8, 1.0, 1.3, 1.5} of the GasLib diameter (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis & proof technique**: One theorem. Proof of Theorem 1 (Appendix A, p. 27–28): write the KKT system of CVXNLP; show q+ and q− cannot both be positive (reducing both lowers the objective by strict monotonicity); identify potentials with the flow-conservation multipliers (πv = λv); for the converse, build multipliers µ± explicitly by max{0, ·} and verify stationarity; convexity of Φ makes KKT sufficient and gives uniqueness (p. 27–28). Validity of the bounds is argued in prose: the no-good cuts only remove designs shown infeasible, so (Im) with cuts gives "an initial dual bound" (p. 15), and an infeasible (Pm) certifies an infeasible budget (p. 13). Second-order cone representation of q³ ≤ t given in (62) (p. 16–17).
- **D5 Experiments**: GasLib-11, -24, -40, -134 and -582; GasLib-4197 dropped after a preliminary study as still too hard (p. 15). Nominations: one per small network, two days for GasLib-134, one per temperature class for GasLib-582; stress multipliers 0.1–2.0 on demand (p. 16). Implementation choice made by trying three formulations of the cubic master: cubic with BARON 22.9.1 and SCIP 7.0.1, SOCP with MOSEK 9.3.10, quadratic-plus-perspective with Gurobi 9.5.1; Gurobi chosen as fastest (p. 16–17). Baseline: the direct MINLP with BARON (the better of two formulations); SCIP also tried; Gurobi dropped as a MINLP baseline because it "at times returns solutions with large constraint violations" (p. 18). An adapted Borraz-Sanchez et al. MISOC approach gave no feasible solution for warm 31 at any stress level (p. 23). Hardware stated (i7, 16 GB, Ubuntu 20.04, Python/Pyomo) and time budget (10 min initial search, 5 h binary search, 45 min per budget check) (p. 17). For GasLib-582, BARON runs 45 min first and the better lower bound is reported (p. 19). Metrics: bounds, gap (63), time, and time to reach 20% gap (p. 18); iteration counts (budgets checked, no-good cuts) (Tables 7–9, 12–13). Results, by size and stress: on small networks comparable to BARON up to stress 1.0, larger gaps above it (p. 19; Tables 4–6 p. 20); GasLib-134: initial search solves low stress and beats BARON there, BARON reaches 20% faster at high stress (p. 19; Tables 10–11 p. 22); GasLib-582: feasible budget for all 25 instances, optimal for 12, under 20% gap for 6 more (p. 23); perspective strengthening never worse on GasLib-582, average improvement about 55% and maximum 97%, all from better feasible solutions (p. 24). Reported failure: no infeasible budget is ever proved by the primal bound loop, because each no-good cut removes one design (p. 24).
  - Transferable technique: before fixing the solver for a subproblem, implement the same convex model in all its exact representations (native nonlinear, SOCP, quadratic plus perspective) and keep the fastest; say which ones lost (p. 16–17).
  - Transferable technique: report "time to 20% gap" alongside final gap for instances that do not close (p. 18).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on CVXNLP (Cherry 1951; Collins et al. 1977/78; Raghunathan 2013) and on Babonneau–Nesterov–Vial's two-stage scheme for continuous diameters (p. 4, p. 9–10); contrasts with Borraz-Sanchez et al. (MISOC relaxation, reinforcement) and with tree-network work (p. 4, p. 18). Prior design-from-scratch work is characterized by instance sizes (14, 36 nodes, Belgian network) rather than criticized (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Contribution paragraph claims "the first paper that solves the gas network design problem on such large-scale instances" with an explicit "To the best of our knowledge" hedge (p. 2). Results text is plain about where BARON wins (p. 19). Minor inconsistency: the cold nomination is "cold 4218" in text and Table 19 but "cold 4128" in the Table 17 caption (p. 19, p. 26); stress levels print as "{0, 1, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}" (p. 16) while the tables use 0.1 (p. 20).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Algorithmic framework plus computational study. Limits stated: GasLib-4197 still too hard (p. 15); large gaps at high stress and cold nominations (p. 23–24); no infeasible budget ever proved (p. 24); the lower bound on GasLib-582 needs BARON (p. 19, p. 24). Future direction: include operating cost (p. 27).

**Method links**: Method 5 ⚠ variant (the convex program is used to generate primal designs, not as a relaxation for a dual bound; lower bounds come from a combinatorial relaxation (Im) with no-good cuts and from BARON; p. 13–15, p. 19). Method 2 ✅ (drop a solver that returns violated solutions, p. 18; the stated limit that the framework cannot prove infeasible budgets, p. 24). Also evidence for Heuristic 2 (competitor approach adapted to the design variant, p. 18, p. 23) and Heuristic 3 (preliminary tests of three master formulations and of longer initial search written in briefly, p. 17–18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Binary search on a budget, with each probe a decomposition (convex master + nonconvex validation + no-good cuts) and the upper and lower bounds updated by the probe's outcome (Algorithm 1 p. 14).
- A cheap pure-cost integer program plus no-good cuts as a lower-bounding warm start for the budget search (p. 13–15).
- Perspective-strengthen the quadratic auxiliary constraint of a cubic objective (q_qua·z ≥ q²) when diameter choice is binary, and measure the effect separately (p. 17, p. 24).
- When a global solver gives good lower bounds but poor primal solutions, run it for a fixed time and combine its lower bound with your framework's upper bound (p. 19).
**Quotes**:
- "We also discovered that Gurobi at times returns solutions with large constraint violations for this MINLP formulation." (p. 18)
- "In addition, for all instances, we are not able to prove infeasible budget from the primal bound loop." (p. 24)
