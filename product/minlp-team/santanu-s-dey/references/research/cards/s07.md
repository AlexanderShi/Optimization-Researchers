- **D1 Problem entry**: a known result, not an open problem. BPO with treewidth d admits an LP with O(k2^d) variables and constraints (the "treewidth-convex hull theorem", Theorem 1, p. 4), previously proved by Laurent (2009) and Bienstock-Muñoz (2018) by induction on the number of tree-decomposition nodes (pp. 4-5). The entry is to give two alternative proofs and to derive TDI as a by-product (abstract; p. 5). Stated motivation for having the LP: generating cutting planes for BPO relaxations and sensitivity analysis in the objective (p. 2, citing Martin-Rardin-Campbell 1990).
  - Transferable: when a theorem's only proofs are inductive, a re-proof via LP duality can yield a structural corollary (here TDI) that the induction hides (p. 5, p. 15).
- **D2 Key idea**: write each bag's feasible multilinear set as a convex combination of its binary points (multipliers λ^{iP}, p. 7), project out the monomial variables w, so that parent-child consistency becomes equalities among λ sums (constraint (7), p. 9); then apply |B_i ∩ B_j| rounds of elementary row operations (Algorithm 1, p. 11) to reach a normal form (9)/(11) in which every λ of a parent-child pair appears in exactly one equation (Claim 1, p. 10; Observation 1, p. 12). Both proofs rest on that normal form (p. 5: "The central ingredient in both proofs is a new reformulation").
  - Transferable: before proving integrality, row-reduce the coupling constraints into a "each variable appears once per block" form; the decoupling is what lets local perturbations and DP recursions go through.
- **D3 Assumptions**: binary variables, polynomial objective and constraints (p. 1); a tree decomposition of the associated graph achieving treewidth d, with k nodes, is given (Definition 1, p. 3; Theorem 1, p. 4); each constraint's support sits inside one bag (clique-in-a-bag fact, p. 6). Constraints are handled only through the per-bag feasible point sets P_i (p. 7), i.e. the bag sets are enumerated, which is where the 2^d factor comes from (p. 7).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**:
  - Proof 1, perturbation / midpoint argument (§3.1, pp. 12-13): assume a fractional extreme point, take ε = the smallest fractionality, move two fractional root multipliers by ±ε and propagate ±ε to children down to the leaves using Observation 1; the two perturbed points average to the original, a contradiction.
  - Proof 2, constructive complementary slackness (§3.2, pp. 13-15): write the dual of LP; set dual values bottom-up from leaf-parent pairs by a min-recursion (the DP); set primal values top-down by picking one active dual constraint per node; the pair is feasible and complementary, hence the integral primal is optimal for every objective. "The dual to the LP is the DP formulation" (p. 5).
  - Corollary 2 (TDI, p. 15): the constructed dual is integral for every integral objective.
  - Reverse induction on the row-operation iteration index proves Claim 1 (pp. 10-11).
  - A running example (Example 1-6, pp. 5-15) is carried through every reformulation and both proofs.
  - Rigor level: Proof 1 is a sketch at the propagation step ("Propagate the perturbations throughout the tree down to the leaves", p. 13); the notation switches between root r and node j inside the same paragraph (pp. 12-13).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove integrality of a tree-structured polytope two ways: (i) a fractional point is the midpoint of two feasible points obtained by ±ε perturbations propagated root-to-leaves (pp. 12-13); (ii) build a dual bottom-up by the DP min-recursion and an integral primal top-down from active dual constraints, then check complementary slackness (pp. 13-15).
- Read TDI off a constructive dual proof: if the constructed optimal dual is integral for every integral objective, state TDI as a corollary (p. 15).
- Row-reduce parent-child consistency equations so each variable appears in exactly one equation per pair (Algorithm 1, Claim 1, Observation 1, pp. 10-12) before attempting any perturbation or duality argument.
- Thread one small running example (three bags, five variables) through every reformulation and both proofs (Examples 1-6, pp. 5-15), and check its numbers against the original objective before submission (the coefficient drift above, pp. 5, 9, 12, 15).
