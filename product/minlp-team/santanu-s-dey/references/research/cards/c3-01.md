### S006 · Inexactness of SDP relaxation and valid inequalities for optimal power flow (IEEE Transactions on Power Systems 2016, doi:10.1109/tpwrs.2015.2402640; arXiv:1410.1004)

**Read level**: full. All of pp. 1–25: abstract, §1 introduction (pp. 1–3), §2 OPF formulation and SOCP relaxation (pp. 4–6), §3 two-bus analysis and Theorem 3.1 (pp. 6–11), §4 two- and three-bus counterexamples (pp. 12–15), §5 instance library and SDP vs global optimum (pp. 15–17), §6 bound tightening, Algorithm 1 and experiments (pp. 17–22), §7 conclusions (p. 22), Appendix .1 on BARON (p. 23), references (pp. 23–25). Version read: arXiv v3; title and authors (Burak Kocuk, Santanu S. Dey, X. Andy Sun) match on p. 1.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Burak Kocuk (PhD student), X. Andy Sun (co-advisor) · **Position in trajectory**: first paper of the AC-OPF arc (SKILL.md Signature Work Anatomy: this paper → Strong SOCP, doi:10.1287/opre.2016.1489 → later OPF papers); Dey's first power-systems paper in this corpus, at the start of his move from MILP cutting-plane theory to nonconvex QCQP.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is a recently proved positive claim and the technical condition it needs. The abstract opens with it: SDP relaxation over radial networks has been proven exact "under technical conditions such as not including generation lower bounds or allowing load over-satisfaction" (p. 1). The introduction lists the SDP exactness results with the condition each requires (resistive networks with over-satisfaction; phase shifters; no generation lower bounds on radial networks; fixed voltages with small angles) and draws the common thread: exactness "can only be guaranteed for special classes of OPF instances, often when we disregard some generation lower bounds" (p. 2). The question is what happens when generation lower bounds on both real and reactive power are present (p. 3). The dropped condition is justified by practice: ramping constraints keep next-period generation near the current one, so lower bounds matter (p. 3); radial networks are justified by sparsity and distribution networks (p. 3). The goal is stated as "two fold": show inexactness and make algorithmic progress on solving such instances globally (p. 3).
  - Transferable technique: when a field rests on an exactness theorem, tabulate every published exactness result with the hypothesis it needs, find the hypothesis they all drop, and argue from practice (here ramping) why that hypothesis matters (pp. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Two ideas. (a) Analysis: in the c/s reformulation (2), all nonconvexity of radial OPF sits in the coupling equations c_ij² + s_ij² = c_ii c_jj (p. 6); for the two-bus one-generator system the linear equalities let every variable be written in terms of (c11, c22), so OPF and SOCP feasible regions can be drawn and compared in the plane (§3.1, pp. 8–9), giving a complete classification of outcomes (Theorem 3.1, p. 11). (b) Algorithm: help the general global solver BARON rather than build a new one: compute bounds on c_ij and s_ij by solving SOCP relaxations over S′ (optimization-based bound tightening), then cut off the part of the box B_ij that lies inside the inner circle of the ring R_ij, which is infeasible because voltage magnitudes are bounded below (§6, pp. 18–20, Algorithm 1). What is kept: the standard SOCP relaxation (6) and BARON's spatial branch-and-bound (p. 6, p. 17, p. 23). What changes: BARON is fed the radial reformulation (2) instead of the rectangular formulation (1), plus bounds and cuts (p. 15, p. 18). Why: BARON's success "heavily depends on the quality of convex relaxations", and good bounds are "crucial" (p. 23).
  - Transferable technique: when the nonconvexity of a problem lives in one low-dimensional coupling constraint per edge, look at the projection of each edge's variables onto that 2D set (box vs ring) and derive cuts from the gap between the bound box and the nonconvex set (pp. 18–20).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Radial networks throughout; reformulation (2) is exact only for radial networks because the angle equations (3)–(4) have a unique solution there (pp. 5–6). SOCP and SDP relaxations give the same bound on radial networks by a cited result ([21]), so "any result stated for SOCPs in this paper holds for SDP relaxations and vice-versa" (p. 3, p. 6); this equivalence is cited, not proved here (SKILL.md Method 5 limitations records a later critique of the real/complex SDP equivalence his OPF papers cited; not checked against this paper's reference [21]). Two-bus analysis: g_ii = b_ii = 0, G < 0, B > 0 ("the analysis for B < 0 is similar"), linear cost (p. 6). Counterexamples: large line capacity, V^min = 0.9, V^max = 1.1, linear costs (p. 12). Cuts: c_ij lower bound > 0 is assumed because it was observed in the experiments, with the claim that it "is not a restrictive assumption" since similar inequalities exist otherwise (p. 18; the general case is not worked out).
  - Transferable technique: state each convenience assumption with its justification on the spot (observed in experiments; symmetric case similar), so the reader can see which are structural and which are for exposition (pp. 6, 18).
- **D4 Analysis & proof technique**: The proof of Theorem 3.1 is a constructive geometric case analysis in two variables:
  - **Elimination to a plane** (eqs. (7)–(9), p. 8): solve the 4×6 linear system for (p, q, c12, s12) in terms of (c11, c22) via constants α, β (eq. (8)).
  - **Hyperbola with asymptotes** (eq. (10), p. 8): the coupling equation becomes c11 = c22 − 2β + (α² + β²)/c22, with asymptotes c11 − c22 = −2β and c22 = 0; generator bounds become a band parallel to the first asymptote (p. 8).
  - **Objective parallel to the band** (p. 8): with linear cost, level sets are parallel to the first asymptote, so only the generation lower bounds can affect the optimum; they combine into one effective lower bound Δ on c11 − c22 (eq. (11), p. 9).
  - **Named intersection points** c^O, c^R, c^E, c^L, c^I (eqs. (12)–(16), pp. 9–11): the five configurations follow from comparing these points with the voltage box; the gap in the inexact case has the closed form −G(c^L_22 − c^I_22) (p. 11).
  - **Repair argument** (p. 11): in the exact-but-non-unique case every SOCP-optimal point can be corrected to the OPF optimum by reducing c11, c22 and c12 by the same amount until reaching c^E.
  - **Proof before theorem**: the case analysis is written as bullets with figures, and then "The above analysis proves the following theorem." (p. 11).
  - **Validity of the cut** (Algorithm 1, pp. 18–20): four cases of the box corners relative to the inner radius; the cut is the secant line through two points on the inner circle; validity holds because the points cut off have norm below the lower radius (p. 18); in Case 4 the procedure only returns the bound c_ij ≥ lower bound, so it is omitted (p. 18).
  - Transferable technique: to classify all outcomes of a relaxation on a small instance, eliminate the linear equalities until two variables remain, draw the nonconvex curve and the relaxation region together, and name each intersection point; the theorem is then a table of inequalities between those points (pp. 8–11).
- **D5 Experiments**: Three tiers. (i) Small counterexamples with a one-parameter load sweep γ: 2-bus 2-generator (Table 2, p. 14) shows exact for γ ∈ [0.81, 0.99], gap up to 21.44% at γ = 1.01, OPF infeasible from γ = 1.02 while SOCP stays feasible until γ exceeds 2.93 (p. 12); with fixed voltages, a gap (573.82 vs 503.37) with angle difference below 1°, against the extension of Lavaei–Tse–Zhang to reactive lower bounds (p. 12); 3-bus 1-generator (Table 3, p. 14), with infeasibility "exactly caused by the lower bound on reactive generation power" (p. 15). OPF solved to global optimality with BARON, SOCP with MOSEK (p. 12). (ii) Instance library: radial networks built from MATPOWER 9-, 14-, 30-, 39-, 57-bus cases by switching off lines to a spanning tree, changing only loads and generation bounds, keeping realistic features rather than shrinking loads for larger networks; posted on Kocuk's site (p. 15). SDP (MOSEK, C#) vs BARON (relative gap 0 to certify, on reformulation (2) because the rectangular form "requires hours") vs MATPOWER vs IPOPT, each instance with quadratic and linear costs; reported objective, time and rank (eigenvalues > 10⁻⁵) (pp. 15–17, Table 4). Gaps up to more than 52% (case9 quadratic) and SDP rank up to 20 (p. 17). To show the gaps are "not artifacts of the cost parameters", they also report the 2-norm distance between SDP and BARON dispatches, 0.16 to 3.16 p.u. (p. 17). Local solvers: MATPOWER fails on seven instances, IPOPT fails on two and is suboptimal on three (p. 17). Hardware stated: Intel Core i5 3.33 GHz, 4 GB RAM (p. 17). (iii) Bounds and cuts in BARON (Table 5, p. 21): preprocessing, BARON and total times plus root gap. Results include the unfavourable ones: on 9-bus instances preprocessing dominates and total time rises (p. 20); on case39 quadratic, cuts slightly increase time (p. 20); root gap is sometimes slightly worse with cuts, explained by changed solver preprocessing (p. 22). Favourable: case_ieee30 from 8347.79 s to 17.63 s total (Table 5, p. 21; "less than 0.1%", p. 20); 57-bus from unsolved in 3 h (final gaps 38.40% and 29.37%, p. 17) to solved within 46 s, at the root node (p. 22).
  - Transferable technique: report total time including preprocessing (bound and cut computation) next to solver time, and say where the overhead makes things worse (pp. 20–21).
  - Transferable technique: when a relaxation's objective gap could be blamed on cost scaling, also report the distance between the relaxed and the global optimal decisions (p. 17).
  - Transferable technique: sweep a single scalar (load scaling γ) through the regimes exact → finite gap → OPF infeasible but relaxation feasible, and tabulate it (pp. 12–15).
- **D6 Positioning**: OPF literature is sorted into three categories: local methods with no quality guarantee, convex relaxations, and global methods, which are "in their infancy" (pp. 2–3). Prior SDP work is restated with its hypotheses rather than criticized; Lesieutre et al. are cited for the 3-bus cycle counterexample (p. 2). The two-bus exact case is "in accordance with the results in [26]" (p. 9); the fixed-voltage example shows that [13] "does not extend" to reactive lower bounds (p. 12). The Lasserre/moment hierarchy is set aside as possibly impractical because SDP size grows with the order (p. 3). The conclusion is conciliatory toward the SDP school: SDP relaxations "are very powerful and their importance is definite" (p. 22). Global optimization literature cited: Phan's Lagrangian B&B (p. 3), BARON (Tawarmalani–Sahinidis), McCormick (p. 23).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Abstract built on an enumerated trichotomy "(1) ... or (2) ... or (3)" and ends with the call for global methods (p. 1). A section roadmap closes the introduction (p. 3). Geometry is shown in figures before and alongside the theorem (Figs. 1–3, pp. 7, 10, 13). The order of the three outcomes differs between the abstract and §3 (p. 1 vs p. 6). Mild emphasis words ("Surprisingly", p. 6; "Quite impressively", p. 20). The theorem follows the analysis instead of preceding it (p. 11). An appendix explains BARON's algorithm for a power-systems audience (p. 23). Hedging is low; the negative findings on cuts are stated plainly (p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Contributions: analytic classification for the two-bus system (Theorem 3.1, p. 11); counterexamples on two and three buses (§4, pp. 12–15); a public library of radial instances with inexact SDP/SOCP (§5, p. 15); bound tightening and cuts that speed up BARON (§6, pp. 17–22). Limits stated specifically: reformulation (2) is valid only for radial networks, so "using BARON as it is may not be applicable to large-scale OPFs" (p. 17); the cuts did not improve the SOCP/SDP lower bound itself, only BARON's root relaxation (p. 22); on small instances preprocessing dominates (p. 20). Future directions: efficient global methods for OPF (pp. 1, 22); the cuts apply to meshed networks and, via c_ij = e_i e_j + f_i f_j and s_ij = e_i f_j − e_j f_i, to SDP relaxations as linear matrix inequalities (p. 22; claimed, not tested).

**Method links**: Method 4 ✅ evidence: a published exactness theorem is demarcated by a complete case analysis of the smallest system (all three outcomes with parameter ranges, Theorem 3.1, p. 11), plus a counterexample showing that a second positive result does not extend when reactive lower bounds are added (p. 12). Method 2 ✅ evidence: the relaxation is judged against a certified global optimum (BARON with relative gap 0, p. 15) and against local solvers, with a sanity check that the gaps are not cost artifacts (p. 17). Method 5 ⚠ variant: here the cheap relaxation (SOCP ≡ SDP on radial networks) is shown to fail, and the certificate comes from a general global solver strengthened by SOCP-based bound tightening and cuts (pp. 17–22); the cuts do not improve the SOCP bound (p. 22). This is the starting point that the later Strong SOCP paper answers; the "cheap relaxation as the deliverable" stance is not yet present. Method 3 ⚠ variant (weak): the sentence that all the nonconvexity is captured by the coupling constraints (2i) (p. 6) prefigures Method 3 step 2, and the cuts come from one small set per line (box ∩ ring, pp. 18–20), but there is no hull or substructure theory. Method 1: not applicable (the entry is an accepted theorem's hypothesis, not a solver habit). Heuristic 1 (make the hard instances and release them): evidence, the radial library from MATPOWER meshes (p. 15); this is the case cited in SKILL.md Heuristic 1. Heuristic 2 (strongest baseline): partial evidence, BARON run on the reformulation where it performs better (p. 15). Heuristic 6 (count every overhead): evidence, preprocessing time reported and counted against the cuts (pp. 20–21). Heuristic 3 (write dead ends in): evidence, the cuts' lack of effect on the SOCP/SDP bound is reported (p. 22). New-pattern candidates: **complete classification on the smallest instance** (exhaustively characterize a relaxation's outcomes on the simplest nontrivial system in closed form before showing larger counterexamples, pp. 6–11); **single-parameter regime sweep** (one load-scaling parameter moves a case through exact, gap and infeasible regimes, pp. 12–15).

**Transferable techniques**:
- List each published exactness/positive result with its hypothesis and find the hypothesis they all drop; justify why it matters in practice (pp. 2–3).
- For a relaxation on the smallest nontrivial instance, eliminate linear equalities down to two variables and classify outcomes by named intersection points; state the theorem as inequalities among them (pp. 8–11).
- Build counterexample instances from standard benchmarks by a structural restriction (spanning tree of a meshed network) plus parameter changes only, keep them realistic, and post them (p. 15).
- Optimization-based bound tightening with the convex relaxation, then a secant cut separating the bound box from the infeasible inner disc of an annulus constraint (Algorithm 1, pp. 18–20).
- Report preprocessing, solver and total times plus root gap, including instances where the added machinery hurts (Table 5, pp. 20–22).

**Quotes**:
- "Although, for our instances, we have not observed any lower bound improvement in SOCP/SDP relaxations by the inclusion of the valid inequalities, we obtain stronger root node relaxations in BARON." (p. 22)
- "Our work only serves to demonstrate the limitations of SDP relaxations and emphasizes the importance and the need to develop efficient global methods in solving OPF problems." (p. 22)
