### S105 · Solving sparse separable bilinear programs using lifted bilinear cover inequalities (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2024, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2022.0230; read as arXiv:2208.00345v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-23, the whole arXiv v1 text including all figures captions and references). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Xiaoyi Gu (Georgia Tech), Jean-Philippe P. Richard (Minnesota) · **Place in trajectory**: computational follow-up to their 2022 Math. Programming paper "Lifting convex inequalities for bipartite bilinear programs" (ref [12], p. 23); part of the 2019-2024 QCQP substructure line.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is a gap between theory and use: the lifted bilinear cover inequalities of [12] exist, but "its computational usefulness has not been evaluated" (§1.2, p. 4). Exact convex hulls of a separable bilinear set S are known ([11], [21]) but have "limited computational usefulness" because the description is exponential in the number of variables (p. 2), which motivates cut families. The model is explicitly patterned on the MILP story: lifting and lifted cover inequalities for 0-1 knapsacks (p. 1), and the experiment on the classical Crowder-Johnson-Padberg paper [7] that tested lifted covers on sparse instances "since most practical instances are sparse" (p. 1). Problem: min linear objective s.t. m separable bilinear rows sum_i a_i^j x_i y_i >= d_j, x, y in [0,1]^n (Definition 1, p. 2).
  - Transferable: when you have derived a cut family, write the follow-up by naming the classical MILP computational paper that did the same for the MILP analogue and copying its test design (sparse random rows), so the reader knows what "success" should look like (pp. 1-2, 9).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Three moves (p. 4): (i) clear away the natural stronger competitor: Proposition 1 shows McCormick+SDP gives the same bound as McCormick alone for (4), a generalisation of separable bilinear programs (pp. 5-7); (ii) a "simple randomized" guess-and-adjust separation heuristic for the cut (Algorithm 2, p. 8): label each variable J0 if x_i y_i < eps, J1 if > 1-eps, else I for positive coefficients and random J0/J1 (probability x_i y_i) for negative ones; if the partition is not a minimal cover yielding partition, flip labels according to which condition failed (right-hand side not positive, not a cover, not minimal), up to S_j attempts (pp. 7-9); the adjust step "shares similarity to the WALK SAT approach of [22]" (p. 9); (iii) a root-node cutting loop over SOCP relaxations, stopping on time, iteration limit or relative bound improvement < z (Algorithm 1, p. 7), then hand the strengthened model to a global solver. What did not change: the cuts are the ones from [12] (Theorems 1-3 restated, pp. 3-4); the global solver is used as is.
  - Transferable: a separation heuristic for a combinatorially indexed cut family (partition-based) can be "guess from the LP point, then repair the specific violated definitional condition by a random flip"; write one repair rule per failure mode of the definition (Algorithm 2 steps 14-16, p. 8).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Variables in [0,1]; each bilinear term appears once per row (separability, p. 2). Instances: random with m, n in {100, 250, 500}, p in {0.01, 0.02, 0.05}, kept only when np in [5, 20] ("reasonably" sparse), 15 settings × 10 instances × 2 sign classes (non-negative a in [0,1]; mixed signs a in [-1,1]); d_j = r_j s_j with r_j ~ U[0,1] or U[1,2] (pp. 9-10). The MT cut (7) of Tawarmalani-Richard-Chung [23] only applies to non-negative rows since there is "no easy way to complement the variables in mixed-signs instances" (p. 10).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Proposition 1 (p. 5): build an explicit PSD completion. Take an optimal McCormick point, fill the moment matrix with u_i u_k off the bilinear pairs and with w on them (p. 6); subtract the rank-one matrix G = (1,u*)(1,u*)^T so that W* - G has one non-zero off-diagonal entry per row; then Gershgorin's circle theorem reduces PSD-ness to the scalar inequality x_i - x_i^2 >= |w_i - x_i y_i|, proved by four cases at the extreme values of w allowed by McCormick (pp. 6-7). No theory for the heuristic; separation complexity left as a conjecture (p. 19).
  - Transferable: to show an SDP constraint adds nothing on top of an LP relaxation, complete the LP solution to a matrix as "rank-one part + sparse correction" and use Gershgorin row-dominance on the correction; when the correction has one off-diagonal per row, PSD-ness reduces to n scalar inequalities (pp. 5-7).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Python 3.9.7 + Gurobi 9.5.0 for both the SOCP and the nonconvex solver (NonConvex=2), one core and 8 GB per task on the ISyE cluster (p. 10). Two arms: Gurobi default with vs without root cuts; 1,800 s for the heuristic and 1,800 s for Gurobi, defended as fair because heuristic time is "significantly smaller" (p. 10) and confirmed by Figures 5-6 (heuristic a few seconds; total times nearly identical, p. 13). Parameters z = 5e-3, eps = 1e-2, T = 10np, S_j = 10 × row support (p. 10). Metric: relative gap closed rho = (z* - z_Mc)/(z_opt - z_Mc), with z_opt the best primal value found by either arm, and the difference Delta rho (p. 11); root gap closed by the cuts alone rho_Heu (p. 11). Results only in bar charts, no tables (Figs. 1-14, pp. 12-21): Delta rho positive "for most instances" (non-negative, p. 11) and "across every instance" (mixed signs, p. 15); root gap closed about 60% for most non-negative classes, independent of p (p. 13); the gain shrinks as density p grows (p. 11); MT cuts close much less root gap and end similar to default Gurobi (p. 15). No failure cases listed beyond the density trend; no node counts; no instance solved to optimality (all unsolved at 30 min, p. 13).
  - Transferable: when a separation routine runs before the solver, give both arms the same solver time limit and then show separately (a) the separation time and (b) total times, so a reader can see the time budget did not decide the comparison (pp. 10, 13).
  - Transferable: report gap closed both at the root by the cuts alone and at the time limit inside the solver; the first measures cut strength, the second whether the solver still benefits (pp. 11, 13).
- **D6 Positioning**: Continues [12] (own), and positions against two baselines: SDP strengthening of McCormick, which is "known" to give good bounds in general ([5] Burer) but is shown useless here (p. 4), and the closed-form cut (7) of [23] (Richard is a coauthor of both) (p. 10). MILP lifting and cover literature is cited as the inspiration (Balas, Wolsey, Padberg, Gu-Nemhauser-Savelsbergh; p. 1). Own sparse-cut papers [8, 9] cited for why sparsity matters (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contribution list of three bullets in §1.2 (p. 4), the SDP equivalence flagged as "surprisingly" (p. 4). Conclusions use "major and robust improvement" (p. 19) and "we believe the strength and potential of the lifted bilinear cover cuts were demonstrated by our experiments, which were most conclusive for sparser problems" (p. 19), a hedge tied to the density trend. The heuristic is called "basic" and its success is framed as evidence for the cuts rather than for the heuristic (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: computational (plus one small theorem). Limits stated concretely as four future directions (pp. 19, 22): exact separation unknown, conjectured NP-hard; randomness of the heuristic and separation from aggregations of rows; cuts only at the root, not integrated in the solver's tree; extension to general QCQPs through a separable-bilinear relaxation. Unstated limits (my reading): only synthetic random instances; z_opt is the best primal found, not a proven optimum (p. 11); results reported as class averages in figures only.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: lifting and cover inequalities imported from 0-1 knapsack MILP into a separable bilinear substructure, with the Crowder-Johnson-Padberg sparse-instance test design copied (pp. 1-2, 9).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the strengthened relaxation is SOC-representable "making it easy to incorporate in relaxations" (p. 4), and the heavier SDP option is shown to add nothing on this class (Prop. 1, pp. 5-7); deliverable is dual-bound gap closed (p. 11).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (weak): open separation complexity stated as a conjecture rather than left silent (p. 19).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: there is no ground-truth ceiling here; the comparison is against default Gurobi with z_opt = best primal found by either arm (p. 11), not against an exact optimum.
- Heuristic 7 (theory paper followed by measurement-only paper): this is the case the SKILL cites; the sentence is on p. 4. Heuristic 9 (borrow one tool): WalkSAT-style repair (p. 9).
- New-pattern candidate: "rule out the heavier relaxation by an equivalence theorem before offering cheap cuts" (Prop. 1, pp. 5-7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Gershgorin-based PSD completion proof that SDP = LP on a structured class (pp. 5-7).
- Guess-and-repair randomized separation with one repair rule per violated definitional condition (pp. 7-9).
- Equal-time two-arm protocol with separate timing plots for the add-on step (pp. 10, 13).
- Restrict random instance generation to a band of expected row support (np in [5, 20]) so that "sparse" has a fixed meaning across sizes (p. 10).

**Quotes**:
- "We show that, surprisingly, for the class of separable instances, the SDP relaxation gives a bound equal to that of the McCormick relaxation." (p. 4)
- "We developed a heuristic for separation since we could not design a polynomial-time exact separation routine." (p. 19)

### S075 · Optimizing the design and operation of water networks: Two decomposition approaches (Computers & Chemical Engineering 2025, doi:10.1016/j.compchemeng.2024.108897; read as the preprint dated August 7, 2024)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-29, whole preprint including references). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Yijiang Li, Nikolaos V. Sahinidis (Georgia Tech); Naresh Susarla, Miguel A. Zamarripa, Markus G. Drouven (NETL) · **Place in trajectory**: application paper with a DOE/NETL produced-water project (acknowledgment, p. 26); reuses the CVXNLP decomposition of the same first three authors' gas-network paper (Li et al. 2024, arXiv:2307.07648; p. 3, p. 28).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Application-driven: produced water co-produced with oil and gas, with volumes rising while disposal capacity falls (p. 2). Three modelling facts drive the problem: production decreases over time (multi-period), node elevations matter, and the number of pump stations is limited (p. 2). The paper co-optimises design (pipe diameters, pump locations) and operation (pump schedules, relief valves) because "network operators in many cases are also the network designers" (p. 3). The algorithmic entry is a diagnostic: in preliminary runs BARON and SCIP improve dual bounds consistently but fail to find feasible points (p. 11; abstract p. 1), so the paper targets primal solutions.
  - Transferable: before designing an algorithm for an application MINLP, run two global solvers and record which side (primal or dual bound) stalls; aim the method at that side only and hand the result back to the solver (pp. 11, 22-23).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Two primal-side decompositions, each ending by warm-starting SCIP for the remainder of 5 hours (Algorithm 4, p. 23). (a) CVXNLP-based decomposition (adapted from the gas-network paper): master problem over diameters with a convex flow-energy objective and a budget constraint C; subproblem fixes diameters and checks feasibility of flows, pressures, pumps and schedules; infeasible → integer no-good cut (41); time-out → double C; initial C from a cheapest-diameter master with no-good cuts (§4.1, pp. 13-16; Algorithm 1, p. 16). (b) Time decomposition: since production is high early and falls, solve the restricted problem on periods {1..T~} to accuracy eps (with the pump-on budget scaled by T~/T, eq. (48)), fix diameters and pump locations, then solve a feasibility problem for the remaining periods, carrying minimum-up/down states over the boundary by a fixing procedure (Algorithm 2, p. 20); sweep T~ in a set and eps in a set, keep the cheapest feasible result (Algorithm 3, p. 21). Justification for (b) is a physical argument: larger flows mean larger friction loss, which can only be fixed by a pump (p. 17).
  - Transferable: when demand in a multi-period design problem is monotone, solve the design on the hardest prefix of periods and check the remaining periods as a pure feasibility problem with design fixed; sweep the prefix length and solve accuracy and keep the best feasible (pp. 16-21).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Steady-state flow; Hazen-Williams head loss with eta = 0.852 (Darcy noted as alternative) (pp. 7, 11); bidirectional flow modelled by a binary direction variable and split flows (p. 10); discrete diameter choices with copies of flow variables per diameter (p. 9); one-variable min-up/min-down constraints because start-up costs are ignored (p. 8); every pipe has a relief valve with unbounded relief (p. 8); source pressures fixed (p. 9). Objective = construction cost of pipes only; pump and valve costs left out (p. 10, future work p. 26). Time decomposition assumes production decreasing over time; a non-monotone variant is sketched but its fixing procedure "will require modifications" (p. 19).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a. No theorems; the only arguments are the modelling rewrite (33) that absorbs pump boost and valve relief into node pressures so the master can ignore them (pp. 13-14) and the physical argument for time decomposition (p. 17). Validity of the CVXNLP master as a relaxation is taken from Raghunathan (2013) and Li et al. (2024) (p. 11), not re-proved here.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: One network derived from the PARETO strategic case study: 19 sources, 7 sinks, 29 in-nodes, 58 pipes (Table 2, p. 22); T in {24, 53}; base scenario plus stress factors {0.1, 0.5, 1.5, 2.0} multiplying demand and supply (p. 22); four diameter choices with cost proportional to circumference (p. 22). Intel i9 3.70 GHz, 64 GB; Pyomo, SCIP via GAMS chosen after SCIP did "slightly better" than BARON (p. 22). Baseline: SCIP on the compact formulation for 5 hours. Metrics: primal bound, dual bound, gap (C_up - C_low)/C_up (p. 23). Results: T = 24, SCIP finds primal points only at stress 1.0 and 2.0; both decompositions find points at all stresses, time decomposition gaps 0-8.92% vs CVXNLP up to 26.23% (Table 3, p. 24); T = 53, SCIP finds none; time decomposition gaps <= 13.53%, CVXNLP fails at stress 2.0 and reaches 86.66% and 94.77% gaps at 1.0 and 1.5 (Table 4, p. 25). Times to first primal solution are said to be recorded (Algorithm 4, p. 23) but not tabulated. Single run per instance; no seeds.
  - Transferable: when the comparison is "solver alone vs solver warm-started by my heuristic", report primal bound, dual bound and gap side by side for both arms in the same time budget; the dual-bound column shows what the heuristic's own time cost the solver (p. 24).
- **D6 Positioning**: Review splits water-network design into water-using networks (contaminants; Castro and Teles) and hydraulic design, and places the paper in the second (p. 4); cites Bragalli et al. (Bonmin), Raghunathan (linearisation-based B&B, CVXNLP), Pecci et al., Ghaddar et al. (Lagrangian pump scheduling), Bonvin-Demassey-Lodi (LP/NLP B&B) (pp. 4-5), unit-commitment min-up/min-down polytopes (Ostrowski et al., Lee-Leung-Margot, Rajan-Takriti) (pp. 5-6), and rolling horizon (Glomb et al.) for time decomposition (p. 6). Prior work is described neutrally; no direct critique. Novelty claimed: unified design-plus-operation model and two decompositions (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contributions in one paragraph, two numbered claims (p. 3); headline comparative claim ("the time decomposition approach is more effective", p. 3; "outperforms", p. 26). Results prose explains anomalies with a stated belief: SCIP's two successes came from heuristics, "We believe that certain problem structures may trigger heuristics in SCIP" (p. 24). Model presented as blocks with a summary table (Table 1, p. 12) and subproblems described block by block as changes to the compact model (pp. 14-15, 18-19).
  - Transferable: describe each decomposition subproblem as a list of block-by-block edits to one compact formulation (objective, flow, pressures, pipes, pumps), with "this block remains unchanged" where true (pp. 14-15).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: modelling + algorithmic heuristics for an application, evaluated on one network. Limits stated: pump-station and relief-valve costs not modelled (future work, p. 26); the restricted problem in time decomposition may be too slow for larger networks or horizons, suggesting CVXNLP inside it (p. 25); non-monotone production needs a modified fixing procedure (p. 19). Unstated (my reading): single network, single run, no time-to-first-solution numbers, no comparison with BARON or with rolling horizon in the final tables, and the decompositions carry no optimality guarantee; certified gaps come only from SCIP's dual bound.

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: the deliverable is still a certified gap (primal + dual bound, Tables 3-4, pp. 24-25), but here the dual side comes free from the solver and the contribution is on the primal side; there is no own relaxation.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: baseline is the full solver run for 5 hours (p. 22), no ground truth or stripped instrument; the protocol is warm-start vs no warm-start.
- Workflow A step 2 (ask what standard tools fail at in an application) ✅ evidence: preliminary BARON/SCIP runs locate the failure on the primal side (p. 11).
- New-pattern candidate: "Diagnose the stalled side (primal vs dual) with off-the-shelf solvers first, then build only for that side and feed it back" (pp. 1, 11, 23).
- New-pattern candidate: "Reuse your own decomposition across application domains" (gas networks → water networks, pp. 3, 11).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Time decomposition on the peak-demand prefix plus feasibility check on the tail, with a min-up/min-down state hand-over procedure (Algorithms 2-3, pp. 19-21).
- Budget-doubling outer loop with no-good cuts around a convex master and feasibility subproblem (§4.1, pp. 13-16).
- Stress factors applied to the base scenario to build an instance ladder from one real network (p. 22).

**Quotes**:
- "In the preliminary study, we noticed that both solvers are able to improve the dual bounds consistently but have trouble obtaining primal (feasible) solutions." (p. 11)
- "Moreover, our results show that state-of-the-art solvers struggle to find a feasible solution within the same amount of computational time." (p. 3)

### S104 · Lagrangian dual for integer optimization with zero duality gap that admits decomposition (IPCO 2025, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-93112-3_14; read as arXiv:2411.12085v1)
**Read level**: full for the main text pp. 1-16 and Appendices A-E and G (pp. 17-31, 37-38); Appendix F (pp. 31-37: sub-gradient analysis and the proofs of Propositions 6-7) skimmed, reading the statements of Lemma 4, Corollary 2, Propositions 15-17 and the full proof of Proposition 7 (pp. 36-37). The text read carries the arXiv title "Lagrangian dual with zero duality gap that admits decomposition" (p. 1). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Diego Cifuentes, Jingye Xu (Georgia Tech) · **Place in trajectory**: Lagrangian/duality line (exact augmented Lagrangian for MIQP with Gu and Ahmed, ref [21], p. 15) joined with the sparsity/tree-decomposition line and the sparse-cut proof technique of Dey-Molinaro-Wang (ref [14], p. 6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Two-block MIP with binary coupling x^(1) = x^(2) (eq. (1), pp. 1-2). Dualising the coupling gives a decomposable but gapped dual; solving the MIP directly closes the gap but loses decomposition (p. 2). Main question: can a Lagrangian-style relaxation have zero duality gap and still decompose (p. 2)? Secondary motivation against dynamic programming: DP also decomposes along a tree decomposition but gives no bound until it ends (p. 2). Augmented Lagrangian gets strong duality but not decomposability because penalties must be norms (p. 3).
  - Transferable: frame a methods paper as a "twin goal" that the two obvious approaches each half-meet (classical Lagrangian: decomposable, gapped; monolithic solve: exact, not decomposable), and say what each competitor lacks in one sentence (pp. 2-3).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Add primal-redundant constraints that are separable after dualising, F^(1)(x^(1)) + F^(2)(x^(2)) >= 0 (eq. (4), p. 3); they do nothing primal but can raise the dual. Proposition 1: if either F is affine the dual does not change (p. 4), so the redundant constraints must be nonlinear. Two choices (p. 4): monomial-based, prod_{j in S} x_j^(1) = prod_{j in S} x_j^(2) for S in a family (M-Lagrangian, an RLT-like hierarchy, but unlike RLT products of (x^(1) - x^(2)) terms it stays separable, Remark 1, p. 5); vertex-based, one indicator product per vertex of [0,1]^n (V-Lagrangian), whose subproblem is solved in the original space with no-good cuts (Algorithm 1, p. 7), acting like column generation and related to Ahmed's scenario decomposition algorithm (SDA) (p. 5). For general sparse MIPs, blocks come from a tree decomposition of the constraint intersection graph (Definitions 1-2, p. 9). What did not change: the subgradient/bundle dual machinery. Cost: subproblems now have nonlinear objectives (p. 13).
  - Transferable: to strengthen a Lagrangian dual without losing separability, add constraints that are redundant in the primal and separable in the copies; first prove that affine ones cannot help (Prop. 1, p. 4), which tells you to search among nonlinear ones (pp. 3-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Binary coupling variables, rational data, mixed-integer non-negative y (p. 2); coupling only on binaries in the general case (p. 10). Down-closed monomial families (Assumptions 1 and 3, pp. 6, 10). Packing bounds need relatively complete recourse (Assumptions 2 and 4, pp. 6, 10), which the authors say can be enforced by adding constraints (pp. 6, 11). Proposition 6 assumes non-negative objective data (p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) Geoffrion's primal characterization of the Lagrangian dual as optimisation over convex hulls of the blocks (p. 5, proof of Prop. 1 pp. 17-18). (b) Strong duality (Theorems 2, 8, pp. 6, 10; proof Appendix C, pp. 18-22): the monomial vectors of a 0-1 point over all subsets of the shared variables are affinely independent (Proposition 14, proved by choosing a maximal support, pp. 19-20); with affinely independent shared coordinates, "convex hulls glued on equal shared coordinates" equals "convex hull of glued points" (Proposition 13 via the simplex lemma, pp. 18-19); induction removes a leaf of the tree decomposition (pp. 20-21). (c) A cycle counterexample (17): three blocks pairwise coupled on single variables, MIP value 1 but even the all-monomial dual gives 0, so the tree structure is needed (p. 9). (d) Multiplicative bounds for packing/covering at level k (Theorems 3-4, 9-10, pp. 6, 11; proofs pp. 22-28): cover the variables fractionally by "good"/"k-good" subsets (LPs (23)-(24), p. 11), build a feasible MIP point from a convex combination restricted to a good set (Lemma 3, pp. 22-25), "techniques similar to those presented in [14]" (p. 6); symmetry reduces the covering LP to two dimensions for two-stage problems (Corollary 1, pp. 28-29). (e) Tightness probed with small examples: n = 2, k = 1 worst found 5/4 vs bound 4/3 (packing) and 3/4 vs 2/3 (covering); tightness open (Remark 2, p. 12; examples p. 30). (f) Complexity comparison with SDA: subgradient iteration bound via a bound on the optimal multiplier norm (Props. 15-17, pp. 32-36) and an instance where SDA needs at least 2^{n-3} iterations (Prop. 7, proof pp. 36-37).
  - Transferable: to prove that coupling copies through all subset-monomials of the shared variables gives exact convexification, use affine independence of the monomial vectors of 0-1 points plus induction on leaves of the tree decomposition (pp. 18-22).
  - Transferable: for packing/covering approximation ratios of a relaxation family indexed by k, write an LP that fractionally covers the variables by subsets on which the relaxation is exact, and read the ratio off its optimal value; exploit symmetry to shrink that LP to two variables (pp. 11, 22-29).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Called "Preliminary" (§4, p. 12). Windows PC, i7 12th gen, 16 GB; Julia 1.9, Gurobi 9.0.2, Clarabel for bundle QPs (p. 12). Two classes, 10 instances each, 10 blocks of maximum-cardinality stable set on random graphs, coupled as a star (STAR-STAB, 33 shared variables with block 1) or a path (PATH-STAB) (p. 12). Methods: Gurobi on the monolith, classical Lagrangian (L), M-Lagrangian with quadratic monomials only (QL), V-Lagrangian (VL, star only), SDA (star only); each Lagrangian warm-started from 600 s of L; bundle methods (proximal for L and QL, bundle-level for VL, chosen empirically, Appendix G.3, p. 37); subproblems solved sequentially "In our naive implementation" (p. 12); 1,200 s limit; no method terminated; upper bounds from Gurobi's feasible solution (p. 12). Metric: 1 - dual/primal. Results: STAR gaps Gurobi 10.0%, L 6.0%, QL 4.4%, VL 3.9%, SDA 8.6%; PATH gaps Gurobi 10.8%, L 3.5%, QL 1.2% (Tables 1-2, p. 13). Tables do not say whether gaps are averages over the 10 instances. Inconsistency: §4 says random graphs on 100 nodes (p. 12); Appendix G.1 says "on 75 nodes" while sampling edges from a clique of 100 nodes (p. 37). A scaling factor 2 on the vertex constraints is reported to help numerically (G.2, p. 37).
  - Transferable: when comparing Lagrangian variants, warm-start every richer dual from the same classical-dual solution (append zero multipliers) and give all methods the same wall-clock limit, so differences come from the extra multipliers (p. 12).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Geoffrion (Lagrangian relaxation), Lemaréchal-Renaud (Prop. 1 is "a strengthening on a result from [26]", p. 4), Adams-Sherali RLT (contrasted, p. 5), Burer copositive and own sensitivity-analysis reformulation [11, 13] and Minoux-Ouzia DRL* [27] (p. 5); compares with Ahmed's SDA [2] theoretically and computationally (pp. 7-8, 12-13); cites exact AL [16, 21] (one own) and the decomposable AL variant of Sun-Sun-Yin [33] (p. 3). Explicit caution that "significantly more sophisticated methods" exist for two-stage stochastic problems (p. 13).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contributions enumerated (1.)-(5.) with section pointers (pp. 2-3); main question stated as a question in the introduction (p. 2); all proofs deferred to appendices, main text gives statements and short remarks. Hedging is explicit: "Preliminary", "naive implementation", "may not necessarily be competitive", "the preliminary results are encouraging" (pp. 12-13). Editing slips: the main text sends proofs of Theorems 2-4 to "Appendix D" (pp. 5-6) while Theorem 2's proof is in Appendix C (p. 18); Proposition 7 says SDA "requires O(2^n) iterations" (p. 8) where the proof gives a lower bound of 2^{n-3} (p. 37).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: theory (two exact decomposable duals, strong-duality theorems, hierarchy bounds) plus a proof-of-concept computation. Limits stated concretely (p. 13): zero gap costs harder subproblems with nonlinear objectives, so the approach suits "a large number of sub-problems" each "not too large"; open question of decomposable zero-gap duals with "easier" subproblems; implementation engineering and instances with general tree decompositions (MIPLIB sparsity, [7]) left as future work. Also open: tightness of Theorems 9-10 (p. 12). V-Lagrangian does not generalise easily beyond star structures (p. 8).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the motivation for a Lagrangian route over exact DP is anytime dual bounds (p. 2), and results are reported as dual-bound gaps (p. 13); the SKILL already lists this DOI under Method 5.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: affine redundant constraints provably give nothing (Prop. 1, p. 4); the cycle counterexample shows where the monomial dual fails without tree structure (p. 9); tightness tested on small cases and reported open (Remark 2, p. 12); an exponential lower-bound instance for the competitor SDA (Prop. 7, pp. 8, 36-37).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the direction is reversed. Instead of taking MILP substructure ideas into QCQP, the paper takes a polynomial-lifting idea (monomial variables in the spirit of RLT, copositive reformulations and Bienstock-Munoz) back into MIP Lagrangian decomposition (pp. 5, 9).
- Research Taste 5 ✅ (sparsity as structure): decomposability for general MIPs comes from the tree decomposition of the intersection graph (pp. 2, 9).
- New-pattern candidate: "Reuse your own approximation-ratio device in a new line" (packing/covering multiplicative bounds via fractional covering by good subsets, "techniques similar to those presented in [14]", p. 6; pp. 22-29).
- New-pattern candidate: "Rule out the cheap version by a no-gain proposition before building the expensive one" (Prop. 1, p. 4; compare S105 Prop. 1).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Primal-redundant but dual-separable constraints as a general way to tighten Lagrangian duals (pp. 3-5).
- Affine independence of 0-1 monomial vectors + leaf induction for exactness along a tree decomposition (pp. 18-22).
- Fractional covering LP over "good" variable subsets for multiplicative bounds of a hierarchy on packing/covering problems (pp. 11, 22-29).
- A three-block cycle example to show why a tree (not a cycle) of couplings is needed (p. 9).
- Warm-start every dual variant from the classical dual solution with appended zeros (p. 12).
