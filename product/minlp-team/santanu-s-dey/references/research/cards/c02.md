### S033 · Split rank of triangle and quadrilateral inequalities (Mathematics of Operations Research 2011, doi:10.1287/moor.1110.0496; arXiv:0906.0887)
**Read level**: full. All 39 pages read, including the case proofs of §7.1.3-7.1.6, §7.2-7.3 and §8, and Appendix 1 (p. 39). The text read is arXiv v1 (4 Jun 2009), not the journal version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Quentin Louveaux (Dey was then at CORE, UCLouvain, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the multi-row (two-row) cutting-plane period, the line that Heuristic 4 says later turned into sparse cuts.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The two-row relaxation P(R,f) of a simplex tableau has facets that are split cuts or triangle/quadrilateral intersection cuts (Andersen et al.; Cornuéjols-Margot), p. 1. A known negative fact sets the question: Cook-Kannan-Schrijver's T1 triangle has infinite split rank. Hence "Which facet-defining inequalities for (1) have a finite split rank?" (p. 1). The motivation is to understand the new multi-row cuts against the classical split family, citing Espinoza's experiments that "these new inequalities may be useful computationally" (p. 1).
  - Transferable: when a known counterexample shows one member of a family is out of reach, make the question "which members other than that one are reachable?" and aim for an if-and-only-if.
- **D2 Core idea**: Theorem 3.1 (p. 4): a non-trivial facet has finite split rank iff its induced lattice-free set Lα is not a T1 triangle. The proof is constructive: for each type (T2B, T2A, T3, Q1, Q2) it gives an explicit sequence of split disjunctions, alternating (x1≤0)∨(x1≥1) and (x2≤0)∨(x2≥1) and keeping only one valid inequality per step, which converges to the target cut in finitely many steps (outline p. 15; Disjunction Sequence 7.1 p. 16; 7.2 p. 21). The harder types are built from easier ones: T2A and T3 need one rank more than auxiliary T2B cuts built by the "∧" construction (pp. 28-31); Q1 and Q2 need one rank more than two T2/Q1 cuts (pp. 32-37).
  - Transferable: to show an inequality has finite rank, build it, i.e. give an explicit sequence of disjunctions with a selection rule that keeps a single inequality per step, so that each step's validity is checked on a few vertices.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: f ∈ Q²\Z², R rational, P(R,f) ≠ ∅, no zero columns (p. 2). Split rank depends on the formulation, and the paper says so (p. 2). The main reduction assumes cone(R) = R² (Prop. 4.1 handles the case where it is not, pp. 6-7). The T3 and Q2 proofs assume, "for simplicity", that the auxiliary sets are not subsets of split sets, and say the proof "can be modified" otherwise (pp. 30, 35); those modifications are not written out.
  - Transferable: when a degenerate subcase is routine, state the simplifying assumption at the point of use and name it.
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) reduction lemmas that shrink the problem: Shape Lemma 5.1 (rank is monotone in the induced lattice-free set, p. 8), Projection Prop. 5.2 (dropping a column does not raise the rank, p. 9), Simple/General Lifting Props. 5.3/5.1 (pp. 9-10); with these it is enough to study at most four continuous variables (p. 5). (ii) Invariance under integral translation and unimodular maps (Observation 5.1, p. 11), used to reduce to "standard" triangles (Prop. 7.2, p. 13). (iii) Validity of a split cut checked by the "x-vertices" of Q≤ and Q≥, which have support ≤2 (Observation 7.1, Prop. 7.4, p. 14). (iv) Convergence by a closed-form recursion on intersection points (Lemma 7.5, p. 19) and a monotone-bounded-sequence argument (Lemma 7.6, pp. 19-20); for Case 4, a two-phase divergence argument showing step lengths increase (Lemmas 7.12-7.13, pp. 25-27; Appendix 1 computation p. 39). (v) Case enumeration of vertices by support size (T3 p. 31; Q2 pp. 35-37).
  - Transferable: shrink a rank question to the smallest canonical instance through three monotonicity lemmas (shape, projection, lifting) plus unimodular invariance, and only then do the case analysis.
  - Transferable: prove finite convergence of an alternating construction by tracking one coordinate of a moving intersection point as a recursion, then showing it is monotone and its limit passes the target.
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The figures (pp. 4, 8, 12-13, 17, 23, 25, 27-28, 30, 32, 34, 39) illustrate the constructions; they are not data.
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds directly on Andersen-Louveaux-Weismantel-Wolsey and Cornuéjols-Margot (facet classification, p. 1) and on Cook-Kannan-Schrijver (the one negative case, p. 5). Recovers known ranks as a check: sequential-merge inequalities give rank 2 (Dey-Richard, p. 29); two-row mixing inequalities give rank 2, matching Dash-Günlük's bound, tight by Andersen et al., Dey, Dash-Günlük (p. 31). Draws heavily on the authors' own prior work (Dey-Wolsey lifting and standardization, pp. 13, 28-29).
  - Transferable: after a general theorem, rederive the known special-case ranks from your construction to show it is not loose.
- **D7 Writing**: The main result and a numbered proof outline (six steps, each tied to a section) come first, in §3 (pp. 4-6). The paper admits the proof "is technical" (p. 5). The reader is guided by figures with fixed legends (dotted = target, dashed = previous, solid = new, pp. 17, 23). It states plainly what the authors cannot prove: they are "unable to proof" that Disjunction Sequence 7.1 converges when r³₁ > 0, and Sequence 7.2 "does not seem to apply" in the other case (p. 21).
  - Transferable: give a figure convention for iterative geometric constructions and reuse it in every frame.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theoretical; it settles finiteness of split rank for all two-row facets except T1. Limits: no bound on the rank in general (finite but not quantified for T2B Cases 1, 2, 4; only Case 3 gives exactly two, p. 20). Two non-split-set assumptions are left to the reader (pp. 30, 35). No future-work section; the paper ends with the Q2 proof (p. 37).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the headline is an if-and-only-if whose boundary (T1) is the known negative case (Thm 3.1, pp. 4-5). The paper also says where its own proof technique fails (p. 21), as in Method 4 step 6.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: this is the MILP side of the paradigm (a two-row relaxation as the substructure, compared with split closures, pp. 1-2). It is the source that Method 3 later carries over to QCQP, not an import into MINLP.
- Heuristic 4 ✅ evidence (context): the paper sits in the multi-row-cut line, motivated by early computation (Espinoza, p. 1).
- new-pattern candidate: **reduce to a canonical instance through monotonicity lemmas** (shape/projection/lifting + unimodular standardization before case analysis, pp. 5, 8-11, 13).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Show finite rank constructively with an explicit disjunction sequence and a "keep one inequality" rule, so that each step's validity is a finite vertex check (pp. 14-16).
- Use shape, projection and lifting lemmas to reduce any number of columns to ≤4 before the case work (p. 5, pp. 8-11).
- Get the hard shapes from easy ones: build auxiliary cuts of a type already handled (the "∧" construction) and add one disjunction (pp. 28-30).

**Quotes**:
- "The proof is constructive and given a facet-defining triangle or quadrilateral inequality we present an explicit sequence of split inequalities that can be used to generate it." (p. 1)
- "Instead of considering only facet-defining inequalities, we analyze the split rank of the larger set of valid inequalities whose induced lattice-free set is described in Proposition 2.3" (p. 5)

### S042 · The Chvátal-Gomory closure of a strictly convex body (Mathematics of Operations Research 2011, doi:10.1287/moor.1110.0488)
**Read level**: full. All 15 pages read, including the Appendix (omitted proofs of Prop. 2.1, Lemma 3.1, Prop. 5.2, pp. 13-14).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Daniel Dadush, Juan Pablo Vielma · **Place in trajectory**: the convex-MINLP closure period, which SKILL.md Method 3 cites (doi:10.1287/moor.1110.0488); it extends the Dey-Vielma IPCO 2010 ellipsoid result [11] (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Schrijver's theorem (the CG closure of a rational polyhedron is a rational polyhedron, Thm 1.1, p. 1) had been extended beyond polyhedra only for rational ellipsoids [11] (p. 2). With convex nonlinear IP and its cutting planes growing, "Many of these inequalities are based on the generalization of ideas used in linear IP" (p. 2), so the question is whether the CG closure of a non-polyhedral convex set is polyhedral. The paper notes that the original finite-convergence proof neither uses nor implies polyhedrality of C¹ (p. 3), so the question is genuinely open.
  - Transferable: pick a classical MILP structural theorem and ask whether it survives when the relaxation is nonlinear; check first that no existing proof already covers it.
- **D2 Core idea**: Prove polyhedrality at the opposite end of the "curvature spectrum" from polyhedra: strictly convex bodies (Thm 2.2) and their intersections with rational polyhedra (Thm 2.3) (p. 4). Key step: every non-integral boundary point of a strictly convex body is separated by a CG cut (Prop. 3.1 S1, p. 8). Compactness of the boundary then gives finitely many cuts (Step 1, Fig. 1, p. 4), and only finitely many further cuts can matter (Step 2, Prop. 4.2, p. 10). What changes relative to the polyhedral proofs: TDI systems, integral generating sets and cut domination do not extend (p. 2), so the separation step is new. The ellipsoid proof strategy of [11] is kept (p. 4).
  - Transferable: when the classical proof relies on structure the new class lacks (facets, TDI), keep its high-level two-step scheme (finite cover, then finitely many extra cuts) and replace only the step that broke.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: C compact, full-dimensional, strictly convex (Def. 2.1, p. 3). Thm 2.3 allows a rational polyhedron P, which relaxes full dimensionality only when aff(C) is rational (p. 4). Constraint qualification P ∩ int(C) ≠ ∅ is obtained for free from strict convexity when dim(C∩P) ≥ 1 (Lemma 5.2, p. 12). The sufficient conditions are isolated: Props. 4.1-4.2 need only boundary-separation properties S1, S2, "satisfied by every strictly convex body ... but they may also be satisfied by some convex bodies that are not strictly convex" (p. 9).
  - Transferable: restate the theorem in terms of the property the proof actually uses (S1/S2, C1'/C2') so that it can be reused beyond the stated class.
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) Two worked counterexamples showing why the naive choice of an integral normal fails (Examples 2.1-2.2, pp. 4-5, continued as Example 3.1, pp. 5-6). (ii) Dirichlet simultaneous Diophantine approximation plus a fixed perturbation w, so that the sequence satisfies (P1) vanishing gap and (P2) fractional part bounded away from 0 (Thm 3.1, Lemma 3.2, pp. 6-7). (iii) Continuity of the unique maximizer map m_C under strict convexity (Lemma 3.1, p. 6; proof p. 14). (iv) An open-cover / finite-subcover argument on the compact boundary, and on the sphere minus the tangent cone at integer boundary points (Cor. 3.1, pp. 8-9; Prop. 4.1, p. 10). (v) A finiteness argument: cuts with ‖a‖ ≥ 1/ε cannot cut interior vertices (Prop. 4.2, p. 10). (vi) Reduction of lower-dimensional sets by integer Farkas and unimodular maps (Prop. 5.1, p. 11). (vii) Support-function splitting σ_{P∩C} = σ_P(a_P)+σ_C(a_C) via Fenchel duality (Prop. 5.2, p. 12; proof p. 14), used to lift a face cut to a cut for P∩C (Prop. 5.3, pp. 12-13). (viii) Induction on dimension (proof of Thm 2.3, p. 13). Remark 3.1 explains why S1 must fail for general convex bodies: otherwise CG cuts would separate fractional points of facets of integral polytopes (p. 8).
  - Transferable: to turn "each bad point can be cut" into "finitely many cuts suffice", make each separating cut's region open and use compactness.
  - Transferable: to get an integer vector close in direction to a real normal while keeping a controlled fractional part, take Dirichlet approximants plus a fixed integral perturbation.
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D6 Positioning**: Says it generalizes both Schrijver [29] and its own ellipsoid result [11] (pp. 1-2, 4). Explains why the earlier polyhedral proofs [10, 29, 30] do not extend (p. 2), and why a natural sandwich-by-rational-polytopes approach does not obviously work (p. 2). It places itself in the convex MINLP literature (FilMINT, Bonmin, perspective cuts, conic MIR; refs [1-8, 13, 16-18, 28]).
  - Transferable: in the introduction, rule out the two obvious proof routes and say why they fail before giving your own.
- **D7 Writing**: The main results and a proof outline (Fig. 1 procedure, §2.1-2.2) come before any technical section (pp. 3-5). Small worked examples show the obstacle before the general lemma (pp. 4-6). Hedged and limited: "Unfortunately, it is not clear how to use these results or adapt their proofs to generalize the result to every convex body" (p. 4). The appendix makes the paper self-contained for convex-analysis facts not stated in [20] (p. 3).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theoretical (a structural closure result for nonlinear IP). The stated limit is that general convex bodies remain open: "a whole new set of methodologies and insights need to be developed" (p. 2). Strict convexity is needed in the proof for continuity of m_C (Remark 3.1, p. 8). There is no algorithmic consequence, and finiteness is not quantified (no bound on the number of cuts).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: imports a MILP closure theorem into convex nonlinear IP; the intro names the "generalization of ideas used in linear IP" as the driver (p. 2). This is the 2010-17 convex MINLP practice that SKILL.md cites.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the result is placed between two ends of the curvature spectrum, the open middle is named (pp. 2, 4), and Remark 3.1 shows why the key separation property cannot hold for every convex body (p. 8).
- new-pattern candidate: **state the theorem through the property the proof uses** (S1/S2 and C1'/C2' replace strict convexity, pp. 9-10).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Finite-subcover argument: open sets of points cut by each CG cut, compact boundary, finitely many cuts (pp. 8-10).
- Dirichlet approximation plus a fixed integral perturbation to manufacture separating integral normals (pp. 6-8).
- Fenchel/support-function decomposition to lift cuts from a face F∩C to P∩C (pp. 12-13).

**Quotes**:
- "We observe here that while various proofs of Theorem 1.1 have been presented, unfortunately none of them seem to extend to the case of non-polyhedral convex sets." (p. 2)
- "we believe that a whole new set of methodologies and insights need to be developed to understand the structure of the CG closure of a general convex set." (p. 2)

### S002 · Solving mixed integer bilinear problems using MILP formulations (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2013, doi:10.1137/110836183)
**Read level**: full. All 24 pages read: theory §2-3, all computational §4 tables 4.1-4.11 and figures 4.1-4.3, conclusion, references.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Akshay Gupte, Shabbir Ahmed, Myun Seok Cheon (ExxonMobil; research funded by ExxonMobil, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: first MINLP/bilinear paper in the corpus; it precedes the pooling MILP-relaxation line (2013-17) of SKILL.md "Signature Work Anatomy".

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Mixed integer bilinear programs where every bilinear term is (nonnegative integer) × (nonnegative continuous) (BLP1, p. 1). The paper argues this covers approximations of general bilinear problems by discretizing some continuous variables (p. 1). Entry: earlier work strengthens relaxations inside spatial B&B; instead, use MILP technology when the integrality constraints are "hard" (p. 2). Binary expansion, known to be inefficient for general MILP (Owen-Mehrotra), here gives an exact reformulation (p. 2), and the polyhedral structure of the resulting sets had not been studied (p. 2).
  - Transferable: an encoding known to be poor for one problem class can be exact for another; check what it buys before discarding it.
- **D2 Core idea**: Binary-expand y, linearize v_i = x z_i exactly (McCormick is exact on binaries) to get the exact extended MILP B-BLP (pp. 3, 7). Then compute conv(B) in closed form: conv(R_S) for z ∈ S and v = xz is obtained by multiplying each inequality of conv(S) by x and a−x (Prop. 3.1, p. 8). The binary-expansion knapsack K has superincreasing weights, so its minimal covers are explicit (Laurent-Sassano, Prop. 3.3; explicit form via the binary digits of b, Prop. 3.4, pp. 10-11), giving a polynomial-size description (Cor. 3.5, pp. 11-12). The alternative M-BLP keeps McCormick envelopes and branches only on integer variables, with no spatial branching on continuous ones (§2.2.1, pp. 7-8).
  - Transferable: for a set {z ∈ S, x ∈ [0,a], v = xz}, the hull is the hull of S multiplied by x and by (a−x). Use this to turn any known hull of a discrete set into the hull of its bilinear product with a bounded continuous variable.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Bounded x ∈ [0,a], integer y ∈ [0,b] with b ≥ 1 integer, a > 0 (p. 3). Nonnegative lower bounds are imposed by translation (p. 12). Prop. 3.1 assumes conv(S) is a polytope (p. 8). Bilinear terms between two integers are covered as well (p. 14). Remarks 1-2 extend to semi-integer y and to missing powers of two (pp. 11-12).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) Exact comparison of LP strengths with explicit separating points: relax(M) ⊆ proj relax(B), strict iff b ≠ 2^γ−1, and relax(M) ⊆ proj relax(U), strict iff b ≥ 2 (Prop. 2.2, pp. 4-5); B and U incomparable in general (Prop. 2.3, pp. 5-6), with a continuity / intermediate-value argument for existence of ẑ (p. 5). (ii) Convex hull of a union of two polyhedra T0 (x=0), T1 (x=a) plus Balas's disjunctive extended formulation and projection (Prop. 3.1, pp. 8-9). (iii) A combinatorial characterization of minimal covers from the binary expansion of b, using the superincreasing property σ(C) < σ(i*) (eq. 3.9; Prop. 3.4, pp. 10-11). (iv) Valid variable bounds for the bundling model by an optimality argument (Prop. 4.1, pp. 15-16).
  - Transferable: when comparing formulations, give for each strict inclusion an explicit point that lies in one relaxation and not the other, and state the exact data condition (here b = 2^γ−1) under which the inclusion is an equality.
- **D5 Experiments**: Five classes: 14 MINLPLIB instances (tln*, ex126*, prob*), product bundling (54 random from the Freire-Moreno-Vielma generator + 5 real "watts"), 8 MIPLIB 2003 instances with a constructed bilinear objective (eq. 4.3), 54 integer BoxQP built from Vandenbussche-Nemhauser instances, 100 disjoint bilinear instances from the Vicente-Calamai-Júdice generator (pp. 14-21). Four methods: Couenne 0.3; M-BLP; B-BLP; B-BLP + all hull cuts in the CPLEX 12.1 user-cut pool. Tolerances are aligned across solvers, CPLEX runs single-threaded, the time limit is 1 h (p. 12). Metrics: nodes, time, cuts, % root gap closed, and two gaps against OPT_I, the best value found by any MILP in 1 h or by Couenne in 24 h (eqs. 4.1-4.2, p. 13); performance profiles for solved and unsolved instances (p. 14). Failures are reported: Couenne solved the most bundling instances (Tables 4.3-4.4, pp. 16-17; Fig. 4.1 "our MILP formulations are not efficient in solving these instances", p. 17); cuts closed little root gap on bundling (p. 17) and slowed CPLEX on 9x60/10x60 (p. 17). Strong cases: disjoint bilinear (B-BLP+cuts fastest on 30/50 instances, ~34% root gap closed, p. 21), BoxQP (41% root gap closed, p. 20), tln4-6 (p. 14).
  - Transferable: when the true optimum is unknown, define the reference value as the best found by any method, including a long (24 h) run of the competitor, and report both a bound gap and a primal gap against it.
  - Transferable: when a test library has no instances with your structure, build them from a standard library with a stated deterministic recipe (MIPLIB + bilinear objective, with the variable pairing rule in a footnote, p. 18).
- **D6 Positioning**: Surveys bilinear methods (McCormick, convex envelopes of whole functions, branch-and-contract, RLT, SDP, Lagrangian, piecewise relaxations, pp. 1-2) and positions the paper as "solving (BLP1) as a MILP" rather than strengthening spatial B&B (p. 2). Explicitly refuses a solver horse-race: Couenne vs CPLEX is not a fair test of spatial B&B (p. 12). Acknowledges the referees for more instances, a simpler proof of Prop. 3.4, and correcting Prop. 2.3 (p. 23).
  - Transferable: when your baseline is an immature open-source solver against a commercial MILP solver, write down what the comparison cannot show.
- **D7 Writing**: Contribution sentence at the end of the intro: "Our contribution is to obtain complete descriptions of the convex hulls of these reformulated single term bilinear sets and use them in a branch-and-cut algorithm" (p. 2). Results are discussed instance class by instance class, with mixed outcomes named. The conclusion is hedged: "on certain class of problems ... can be beneficial" (p. 23). A preliminary dead end is reported in one sentence: local cover cuts on branching "found no performance gain" (p. 13).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory (polynomial-size hull of the binary-expanded single-term set) + algorithmic/computational study. Limits stated: cuts effective mainly with bilinear objective and linear constraints (p. 23); root gap often not closed on general bilinear problems (p. 23); the cuts are "by no means exhaustive" (p. 23). Future direction: exploit binary-expansion structure inside problem-specific constraints (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: a single bilinear term with an integer variable is the small recurring substructure; its hull is computed jointly in an extended space rather than term-wise McCormick (pp. 8-12), and then tested in branch-and-cut on several libraries.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: each formulation inclusion comes with the exact condition and explicit witness points (Props. 2.1-2.3, pp. 3-6).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: the emphasis is an exact MILP and good primal solutions; bounds are reported (µ_I), but certified gaps are not the headline, and binary formulations often win on primal quality ω_I rather than on bounds (pp. 16-20).
- Heuristic 3 ✅ evidence: dead end written in briefly with its result (local cuts on branching, p. 13).
- Taste warning sign 3 ✅ evidence: explicit refusal to conclude about spatial B&B from a Couenne-vs-CPLEX comparison (p. 12).
- new-pattern candidate: **multiply-the-hull lemma** (conv of {z ∈ S, v = xz, x ∈ [0,a]} from conv(S) multiplied by x and a−x, p. 8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prop. 3.1 recipe: write the set as a union of x=0 and x=a pieces, apply Balas's disjunctive formula, project (pp. 8-9).
- Use known knapsack structure (superincreasing weights → explicit minimal covers) to make an extended hull polynomial (pp. 10-11).
- Report OPT_I from the best of all methods plus a 24 h competitor run; separate bound gap µ from primal gap ω (p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "Although the use of binary expansions is known to be inefficient for general MILPs [27], in our case, it gives us an exact MILP reformulation of (BLP1)." (p. 2)
- "Since CPLEX is a sophisticated commercial MILP solver whereas Couenne is a relatively new open source MINLP solver, we cannot and do not wish to draw conclusions regarding the performance of the spatial branch-and-bound algorithm." (p. 12)

### S010 · Covering linear programming with violations (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2014, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2013.0582)
**Read level**: full. All 22 pages read, including the appendix vaccination model (pp. 21-22).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Feng Qiu, Shabbir Ahmed, Laurence A. Wolsey · **Place in trajectory**: Georgia Tech period; chance-constrained / k-violation MIP work alongside the bilinear paper (S002), with the same Ahmed group.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Covering-type k-violation LP (CKVLP): at most k of m covering constraints may be violated (p. 1). Applications: sample-average approximation of probabilistically constrained portfolio problems (pp. 1-2), vaccine allocation, IMRT, broadcasting (p. 2). Entry: the natural MIP (4) "performs very poorly" at scale (p. 2). The complexity of the covering subclass was open (p. 2). The paper first measures CPLEX's difficulty (Table 1, p. 6) before proposing anything.
  - Transferable: open with a small table of what the default solver does on your problem class, split by the data features (k, density, objective), so that the instance class you target is justified by data.
- **D2 Core idea**: Three MIP enhancements, each applicable with side constraints because they only tighten the LP relaxation of (4) (p. 2): (i) iterative coefficient strengthening: replace big-M coefficient 1 by (1−ℓ_i), where ℓ_i is a valid lower bound on a_iᵀx, recomputed by LPs over the current strengthened relaxation until a fixed point (Algorithm 1, Props. 2-3, pp. 7-8); (ii) mixing-set inequalities adapted from chance-constrained programming (Luedtke et al.) (§4, pp. 10-14); (iii) a branching rule that removes the overlap between z_j = 0 and z_j = 1 branches by adding the reversed constraint a_jᵀx ≤ 1 as a local cut (§5, pp. 14-15).
  - Transferable: when a binary "switch" branch duplicates part of its sibling, add the reversed constraint as a local cut on the switched branch; it is free, and it triggers infeasibility pruning.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: a_i, b_i ≥ 0, b_i > 0 scaled to 1 (p. 2). The analysis assumes c_j > 0 and a_ij > 0 "for simplicity of the analysis" (p. 8); gap bounds depend on the data-variability measure ρ (eq. 15, p. 8) or on max/min coefficients (Prop. 10, p. 13).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) Strong NP-hardness via vertex cover, using an intermediate decision problem and two "rounding" claims that move any feasible point to an integral one without increasing f (Lemma 1 Claims a-b, pp. 3-5); then a reduction that calls the optimization oracle for every p (Thm 1, p. 5). (ii) Monotone-bounded-sequence convergence of Algorithm 1 (Prop. 3, pp. 7-8). (iii) A comparison sequence u^t that lower-bounds ℓ^t, obtained by aggregating all m rows into the average row µ and solving a one-constraint LP (Lemma 2, pp. 8-9) → gap bound (v*−v^L)/v* ≤ m(1−ρ)/(m−ρk) (Thm 4, pp. 9-10), with a simple heuristic upper bound v^H. (iv) Closure equivalence: the projected "basic mixing closure" M equals the "simple disjunctive closure" D, so M is polyhedral (Prop. 8, Cor. 9, p. 13); gap bound (ā−a̲)/a̲ (Prop. 10, p. 13). (v) The separation over D is NP-hard (p. 14); "we do not know the complexity of the separation" over the true mixing closure projection (p. 14).
  - Transferable: to bound the strength of a relaxation, aggregate all rows into one averaged row, solve that one-constraint LP in closed form, and compare with a trivial feasible solution; the gap bound comes out in terms of one data-variability parameter.
  - Transferable: to understand an infinite family of cuts parameterized by α, show its projection equals a finite, explicit closure (here simple disjunctive cuts).
- **D5 Experiments**: CPLEX 12.1 callable library, default settings, Xeon 2.27 GHz, 4 GB (p. 15).
