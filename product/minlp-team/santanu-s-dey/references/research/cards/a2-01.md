### S066 · Sequential-merge facets for two-dimensional group problems (IPCO 2007, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-72792-7_3)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: Jean-Philippe P. Richard

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: strong cuts for unstructured MIPs via high-dimensional (two-dimensional) group problems, rather than one-row relaxations (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: a new operation, the sequential merge, combines two facet-defining inequalities of one-dimensional group problems into a facet of the two-dimensional group problem; any one-dimensional facets can be used as constituents (abstract).
  - Transferable technique: build higher-dimensional valid inequalities by an operation that composes known lower-dimensional facets, so that a whole library of one-dimensional results lifts into the new setting at once.
- **D8 Contribution**: large families of new cuts ("sequential-merge inequalities") with stronger continuous-variable coefficients than one-dimensional cuts; recovers the earlier three-gradient facet of Dey and Richard (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 3 variant ⚠ (pre-QCQP form: ask of the two-row MILP relaxation what is known for one row, abstract). New-pattern candidate: compose lower-dimensional facets into higher-dimensional ones.
**Quote** (abstract): "We present a new operation that generates facet-defining inequalities for two-dimensional group problems by combining two facet-defining inequalities of one-dimensional group problems."

### S074 · Strong cutting planes for unstructured mixed integer programs using multiple constraints (PhD thesis, Purdue University 2007, no DOI)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: none (advisor not stated in metadata)

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: cuts for unstructured MIPs that use the information in several constraints simultaneously, extending group and lifting approaches to multiple constraints (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: two parts. (i) Multi-dimensional infinite group problems: properties of extreme functions (pure integer can have discontinuous extremes; mixed integer only continuous), then the first two facet families for the two-dimensional group problem, generated in linear time. (ii) Approximate lifting coefficients from at most two LPs instead of a nonlinear MIP, applied to Glover's primal algorithm (abstract).
  - Transferable technique: replace an exact lifting problem (a nonlinear MIP) by an LP-computable approximation and test it inside a concrete algorithm where its effect can be measured.
- **D5 (one line)**: experiments compare the gap closed by the new two-row cuts with Gomory mixed integer cuts alone and with aggregation (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: one facet family gives "a theoretical explanation for the empirical success of aggregation-based cut-generation schemes"; claimed best known performance for a cutting-plane primal algorithm (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅ (explaining why aggregation-based cut generation works, abstract) — the earliest instance in the record. Method 3 variant ⚠ (multi-row MILP version, abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "The first family provides a theoretical explanation for the empirical success of aggregation-based cut-generation schemes."

### S015 · Lifting integer variables in minimal inequalities corresponding to lattice-free triangles (IPCO 2008, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-68891-4_32)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: Laurence A. Wolsey

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: the two-row minimal inequalities of Andersen et al. and Borozan–Cornuéjols only cover continuous variables; using them on a general tableau needs lifting functions for integer variables (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: classify maximal lattice-free triangles into three categories and analyse lifting for each; for the third, use Johnson's fill-in inequality under sufficient conditions (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: unique lifting functions characterized for two categories; non-uniqueness shown for the third, with conditions for the fill-in inequality to be minimal and extreme (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 4 evidence ✅ (positive result paired with where it stops: uniqueness for two categories, "not necessarily unique" in the third, abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "We show that the lifting function is not necessarily unique in the third category."

### S054 · The Chvátal-Gomory closure of an ellipsoid is a polyhedron (IPCO 2010, doi:10.1007/978-3-642-13036-6_25)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: Juan Pablo Vielma

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: the CG closure of a rational polyhedron is a rational polyhedron; does this extend to a non-polyhedral convex set (abstract)?
- **D2 Key idea**: show that every non-integral boundary point of the ellipsoid is separated by some CG cut, via a constructed sequence of CG cuts, then conclude polyhedrality by compactness (abstract).
  - Transferable technique: reduce a finiteness/polyhedrality claim to "every boundary point is separated by some cut" plus a compactness argument.
- **D8 Contribution**: first extension of CG-closure polyhedrality to a non-polyhedral set; sufficient conditions for general compact convex sets (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 3 evidence ✅ (a MILP closure result asked of a convex nonlinear set, abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first extension of the polyhedrality of the CG closure to a non-polyhedral set."
