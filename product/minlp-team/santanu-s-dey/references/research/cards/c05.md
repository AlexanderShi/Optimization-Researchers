### S001 · Strong SOCP relaxations for the optimal power flow problem (Operations Research 2016, doi:10.1287/opre.2016.1489; arXiv:1504.06770)

**Read level**: full. Pages 1–35 read closely (abstract, intro, §2 formulations, §3 comparison theorem and proofs, §4 three strengthenings and bound tightening, §5 experiments, §6 conclusions); Appendix A (Table 7 timings, p. 36) skimmed; references (pp. 37–40) skimmed. Version read: arXiv v4.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Burak Kocuk, X. Andy Sun · **Place in trajectory**: middle paper of the AC-OPF arc (after the "Inexactness of SDP relaxation" paper cited as Kocuk et al. 2015, p. 38; before the spatial branch-and-bound work promised on p. 35).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Enters from an operational constraint: OPF "is solved every few minutes, hence the computational budget is limited" and is nonconvex at thousands of buses (p. 1). Industry uses DC OPF, which "cannot guarantee any optimality" (p. 2); local IPMs give no quality certificate (p. 2); SDP is strong but exact only under restrictive assumptions and too costly at scale, and moment hierarchies may need arbitrarily high order (p. 2). The cut-in: the classic SOCP of Jabr's alternative formulation simply drops the nonconvex arctangent (angle) constraints, so adding convex outer approximations of exactly those constraints should give relaxations stronger than SOCP and possibly incomparable to SDP (p. 3).
  - Transferable: locate the constraint family a cheap relaxation throws away entirely, and make "convexify what was dropped" the research question.
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: First rank six relaxations (McCormick, SOCP, SDP of the rectangular and alternative formulations) in the projected (p, q) space (Theorem 3.1, p. 11); this ranking justifies strengthening the classic SOCP A*_SOCP (§3.3, p. 14). Three strengthenings (§4, p. 15): (a) cycle constraints cos(sum of angle differences) = 1 written as degree-|C| polynomials, then reduced to O(|C|) bilinear equations by triangulating cycles into 3- and 4-cycles with artificial edges, then McCormick plus an LP separation (Props 4.1–4.4, pp. 16–20; CGLP (39), p. 21); (b) four-plane polyhedral envelopes of arctan(s/c) over a box with shifts computed by enumerating KKT points (Props 4.6–4.7, p. 22); (c) separate the SOCP point from the SDP cone restricted to each cycle of a cycle basis via a small conic dual (48) (pp. 23–24). Plus a bound-tightening step solving reduced SOCPs on r-hop neighbourhoods, r = 2 (§4.4, p. 25). What is kept: the SOCP core and MOSEK; what is changed: only valid linear inequalities are added.
  - Transferable: replace a full-matrix SDP constraint by separation over the SDP cone of small substructures (cycles of a cycle basis), adding only linear cuts to a cheaper base relaxation.
  - Transferable: when a polynomial constraint has degree proportional to a structure's size, triangulate the structure with artificial variables to get an O(size) bilinear system, then apply McCormick.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Cost linear or convex quadratic in real power (p. 5); voltage magnitudes near 1 p.u. (p. 5); the alternative formulation is exact on radial networks and needs the atan2 angle condition (3) on meshed ones (p. 6); line-flow limits omitted from the exposition but included in the experiments when given (p. 7); a cycle basis is given (p. 18); the arctangent envelopes assume c > 0 (p. 22); methods (a) and (b) need good bounds on c and s, called "the key ingredient in the success of the first two proposed methods" (p. 25). No explicit angle-difference bounds are exploited in the experiments, by choice (p. 30).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Comparison of relaxations in a common projected space, because the objective depends only on p (p. 11). Inclusion proofs by explicit lifting maps (e.g., set e = f = 0 to embed AM in RM, Prop 3.1, pp. 11–12; Z = zz^T + diagonal correction, Prop 3.4, p. 12). Strictness shown by examples: a 9-bus instance with BARON's global optimum (Table 1, p. 13), a hand-built 2-bus point feasible for the SDP of the alternative formulation but violating the SOC constraint (pp. 13–14), and case6ww for SDP vs SOCP (p. 14). Exact-reformulation lemmas for 3- and 4-cycles by sine/cosine sum formulas, needing both sine and cosine equalities (pp. 16–18). Validity of the decomposition by summing 3-cycle identities (Prop 4.3, pp. 18–19). Incomparability with SDP (Props 4.5, 4.8) is "verified by an example" from computational instances (p. 21; Table 5, p. 33). Separation via Farkas-type duals ((39), p. 21; strong duality by strict feasibility W = I, (48), p. 24).
  - Transferable: prove "neither dominates" by exhibiting one benchmark instance each way with computed gaps (Table 5, p. 33) instead of a constructive proof.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: MATPOWER IEEE cases 6–3375 bus and NESTA 0.3.0 (typical, congested, small angle-difference) (p. 26, p. 31); code in C#, MOSEK 7.1, a 64-bit laptop, Intel i7 2.00 GHz, 8 GB (p. 26); five rounds of cut generation (p. 27). Baselines: OPF Solver (chordal-sparsity SDP) with the authors' suggested penalty parameters and fine-tuning time excluded (pp. 27, 29); QC relaxation numbers taken from Coffrin et al. (2015) as reported, where only gaps above 1% were published (p. 30). Metrics: lower-bound ratio to SDP (Table 2, p. 28); %gap = 100(zUB − zLB)/zUB with the UB from IPOPT warm-started at the relaxation solution (pp. 28–29); total time including the local solve (p. 29). Robustness: 10 instances per case with loads perturbed 5% (p. 33); infeasibility detection reported (p. 34). Failure reporting: SDP is tight (0.01%) on 14 of 19 instances versus 8 for SSDP (p. 30); QC beats all strong SOCPs on 3 of 19 small-angle instances (p. 31). An awkward fact stated plainly: IPOPT converges to the same solution whichever relaxation starts it, and also from MATPOWER's flat start (p. 28).
  - Transferable: report the certified gap with a timing that includes producing the feasible point, and add a load-perturbation robustness table.
- **D6 Positioning**: Extends Jabr (2006, 2007, 2008) (pp. 3, 6); positions against the SDP line (Lavaei–Low, Bose et al., Madani et al.) by listing the restricted exactness conditions (p. 2) and SDP rank-recovery difficulty (p. 29); aligns with the LP/SOCP trend (Coffrin–Van Hentenryck, Hijazi et al., Bienstock–Munoz) (p. 3). Relies on Taylor (2015) §3.3 for the equality of real and complex SDP bounds (p. 9). Criticism is restated as conditions rather than attacks (e.g., QC relies on tight angle bounds "typically not available in practice", p. 30).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Abstract leads with a single headline certificate: 3375-bus, 0.13%, 157.20 seconds "on a modest personal computer" (p. 1, repeated p. 4). Contributions are a numbered list with sub-items (a)–(e) (pp. 3–4). Superlatives are strong ("consistently outperforms", p. 1, p. 4) while the body hedges (p. 31). Selective average: for small-angle cases the text says "both SOCPA and S34A outperform QC" (p. 31), but Table 4 shows SSDP's average 4.38 against QC's 3.76 (p. 32). Intuition for choices is given in a short "Our choice of convex relaxation" section (p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory (relaxation ranking, cycle reformulations) + algorithm (three strengthenings) + computation (certified gaps at 3375 buses) (pp. 3–4, 34–35). Limits stated concretely: SDP remains tighter on most instances (p. 30); bound preprocessing is "a sizable portion in the computational cost" (p. 27); some instances not globally solved (p. 35). Future (p. 35): spatial branch-and-bound to close gaps; OTS and multiperiod OPF; verifiable sufficient conditions for exactness of the SOCP approaches. The "warm start" advantage claimed in the abstract (p. 1) is weakened by the observation that the flat start gives the same OPF solution (p. 28); the relaxation's value is in the lower bound.

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the paper is the template: rank relaxations, strengthen the tractable SOCP with structure (cycles, arctangent envelopes, cycle-SDP cuts), pair with IPOPT, report the certified gap at the largest size with hardware (pp. 1, 4, 14, 26–29). The stated aim "to avoid using SDP relaxations" (p. 14) is Taste 4 evidence.
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: substructure convexification: the nonconvexity of meshed OPF sits in per-cycle angle conditions, convexified jointly over a cycle rather than per term (pp. 15–24).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: every inclusion in Theorem 3.1 is shown strict, but strictness and incomparability (Props 4.5, 4.8) are shown by computed instances, not by constructed tight examples (pp. 13–14, 33).
- Heuristic 2 ⚠ variant: the SDP baseline is given its authors' parameters with tuning time excluded (p. 29), but the QC baseline is not rerun; reported numbers are copied, with blanks for gaps under 1% (p. 30).
- Workflow D checkpoint ✅ evidence: the "consistently outperforms" superlative (p. 1) does not hold on the small-angle class (pp. 31–32), the case the checkpoint warns about.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Rank all candidate relaxations in one projected space before strengthening one of them, and state the ranking as a theorem with strictness examples (Theorem 3.1, pp. 11–14).
- Cycle-basis separation: generate cuts per cycle of a cycle basis, in parallel, for a few rounds only (p. 27).
- Cheap bound tightening: solve the base relaxation restricted to an r-hop neighbourhood of each edge (r = 2 found to be the trade-off) (p. 25).
- Tightest-valid-plane envelopes: fit planes through corner points of a box, then shift each by the maximum violation found by enumerating KKT points (pp. 22–23).

**Quotes**:
- "Overall, the proposed strong SOCP relaxations provide a practical approach to obtain feasible OPF solutions with extremely good quality within a time framework that is compatible with the real-time operation in the current industry practice." (p. 1)
- "We note that our SOCP based relaxations are not as successful according to this comparison." (p. 30)

### S004 · Improving the integer L-shaped method (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2016, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2016.0695)

**Read level**: full. Pages 1–21 read closely (intro, §2 alternating cuts, §3 CGLP construction and Propositions 3–4, §3.2 incremental update, §4 combined method, §5 results, §6 conclusions); references (pp. 21–22) and appendix Tables 6–10 (pp. 23–26) skimmed. Version read: preprint dated April 29, 2014.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Gustavo Angulo, Shabbir Ahmed · **Place in trajectory**: applies the authors' own forbidden-vertices polyhedral result ([3] Angulo, Ahmed, Dey, Kaibel, p. 21) inside a decomposition algorithm for two-stage stochastic integer programs.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The integer L-shaped method of Laporte–Louveaux uses an optimality cut (6) that needs an exact, costly evaluation of Q(x) and "only depends on the point to be cut-off", ignoring other solutions already evaluated (p. 4). Two weaknesses, two fixes: avoid unneeded exact evaluations; use all evaluated points to approximate the epigraph better (p. 4). Entered from the algorithm's practice (callback in a solver tree, Algorithms 1–2, p. 3), not from an application.
  - Transferable: read a classical algorithm's pseudocode line by line and ask, for each oracle call, whether it is needed and whether its output uses all information gathered so far.
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: (i) Alternating cuts: since QLP(x) ≤ Q(x), first check θ* < QLP(x*) and add a subgradient cut; compute Q(x*) only if the LP check passes (pp. 4–5, Algorithm 3). (ii) CGLP-based optimality cuts: conv(S(V)) = conv(PQ(V) ∪ PL(V)), with PQ the convex hull of known epigraph points and PL = conv({0,1}^n \ V) × {θ ≥ L}; an extended formulation of size O(n|V|) for conv({0,1}^n \ V) via the forbidden-vertices recursion; disjunctive programming plus Farkas gives a CGLP whose feasible solutions are cuts tight at x* (Prop 4, p. 13). (iii) Rows are updated incrementally (at most n row changes per new point, Algorithm 5, pp. 14–15). Unchanged: the L-shaped tree search, subgradient cuts, and termination logic.
  - Transferable: screen with a cheap valid relaxation oracle before calling the exact one; the cheap check alone suffices to reject most candidates (Table 2, p. 18).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Binary first stage x ∈ {0,1}^n (p. 1); "A fundamental assumption is that given x, Q(x) can be computed with a reasonable amount of effort" (p. 1); a lower bound L on Q over X (p. 2); finitely many scenarios for the expectation (p. 2). The relaxation S(V) drops X, partially recovered by adding Ax + Cz ≤ b and known lower-bound cuts to PL(V) (p. 11). Lemma 1 needs polyhedra with a common recession cone, checked for PL and PQ (p. 11).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Lemma 1 (Balas disjunctive programming for a union of polyhedra with the same recession cone) (p. 6); the recursion {0,1}^{j+1} \ V^{j+1} = [({0,1}^j \ V^j) × {0,1}] ∪ V̂^{j+1} from forbidden vertices (p. 8, with a worked n = 3 example, Example 2, p. 8); Proposition 3 proved by induction on j, applying Lemma 1 at each step (pp. 8–11); Farkas' lemma to turn infeasibility of the membership system into a cut-generating system (p. 12); a tightness equality plus normalization β = 1 to guarantee cuts tight at x* and hence finite termination, with the standard cut (6) shown feasible so the LP is always feasible (p. 13). Size count: 3n rows, 3n + O(n|V|) columns, only the width grows with V (p. 11). No convergence-rate analysis.
  - Transferable: when a cut LP must be tight at the current point for finite convergence, replace the violation objective by a tightness equality and a normalization, so that the LP becomes a pure feasibility problem with a known feasible point (p. 13).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: CPLEX 12.5.0.1 Callable Library, 3.33 GHz PC, 4 GB, Linux; 0.01% relative gap; 7200 s limit (p. 16). Four variants in a 2 × 2 design: {standard, alternating} strategy × {standard, CGLP} cut (pp. 15–16). Instances: SSLP from SIPLIB (n = 10, 15; five replications; n = 5 dropped as all solved in under 1 s) (p. 17); 30 generated stochastic multiple binary knapsack instances (n = 120, 20 scenarios, i.i.d. uniform data), put in SIPLIB, grouped Easy/Medium/Hard by Std-Std time, instance 30 dropped since none solved it (pp. 18–19). Diagnostics separate LP and MIP evaluation counts and times (Tables 2, 4) and CGLP overhead (Table 5, already included in totals) (pp. 18–20). Results: alternating cuts give one-order-of-magnitude speed-ups on SSLP and none on knapsack; CGLP cuts reduce nodes and time on knapsack (pp. 17, 19). The extensive form is not reported because it is "much slower" (p. 16): a claim without a table.
  - Transferable: a factorial design (strategy × cut type) plus per-component time tables to attribute a speed-up to its cause (Tables 1–5, pp. 18–20).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds directly on Laporte–Louveaux [9] (p. 2) and Benders/L-shaped [5, 17] (p. 2); contrasts with sequential-approximation schemes (Sen–Higle D², Gade et al., Küçükyavuz–Zhang, Qi–Sen) whose "convergence can be very slow" (p. 4), and claims the alternating strategy "has not been reported in the literature" to their knowledge (p. 4). Uses own prior work [3] as the engine (pp. 4, 8).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Generic problem (IP) first, specialised to stochastic programming second (p. 1). Every variant is given as a full pseudocode of the optimality-cut callback, so the changes show line by line (Algorithms 2, 3, 4, 6). A 2-D picture explains why standard cuts at evaluated points do not give conv(S(V)) (Figures 1–2, pp. 6–7). Attribution is hedged openly (p. 19). Conclusions are numbered conditions under which each modification helps (p. 20).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic + computational, with a polyhedral construction as the theoretical piece. Limits stated concretely: the gains of the two modifications do not add up, since "favorable conditions for both modifications are unlikely to be attained at the same time" (p. 20); CGLP overhead grows with |V| and could be limited by calling it only now and then (p. 20); CGLP cuts give no guarantee on the number or order of evaluated solutions (p. 20). Future (pp. 20–21): put subgradient or ad-hoc estimates of Q in the CGLP instead of exact values only; nonconvex easy-to-evaluate Q without convex underestimators; black-box Q "remain a case study to be explored".

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: imports MILP polyhedral machinery (disjunctive programming, extended formulations of a 0/1 set minus vertices) into a decomposition setting, not into QCQP/MINLP; the substructure is the evaluated-point epigraph conv(S(V)) (pp. 5–13).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (partial): the speed-up is decomposed into LP vs MIP evaluation counts and times to show where it comes from (Tables 2, 4, pp. 18–19); no ground-truth ceiling is computed.
- Heuristic 6 ✅ evidence: CGLP update and generation overhead is measured and included in total time (Table 5, pp. 19–20).
- Heuristic 3 ✅ evidence: honest attribution doubt written into the paper (p. 19); the combined method's non-additivity is stated (p. 20).
- Heuristic 1 ✅ evidence: the generated knapsack instances are released in SIPLIB (p. 19).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: demarcation is empirical: each modification is tied to the condition where it pays (evaluation-bound vs master-bound problems) (p. 20), with no theorem on when it helps.
- New-pattern candidate: recycle own polyhedral theorem as an algorithm's engine (forbidden-vertices extended formulation → CGLP for optimality cuts; pp. 4, 8).
- New-pattern candidate: cheap-oracle screening before exact evaluation (alternating cuts, pp. 4–5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Screen candidate solutions with the LP relaxation value before evaluating the exact integer recourse; keep two visited lists (VLP, V) so that each point costs at most one LP and one MIP evaluation (Algorithm 3, p. 5).
- Build history-aware cuts: convex hull of the union of "known values at evaluated points" and "trivial bound elsewhere" via disjunctive programming, separated by a CGLP (pp. 6–13).
- Maintain the CGLP incrementally: add or drop at most one row per coordinate per new point (Algorithm 5, p. 15).
- Group generated instances by baseline solve time (Easy/Medium/Hard) and report averages per group with per-instance appendix tables (pp. 19, 25–26).

**Quotes**:
- "The key idea is to use QLP(x) as a proxy for Q(x) to check feasibility of a candidate solution, preventing unnecessary, and more costly, computations of Q(x)." (p. 4)
- "We would like to conclude that these improvements are due to the fact that CGLP-based cuts help to explore the master tree. However, at this point, that is not completely clear, as for example, time reductions could be consequence of less evaluations of Q(x) and not because of the strength of the new cuts." (p. 19)
