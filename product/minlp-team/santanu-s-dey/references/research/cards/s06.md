### S034 · Lower bounds on the size of general branch-and-bound trees (Mathematical Programming 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-022-01781-z; read as arXiv:2103.09807v2)
Read level: full (pp. 1-20: introduction, contributions, §§2-7 with all statements and proofs. The proof of Proposition 4 on pp. 14-15 was skimmed. The paper has no conclusion section.)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Y. Dubey (student), M. Molinaro · **Position**: the negative half of the 2020-23 branch-and-bound theory line. The positive half is the companion SODA 2021 result that simple B&B solves random binary IPs in polynomial time (ref. [16], p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: the paper answers the open question of Beame et al. on superpolynomial lower bounds for *general* (integer split) branching. It extends Dadush-Tiwari's cross-polytope bound to natural combinatorial problems (p. 3). The motivation is solver behaviour: instances that need exponentially many nodes "are likely to also be challenging for MILP solvers" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: define BB hardness, BBdepth and BBrank (pp. 6-7), which are analogues of Chvátal-Gomory rank. Lower bounds then move between problems by integral affine maps (flipping, embedding, duplication) (pp. 8-11). The paper calls itself "the branch-and-bound analog" of Chvátal-Cook-Hartmann (p. 1). Results: 2^Ω(n) for a packing instance and a set-cover instance (pp. 12-14); a simple 2^n-leaf bound for the cross-polytope, improving Dadush-Tiwari's 2^n/n (pp. 5, 14); 2^Ω(n) with high probability under Gaussian perturbation, so no polynomial smoothed-analysis bound is possible (pp. 15-17); 2^{n/16-2} for TSP (p. 18). None of the bounds depend on the size of the branching coefficients, unlike the bound in Fleming et al. (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions**: the B&B tree is abstracted as a full binary tree of legal disjunctions. Two usual algorithmic requirements are dropped, which only strengthens the lower bounds (p. 6). The packing and covering polytopes have exponentially many constraints (p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis**: Lemma 1 (BB rank ≤ BB hardness) uses hyperplane separation (p. 7). Lemma 2 turns infeasibility hardness into optimisation hardness (pp. 7-8). Lemma 5 is a simulation lemma for integral affine maps, with Corollaries 3-4 and the hardness Lemma 6 (pp. 8-10). A generalised Dadush-Tiwari lemma uses Helly's theorem: if every constraint in D is needed for infeasibility, the tree has at least |D|/n leaves (p. 11). For the cross-polytope, a midpoint argument shows that any two integer points in one leaf leave the atom non-empty (p. 14). The perturbed instance combines a Gaussian tail bound, a union bound, Sauer-Shelah and a binary-entropy count (pp. 16-17). TSP follows by composing with the Cook-Dash construction (p. 18).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the contribution is theoretical. The limit is stated plainly: the packing and covering instances use exponentially many constraints, so the Dadush-Tiwari question for polynomially described polytopes stays open (p. 4). The results transfer to branch-and-cut with split-like cuts through Basu et al. (p. 5). No future-work section.

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅ (the object of study is solver tree size, justified by solver difficulty, p. 2). Method 4 evidence ✅ (the boundary is stated next to the result: exponential constraint count, p. 4; coefficient independence, p. 5; smoothed analysis ruled out, p. 4). Method 3 variant ⚠ (the import runs from cutting-plane rank theory into branch-and-bound, not from MILP into QCQP; the reduction lemmas are modelled on the Chvátal-Cook-Hartmann cutting-plane-rank lower bounds, p. 8). New-pattern candidate: "transfer hardness through integral affine maps" (pp. 8-11, 17-18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- To prove tree-size lower bounds for many problems at once, first define a depth/rank notion for separating one point (BBdepth). Then show that integral affine one-to-one maps cannot decrease it (Lemma 5, Lemma 6, pp. 8-10). Then reduce each new problem to one hard base polytope.
- For infeasibility certificates, apply Helly's theorem to each leaf: every leaf can "use" at most n original constraints. If every constraint in a family D is needed for infeasibility, at least |D|/n leaves are required (p. 11).
- To rule out a smoothed-analysis upper bound, perturb the hard instance's coefficients with Gaussian noise. Then show with a union bound that infeasibility and a large set of half-integral points both survive (Lemmas 10-11, pp. 16-17).
**Quotes**:
- "In contrast, in this paper we provide lower bounds for the size of general branch-and-bound tree for packing and set-cover instances, which are more natural combinatorial problems than those mentioned above." (p. 5)
- "We note that the packing and covering instances are described using an exponential number of constraints" (p. 4)

### S057 · Solving sparse principal component analysis with global support (Mathematical Programming 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-022-01857-w; read as arXiv:2010.11152v3)
Read level: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-8); §2.1 up to the sparsification construction (pp. 8-11); §3 set-up (pp. 18-19); §4 opening (p. 21); §5 experiments, §6 conclusion and acknowledgements (pp. 23-31). Not read: proofs pp. 11-18 and 19-21, Appendices pp. 31-38.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: M. Molinaro, G. Wang (student) · **Position**: the r > 1 (common-support) extension of the sparse PCA dual-bound line. Theorem 3 "generalizes the main theoretical result in [19] for the case r = 1" (p. 6).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: existing SPCAgs methods are either greedy heuristics with guarantees only under statistical models, or stationary-point algorithms for regularised versions. None can check solution quality against dual bounds (abstract p. 1; literature survey in five categories, pp. 3-4). The target is the "model-free case" with worst-case guarantees (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: relax the row-sparse orthonormal set F with operator norms. CR1 (‖V‖op ≤ 1, ‖V‖2→1 ≤ √k, Σ‖Vi,:‖2 ≤ √(rk)) is within O(√log r) of conv(F) (Theorem 1, pp. 4-5). An SDP version CR1' keeps the same order (Theorem 2, p. 5). A SOC-representable CR2 is within 2√r (Theorem 3, p. 6). The objective is non-concave, since it maximises a convex function. Each quadratic term is therefore upper-approximated piecewise-linearly with SOS-II constraints. The result is a convex IP whose bound satisfies opt ≤ ub ≤ ρ²·opt + additive term, with the additive term ≤ Tr(A)·r/(4N²) (Theorem 4, p. 6; construction pp. 18-19). Primal side: a greedy local search that needs one eigendecomposition per round (pp. 7, 21).
- **D3 Assumptions**: no statistical model (p. 4); A is a sample covariance (PSD). Lemma 1 assumes k ≥ 40, and smaller k is covered by Theorem 3 (p. 9). Computing ‖·‖2→1 is NP-hard, which motivates CR1' (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis**: the inclusion is proved by comparing support functions (p. 9). The key step is a new random row sparsification: rows are kept with probabilities built from Pietsch-Grothendieck factorisation weights (rather than squared row norms), which gives an unbiased estimate with controlled operator norm (pp. 10-11). The bound is invariant to rescaling the data (p. 6).
- **D5 Experiments**: artificial block-spiked covariance instances (d = 500, M = 3000) and real instances CovColon, Lymph, Reddit1500, Reddit2000 (up to 2000×2000) (pp. 24-25). Metric: gap (ub−lb)/lb against the best of 400 random-start greedy runs; Gurobi 7.0.2 and Mosek 9.1 on a MacBook Pro (p. 25). On the 42 instances of size 100×100, the CIP bound was best on 26, Baseline 1 on 9 and the SDP on 11. The SDP was then dropped (p. 27). The large instances used a sub-matrix technique with 20 s CIP limits. On CovColon/Lymph the CIP is only slightly better than the trivial Baseline 1, and badly worse for m = 10. On Reddit it "vastly outperforms" Baseline 1 (pp. 27-30).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the contribution is theory plus a computational dual bound. The conclusion admits "we still need more techniques to close the gap" and claims there are "no comparable theoretical or computational results" for model-free SPCAgs (p. 30). This is the journal version of a preliminary paper: CR1, CR1' and their strength results are new, and the experiments are "completely revamped" (p. 7). The acknowledgements credit reviewers with suggesting the SDP of §2.2 and a heuristic in the appendix (p. 31).

**Method links**: Method 5 evidence ✅ (the deliverable is a dual bound from a SOC/convex-IP relaxation with a gap reported at 2000×2000 scale on a laptop, pp. 6, 25-30). Method 4 evidence ✅ (each relaxation has an explicit factor; hardness of ‖·‖2→1 and of CRi-Relax is stated, pp. 5, 18). Method 2 variant ⚠ (no exact optimum is available, so the yardstick is the authors' own primal heuristic; the 100×100 sub-matrices serve as the first rung of the instance ladder, pp. 24-25). New-pattern candidate: "convex-IP dual bound for convex maximisation: norm-ball relaxation of the support set + SOS-II piecewise-linear upper approximation of the objective" (pp. 6, 18-19; same device reused in S064 p. 14).
**Transferable techniques**:
- For a problem that maximises a convex function over a sparse set, first relax the feasible set with norm constraints that have a provable factor. Then replace each squared linear form (a_j^T v)^2 by a piecewise-linear SOS-II overestimator on [−θj, θj]. The resulting convex IP has a bound with an explicit additive error that shrinks as 1/N² (pp. 6, 18-19).
- To prove a relaxation is within a factor ρ of a nonconvex set's hull, compare support functions. Exhibit a randomly sparsified feasible point whose sampling weights come from a factorisation tuned to the norm that the relaxation controls (pp. 9-11).
- When one baseline is dominated and expensive (the SDP), report the small-instance comparison in full and say why it is dropped for larger instances (p. 27).
**Quotes**:
- "Recall that dual bounds are typically crucial for effective computational procedures for non-convex problems, which are usually solved using branch-and-bound type algorithms that use dual bounds to prune the solution space." (p. 6)
- "These problems are quite challenging, and in some instances, we still need more techniques to close the gap." (p. 30)

### S064 · Variable selection for kernel two-sample tests (arXiv preprint 2023, arXiv:2302.07415; read as v4, 20 Dec 2024)
Read level: partial. Read: abstract, §§1-4 (pp. 1-14), §5 assumptions and main statistical results (pp. 15-16), §§6-8 experiments and conclusion (pp. 16-24). Not read: concentration preliminaries on pp. 14-15 (skimmed) and the e-companion proofs, pp. 25-42.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. Wang (first author, student), Y. Xie (statistics) · **Position**: an application of Dey's sparse-PCA relaxation toolkit to a statistics problem. Dey is the middle author. Which parts are his is not stated, so the attribution is unknown.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: the goal is to select d of D variables that best separate two distributions. Earlier work is parametric (Gaussian, graphical models) or Bonferroni-style (pp. 2-3). Sparse logistic regression provably fails on a variance-shift example (Example 1, p. 7).
- **D2 Core idea**: maximise a variance-regularised empirical MMD, MMD² − λσ², as a surrogate for the power-optimal fractional criterion (pp. 5). For a linear kernel this is a sparse trust-region subproblem (STRS), max z^T A z + z^T a over ‖z‖2 = 1, ‖z‖0 ≤ d (p. 7). Quadratic and Gaussian kernels are handled by iterating quadratic approximations (p. 9). Exact methods: an MISDP (Theorem 1, p. 10), strengthened by sparse-PCA valid inequalities (Corollary 1, p. 11), then a saddle-point form solved by outer approximation à la Duran-Grossmann (Theorem 2, Algorithm 3, pp. 11-12). Approximations: an SDP relaxation solved by ADMM (Theorem 3, p. 12), truncation (Theorem 4, p. 13), and an ℓ1-relaxation plus a convex-IP built from [21, §2.2] (Theorem 5, p. 14).
- **D3 Assumptions**: A ⪰ 0 without loss of generality by an eigenvalue shift (p. 7); λ chosen by cross-validation (p. 5). Assumption 1 gives kernel-specific data conditions, weakest for the Gaussian kernel (p. 15). The kernel is bounded and Lipschitz (EC.1, p. 38; statement only).
- **D4 Analysis**: the MISDP is exact by rank-1 SDP exactness (p. 10). The approximation ratios extend proofs written for a = 0 to inhomogeneous objectives (pp. 12-14). A constant-ratio algorithm is NP-hard, citing Chan et al. (p. 13). Statistics: training sample sizes Ω(d² log(D/d)), Ω(d⁴ log(D/d)) and Ω(d log(D/d)) for the three kernels, and type-II error O(n_Te^{-1/2}) (Proposition 3, Theorems 6-7, pp. 15-16).
- **D5 Experiments**: the STRS algorithms are first checked against the "Ground Truth" on four synthetic families, 100 trials each (Figure 1, Table 1, pp. 17-18). Then come testing power against n and D, and FDP/NDP support recovery (pp. 18-21), followed by MNIST and a sepsis dataset, where quadratic MMD reaches power 0.915 (pp. 22-24). Projected Wasserstein wins the Gaussian-mixture case (p. 19).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the contribution is a framework plus exact and approximation algorithms plus a power analysis. Future work: theory for kernel hyperparameters and support recovery, and faster algorithms (p. 24). There is a trade-off: the Gaussian kernel needs the fewest samples but is the hardest to optimise (p. 2). Writing defect: Proposition 3 still contains the placeholder "see its detailed range in xxx" in v4 (p. 15).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅ (approximation algorithms are ranked against ground truth before the method is used, pp. 17-18). Method 4 evidence ✅ (each algorithm has a ratio, and the paper names the hardness of a constant ratio, p. 13). Method 5 variant ⚠ (the relaxation machinery is reused, but the deliverable is test power, not a certified gap, pp. 17-24). New-pattern candidate: "convex-IP dual bound for convex maximisation" (p. 14 reuses S057's device).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Before plugging an approximation algorithm into a downstream statistical pipeline, run it against the exact optimum on small synthetic instances over many trials. Tabulate time beside quality, then recommend one algorithm on that basis (pp. 17-18).
- When a fractional objective (signal/standard deviation) is hard, optimise the difference signal − λ·variance with λ tuned by cross-validation (p. 5).
- Show a baseline's failure with a one-line population example where its statistic is identically zero (Example 1, p. 7).
**Quotes**:
- "While the Gaussian kernel requires a smaller sample size to be statistically powerful, the corresponding MMD optimization is challenging." (p. 2)
- "Since the convex integer programming algorithm has satisfactory performance with relatively fast computational speed, we recommend using this approximation algorithm when solving (STRS)." (p. 18)

### S085 · Lifting convex inequalities for bipartite bilinear programs (Mathematical Programming 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-021-01759-3; read as arXiv:2106.12625v1)
Read level: partial. Read: abstract, §1, §2 main results, §3 future directions, start of §4 (pp. 1-9). Not read: the proofs in §§4-10 (pp. 9-26).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: X. Gu (student), J.-P. P. Richard · **Position**: the theory paper behind the lifted bilinear cover inequality. Its computational test came later in a separate paper (SKILL Heuristic 7). An extended abstract appeared at IPCO 2021 (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: lifting drove MILP cutting planes (lifted covers, GMI, mixing; pp. 1-2) but is rare in nonlinear settings (p. 2). The entry is the knapsack analogy: single-row relaxations were "the first, and arguably the most important step" in MILP cuts (p. 2). The paper claims to be the first study of lifted valid inequalities for general nonconvex quadratic constraints with any number of variables (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: (1) For one bipartite bilinear constraint over [0,1] bounds, a concave seed inequality can always be lifted sequentially, with affine terms in variables fixed at their bounds (Theorem 1, p. 4). (2) A subadditive-upper-bound framework, modelled on Gu-Nemhauser-Savelsbergh, gives sequence-independent lifting for separable bipartite bilinear sets (Proposition 1, p. 5). (3) For minimal-cover coefficients there is an SOC-representable bilinear cover inequality (Theorem 3, p. 6). It dominates the known inequality for the unbounded case (Proposition 2, p. 6) and is within a factor 4 of conv(Q) (Theorem 4, p. 7). (4) A two-slope subadditive bound on its lifting function (Theorem 5, p. 7) yields a closed-form lifted bilinear cover inequality (Theorem 6, pp. 7-8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: variables lie in [0,1] and are fixed at bounds. Example 3 shows lifting can fail when a variable is fixed at an interior value (p. 4). Sequence independence needs separability (p. 4). Theorem 2 says that either a minimal-cover partition exists or conv(Q) is polyhedral, so the cover case is the only nonpolyhedral one (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis**: an extreme-point characterisation of bipartite bilinear sets reduces Theorem 1 to three-variable case analysis (p. 4). Theorem 3 is proved with disjunctive programming, an extended formulation and an "approximate" Fourier-Motzkin projection (p. 6). The factor-4 bound compares linear optimisation with nonnegative objectives over R and Q (p. 7). Theorem 5 computes the lifting function exactly near the origin and extends the linear bound globally (p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The authors call for "extensive numerical experiments" (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the contribution is theoretical. Open questions (pp. 8-9): when lifting with non-affine forms is possible (Example 4 lifts with √x2 where affine lifting fails); sequence-independent lifting for non-separable sets; the complexity of separating the lifted inequality; and computational value. The proof of Theorem 1 can be turned into an algorithm for the lifting coefficients, "although not necessarily an efficient or practical one" (p. 4).

**Method links**: Method 3 evidence ✅ (the MILP toolkit of lifting, subadditivity, minimal covers and knapsack-as-first-step is carried over to a single bilinear row, pp. 1-7). Method 4 evidence ✅ (counterexamples are given for dropped hypotheses, Examples 2-3, pp. 3-4; there is a dichotomy theorem, p. 6; the hull is approximated within a factor 4, p. 7). The theory-first, test-later sequence matches Research Heuristic 7 (future-directions call, p. 9).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When porting lifting to a nonlinear set, first write the lifting coefficient as a sup problem (Example 1, p. 3). Check finiteness by splitting the range of the fixed variable. Use extreme-point structure, since maximising a convex function attains its maximum at an extreme point.
- Justify a seed inequality by its approximation factor to the hull (a factor-4 sandwich), not only by dominance (Theorem 4, p. 7).
- Pair every positive lifting theorem with a small counterexample for each hypothesis: convex set with interior fixing, and affine form versus √ form (Examples 2-4, pp. 3-4, 8-9).
**Quotes**:
- "Generating valid inequalities for single row relaxations (together with bounds and integrality restrictions), i.e., for knapsack constraints, was the first, and arguably the most important step in the development of computationally useful cutting-planes in MILP." (p. 2)
- "Finally, extensive numerical experiments should be conducted to understand the practical strength of these inequalities and to determine how useful they can be in the solution of QCQPs." (p. 9)

### S106 · A covering decomposition algorithm for power grid cyber-network segmentation (Sandia National Laboratories (SNL-NM) 2023, doi:10.2172/2431079)
Read level: partial. All 35 slides were read. The text is the SIAM OP23 slide deck SAND2023-04388C, not the homepage preprint. Many slides carry only a title, with the figures not extracted (e.g. pp. 3-6, 12, 20-23, 26, 28-32).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: E. S. Johnson (presenter, Sandia), J. Eckstein, C. A. Phillips, J. D. Siirola · **Position**: a follow-up to Johnson & Dey (IJOC 2022) on the worst-case relay attack lower bound, which is cited on p. 24.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: a trilevel game. The network designer segments the SCADA/communication network within budgets to minimise load shed. The attacker compromises subnetworks. The defender redispatches by capacitated network flow (p. 2). Prior trilevel methods assume the designer can make assets invulnerable, or that the attacker's feasible region does not depend on the design. The talk drops both assumptions as unrealistic for cyberattacks (p. 19).
- **D2 Core idea**: adapt Israeli-Wood covering decomposition (p. 17). Version 1 alternates between a design with a lower bound and the worst-case attack on it with an upper bound (p. 25). Version 2 searches for a design that blocks all attacks generated so far (p. 27). The inner DC-OPF is relaxed to capacitated network flow ("Drop the only hard thing in DCOPF: Ohm's Law"), which gives a restriction of the bilevel problem (p. 24).
- **D3 Assumptions**: the example uses attack budget 7, a balancing-authority/control-centre budget of 3 and a substation budget of 1. Worst-case load shed falls from 119.2 MW to 75.4 MW (pp. 5, 13-14). Subdivision rules are on pp. 7-11.
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a (slides; no proofs).
- **D5 Experiments**: 2000-bus and 500-bus cases (pp. 31-32; the plots were not extracted, so the results are not read).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: conclusions: the method scales to realistic grids with moderate budgets; larger substation budgets help little and cost much computation (p. 33). Future work: constraint programming in place of MIP for the feasibility problem, Benders for large attacker problems, and Version 1 as an early-termination gap (p. 34).

**Method links**: Method 5 variant ⚠ (the bounding logic is "We've lost nothing", since the original only gave a lower bound, p. 24, and the bounds close by alternation, p. 25; this is a decomposition, not a cheap convex relaxation). Dey's role in the talk cannot be separated from the coauthors'.
**Transferable techniques**:
- In a multilevel model whose inner problem has no valid dual-variable bounds, swap the inner model for a tractable restriction (network flow without Ohm's law). Say explicitly what bound property survives the swap (p. 24).
**Quotes**:
- "We’ve lost nothing: All we had was a lower bound anyway because we don’t know valid bounds on the dual variables of DCOPF!" (p. 24)
- "Increases in the substation budget do not help the network designer significantly, and come at high computational cost" (p. 33)

### H001 · Modeling and Solving the Large Scale EV Charger Location Planning Problem (homepage preprint, undated)
Read level: full (pp. 1-18; reference list pp. 18-22 skimmed).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: N. Redder (first author, student), V. M. Thomas (public policy) · **Position**: an application MIP with an equity focus. It is possibly related to the EV-charging rows S132, S154 and S155 (same authors), but that is not verified.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: the earlier EV-charger siting literature uses genetic algorithms and other heuristics on small candidate sets. The US standard EVI-Pro (Lite) is a simulation, not an optimisation (p. 2). Equity has not been built into location models (p. 3). The claimed gap is optimal solutions at full-city scale (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: a facility-location MIP in which each commuter type (an origin-destination tract pair) may be served by any station within r miles of home *or* work, with charging capacity in miles (pp. 3-4). Five variants: Serve-All (minimise chargers); Station-Limit (maximise commuters served with B chargers); ≥40% of stations in disadvantaged tracts; ≥40% of served commuters disadvantaged, with a binary switch once all disadvantaged commuters are served (pp. 4-6). A relaxation keeps charger counts integer and makes assignments continuous, then rounds down (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions**: commuters charge only near the origin or destination, within 1 mile (pp. 4, 9). A fast charger gives 250 miles per hour and runs 12 h/day (p. 9). Commuters beyond 2 SD in travel distance are removed (p. 9). Home-charging access is estimated by housing type (pp. 9-10). **Transferable techniques**:
- When comparing solutions across budgets, check whether independent solves move facilities back and forth. An extra constraint (here the equity constraint) can make the solutions grow monotonically. Report this as a planning property (pp. 15-17).
- Bracket an unknown behavioural parameter (here home charging) by solving two extremes. Assuming no one charges at home gives an upper bound on chargers needed. Assuming everyone who can does gives a lower bound (pp. 10-11).
