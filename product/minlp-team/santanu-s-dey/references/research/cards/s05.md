### S083 · Upper bounds for model-free row-sparse principal component analysis (ICML 2020, PMLR 119; no DOI/arXiv in list)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-8, the whole main paper; the appendix referred to on pp. 2, 4, 5 is not in the file, not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Guanyi Wang (Georgia Tech, student; equal contribution, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: conference precursor of the sparse PCA dual-bound line (Method 5 evidence cites doi:10.1287/opre.2021.2153).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: existing sparse PCA methods give primal solutions under statistical assumptions or stationary points of regularized forms, but "none" can verify solution quality via dual bounds in the model-free case (abstract, p. 1; literature critique pp. 2-3). Prop. 1.1 argues that the model-free gap Δ on the sample covariance also certifies quality w.r.t. the true covariance (p. 2).
  - Transferable: before building a bound, prove a short proposition that the quantity you can certify (sample gap) controls the quantity users care about (population gap) (p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea**: relax the Stiefel-plus-row-sparsity feasible set to an SOC-representable convex set C′ (ℓ1 per column, group ℓ2 row-sum ≤ √(rk), pairwise column SOCs replacing the SDP condition), then upper-approximate the remaining convex-maximization objective by piecewise-linear SOS-II terms, giving a second-order cone IP (SOCIP) (pp. 3-4). A local-search swap heuristic (Alg. 1) supplies primal bounds (pp. 4-5); thresholding eigenvalues, Bessel and trace cuts, and symmetry breaking make the SOCIP practical (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Assumptions**: model-free (any covariance A); row-sparse PCA with shared support; statistical assumptions only for Theorem 4 (in appendix, not read) (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1: opt_F ≤ opt_C′ ≤ (1+√r)² opt_F (p. 4); Prop. 2.4: SOCIP bound ≤ (1+√r)² opt_F + Σ rλ_jθ_j²/(4N²), "an affine function" of opt_F (p. 4); inapproximability cited to show the relaxation itself is NP-hard to optimize (p. 4); Theorem 5: SOCIP-impl solvable in polynomial time for fixed N, φ, |J+| (p. 6). No tightness example given.
- **D5 Experiments**: Gurobi 7.0.2 on a MacBook Pro; gap (ub−lb)/lb vs Baseline-1 (sum of top-k diagonal entries) and Baseline-2 (SDP); spiked-covariance artificial d = 500 and real sets d = 79 to 2000; SDP runs out of memory at d ≥ 500 (pp. 7-8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: framework = convex IP relaxation + affine guarantee + heuristic + practical speed-ups (p. 8). Limits stated: none beyond hardware limits on the SDP baseline; no tightness of the (1+√r)² factor discussed (not found in pp. 1-8).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ (dual bounds from an SOC rather than SDP relaxation, scaled to d = 2000 where SDP fails, pp. 1, 7-8); Method 4 ⚠ variant (guarantee stated without a tight example or lower bound, p. 4); Taste warning 1 ✅ (critique of guarantees resting on statistical assumptions, pp. 2-3).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace an SDP description of conv(Stiefel) by a handful of SOC constraints (column norms and pairwise sums/differences of columns) to scale a relaxation (p. 3).
- Make a convex-maximization objective tractable by eigen-decomposing it and upper-approximating each square by an SOS-II piecewise-linear function, then bound the approximation error by θ²/(4N²) per term (p. 4).
- Use the primal heuristic's value to set the threshold φ that removes most SOS-II variables while keeping the bound valid (p. 6).
**Quotes**: "However, none of these methods can efficiently verify the quality of the solutions via comparing current objective values with their dual bounds, especially in model-free case." (p. 1) · "To the best of our knowledge, there is no comparable theoretical or computational results for solving model-free SPCA." (p. 8)

### S027 · Using submodularity within column generation to solve the flight-to-gate assignment problem (Transportation Research Part C 2021, doi:10.1016/j.trc.2021.103217; read as arXiv:2011.05894v3)
**Read level**: full main text (pp. 1-27: all sections including tables and conclusion); Appendix A read in part (p. 27), Appendices B-C (pp. 28-35) not read. **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Yijiang Li (Georgia Tech, student), John-Paul Clarke (UT Austin, aerospace) · **Place in trajectory**: applied OR collaboration outside the MINLP core.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: compact MIPs for gate assignment are too slow; a prior column-generation paper gave no explicit formulation or pricing method (pp. 2-3). Objective chosen: minimize on-ground arrival delay, with gates assumed independent, justified by ramp-tower practice (p. 4).
- **D2 Key idea**: set-covering master (more stable than set partitioning, p. 5); pricing decomposes per gate (p. 6); the per-gate pricing objective is a non-monotone submodular set function (Lemmas 4.1-4.2, pp. 8-9), so the randomized double-greedy algorithm gives E f ≥ OPT/2 under f(F′) ≥ 0 (Thm 4.1, p. 10); exact DP recursion (Lemma 4.3, p. 11), O(nc) for integer data (Thm 4.2, pp. 13-14); block-decomposition 2-approximation for large instances (Thm 4.3, pp. 15-16); branching on assignment decisions (pp. 17-18).
- **D3 Assumptions**: gate independence (p. 4); no upper bound on park times (p. 5); f(F′) ≥ 0 for the 1/2 guarantee (p. 10); integer data for pseudo-polynomial DP (p. 13).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: submodularity by a case analysis on which park times are "pushed to the right" (p. 9); worst-case 2^(n−i+1) calls for the naive recursion (Prop. 4.1, p. 12); 2-approximation by taking the better of odd- and even-indexed blocks separated by the adjacency parameter σ (pp. 15-16).
- **D5 Experiments**: 37 instances (synthetic and ATL Aug 2019 data, up to 1,125 flights and 192 gates); Python + Gurobi 9.1, 8 h limit; compact MIP fails from 50 flights/20 gates (Table 3, p. 20); SM+DP best on small/moderate, SM+rolling horizon best on large, up to about 50% time reduction on ATL cases (pp. 21-26); parameters not tuned (p. 21); instances posted online (p. 26).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic/application. Limits: gate interference and stochastic arrival/turn times left as extensions (p. 27); σ-based decompositions (RHD, BD+RHD) were "very ineffective" and not reported (p. 24).

**Method links**: Method 5 ⚠ variant (LP lower bound vs binary-program upper bound reported as gap_root, pp. 16-17, but the deliverable is speed, not a certified gap); Heuristic 1 ✅ (instances released, p. 26); Heuristic 3 ✅ (dead options named and dropped with a reason: RHD/BD+RHD, p. 24; SPPTWTC reformulation not pursued because no park-time upper bounds, pp. 14-15); new-pattern candidate: structure-exploiting pricing.
**Transferable techniques**:
- Before calling a MIP solver for column-generation pricing, test whether the pricing objective is submodular; if so, a linear-time randomized double greedy generates good columns, and an exact DP is only needed to prove LP optimality (pp. 8-10, 21-22).
- Get a 2-approximation for a long time-ordered pricing problem by solving blocks exactly and keeping the better of odd and even blocks, separated by an interaction-length parameter (pp. 15-16).
- Prefer a set-covering master and argue explicitly why a covering solution can be repaired to a partition without loss (p. 5).
**Quotes**: "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first use of submodularity property to efficiently solve pricing problems and improve the performance of column generation algorithm." (p. 1) · "Furthermore, in contrast to the worse case theoretical analysis, the dynamic programming algorithm performs well." (p. 21)

### S060 · Finite element model updating of an 18-story structure using branch-and-bound algorithm with epsilon-constraint (Journal of Civil Structural Health Monitoring 2021, doi:10.1007/s13349-020-00468-3)
**Read level**: full main text (pp. 1-27; reference list pp. 27-29 skimmed). Version: author manuscript with printed page numbers. **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Yu Otsuki, Dan Li, Masahiro Kurata (Kyoto), Yang Wang (corresponding, civil engineering) · **Place in trajectory**: the FEM model-updating application that SKILL.md lists as an origin of the QCQP substructure line ("Commercial solvers (for hours)").

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: model updating is non-convex; randomized local search and stochastic search give no global guarantee; SOS methods apply only to restricted polynomial forms and tiny sizes; B&B on model updating has no experimental validation (pp. 3-4).
- **D2 Key idea**: make the implicit eigen-dependence explicit by taking eigenvalues and eigenvectors as variables and relaxing the generalized eigenvalue equation to −ε ≤ [K(α) − λM]ψ ≤ ε (Eq. 4), a bilinear program that a spatial B&B solver (BARON, McCormick relaxations) can handle (pp. 7-11).
- **D3 Assumptions**: accurate mass, stiffness-only updating, 18-DOF shear model, 6 of 18 DOFs measured, ±30% bounds, ad hoc eigenvalue/eigenvector bounds, ε chosen by "trial-and-error" (pp. 5-8, 13-14, 20-21).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a (no theorems; B&B is described generically, pp. 8-11).
- **D5 Experiments**: simulation (4 modes): BARON with L1 and ε = 1e−8 k_max gives e_avg = 0.004% in 0.30 s vs 1,000-start interior point (0.03% best, L2) and GA (pp. 14-18, Table 2); shaking-table data (2 modes): BARON stops at 600 s with gap 0.3825 but its upper-bound solution beats the L1 interior-point optimum in cross-validation (0.4998 vs 0.5676) (pp. 21-24); a line walk between two local optima shows non-convexity (pp. 23-24).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: application/validation. Limits stated: gap still large on experimental data, slower convergence; proposed binary expansion to MILP and novel relaxation/branching schemes; probabilistic updating left open (p. 27).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ (the selling point is the "tolerance certificate" that local and stochastic search lack, pp. 18, 25-26) with ⚠ variant (on real data the certified gap of 0.3825 against an upper bound of 0.4993 is not small, pp. 21-23); Method 3 ✅ as origin evidence (off-the-shelf McCormick B&B leaves a large gap on this bilinear structure, motivating stronger relaxations, p. 27; consistent with SKILL.md "Commercial solvers (for hours)"); Method 2 ✅ (planted "actual" parameters give ground truth in simulation before real data, pp. 13-18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn an implicit-function objective (eigenpairs of K(α)) into an explicit bilinear program by lifting the implicit quantities to variables and relaxing the defining equation by a tolerance ε; then sweep ε to show where accuracy saturates (pp. 7, 17).
- Compare a global solver against the strongest local baseline fairly: 1,000 random starts with analytical Jacobians, then cross-evaluate every solution under every formulation (pp. 15, 24).
**Quotes**: "However, the application of the B&B algorithm on FE model updating has been scarce." (p. 4) · "Since such randomized local optimization algorithms may or may not yield a good solution even with thousands of searches, there is a need for algorithms such as the B&B algorithm that can guarantee the tolerance of a solution from the global minimum." (p. 27)

### S135 · Lifting Convex Inequalities for Bipartite Bilinear Programs (IPCO 2021, LNCS pp. 148-162, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-73879-2_11; read as arXiv:2106.12625v1)
**Read level**: partial. Version read: arXiv:2106.12625v1 (23 Jun 2021, 26 pp.), which describes itself as the full paper whose "extended abstract" appeared in IPCO 2021 (p. 2); the journal version is S085. Read: pp. 1-9 (abstract, introduction, contributions, all main results §2, future directions §3) and the start of the proof of Theorem 1 (p. 9); proofs in §§4-10 (pp. 9-26) not read beyond section headings. **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xiaoyi Gu (Georgia Tech, student), Jean-Philippe P. Richard (Minnesota) · **Place in trajectory**: theory step of the QCQP substructure line; computational follow-up is S105 (card c16).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: lifting produced many of MILP's best cuts, but it is almost unused for nonlinear sets; single-row (knapsack) relaxations were "the first, and arguably the most important step" in MILP cuts, so study one bipartite bilinear row with bounds (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Key idea**: (i) Theorem 1: for a bipartite bilinear set, a concave seed valid on a face with variables fixed at 0/1 can always be lifted with affine terms (p. 4); (ii) Prop. 1: a subadditive upper bound ψ on the lifting function gives sequence-independent lifting, as in MILP [55] (p. 5); (iii) Theorem 3: the SOC-representable bilinear cover inequality for minimal-cover coefficients (p. 6); (iv) Theorems 5-6: a two-slope subadditive ψ and closed-form lifted bilinear cover inequalities (pp. 7-8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: variables in [0,1]; fixing at bounds (Example 3 shows lifting may fail at interior values, p. 4); affine lifting functions (Example 2 shows failure even for a convex set, p. 3); separable bilinear rows for sequence independence (p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis (brief, statements only)**: Theorem 1 reduces via extreme-point characterization to three-variable case analysis (p. 4); Theorem 2 dichotomy: a minimal cover partition exists or conv(Q) is polyhedral (p. 6); Theorem 3 via disjunctive extended formulation plus "approximate" Fourier-Motzkin (p. 6); Theorem 4: (4·R) ∩ [0,1]^2n ⊆ conv(Q) ⊆ R ∩ [0,1]^2n, a factor-4 approximation by bounding ratios of linear objectives (p. 7); Prop. 2: dominates Tawarmalani et al.'s inequality (4) (p. 6).
- **D5 Experiments**: none ("extensive numerical experiments should be conducted", p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory. Open: characterize liftability with non-affine lifting (Example 4, pp. 8-9), non-separable sequence-independent lifting, separation complexity, computational value (p. 9).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (explicit import of MILP lifting, cover inequalities and subadditive sequence-independent lifting into a single bilinear row, pp. 1-2, 4-5); Method 4 ✅ (counterexamples for each dropped hypothesis, Examples 2-3, pp. 3-4; constant-factor strength result, p. 7; dichotomy with the polyhedral case, p. 6); Heuristic 7 ✅ (theory first, measurement-only follow-up later: future-work call p. 9, realised in S105).
**Transferable techniques**:
- To show a new nonlinear cut is strong without computing the hull, prove a scaling sandwich (θ·R) ∩ box ⊆ conv(Q) ⊆ R ∩ box by comparing optimal values of nonnegative linear objectives over both sets (p. 7).
- When the exact lifting function is not subadditive (convex near 0), compute it exactly near the origin and extend its linear bound globally to get a subadditive over-approximation (p. 7).
- Accompany each positive lifting theorem with a two-variable counterexample showing which assumption (bound-fixing, affine form) is needed (pp. 3-4).
**Quotes**: "Generating valid inequalities for single row relaxations (together with bounds and integrality restrictions), i.e., for knapsack constraints, was the first, and arguably the most important step in the development of computationally useful cutting-planes in MILP." (p. 2) · "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that derives lifted valid inequalities for general non-convex quadratic constraints with arbitrary number of variables." (p. 2)

### S055 · Hyperbolic Relaxation of k-Locally Positive Semidefinite Matrices (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2022, doi:10.1137/20m1387407; read as arXiv:2012.04031v2)
**Read level**: partial (pp. 1-9: abstract, introduction, main results §2, conclusions and open questions §3, preliminaries §4 in part, proof of Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2 §5; §§6-8 pp. 9-21 not read beyond theorem statements found by search). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Grigoriy Blekherman (Georgia Tech mathematics), Kevin Shu, Shengding Sun (students) · **Place in trajectory**: second paper on how good sparse (submatrix) PSD relaxations are; improves the earlier bound of [3] (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: enforcing PSD-ness only on principal submatrices works well in OPF and BoxQP computations; explain why by asking how close k-locally PSD matrices S^{n,k} are to PSD (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Key idea**: X ∈ S^{n,k} has nonnegative sums of ℓ×ℓ principal minors for ℓ ≤ k, so its eigenvalue vector lies in the hyperbolicity cone H(e_k^n) of the elementary symmetric polynomial, a convex relaxation of the eigenvalue set (Observation 1, pp. 2-3).
- **D3 Assumptions**: normalization by any unitarily invariant norm or the trace (p. 4); 2 ≤ k ≤ n.
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 2.1: tight lower bound on λ_min attained by G(n,k) = (k/(k−1))I − (1/(k−1))11ᵀ; proof relaxes to a convex program over H(e_k^n), uses symmetry to collapse to two variables, and locates the optimum on the cone boundary (pp. 4, 8-9); Corollary 2 improves the Frobenius distance bound of [3] (p. 5); Theorem 2.2: relaxation exact for k ∈ {1, n−1, n} (p. 5); Theorem 2.3 structure theorem: invertible locally singular matrices are diagonally congruent to G(n,k), so the relaxation is strict for 2 ≤ k < n−1 (Corollary 4, p. 6); Lemma 1 for (4,2) proved via quantifier elimination (p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: none (one numerical check for the (4,2) case, p. 6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory. Open: convexity of eigenvalue sets for k < n−1 (other than (4,2)), singular boundary matrices, relaxations for structured submatrix collections (p. 7).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (the object of study is an empirically successful solver practice, sparse PSD enforcement, with the distance of S^{n,k} from the PSD cone as proxy measure, p. 2; matches Method 1 step 3 example); Method 4 ✅ (tight bound with attaining matrix, p. 4; exactness and strictness of the relaxation characterized in k, pp. 5-6); Heuristic 8 ✅ (real-algebraic step with Blekherman, p. 1; computer algebra where no human proof is known, p. 7); new-pattern candidate: computer-algebra certificate for a small case.
**Transferable techniques**:
- To bound an extremal spectral quantity over a matrix cone, map the cone to a convex set of eigenvalue vectors (here via characteristic-polynomial coefficients and hyperbolicity cones), solve the symmetric convex relaxation, then exhibit a matrix attaining it (pp. 2-5, 8-9).
- Use a dimension count (number of vanishing-minor equations versus dimension of Sym^n) to predict that a boundary class is rare before proving a structure theorem (p. 6).
**Quotes**: "It has been empirically observed that the resulting relaxation has an optimal objective function value close to the optimal objective function of the original problem." (p. 2) · "We do not know of a proof of this result that does not rely on computational methods." (p. 7)

### S058 · A scalable lower bound for the worst-case relay attack problem on the transmission grid (INFORMS Journal on Computing 2022, doi:10.1287/ijoc.2022.1178; read as arXiv:2105.02801v2)
**Read level**: partial (pp. 1-17 and 23-24: abstract, introduction, formulation, network-flow restriction, theory statements, computational design, quality and tractability discussion, conclusion; result tables pp. 18-22 and appendices pp. 25-33 with the proofs not read). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Emma S. Johnson (Georgia Tech student, Sandia) · **Place in trajectory**: power-systems line (bilevel interdiction).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: exact single-level reformulations of power-grid interdiction need valid bounds on DCOPF duals; valid ones are large and weaken LPs, heuristic ones silently give only a lower bound (abstract, pp. 1-4); verifying big-M values in bilevel problems is as hard as solving them (pp. 3-4).
- **D2 Key idea**: drop Ohm's law in the defender problem (network-flow restriction, from Sundar et al. 2018): it is a valid lower bound for the attacker's max, its inner LP is totally unimodular with unit costs so all duals are bounded by 1 (Observation 1, p. 8), giving an exact MILP with big-M = 1; then re-evaluate the attack under true DCOPF (Algorithm 1, pp. 9-10).
- **D3 Assumptions**: one relay per bus controlling bus, generators and adjacent lines (assumed for demonstration, p. 14); generator minimum dispatch 0 (p. 7); no phase-angle-difference bounds (w.l.o.g. argument, p. 10); one generator per bus (p. 10).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1: if thermal limits on non-cut arcs exceed √(Bmax/Bmin)·√(r(G)−1)/2·‖d‖₁, every flow-feasible injection is DCOPF-feasible, so network flow is exact in injection space (p. 11; proof in App. B, not read); Prop. 1: tight up to a constant via triangle-gadget digraphs (p. 12).
- **D5 Experiments**: 16 networks (118 to 6,468 buses, incl. deliberately congested ones), 10 budgets; Pyomo + Gurobi 9.0.2; NFLB best bound in 113 of 120 instances, otherwise 89-99.9% (p. 17); about 150 times faster than the heuristic-M MILP (p. 23); competitor M set from LP duals (2 or 3), larger M tried and dropped (p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory + computation of a primal bound. Limits stated: no non-trivial upper bound known, so quality is relative to best known solutions (pp. 14, 17); future: upper bounds, exact scalable methods, AC restoration models (p. 24).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (gives a theorem explaining why a cheap practice works: grids are rarely congested, pp. 4, 11, 17); Method 4 ✅ (sufficient condition plus a matching-up-to-constant construction, pp. 11-12); Method 5 ✗ contradiction (the deliverable is a scalable primal bound and the paper states no dual certificate is available, p. 14); Heuristic 2 ✅ (competitor's M tuned from Algorithm 1's own duals, warm-started, 4 h, p. 14).
**Transferable techniques**:
- In a min-max interdiction model, look for a restriction of the follower whose LP is totally unimodular with unit objective, so dual bounds of 1 are provably valid and the single-level MILP needs no guessed big-M (p. 8).
- Without an upper bound, measure quality as a percentage of the best known solution over all methods and report the exceptions explicitly (pp. 15-17).
- Report instance congestion (share of tight thermal limits and angle bounds at no attack) so readers can see when the theory's hypothesis holds (p. 15).
**Quotes**: "Valid dual bounds tend to be large, resulting in weak linear programming relaxations and hence making the problem more difficult to solve at scale." (p. 1) · "This is difficult to answer since we do not know of a non-trivial upper bound for the worst-case relay attack problem." (p. 14)

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: DC-OTS MILPs have weak big-M LPs; only [8] studied a polyhedral substructure (p. 2). New substructure: one bus, its generator and its n incident switchable lines (set S, p. 4).

- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: NP-hardness by reduction from Subset-Sum (pp. 4-5); TU network matrix (p. 8); integrality via perturbation of fractional extreme points (Lemma 1, p. 9); disjunctive union of integral polytopes (p. 10).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (identify a recurring local substructure of the application MINLP and study its integer hull, as for knapsack/flow sets, pp. 2, 4); Method 4 ✅ (NP-hardness paired with a polynomial special case and an exact special case, pp. 4-10); Method 3 stop rule ⚠ (gap closed near zero is reported, but the cuts are still recommended for solve-count gains, pp. 21-22).

(p. 5) · "The OTS Problem is known to have dual degeneracy (the cuts separate the current fractional point but there exist other optimal solutions of the LP with the same objective function value) and these results are not very surprising in this respect." ### S086 · Lower bound on size of branch-and-bound trees for solving lot-sizing problem (Operations Research Letters 2022, doi:10.1016/j.orl.2022.04.008; read as arXiv:2112.03965v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-6, the whole arXiv v1 text). **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Prachi Shah (Georgia Tech, student) · **Place in trajectory**: B&B complexity line (2020-2023) with Dubey, Molinaro and Shah (refs [8]-[10], pp. 5-6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: tree size measures B&B efficiency (p. 1); prior exponential lower bounds for general (split) B&B cover cross-polytope, packing, covering and TSP (p. 2); lot-sizing is "simple" (O(n log n) DP, known hull) yet may still need exponential general B&B (p. 3).
- **D2 Key idea**: instance f_j = 1, p_j = n − j + 1, d_j = 1 (Theorem 1, p. 3): any general B&B tree has at least 2^((n/2)−1) leaves.
- **D3 Assumptions**: uncapacitated lot-sizing formulation (1), branching on arbitrary integer split disjunctions, leaves pruned by bound (pp. 1-4).
- **D4 Analysis**: Claim 1 computes OPT = n(n+1)/2 + n by an exchange argument (p. 3); choose S = binary y with odd coordinates fixed to 1 (|S| = 2^((n−1)/2)); if two points of S share a leaf, their midpoint (with a constructed x) is LP-feasible at that node, since branching constraints are convex, and has value OPT − 0.5·N_0.5 < OPT, so the leaf cannot be pruned; hence each leaf holds at most one point of S (pp. 4-5).
