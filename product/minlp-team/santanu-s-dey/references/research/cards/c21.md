### S120 · Probabilistic analysis of dual decomposition on two-stage stochastic integer programs (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2604.23383)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-29: main text pp. 1-26 read line by line, Appendices A-D pp. 27-28 read in full, references p. 28-29). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Marco Molinaro (Microsoft Research / PUC-Rio), Jingye Xu (Georgia Tech) · **Place in trajectory**: joins two Dey lines: the random-instance branch-and-bound theory (Dey-Dubey-Molinaro, "Branch-and-bound solves random binary IPs in polytime", SODA 2021, ref [11], p. 28) and the 2023-2026 duality/decomposition line (Cifuentes-Dey-Xu IPCO 2025, ref [9], p. 29); also cites his stochastic-IP work (Angulo-Ahmed-Dey, ref [1]; Dey-Molinaro-Wang sparse cuts for stochastic MILPs, ref [12], p. 28).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is a gap between practice and theory: decomposition methods (Benders, Branch-and-Price) "demonstrate excellent empirical performance in practice" but guarantees are "almost exclusively based on worst-case analyses" of order Θ(2^n), resting on the count of 2^n first-stage solutions (abstract p. 1; p. 2). Evidence of practice is cited, not produced: computational studies show convergence in few iterations, "in many cases even a single iteration" (ref [14], p. 2). The question is narrowed to dual decomposition, because nonconvexity forces branching on top of the Lagrangian dual, i.e. Branch-and-Price (pp. 2-3). Problem form: max s·<c0,x0> + Σ_i <ci,xi> s.t. A0 x0 + Ai xi <= b(i), all binary, n first-stage and n second-stage variables per scenario, m rows per scenario (eq. (1), p. 2).
  - Transferable: state the worst-case bound the field relies on, say where its proof comes from (here "at most 2^n possible first-stage integer solutions", p. 2), and set the average-case question against that specific count.
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Two-step argument (p. 3): (i) tree size is controlled by the LP integrality gap (Lemma 1.2), (ii) the integrality gap is small on random instances (Theorem 1.3); together n^{O_m(log s)} nodes with probability >= 0.9999 - 1/n (Theorem 1.1, p. 3). The algorithm analysed is a deliberately plain Branch-and-Price: node bound = value of the dual decomposition relaxation (2) (each scenario convexified jointly with the first stage, p. 4), branch on any unfixed first-stage variable, best-bound node selection, and an incumbent from a node with all x0 fixed (p. 5). Key change relative to their SODA 2021 B&B proof [11]: work only with the projection onto x0, i.e. with A0's m rows, so that the bound is not exponential in the m·s constraints (pp. 5-6). For the gap, the single-block Dyer-Frieze analysis [13] is extended to the multi-block structure: once the optimal dual is fixed all blocks decouple (p. 13), but a naive blockwise application still gives exp(poly(s)) (p. 13); the fix is a stronger right-hand-side assumption that makes average dual size and number of primal zeros reflect each block (p. 13).
  - Transferable: when reusing your own proof for a richer model, identify the single quantity that blows up (here the number of constraints, m·s) and look for a projection or relaxation strength that removes it before redoing the probabilistic part (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Stochastic-input model: entries of c(i), A(i) i.i.d. U[0,1]; b(i)_r = β(i)_r · 2n with β in (1/4, 1/2) (p. 3). β > 1/2 makes the instance trivial with high probability; β >= 1/4 is imposed "For technical reasons (see Section 4.3)" and is "slightly stronger than assumptions used in [13]" (p. 3), i.e. an admitted proof-convenience assumption. Fixed m, growing n and s (p. 3). Exact oracle for the dual decomposition relaxation (p. 3). All binary, equal dimensions; claimed to extend "in a straightforward manner" to heterogeneous dimensions, nonuniform weights and mixed-integer domains, without proof (p. 2). Theorem 4.1 adds explicit size conditions on n relative to m, α = βmin - 1/4 (pp. 11-12). The "high probability" is a constant 0.9999 obtained by choosing f = σ log s (p. 12).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) Best-bound argument: every internal node has DUAL(N) >= OPT, otherwise the node would have been pruned (Lemma 3.1, pp. 6-7). (b) Lagrangian value Γ of first-stage points built from the LP saddle point; a node's relaxation optimum has Γ >= DUAL(N); because Γ is linear, round to a binary x0 with no smaller Γ (pp. 6-7). (c) Injection-type counting: binary points in Ω = {x0 : Γ(x0) >= OPT} map to internal nodes at most n to one, because nodes not on one path differ in a fixed coordinate, so tree size <= 2n|Ω| + 1 (Lemma 3.2, pp. 7-8). (d) Geometric reformulation: Γ loss equals s × the sum of distances of the flipped first-stage columns (c_j, a_j) to the hyperplane H defined by the averaged dual (Lemma 3.4, pp. 8-9); bucket the columns by distance and count flips per bucket (Lemma 3.5, pp. 9-10), with slab counts from Theorem 4 of [11] (Lemma 3.6, Corollary 3.1, p. 10). (e) Integrality gap: decompose ∆ into reduced-cost and constraint-slackness terms after solving the LP with shrunk right-hand side b - 2d1 (Lemma 4.1, pp. 12, 15-16); bound the optimal dual by a perturbation argument on typical instances, with Chernoff bounds (Claims 4.2-4.3, pp. 16-18); bound the number of zeros of the LP solution through an ε-net over duals and a union bound (Claims 4.4-4.6, pp. 18-20; net built in Appendix B, p. 27); condition on the zero set so that the remaining columns are uniform on the nonpositive-reduced-cost region (Lemma 4.6 from [5, 13], p. 22); restrict to a box-like subregion so that the columns are uniform on [α, 2α]^m (Claim 4.8, pp. 23-24; Appendix D, p. 28); apply the Dyer-Frieze discrepancy lemma (Lemma 4.7, p. 20) to blocks of size 2m log n and boost the success probability over f' independent blocks (Claims 4.10-4.11, pp. 25-26); at most m fractional coordinates per block of a basic LP solution (Appendix C, pp. 27-28). No lower bound or tight example is given.
  - Transferable: to bound a B&B tree, bound the number of binary points whose "Lagrangian value" exceeds the optimum and charge each internal node to one such point, at most depth-many per point (pp. 7-8).
  - Transferable: to get independence from an LP solution that depends on the data, condition on the support pattern (zero set) and use that the remaining columns are uniform on a reduced-cost region (p. 22).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: none. No computation is reported; the empirical side is cited from the literature (refs [1, 2, 14], pp. 2-3).
- **D6 Positioning**: Claims "the first theoretical average-case analysis of the Branch-and-Price algorithm" (p. 4) and the first average-case analysis for this class (abstract p. 1). Extends Dyer-Frieze [13] from single-block packing IPs to two-stage multi-block (pp. 4, 11, 13); the tree-size part "is similar to that of [11]" (own) with the key difference stated (p. 5); Lemma 3.5's proof "is the same as that of Lemma 5 of [11]" (p. 9); dual-size and zero-count techniques from Borst-Dadush-Huiberts-Tiwari [5] and [13] (p. 13). Worst-case decomposition analyses [21, 23] are reframed, not criticised (p. 2). Benders is named as the next target (p. 26).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Informal main theorem in the introduction (Theorem 1.1, p. 3) then the two components (Lemma 1.2, Theorem 1.3), each section opening with a numbered "high-level idea" list (p. 5) or a "Main Steps" subsection that states lemmas before proofs (pp. 12-14). The independent-interest claim is spelled out for practitioners: the gap result "helps explain why LP-based heuristics work well in practice" with many scenarios (abstract p. 1; p. 4). Proof-convenience choices are labelled ("For technical reasons", p. 3; "With a lot of hindsight, define", p. 12). A known exception (a measure-zero set of conditionings where Lemma 4.5 may fail) is put in a footnote rather than in the statement (p. 21). The abstract's gap rate O(log s log^2 n / n) is the per-scenario (normalised by 1/s) version of Theorem 1.3's O_m(s log s log^2 n / n) (p. 4), which the abstract does not say.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: theory (quasi-polynomial tree size, logarithmic-in-s gap) for a standard algorithm under a random model. Limits stated: exact oracle (p. 3); fixed m (p. 3); β >= 1/4 for technical reasons (p. 3); Benders-type methods not covered (p. 26); the discrepancy lemma could be improved under other models (p. 20). Unstated (my reading): no lower bound showing n^{O(log s)} is needed; no experiment checking that real decomposition runs behave like the random model; constant success probability 0.9999 rather than 1 - o(1); the extension to mixed-integer and heterogeneous settings is asserted, not proved (p. 2).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the object is an empirical regularity of solvers (decomposition "often converge[s] in only a small number of iterations", p. 2), the proxy is tree size of a plain Branch-and-Price with an exact oracle on random instances (pp. 3, 5), and the paper ends by saying how much practice it explains (LP-based heuristics with many scenarios, p. 4). This is the case the SKILL already cites for Method 1 (arXiv:2604.23383); the card confirms it from the text.
- Method 1 step 5 ✅ evidence: worst case is exponential (Θ(2^n), p. 2), so a random model is chosen and defended as "representative" by a folklore hardness regime β near 1/2 (p. 3).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: the boundary of the result is not shown: no tight instance, no lower bound, no example where dropping β >= 1/4 breaks the result; instead the unproved extension claim on p. 2. Contrast with the "tight, data-independent" standard of Method 4.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (weak): the theorem is framed as support for the dual bounds of LP-relaxation-based decomposition methods with many scenarios (p. 4).
- Heuristic 4 / recycling: the proof template of the SODA 2021 random-IP paper [11] is reused and extended (pp. 5, 9).
- New-pattern candidate: "count near-optimal solutions to bound enumeration effort" (already a candidate from c10 S031): tree size <= 2n|Ω| + 1 with Ω the binary points of high Lagrangian value (pp. 7-8).
- New-pattern candidate: "recycle own proof machinery" (already a candidate from c15 S097): Lemma 3.5 proof "the same as that of Lemma 5 of [11]" (p. 9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Tree-size-from-gap reduction: best-bound rule ⇒ every internal node has bound >= OPT; round the node's optimum to a binary point of no smaller Lagrangian value; charge nodes to points (pp. 6-8).
- Express the cost of flipping coordinates away from the LP solution as distances of data columns to the dual hyperplane, then bucket columns by distance and use slab-count lemmas (pp. 8-10).
- Shrink the right-hand side by a margin 2d before solving the LP so that a later rounding that adds a few columns stays feasible; pay for the margin by LP sensitivity with the optimal dual (pp. 12-13, 15-16).
- Discretise the dual with an ℓ1-net and union-bound over it to handle dependence between the LP solution and the data (pp. 18-20, 27).

**Quotes**:

- "These assumptions are made solely for notational simplicity and clarity of exposition." (p. 2)

### S121 · Accelerating Column Generation in Highly Degenerate Integer Programming Problems with Template Pricing (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2604.12070)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-22: main text pp. 1-17 read line by line; declarations p. 18-19; Appendices A-C pp. 19-21 read in full; Appendix D Table 9 p. 22 read for its caption and the classes it covers, individual rows skimmed). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Luke Marshall (Microsoft Research), Prachi Shah (Georgia Tech ISyE) · **Place in trajectory**: column-generation / decomposition line; cites Dey's earlier column-generation application (Li-Clarke-Dey, flight-to-gate assignment, ref [42], p. 18). The author-contribution statement assigns implementation, data collection and analysis to Marshall and the first draft to Shah, with "input from all authors" (p. 19), so Dey's share of the design is not identifiable from the text.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is a known computational pathology: column generation (CG) with Dantzig pricing converges slowly under dual degeneracy, and the pricing subproblems, solved independently, return columns that compete for the same jobs and rarely enter the basis together (pp. 1, 4). The optimal restricted master problem may not even contain columns that form an integer solution (Ghoniem-Sherali, p. 1). Setting: set-partitioning master with knapsack subproblems, focused on the Generalized Assignment Problem (GAP) (eq. (1)-(2), p. 2). Degeneracy is proxied by the job-to-machine ratio, with Savelsbergh's ratio > 5 and Lübbecke-Desrosiers' ratio > 10 as markers (p. 8).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Template pricing: among columns with negative reduced cost, maximise similarity to a template y_i and use reduced cost only as tie-breaker (lexicographic problem (5)-(7), p. 5). Template = projection of the current RMP solution, y_i = Σ λ*_ip v_ip (eq. (8), p. 5); similarity d(x,y) = Σ f(y_j) x_j with f = +1 / 0 / -1 for y_j near 1 / fractional / near 0, δ = 1e-6 ("soft fixing", eq. (9), pp. 5-6). Exact version via hierarchical MIP (MT, Appendix B, p. 20); heuristic version (LT) via a one-parameter Lagrangian OPT(α) = min -d + α·reduced cost with bisection on α, warm-started from the previous CG iteration, which keeps the knapsack DP intact (pp. 6-7). What did not change: the RMP (only set cover instead of set partition, p. 8), the knapsack solver shared by all methods (p. 11). Rationale: the dual values only break ties, so degenerate, unstable duals steer less (p. 1, p. 6); fractional templates make the rule revert to Dantzig pricing (p. 6).
  - Transferable: to coordinate independent subproblems, give each a target taken from the current master primal solution and make the original pricing objective a tie-breaker inside the feasible "good column" set (pp. 5-6).
  - Transferable: replace a lexicographic subproblem by a scalarised one with a bisected weight so that the specialised subproblem solver (here a knapsack DP) can be kept (pp. 6-7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Independent subproblems, one per machine, number known (p. 16); integer cost data, used to round lower bounds and stop early (p. 9); root node only, no branch-and-price, "Introducing the complexity of branching would distract from our comparison on pricing strategies" (p. 9); no primal heuristics, so integer solutions appear only when the RMP is integral (p. 9). Instances: 57 Yagiura GAP instances (classes a-e, 100-1600 jobs) and 1,735 ISA instances from an instance-space analysis (p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a (no theorems). The only analytic content is a linear-algebra explanation offered as "A possible explanation" for fewer and faster simplex pivots: columns similar to basic columns give B^{-1}a_i ≈ e_r, sparse FTRANs, one-for-one pivots, small dual perturbations and cheap factor updates (p. 13); and the Lagrangian bound RMP_t + rc_t with integrality rounding (p. 9).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: C++, HiGHS 1.11.0 for the RMP, a shared SCIP-derived knapsack DP for all pricing rules except MT, AMD EPYC 64-core, subproblems in parallel, each instance with exclusive hardware (p. 11). Baselines: Dantzig (D), Pessoa directional/adaptive smoothing (P, implemented in full detail in Appendix A, pp. 19-20), and Lagrangian relaxation with L-BFGS (LR) as an "exception" following Savelsbergh's advice on degenerate GAP (pp. 8-9). Fairness design: RMP-modifying stabilisers excluded to "isolate the contribution of pricing" (p. 4); each method initialised by its own best Phase I after showing that a common Template Phase I slows D and P (Table 1, Fig. 3, p. 10); column-management age threshold tuned per method and per degeneracy level by parameter sweeps, 5 replications, rolling geometric means, and a quadratic covering curve (Fig. 4, p. 11; Appendix C, pp. 20-21). Yagiura: 6 h limit, 5 seeds, 285 runs per method; LT and MT solve all, LT each instance in under 7 minutes; RMP time 15.4 s (LT) vs 5,285.2 s (D) and 2,642.3 s (P); pivots per column 3.6 vs 53.8; integer gap 0.8% vs 50.7% (Fig. 5, Table 3, pp. 12-13); per class/size ratios up to 23,224× vs D and 1,022× vs P (Table 9, p. 22). ISA: 1 h limit, one run; solved 96.9% (LT), 58.3% (P), 76.7% (LR, where non-convergence to the optimal bound counts as timeout) (Table 6, p. 15); on the 824 instances all solve, LT still has fewer pivots (Table 7, p. 15); LR fails on 0.5% by DP memory errors (p. 15). Failure cases reported by class: 'f' class hardest for all, LT best (Table 8, p. 16).
  - Transferable: when initialisation interacts with the methods compared, test a common initialisation first, show it is unfair, and then give each method its own best start (p. 10).
  - Transferable: pick a tuned parameter by a covering curve over per-instance best values instead of a fitted curve, so the policy errs on the robust side (p. 21).
- **D6 Positioning**: Placed among dual-stabilisation methods (trust region, interior point, aggregation, dual-optimal inequalities; long citation list, p. 4), Ghoniem-Sherali sequential compatible pricing, and dynamic constraint aggregation (DCA), described as more complex and possibly degenerate in its refinement step (p. 4). Template pricing is said to share the anchoring idea of DCA without enforcing it (p. 6) and to resemble local search around active columns (p. 16). Pessoa is credited as "highly effective" but "substantially more complex" (p. 5). LR is acknowledged as the traditional choice for degenerate GAP (pp. 3, 8, 17).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contribution bullets (pp. 1-2) and a numbered list of five advantages (p. 6). Strong headline numbers ("over 1000x faster", abstract p. 1; p. 2). Explanations are hedged ("A possible explanation", p. 13; "seems to slow convergence", p. 10). Fairness language recurs ("To make the experimental analysis as fair as possible", p. 8; "Reasonable effort has been made to ensure that our LR baseline is a fair comparison", p. 8). Section 4 is an explicit "design choices" section placed before results (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: algorithmic and computational (a pricing rule, an exact and a heuristic solver for it, a tuned experimental protocol, released code and data, p. 19). Limits stated: no branch-and-price (p. 9, p. 16); similarity function chosen for simplicity (p. 15); Lagrangian heuristic may not suit complex subproblems (p. 16); problems with shared subproblems (bin packing, coloring) need a different template notion (p. 16); faster LR variants may exist (p. 8). Future work: combine with Pessoa, LR/LT hybrid, links to degenerate-pivot theory (pp. 15-16). Unstated (my reading): the abstract's "achieve CG optimal bounds on all 1735 ISA instances, finding stronger bounds in 43% and improved integer solutions in 9%" (p. 1) is not backed by a table in the body read, and Table 6 reports LT solving 96.9% within 1 hour (p. 15); the pivot explanation is untested; D and MT are not run on ISA (p. 8).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: a stripped instrument where only the studied decision varies: RMP-modifying stabilisers excluded (p. 4), branching excluded (p. 9), the same knapsack solver for all rules (p. 11). There is no exact ceiling, so the "ground truth" side of Method 2 is absent.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the paper starts from an empirical surprise (fewer and faster pivots, p. 13) but stops at "A possible explanation" without a proxy theorem; theory of degenerate pivots is listed as future work (p. 16).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: CG lower bounds are reported and certified via RMP_t + rc_t (p. 9), but the headline metric is time to convergence, not a certified gap at scale.
- Heuristic 2 ✅ evidence: give the baselines their best shot: per-method initialisation (p. 10), per-method tuned column management (p. 11, pp. 20-21), full documentation of the Pessoa variant implemented (pp. 19-20), LR made "a fair comparison" (p. 8).
- Heuristic 6 ✅ evidence (partial): RMP and pricing times are reported separately, so MT's expensive pricing (372.8 s vs 5.7 s for LT) is visible (Table 3, p. 13).
- New-pattern candidate: "cover-not-fit parameter policy": tuned parameter set by the smallest quadratic lying above all per-instance best values, chosen for robustness (p. 21).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Template = projection of the current master primal; similarity with +1/0/-1 weights by near-integrality; reduced cost as tie-breaker (pp. 5-6).
- Bisection on a single Lagrangian weight with warm start across iterations, plotted to justify the warm start (Figs. 1-2, p. 7).

- Report pivots per column and pivots per second alongside time to separate "fewer iterations" from "cheaper iterations" (Table 3, p. 13).

**Quotes**:

### S122 · Chvátal-Gomory Rounding of Eigenvector Inequalities for QCQPs (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2604.00932)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-25: whole text, all proofs and all tables; references read). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Nan Jiang (HKUST), Aleksandr M. Kazachkov (Florida), Andrea Lodi (Cornell Tech), Gonzalo Muñoz (Universidad de Chile) · **Place in trajectory**: continues the same team's sparse-PCA cut paper (Dey-Kazachkov-Lodi-Muñoz, SIAM J. Optim. 2022, ref [18], p. 23) and draws on Dey's CG-closure work for convex sets (Dadush-Dey-Vielma, ref [13], p. 23) and the cut-selection review's depth measure (Dey-Molinaro 2018, ref [19], p. 23); part of the QCQP cut line.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Eigen-cuts from the PSD condition on [1 x^T; x X] cut off any non-PSD point, but "the main drawback has been repeatedly acknowledged": eigenvectors give dense cuts that cause numerical instability (p. 3). The entry is to apply an IP operation (Chvátal-Gomory rounding) to these cuts after passing to the binary case, using Burer-Letchford's result that inequalities valid for the Boolean quadric polytope BQP_n are valid for the box-QP hull QPB_n (Proposition 1, p. 2; "at the core of our construction", p. 3). Problem class: QCQP on [0,1]^n with lifted matrix X (eq. (1), p. 2).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Eigen-CG inequalities (8): round up the non-constant coefficients ⌈2v_i v_j⌉, ⌈v_i^2 + 2v_i v_0⌉ and round down the constant ⌊v_0^2⌋ (p. 3; validity Lemma 1, p. 5). Through this lens the Boros-Hammer (BH) inequalities are the subfamily with integer v and half-integer v_0 (F0 = BH, Lemma 3, p. 7), and two intermediate families F1, F2 are defined by which products are integral (Definition 1, p. 5). Results: strict containments BH ⊊ F1 ⊊ F2 ⊊ Eigen-CG (Lemma 2, pp. 6-7) but equal conic closures for BH, F1, F2 (Lemma 4, pp. 7-9); conjecture that Eigen-CG has the same closure (Conjecture 1, p. 9); Eigen-CG misses most facets of BQP_6 (pp. 9-12); dense Eigen-CG cuts have small depth over SDP+McCormick (Propositions 6-7, pp. 12-14). The computational part uses sparse BH cuts via lookup tables of BQP_4 and BQP_5 facets and a bounded-integer BH separator for denser cuts (pp. 15-16).
  - Transferable: re-derive a known cut family through a new operation (here CG rounding of a continuous family) and use the parameters of the operation to define intermediate families whose closures you can compare (pp. 3-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Compact QCQP on [0,1]^n (p. 1). Theoretical depth results assume the point to be cut satisfies the SDP constraint (6) and all McCormick inequalities (3) (Props. 6-7, pp. 12-14). Experiments restrict Eigen-CG to BH "Since we have not yet found any Eigen-CG cuts that cannot be expressed as BH inequalities" (p. 14). Instances are binary QPs modelled as continuous QCQPs with x_i(1 - x_i) = 0 (pp. 14-15). The BH separator bounds w in [-2, 2] and Σ|w_i| <= 10 (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) Strict containment by explicit small counterexamples (n = 2 or 3) plus a proof that no positive rescaling lands in the smaller family, by reducing to a small linear system with no integral / nonzero solution (Lemma 2, pp. 6-7). (b) Closure equality by an arithmetic normal form: square-free factorisation shows F2 vectors are v = p·r with r integral, p^2 and 2v_0 p integral (Proposition 2, p. 7); then F2 cut = nonnegative combination of two BH cuts with half-integer shifts a ± 1/2 bracketing v_0/p, with multipliers α± chosen to match coefficients and the constant handled by a perfect-square inequality (Lemma 4, pp. 7-9). (c) Non-representability certificates for facets of BQP_6: a sign-pattern argument (Prop. 3, p. 9), a 2×2 multiplicative-consistency argument β12β35 = β13β25 against ceiling intervals (Prop. 4, p. 10), and a 3-cycle product argument with interval disjointness for λ >= 3 plus case analysis for λ ∈ {1, 2} (Prop. 5, pp. 10-12); the two first arguments are automated over all 113,088 non-Eigen-CG facets, leaving 37,338 handled case by case after symmetry reduction (p. 10). (d) Depth bound: the SDP slack kills the unrounded part, leaving numerator <= 1 (the rounding loss of v_0^2), while the norm of the cut grows with support: same-sign pairs force |⌈2v_i v_j⌉| >= 1, and a one-dimensional convex minimisation over the split |P| + |N| = k gives depth <= 2/sqrt(k(k-2)) (Prop. 6, pp. 12-13); with integrality of v_i^2 in F2, depth <= 1/sqrt((k-1)·||v||_2) as stated (Prop. 7, p. 14).
  - Transferable: to show a dense cut is weak on top of a relaxation, bound the numerator of the depth by what the relaxation already enforces and the denominator from below by counting forced nonzero coefficients (pp. 12-13).
  - Transferable: certify "not in family" for many enumerated facets by cheap necessary conditions (sign patterns, 2×2 product consistency) and automate them before doing case work (pp. 9-10).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Python 3.11.4, Gurobi 12.0.1 (separator, upper bounds) and MOSEK 11.0.14 (SDPs), Xeon Gold 6258R, up to four threads (p. 16). Instances: 30 Biq Mac binary QPs be100.1-10, be120.8.1-10, be150.8.1-10, chosen because SDP gives a good but not closing bound (p. 15). Metric: GAP = |UB - LB|/|LB| with UB the best Gurobi solution in a 4-hour limit, not a proven optimum (p. 15). ### S123 · Extreme strong branching for QCQPs (Operations Research Letters 2026, doi:10.1016/j.orl.2026.107446; read as arXiv:2510.20650v1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-12 of the arXiv v1 preprint: main text pp. 1-8, references pp. 8-10, Appendix A tables pp. 11-12 read row by row). The ORL typeset version was not read; page references are to the arXiv version. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Dahye Han (Georgia Tech ISyE), Yang Wang (Georgia Tech Civil and Environmental Engineering) · **Place in trajectory**: carries the full-strong-branching line (Dey-Dubey-Molinaro-Shah, Math. Program. 2024, ref [7], p. 9) into spatial branch-and-bound, on the FEM model-updating application of the QCQP line (Dey-Han-Wang arXiv:2410.14163, ref [8]; Dey-Santana-Wang SOCP branching rule for bipartite bilinear programs, ref [9], p. 9).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is a transfer question: strong branching is among the most effective MIP variable-selection rules; "Our work investigates whether the success of strong branching can be extended beyond binary variables" (p. 1). Obstacle: continuous (spatial) branching needs a branching point as well as a variable (p. 1). Existing extensions fix the point in advance (convex combination of the relaxed value and midpoint, Belotti et al. [4]) and solve 2n relaxations, or learn it (Kannan et al. [14]) (p. 2). Problem: QCQP with box bounds (eq. (2), p. 5).
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: Extreme strong branching (esb): for every variable, binary-search the branching point on each side, using monotonicity of the child bounds in α (obj_L non-increasing, obj_R non-decreasing, p. 3); a child relaxation that is infeasible or above the incumbent moves the search and tightens the variable's bound as a byproduct (Remark 1, p. 3; Algorithm 2, p. 4); fill in missing child values so every candidate α has both sides; score = max{obj_L - obj_p, ε}·max{obj_R - obj_p, ε}, the MIP product rule, and pick the best (variable, point) pair (Algorithms 1, 3, pp. 3-4; Fig. 1 walk-through, p. 5). What did not change: the product score of MIP strong branching and McCormick relaxations at nodes (pp. 4-6). The two claimed benefits are bound tightening as a byproduct and the objective-driven rationale of MIP strong branching (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when a branching decision has a continuous parameter, exploit monotonicity of the child bound in the parameter to binary-search it, and record bound reductions found along the way (pp. 3-4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Linear objective "For simplicity of presentation" with the claim that the method holds for nonconvex objectives (p. 2). Node relaxation: McCormick for every bilinear term from current local bounds (eq. (3), p. 6). All continuous variables are candidates (p. 8). Fixed iteration limit itermax for the binary search (Algorithm 1, p. 3; its value is not reported in the text read). Benchmark restricted to instances with at most 100 variables (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a (no theorems). The only formal statement is Remark 1, a direct consequence of relaxation validity: an infeasible or over-incumbent child at α proves x_i >= α (or <= α) (p. 3).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Custom B&B in Julia 1.11 built on BranchAndBound.jl; McCormick LPs by HiGHS 1.11.0 rebuilt at every node without warm start; Ipopt 3.14.17 for upper bounds every 10 iterations; best-bound node selection; 100,000-iteration cap; personal MacBook Air M3, 8 GB (pp. 5-6). Incumbent: BARON 25.3.19 run 60 s and passed in, "to isolate the evaluation of the dual bound improvement" (p. 5). Metrics: remaining gap vs best incumbent, wall-clock time capped at 3,600 s, nodes (p. 6). (i) MINLPLib QCQPs (pooling, wastewater, water) with <= 100 variables, comparing basic spatial strong branching [4], the balance rule for bilinear problems [10] and esb inside the same B&B; commercial solvers deliberately not used here "as such solvers are highly optimized for standard instances" (p. 6). On the 35 instances all rules solve, esb is the only rule solving all under 100 s; arithmetic mean time 12.30 s vs 57.89 (balance) and 148.52 (basic) (Table 1, p. 7); on 9 instances unsolved by at least one rule, mean remaining gap 8.81% vs 48.11% and 51.21% (Table 2, p. 7); esb uses far fewer nodes (Table 5, p. 11). (ii) 20 FEM model-updating instances vs Gurobi 12.0.1 (constraint violation 1e-9, defaults): esb solves 8 vs 5, arithmetic mean gap 7.94% vs 11.26%, nodes 58 vs 8.6 million (Tables 3-4, pp. 7-8); BARON and Couenne said to give only marginal lower-bound improvements, no table (p. 8). Per-instance tables in Appendix A (pp. 11-12).
- **D6 Positioning**: Reviews spatial-branching rules (violation transfer of Tawarmalani-Sahinidis, simplicial B&B of Linderoth, complex-variable QCQP branching of Chen-Atamtürk-Oren, the bilinear balance rule of Fischetti-Monaci, own SOCP-guided rule for bipartite bilinear programs, piecewise-linear branching of Hübner-Gupte-Rebennack) and strong-branching extensions (Belotti et al.; Kannan et al.) (pp. 1-2). Bound tightening is set against FBBT/OBBT in global solvers: same spirit, but a byproduct of branching, not a separate step (p. 2). No direct criticism; the incumbent-warm-start practice is attributed to Fischetti-Monaci [10] (p. 5).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Short-note format: two numbered benefits (p. 2), algorithm boxes with inline comments, one illustrated example with a long caption (p. 5). The abstract claims esb "outperforms existing commercial solvers" for "certain types" of QCQPs (p. 1); the results prose is assertive ("consistently performs better", p. 7) and the conclusion hedges with "preliminary computational experiments" and "particularly effective for bilinear bipartite problems" (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: algorithmic plus computational. Limits stated: overhead may outweigh benefits with many variables since all continuous variables are candidates; future reliability-based variant; why it works for bipartite bilinear problems is open (p. 8); overhead may not pay off on small trees (p. 7). Unstated (my reading): on FEM the geometric mean time is worse for esb (2,130.99 s vs 1,974.82 s, Table 4, p. 8; Tgeo 970.51 vs 325.99 on solved instances, Table 3, p. 8) while the text cites only gap and nodes; whether the 60 s BARON warm start is counted in times is not stated (p. 5); in Table 5, wastewater04m1 under balance shows a 17.10% gap at 1,221 s, yet Table 1 counts one balance instance solved in (1000, 3600] at 1,221.00 s (pp. 7, 11); the binary-search iteration limit is not reported; the paper itself contains a typo in obj_R's definition (≤ for ≥, p. 3).

**Method links**:
