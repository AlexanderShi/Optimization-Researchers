### S071 · On obtaining the convex hull of quadratic inequalities via aggregations (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2022, doi:10.1137/21m1428583 / arXiv:2106.12629)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-25, all sections and counterexample proofs; references skimmed). The file is arXiv v1 (23 Jun 2021), not the SIAM typeset version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Gonzalo Muñoz, Felipe Serrano · **Place in trajectory**: the QCQP-substructure line (2019-2024). It cites his own earlier aggregation and rank-one papers [7], [13], [14], [15], [22] (pp. 24-25).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Aggregation (weighted sums of constraints) is "a common approach" for getting relaxations. For linear systems Farkas makes it complete, and for two quadratics Yildiran showed the convex hull is cut out by two aggregations (p. 1). The question is whether aggregation still gives the hull for three or more quadratics (p. 1).
  - Transferable: frame a new result as the next rung of a ladder whose earlier rungs are known (linear → Farkas, two quadratics → Yildiran, three → ?). The open rung is then the paper's question.
- **D2 Core idea**: Thm 2 (p. 4). For three strict quadratic inequalities with n ≥ 3, under a positive-definite linear combination (PDLC) and a non-trivial hull, conv(S) is the intersection of aggregations S_λ with at most one negative eigenvalue. The engine is a new *symmetric* three-quadratic S-lemma (Lemma 1, p. 3). The existing three-quadratic S-lemma is "asymmetrical" and does not give it directly (p. 3). Compared with Yildiran: same high-level route, simpler proof that "essentially only uses the S-lemma", but a weaker result. PDLC is needed and Ω may be infinite (pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: when the classical lemma exists only in a form that does not fit your proof, prove the variant you need, and say plainly what the simpler route costs (pp. 3-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Open (strict) case: n ≥ 3, PDLC, conv(S) ≠ Rⁿ (p. 4). Closed case (Thm 3, pp. 7-8) adds "no low-dimensional components" T ⊆ cl(int T). The authors admit this condition "is not easy to verify" (p. 9) and call PDLC "quite restrictive" (p. 6), "very restrictive" (p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) Proof of the S-lemma (pp. 10-11): SDP duality makes the primal SDP unbounded, so a PSD X̂ exists with ⟨Q_i, X̂⟩ < 0. PDLC makes the face G bounded, and a case split on dim G picks Barvinok's bounded rank-reduction theorem or Pataki's theorem to get a rank-1 point. (ii) Hull theorem (pp. 12-15): take a hyperplane separating x̂, homogenize it, show S^h misses it (Lemma 2), restrict the quadratics to the hyperplane (Lemma 3), and apply Lemma 1. Yildiran's semi-convex-cone characterization (Thm 7, p. 12) then gives a "faux-separating hyperplane", and Cauchy interlacing gives ν(S_λ) ≤ 1 (p. 14). (iii) Closed case (pp. 15-16): the Modaresi-Vielma lemma plus a new Lemma 5 on interiors of *infinite* intersections; the authors note that [17] did not need it because its intersections were finite (p. 15). (iv) Counterexamples, proved formally (pp. 17-24): four quadratics, adapted from the S-lemma survey's example, with boundedness proved by orthant-wise eigenvalue analysis and the box [-8, 8]³ (pp. 17-18), and an LP infeasibility certificate "using exact arithmetic" (p. 19). PDLC failing, shown with a polytope slicing argument (pp. 20-21). SDP not tight even though aggregations give the hull (pp. 21-22). Infinitely many aggregations needed once PDLC fails, with an explicit family λᵃ and an ellipse-complement argument (pp. 22-24).
  - Transferable: an "SDP duality + rank reduction" template for S-lemma-type statements. Choose between Pataki and Barvinok by a dimension case split, and get the boundedness Barvinok needs from a PD combination (pp. 10-11).
  - Transferable: when a numerical tool cannot certify a nonconvex claim, prove a loose but simple bound analytically and say so (the box is "by no means optimal", p. 18). Give the residual LP certificate with approximate coefficients, and leave the exact check to the reader (p. 19).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: No experimental section. Computation serves the theory. Figures plot S and conv(S) (Figs 1-4, pp. 6-9). "Our numerical experiments indicate" the true box is [-1.23, 1.23]³ (p. 18). The search for an example with PDLC and infinitely many aggregations found none: "In all our experiments, we have not discovered an example" (p. 8).
- **D6 Positioning**: Follows Yildiran explicitly: "we follow closely the presentation style in Yildiran [28] and follow a similar high-level proof strategy" (p. 1). The closed case follows Modaresi-Vielma [17] (p. 7). It sets itself apart from SDP-tightness results (Wang-Kılınç-Karzan [25]) with Example 2: the hull comes from aggregations while the SDP is not tight (pp. 2, 8, 21-22). It places itself in the one-quadratic-over-polytope and two-quadratic literature (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Sections 2-3 give all results, examples, counterexamples and open questions *before* the proofs (Sections 4-7; outline p. 2). Theorems are restated before their proofs "To aid the reader" (p. 10). The demarcation is itself the advertised contribution (pp. 2, 5). Hedging is specific: "we currently do not know if we require only a finite subset of Ω" (p. 5).
  - Transferable: put each counterexample next to the hypothesis it shows to be needed, in the main-results section, and move the formal proofs to the end (pp. 5-8, 17-24).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory only. Open questions (p. 9): whether Ω is finite under PDLC, relaxing the no-low-dimensional-components condition, and the role of PDLC (Prop 4 shows the hull can still come from aggregations without it). Four or more constraints are "unlikely" to work "unless further structure is considered" (p. 9).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (aggregation of several quadratics convexified jointly; step 5 "test the cheap practice against the hull", pp. 1-5). Method 4 ✅ (strong evidence: a counterexample for each dropped hypothesis and for the next size up, pp. 5-9, 17-24; step 7, the simple rigorous box, p. 18). Method 1 ⚠ (variant: the "practice" explained is a cutting-plane technique, and the paper asks when it is complete rather than why it works in solvers, p. 1). New-pattern candidate: "prove the lemma variant your proof needs" (symmetric S-lemma, p. 3).
**Transferable techniques**:
- An SDP-duality-plus-rank-reduction proof of S-lemma variants, using Barvinok's bounded theorem when a PD combination exists (pp. 10-11).
- Separating hyperplane → homogenize → restrict to a subspace → apply the S-lemma: a reusable route from "hull" statements to "infeasibility" statements (pp. 12-14).
- For a hypothesis that looks restrictive, give both a counterexample without it and an example that survives without it (Props 3 and 4, pp. 7-9). This shows the hypothesis is sufficient but not necessary.
**Quotes**:
- "We also show, via examples, that this result provides a reasonable demarcation of conditions that allow obtaining the convex hull of quadratic constraints via aggregated constraints." (p. 2)
- "We just focus on a box whose validity proof is simple and rigorous." (p. 18)

### S098 · Exploiting instance and variable similarity to improve learning-enhanced branching (arXiv preprint 2022, arXiv:2208.10028)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-16; references skimmed). The file is arXiv v1 (21 Aug 2022).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Xiaoyi Gu (first author, Georgia Tech student), Álinson S. Xavier, Feng Qiu (Argonne) · **Place in trajectory**: the learning-for-branching line with Argonne power-systems collaborators. It cites his B&B theory papers [16]-[19] (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Operational MILPs such as SCUC must be solved within 15-30 minutes every day (p. 1). ML heuristics find good solutions, but "proving the optimality of these heuristic solutions" still needs large B&B trees (p. 2). Entry point: these instances are "significantly similar to each other". Sizes and structure are the same and only the coefficients, rhs and objective change, so variable identities persist across instances (p. 2).
  - Transferable: start from a property of the deployment setting (the same model is re-solved with new data) that generic methods ignore, and turn it into the method (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Train one ExtraTrees regressor of the strong-branching score *per variable* (ML:PV) or *per group* (per-generator, per-time, per-name) instead of one model for all variables (ML:ET of Alvarez et al.) (pp. 4-7). The single model and the per-variable model are the two extreme groupings (p. 5). What changes: model granularity and hyperparameters (smaller trees against overfitting, p. 4). What stays: the features, the regressor and the training oracle (pp. 4, 6). If a group has fewer than 10 samples, it falls back to ML:ET (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Fixed structure across instances, with A, G, c, b varying only slightly (p. 2). SCUC from UnitCommitment.jl on five European MATPOWER networks; 50 randomized variations each, 40 for training and 10 for testing; up to about 413k variables and 357k rows (pp. 5-6).
- **D4 Evaluation design** (no proofs): A textbook B&B written in Julia for "consistent training data and reproducible experimental results". It runs without cutting planes, "as they make the analysis of branching rules significantly more difficult", and is given the primal optimal value "to eliminate the influence of primal heuristics". Node selection is best-bound with plunging and the LP solver is Gurobi 9.0 (p. 5). The oracle is reliability branching RB:100:inf (p. 4). Labels are log strong-branching scores, because they span several orders of magnitude (p. 6). The B&B code is released in MIPLearn (p. 5).
  - Transferable: to isolate one solver decision, write a stripped B&B, switch off cuts, give it the optimal primal value, and compare at a fixed node budget against the expensive oracle (pp. 5, 8).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Model accuracy by 5-fold cross-validated MSE, shown for one network only ("We omit other networks, since they presented similar results", p. 8). Relative MIP gap after 1,000 nodes, with the RB oracle (Table 4, p. 8): ML:PV averages 0.40% against 0.54% for ML:ET and 0.31% for RB:100:inf, "only about 22% worse than RB:100:inf" (p. 10). After 10,000 nodes, without RB (Table 6, p. 11), ML:PV is 0.29% against 0.45%. Fallback rate as a coverage diagnostic: 9.0% for ML:PV (Table 5, p. 10). Robustness on test instances presolved by Gurobi, with no presolve on training instances (Tables 7-8, pp. 11-12). Not uniform: on case3375wp the per-generator model wins and ML:PV is close to ML:ET (Tables 4 and 6, pp. 8, 11). Running time is deliberately not reported (p. 8).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on and modifies Alvarez-Louveaux-Wehenkel [4], with ML:ET described as "an accurate implementation" to the best of their ability (p. 6). Graph-network approaches [23] have an advantage that is "much less clear" when structure is fixed. GPU strong branching [39] is called orthogonal (p. 12). Branching theory comes from his own group's lower bounds and random-IP results (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Framed as a "simple modification" or "simple enhancement" (pp. 1-2). The labelling cost is played down: "require negligible manual labeling effort" (p. 2). The decision not to time is justified by assertion: "self-evident, in our opinion" (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: An algorithmic and empirical contribution. The authors name the accuracy-versus-coverage trade-off (p. 10). Future work (pp. 12-13): carry the idea to other ML branching methods, train on small instances and test on large ones (for example a shorter SCUC horizon), integrate with cuts and primal heuristics, and try other operational problems (OTS). Limits not stated by the authors: a single problem class (SCUC), no wall-clock evidence, and MSE shown for one network.

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (stripped instrument where only the branching rule varies, with the optimal primal value given and the expensive oracle as ceiling, p. 5; result reported as distance to the oracle, p. 10). Method 2 ⚠ (variant: the overhead count of the taste quick-check is skipped and per-node cost is declared "self-evident", p. 8. This is the Heuristic 6 tension already recorded in SKILL.md [03 C5]). New-pattern candidate: "recurring operational instances as the problem source" (also D004 p. 2).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Per-entity or per-group surrogate models when the same model is re-solved with new data, with a general model as fallback and the fallback rate reported (pp. 4-7, 10).
- Regress the log of strong-branching scores, which span orders of magnitude (p. 6).
- Test robustness to a pipeline step the training data never saw (presolve on test instances only), feeding original-problem static features at inference (p. 11).
**Quotes**:
- "We do not focus on total running time since this metric depends heavily on the quality of the software implementation, instead of simply on the fundamental properties of the branching rule." (p. 8)
- "It is also self-evident, in our opinion, that all evaluated ML-based branching rules, if carefully implemented, would require significantly less computational effort per node than strong branching" (p. 8)

### D003 · Branch-and-bound solves random binary IPs in poly(n)-time (Math. Program. 200(1) 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-022-01895-4 / arXiv:2007.15192)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-15; references skimmed). The file is arXiv v4 (16 Sep 2021), which describes itself as the post-SODA revision: "a significant improvement over [16]" (p. 4). It is not the Springer typeset version. The file is byte-identical to the S031 file. The text layer has lost its fi/ff/tt/Th ligatures ("rst", "aempts", "ese").
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Yatharth Dubey (student), Marco Molinaro · **Place in trajectory**: the start of the "B&B on random instances" programme (2020), which follows the sparse-cut and cut-selection lines (SKILL Heuristic 4).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: All solvers use variable branching and see small trees in practice, yet the authors say little attention has gone to why (p. 1). Worst-case trees are exponential (p. 2). Entry point: "to avoid worst-case lower bounds, a standard idea is to consider random instances", by analogy with smoothed analysis of simplex (p. 2). The model is a packing IP max cx, Ax ≤ b, x ∈ {0,1}ⁿ with A and c i.i.d. uniform on [0,1], m fixed, and b_j = β_j n with β_j < 1/2 (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when the worst case is provably exponential and practice disagrees, move to a random-instance model that keeps the *algorithm* as practised. Reject models that need a different algorithm (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two steps. (1) Deterministic: with best-bound node selection, the tree has at most 2|G|n + 1 nodes, where G is the set of 0/1 points whose reduced-cost gap Δ_rc is at most the integrality gap (Thm 3, p. 7). (2) Probabilistic: for random instances |G| ≤ n^{O(m)}. The integrality gap is O(log²n/n) (Dyer-Frieze, Thm 2, p. 5), and few columns lie close to the Lagrangian hyperplane H (Thm 4, p. 11). Main result: tree size n^{ā₁(m+α log m)} with probability ≥ 1 − 1/n − 2^{−αā₂}, for *any* choice of fractional variable to branch on (Thm 1, pp. 2-3).
  - Transferable: separate "tree size ≤ f(number of near-optimal solutions)", which holds for every instance, from "the number of near-optimal solutions is small", which is where randomness enters (pp. 6-7, 15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Best-bound node selection, variable branching with any fractional variable (adversarial choice allowed), and only pruning by bound used in the count. The authors argue that each of these means real trees are smaller (p. 3). m fixed, uniform data, packing form (p. 2). They state that the bounds extend to "well-behaved" deterministic IPs (p. 3, §7).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Best-bound never branches on a node whose LP value is below IP* (Lemma 1, pp. 4-5). A vertex has at most m fractional coordinates (Lemma 2, p. 5). LP duality gives Δ_rc(x^N) ≤ IPGap for every branched node (Lemma 3, p. 7). Each internal node is mapped to a good point by rounding its LP solution to minimize the affine Δ_rc (Lemma 4, p. 7). A lowest-common-ancestor argument makes the map at most n-to-1 (Lemma 5, pp. 7-8). Δ_rc(x) is at least the sum, over items where x differs from x*, of the column's distance to H (Lemma 6, p. 8), and columns are bucketed by distance in powers of 2 for counting (Lemma 7, p. 10). H depends on the data, so a *uniform* slab bound over all hyperplanes is proved with an ε-net of the sphere, Bernstein's inequality, Ball's cube-slicing bound and a union bound (Thm 4, pp. 11-13). The authors write that VC-type bounds were not multiplicative enough (p. 11).
  - Transferable: if a key object (here H via λ*) depends on the random data, prove the concentration statement uniformly over a net of all candidate objects instead of conditioning (pp. 11-13).
  - Transferable: to bound a tree, charge each node to a combinatorial witness and show the charging is injective along root paths only (Lemmas 4-5, pp. 7-8).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: none.
- **D6 Positioning**: Lenstra-style and Pataki-Tural-Wong basis reduction solves random packing IPs but relies on lattice basis reduction, which the authors say is heavy machinery rarely used in practice (p. 3). Lueker, Goldberg-Marchetti-Spaccamela, Beier-Vöcking and Dyer-Frieze use custom enumeration "not equivalent to the general purpose branch-and-bound" (p. 3). Follow-ups are cited: Borst et al. (Gaussian data, n^{poly(m)}) and Frieze (exponential trees for ATSP) (p. 3). Daniel Dadush is thanked for "suggesting several improvements" (p. 15).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Claims, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first such result for a standard version of branch-and-bound (p. 1). The practical meaning is stated as "an indication" (p. 1) or "compelling indication" (p. 3), never as a full explanation. The Theorem 1 discussion (p. 3) lists why practice should do better than the theorem: a good variable rule, other pruning rules. A version note states what changed from SODA: simpler proofs and a sharper Corollary 3, better by a factor O(n^m) (p. 4).
  - Transferable: write a version paragraph that says what changed from the conference paper and by how much (p. 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory. Corollary 3 (p. 15) is a deterministic bound for any 0/1 IP in terms of the integrality gap and how many columns crowd near a hyperplane, which defines "well-behaved". Limits as stated or visible: m fixed (the exponent grows with m), uniform packing data, best-bound only, and no lower-bound or tightness discussion. No future-work section.

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (solver practice, variable branching with small trees, turned into a proxy theorem on random instances; step 5 chooses random instances because the worst case is exponential, pp. 1-3). Method 4 ⚠ (variant: no tight example and no counterexample for dropped hypotheses inside the paper. The boundary sits in follow-up work cited on p. 3, the ATSP exponential lower bound). Method 1 step 7 ✅ ("how much is explained", stated as "indication" with the reasons why practice is even smaller, p. 3). New-pattern candidate: "deterministic charging bound, randomness only in the count" (pp. 6-7, 15).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Map each B&B node to a near-optimal integer point. Best-bound bounds the reduced-cost gap of every branched node by IPGap (Lemmas 1, 3-5, pp. 4-8).
- Use an ε-net, Bernstein and a union bound to get uniform multiplicative slab counts when the slab depends on the data (pp. 11-13).
- State the deterministic corollary separately, so that readers can plug in other instance distributions (Corollary 3, p. 15).
**Quotes**:
- "We believe that this result provides an indication as to why branch-and-bound with variable branching works so well in practice." (p. 1)
- "We note that most of the above arguments hold not only for random problems but actually for every 0/1 IP." (p. 15)

### D004 · Sensitivity Analysis for Mixed Binary Quadratic Programming (IPCO 2024, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-59835-7_33 / arXiv:2312.06714)
**Read level**: full for the file read (pp. 1-43; references skimmed; appendices A-B read). The file is arXiv v2 (7 May 2025), the extended journal-length version: the first-page footnote says parts were published in the IPCO 2024 proceedings (p. 1). It is byte-identical to the S067 file. The IPCO 2024 proceedings text itself was not read, so which parts are in the 15-page IPCO version is not verified.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Diego Cifuentes, Jingye Xu (student, corresponding author); the three authors "contributed equally" (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the operational recurring-instance motivation (cites S098 as [5], p. 37) meets the copositive and conic duality line (later the decomposable Lagrangian dual [34]).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Operational MBQPs are re-solved with the same constraint matrix and a changing rhs (p. 2). Solvers find good primal solutions quickly but struggle with "matching dual bounds" (p. 2; Appendix A: Gurobi is within 1.6% of the best primal value in 60 s, while its own dual gap stays above 10% at 1200 s, p. 40). Classical IP sensitivity (Cook et al.) gives "trivial bounds" for binaries because it depends on an ∞-norm that is constant on 0/1 vectors (p. 2). Entry point: the copositive dual of Burer's completely-positive reformulation gives rhs-parametric dual bounds by weak duality (eq. 4, pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: when the solver's weak side is the dual bound and instances recur, ask which convex reformulation's dual solution can be reused across rhs changes (pp. 2, 4-5).
- **D2 Core idea**: (1) Approximating the change Δz is NP-hard even when z(b) is known (Thm 1, p. 3). (2) A strong-duality map (Thm 3, p. 5): strong duality holds when P is bounded or Q ⪰ 0; there is a closed-form ε-optimal dual; a gap example exists when neither holds; and the dual optimum is not attained in general. (3) The ε-optimal dual set is never a singleton. Adding the block KK_i keeps optimality but lowers the predicted bound by (Δb_i)², so the *choice* of dual solution matters (Prop 5, Remark 3, pp. 9-10). (4) An algorithm: search the ε-optimal face for a dual with small KK_i weight. The cone COP is replaced by S₊ + S_P (DNN), McCormick columns and a free l are added, and the weights are tuned to maximize the average predicted bound over a target Δb range (eqs. 9-12, pp. 10-12).
  - Transferable: when dual solutions are non-unique and are used downstream, optimize a secondary objective inside the ε-optimal dual face, aimed at the downstream use (pp. 9-12).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MBQP in equality standard form with x ≥ 0 and assumption (A): the linear constraints imply 0 ≤ x_j ≤ 1 for binaries, "without any loss of generality" via slacks (p. 4). Rational data (p. 2). The strong-duality cases depend on P bounded or Q PSD (p. 5). Local stability (Thm 4, p. 7) is the technical hinge. Remark 2 shows it fails when Q is not PSD and P is unbounded (p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Hardness (pp. 16-18): a one-unit rhs change in an edge-colouring IP on cubic graphs moves z by 1 exactly when the graph is 3-edge-colourable (Vizing and Holyer). A two-sided (Γ₁, Γ₂) approximation notion is defined so that an algorithm cannot "cheat" (p. 3). Part (a) (pp. 18-19): H = T + Σ AA_i is strictly copositive under bounded P, which gives a Slater point. Local stability (pp. 19-23): the perturbed problem is split into finitely many polyhedral pieces, Vavasis's characterization of QP optima makes the value function piecewise quadratic, and Lipschitz continuity gives the linear lower bound. Part (b) (pp. 23-33): the dual is built from penalty "building blocks" KK_i = (b_i t − a_iᵀx)² and G_j (binary penalty), which "behave like augmented Lagrangian penalties" (p. 24). A three-case split (far from P / near P and near-binary / far from binary) proves copositivity (pp. 29-30). Strictly positive constants come from small LPs and a Charnes-Cooper linear-fractional LP (pp. 25-27). Part (d): the copositive dual for stable set on the 6-clique is not attained, shown with a sequence of test vectors y₁, y₊, y₋, y₂ (pp. 33-36).
  - Transferable: to show a conic dual is attained only in the limit, prove that any attaining matrix fails copositivity on a few hand-built nonnegative test vectors, with ε → 0 sandwiching (pp. 34-36).
  - Transferable: get the positive constants a penalty proof needs ("there exists h > 0") from the optimal value of an auxiliary LP whose zero value would contradict the standing assumption (Remarks 4-5, Lemma 2, pp. 25-27).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Five generated classes: COMB (stable set with cardinality), KBQP (BQP library, n = 100), SSLP and SSQP (on/off continuous variables), and PACK (n = 100, m = 10, with b reset to Ax* − e because random rhs was "likely to be insensitive", p. 13) (pp. 12-13). Baselines are the duals of Shor1, Shor2 (Shor plus redundant quadratics and McCormick) and Cont (continuous relaxation) (pp. 13-14). The metric is the relative gap to the ground truth z(b+Δb), normalized by Shor1 (p. 14). Hardware and software: Julia 1.9, Gurobi 9.0.2, Mosek 10.1, Windows PC with a 12th-gen i7 and "16 RAM" as printed (p. 13). A weight-sensitivity sweep over σ is included (Table 1, p. 15). Scalability: 4¹⁰ rhs bounds in about 10 s, against solving directly, which is "expected to take more than one day" (an estimate, not measured, p. 16). Check against the text: the claim "our method provides the tightest sensitivity bounds in all cases" (p. 14) does not hold at Δk = 1 for COMB. There "Our method" is 1.28, worse than Shor1 (= 1) and Cont (0.90) (Table 1, p. 15; also Tables 8-10, pp. 40-41). Table 2 is labelled "SBQP" while the class is called KBQP (pp. 12, 15). The Tables 17-19 rows carry two values for "Our method" (pp. 42-43).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on Burer's CPP reformulation [14]. The shadow-price use comes from Guo-Bodur-Taylor [15] (p. 2). Part (a) is credited to [17] (non-constructive, via [18]); for parts (b), (c) and (d) the authors write "To the best of our understanding" that they "were not known before" (p. 5). The IP sensitivity literature (Cook et al. [8], [9]-[13]) is shown to be inadequate for binaries (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Section 2 gives all results with proof sketches, and Sections 3-4 give the full proofs (p. 3). A notation table tracks the many constants (Table 6, p. 18). Each part of Thm 3 is followed by a remark on why it cannot be improved: "part (b) of Theorem 3 is the best we can hope for" (p. 5), and "the sufficient conditions for local stability Theorem 4 cannot be further relaxed" (p. 8). Computations are called "preliminary" (abstract, p. 12). A data-backed claim in the introduction is moved to an appendix table (App. A, p. 40).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Complexity, duality theory, and an algorithm with preliminary computations. Stated limits: instance size is "limited by the SDP solver"; there is no theoretical guarantee for replacing COP by S₊ + S_P (p. 36); the closed-form constants are "rather loose" (p. 28). Future work (pp. 36-37): strong duality for generalized copositive reformulations of QCQPs, scalable SDP solvers, and cutting-plane methods for (3) that use the part (b) structure.
