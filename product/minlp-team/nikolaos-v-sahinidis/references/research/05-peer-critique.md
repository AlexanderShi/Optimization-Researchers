## A. Critiques by theme, with the answer (if any)

### A1. "Complete but not rigorous": the interval-arithmetic school (2003–2025)

BARON computes its relaxations, bounds and feasibility tests in floating-point arithmetic, with tolerances. The interval-analysis and constraint-programming community classifies it as a complete search method that is **not mathematically rigorous**. That community includes Neumaier and the COCONUT project in Vienna, Kearfott (author of GlobSol), and the interval groups in Nantes and Toulouse.

- statuses that tell the user when the claim is weak;

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

- **C1. Ranking, 2005–2007.** Neumaier et al. (2005): "Among the currently available global solvers, BARON is the fastest and most robust one, with OQNLP being close." Pintér (2007): "Our illustrative results certainly do not justify the claim". The two tests used different test sets and different criteria.
- **C2. "Best" against "on par".** His company: "A top competitor conducted a 2023 study that showed BARON is the best MINLP solver." The competitor's paper: "a small lead of BARON before SCIP" and "SCIP's performance is currently on par with the state-of-the-art commercial solver BARON."
- **C3. The 2005 sentence as quoted.** His company: "Among the currently available solvers, BARON is the fastest and most robust one." The original: "Among the currently available global solvers, BARON is the fastest and most robust one, with OQNLP being close. None of the current global solvers is fully reliable".
- **C4. "20%-300% more problems"** (company) against the 2017 page the company links: 52 solved instances against 46/37/24/19, i.e. 13–174% more.
- **C5. Reliability on the same 200 base instances.** Mittelmann 2026 counts 0 fails for BARON. The SCIP 8 study counts 27 fails in 1,000 runs, 26 of them wrong optimal values. The runs differ in BARON version (22.9.30 against the BARON shipped with GAMS 52.3, per Mittelmann's error-file name; the version number is not shown), in permutations and in checks. Both counts stand.
- **C6. "Cannot be trusted" against what BARON claimed.** Vanaret: BARON's results "cannot be trusted". In his own table, BARON's status was "locally optimal", not a global claim, for the very case he cites.
- **C7. Reduced space against full space within one paper.** Bongartz & Mitsos: the reduced space "can be as fast as or even faster". The same paper reports that BARON solved one case in a single iteration and was considerably faster in Case Study III when maximizing net power.
- **C8. Polyhedral stance.** 2005: NLP codes are "significantly slower and numerically unstable". 2018: "a new relaxation paradigm" combining polyhedral and nonlinear relaxations. This is dated, not resolved; see also 01 C8.
- **C9. The 2020 concession and ALAMO.** His group (2020): "avoid unregularized subset-selection algorithms ... because of their poor performance on noisy data". ALAMO's core is best-subset selection, which his group describes as "penalized" (8d). Whether ALAMO's information-criterion penalty counts as "regularized" in the 2020 sense is not settled by anything I read. [inferred tension]
- **C10. ALAMO success, as quoted.** His company: "100% success rate ... outperformed neural networks ... while delivering superior speed and efficiency". The cited study's abstract: "faster at the cost of minor solution error, while the implicit formulation provides exact solutions with similar solve time."
- **C11. Fastest overall, but not on the easy shared subset.** Mittelmann 2026: BARON has the best overall shifted mean (82.5 s). On the 52 instances that all four published solvers solved, SHOT is faster (1.1 s against 2.5 s). The rankings depend on the subset.

## Implications for Phase 2 (for the synthesizer; all [inferred])

- When the skill speaks for BARON's guarantees, it should say "global within tolerances, not mathematically rigorous" and point to the numerically-sensitive status and the missing-bounds warning. The interval school's critique is accurate on its own terms, and he never disputed it.
- When the skill cites benchmark standing, it should pair his group's self-benchmarks with third-party numbers (SCIP 8, Mittelmann, Kronqvist), and never with the company page's wording.
- For structured classes (BoxQP, AC-OPF, GDP, scenario problems, multilinear terms), the skill should expect a tailored method to beat a general solver. In his voice, the advice is to tighten bounds and reformulate before asking for a new algorithm.
- His craft of handling critique is distillable: fix and disclose, absorb the rival's technique, document the limit, cooperate with testers, report losses as editor. The weak spot is the gap between his company's voice and his academic voice.
- For DFO advice, state the scope: bound-constrained problems, small dimensions ("less than about a dozen" degrees of freedom), and rankings that age.
- For ALAMO, flag the accuracy-for-tractability trade, a modest number of inputs ("fewer than a few dozen"), and noise-sensitive subset selection.
