### S097 · Baron (Branch and reduce optimization navigator, user's manual, WWW document 2005, no DOI)

**Read level**: partial. Version read: **v4.0**, the BARON User's Manual Version 4.0 (Version 1.0 of 2 March 1995, Version 4.0 of 29 March 1999, revision of 28 June 2000), sole author line N. V. Sahinidis, UIUC (p. 1); the 2005 edition with Tawarmalani that the Scholar entry dates is not in the file. Read in full: contents and lists (pp. 2-6), ch. 1 introduction, history and acknowledgments (pp. 7-9), ch. 2 algorithmic overview with both worked examples (pp. 10-35), ch. 3 system features (pp. 36-41), chs. 4-7 requirements, installation, options and the core component's user exits (pp. 42-61), ch. 9 parser (pp. 96-102) and the bibliography (pp. 103-105). Skimmed: ch. 8 module input examples, §8.1-8.2 (pp. 62-66), the start of §8.10 (pp. 86-88), the end of §8.11 and §8.12 (pp. 92-95). Not read: §§8.3-8.9 (pp. 66-86), the rest of §8.10 and most of §8.11 (pp. 88-92).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none on the v4.0 title page (p. 1); seventeen students credited in the acknowledgments, Tawarmalani singled out (pp. 8-9); Overton and Wilkins (NYU) thanked for the mixed-integer SDP module (p. 9) · **Position in trajectory**: a free UIUC distribution, downloadable from archimedes.scs.uiuc.edu (pp. 8, 44), five years before the firm-era GAMS manual (S185, p. 3); the only first-hand statement in this batch of the 1995-96 branch-and-reduce method, whose papers SKILL.md lists as not read.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: nonconvex problems with several local optima arise across engineering and the sciences, listed by application (p. 7). BARON's name is explained as interval analysis and duality in a *reduce* arsenal combined with branch and bound (p. 7). The history ties each version to the applications it was first used on: engineering design, just-in-time manufacturing, chip layout, process planning, pooling, fractional and multiplicative programs (pp. 7-8).
- **D2 Core idea**: branch-and-reduce. Reduction tests run at every node in pre- and post-processing to contract the box and the relaxation gap, and a node is solved again when range reduction is significant (pp. 19-20; flow chart Fig. 2.6, p. 21; Example 2, pp. 32, 34). Three families: optimality-based reduction from relaxation duals and the incumbent U, with rows such as x_j >= x_j^U - (U - L)/lambda_j (Tables 2.1-2.2, pp. 15-17); probing of variables that sit at neither bound (pp. 15-16); feasibility-based *poor man's LPs*, which use one constraint and one bound at a time in place of the 2n bound LPs, which are solved once at the root (pp. 17-19). Lower bounds come from McCormick factorable relaxations that are exact at the bounds (pp. 20-24). Branching is by violation, with periodic bisection for exhaustiveness and branching at the incumbent for finiteness (p. 24).
  - Transferable: tabulate a family of bound-tightening rules as active constraint / requirement / valid inequality (Tables 2.1-2.2, pp. 16-17), so that each rule's precondition (sign of the multiplier, an extra probing solve) is explicit.
- **D3 Assumptions**: finite variable bounds are inputs of the core (p. 56). The duality-based tests need a convex relaxation solved by a method that returns duals (p. 20). The factorable module accepts exp, ln, x^alpha and beta^x only (p. 41). The optimality-based inequalities may cut off feasible points but no point better than U (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis devices** (brief): value-function argument: a Lagrange multiplier defines a supporting hyperplane of the perturbation function, and its intersection with the level U bounds the variable (pp. 12, 15). A constructed case where the poor man's LPs improve no bound at all (Fig. 2.5.f), with the remark that it cannot arise after the root LPs (p. 19). Finiteness through exhaustive bisection every few iterations and through incumbent branching (p. 24).
  - Transferable: when a cheap heuristic replaces an exact subproblem, draw the case where it fails completely and say where in the algorithm that case is excluded (p. 19).
- **D5 Experiments**: no benchmark, two traced examples. Example 1 (min -x1 - x2 with x1x2 <= 4): the separable relaxation needs 5 nodes, the factorable relaxation solves it at the root, marginals plus probing prove optimality without branching, incumbent branching needs 3 nodes instead of 5 (pp. 25-31). Example 2 (a concave QP): bisection branch and bound 17 iterations, branch-and-reduce 7 (p. 32).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: a software contribution, shipped as a compiled library (libbaron.a) with a parser (libbarin.a), free for non-commercial use (pp. 8, 44-45), usable at defaults since every option has one (p. 46). Eleven specialized modules sit beside a factorable NLP module that covers them all but does not exploit their structure (pp. 36-41). Stated limits: FP runs only under OSL, few NLP and SDP interfaces, other interfaces left to future plans (p. 43). Defaults in v4.0: absolute tolerance 10^-6, relative 0 (p. 46); one local search in preprocessing (p. 48); probing off (pdo 0), at most 10 reduction passes before a new relaxation and 5 relaxations per node (pp. 49-50); a linear relaxation in the NLP module but a quadratic one in the IQP module (p. 53); a CPU-time breakdown file written by default (p. 51).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the method as stated in the manual: reductions at every node in pre- and post-processing, with the relaxation rebuilt while reduction is significant (pp. 19-21; maxredpass and maxnodepass, p. 50). Cheap reductions come first and the exact bound LPs only once at the root (pp. 17-19; prelpdo, p. 52). The expensive probing is rationed: off by default in v4.0 (p. 49), and only feasibility-based tightening runs in preprocessing (p. 50). Reductions scale with U - L (Table 2.1, p. 16; Example 1, pp. 30-31).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: one code base grown version by version, each applied to new classes, with the students credited (pp. 7-9). It went from 1800 GAMS lines (1991-93) to about 10,000 FORTRAN 77 lines (1994-95), then about 23,000 F90 plus 10,000 C lines (v3.0) and about 26,000 F90 plus 17,000 C lines (v4.0). Every reduction family has its own switch (tdo, mdo, lbttdo, obttdo, pdo; p. 49) and the per-component time file is on by default (times, p. 51).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the factorable NLP module allows an entirely LP-based approach and uses a linear relaxation by default (pp. 8, 53).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: the IQP module defaults to a quadratic relaxation (qplin 0, p. 53) and may call the QP solvers of OSL, MINOS or SNOPT (p. 43). In 2000 the LP default did not hold for quadratic programs.
- Heuristic 1 ✅ evidence: special classes as separate modules (separable concave, power economies of scale, fixed charge, fractional, polynomial, multiplicative, indefinite QP, MILP, mixed-integer SDP) beside the general module (pp. 36-41). The 1994-95 code already had specialized concave-minimization algorithms (pp. 7-8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Teach the algorithm inside the manual on a two-variable instance, rerun it under each device (relaxation type, reduction on or off, branching rule) and count nodes (pp. 25-31).
- Expose every component of the search as a user exit with a fixed signature (range reduction, lower bounding, feasibility test, random start, branching, local search, objective), so that a new problem class reuses the core (pp. 55-61).
- Write a CPU-time breakdown per component on every run (times option, p. 51; logs on pp. 65-66, 94), so that the cost of each device can be read from ordinary runs.

**Quotes**:
- "As the emphasis is on range reduction, we refer to the resulting algorithm as a branch and reduce algorithm." (p. 19)
- "A linear relaxation is used by default." (p. 53)

### S185 · Gams (BARON Solver Manual 2005, no DOI)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-13). The file is the BARON chapter of the GAMS solver manuals, dated 31 March 2005, with a log from BARON v. 7.2.5 (pp. 1, 3).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Mohit Tawarmalani, Purdue (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: BARON as a GAMS solver after the firm: the log names BARON a product of The Optimization Firm, LLC, with parts created at UIUC (p. 3), and a GAMS/BARON license plus a licensed LP solver are required (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: GAMS users who need global solutions of NLP and MINLP. Traditional NLP and MINLP algorithms converge only under convexity assumptions; BARON's branch-and-bound is guaranteed under fairly general assumptions, the first being finite bounds on variables and their expressions (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: branch-and-bound enhanced with constraint propagation and duality for range reduction (p. 2), exposed through options. All four reduction families are on by default, plus probing on all branching variables at every third level (tdo, mdo, lbttdo, obttdo, pdo -1, pfreq 3; pp. 9-10). A node gets up to 10 reduction passes before a new relaxation and up to 5 relaxations (maxredpass, maxnodepass; p. 10). The relaxation is an LP by default, with outer approximators for convex functions (nlpdolin 1, nouter1, noutpervar, noutiter, outgrid; p. 9). Branching-variable and branching-point rules are dynamic by default (p. 10). Several best solutions come from one search tree instead of integer cuts (numsol, isoltol; pp. 5-6, 9). Constraints can be classed as relaxation-only or convex (equclass; p. 12).
- **D3 Assumptions (the model contract)**: every nonlinear variable and expression must be finitely bounded, and finite variable bounds do not imply finite expression bounds (1/x on [0, 1]). If the preprocessor cannot infer the bounds, globality is not guaranteed, and BARON may stop when no numerically stable lower bounding problem can be built (p. 2). The allowed functions are exp, ln, x^alpha, beta^x, x^y and |x|, with no trigonometric functions (p. 2). Bounds can also be given as solver bounds or expression bounds in the option file (pp. 7-8).
- **D4**: n/a (no analysis; option semantics only).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. One annotated log (gear.gms) splits time into parsing, preprocessing, navigating, relaxed, local, tightening, marginals and probing (pp. 3-4).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: documents the contract with the user: each termination message with the action it calls for. Numerical difficulties are to be reported to the developers; *Search Terminated by BARON* means missing bounds and an answer that may not be global (pp. 4-5). Default tolerances are epsa 1e-9 and epsr 0.1, the latter equal to GAMS optcr (p. 8). *Normal Completion* means solved within tolerances (p. 4), which at these defaults allows U - L up to 0.1|L| (my reading of the definitions on p. 8). No future work is stated.
- **Cross-edition observation (S097 v4.0 against S185)**: probing went from off by default (S097 v4.0 p. 49) to on for all branching variables at every third level (pp. 9-10). Local searches in preprocessing went from 1 to 10 (S097 v4.0 p. 48; p. 11), and the default relative tolerance from 0 to 0.1 (S097 v4.0 p. 46; p. 8). The LP relaxation default stayed (S097 v4.0 p. 53; p. 9). The manuals give no reasons for these changes.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: reduction is the default node operation. Its passes and relaxation rebuilds are capped (maxredpass, maxnodepass) and probing is scheduled by tree depth (pstart, pend, pfreq) (pp. 9-10). The preprocessor reduces ranges even in multi-start use (p. 6). The edition also supports the limitation line of Method 1: the guarantee rests on finite expression bounds (p. 2).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: each reduction family can be switched off (tdo, mdo, lbttdo, obttdo, pdo = 0; pp. 9-10; the same list for multi-start sampling, p. 6) and the log splits time by component (p. 3). These are the switches and the instrumentation an ablation needs.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the relaxation is an LP by default, and convex functions enter as outer-approximating cuts with set counts and rounds (p. 9). Constraints declared convex become cutting planes from supporting hyperplanes (p. 12).
- Heuristic 2 ✅ evidence: the manual calls physically meaningful bounds on all variables the best way to give bounds (p. 7). Relaxation-only constraints, for instance first-order optimality conditions, are passed apart from the model (p. 12).
- Heuristic 3 ✅ evidence: numerical difficulties are to be reported to the developers (p. 4).
- Heuristic 7 ✅ evidence: missing bounds void the global guarantee and are reported as such (pp. 2, 5), and the multi-start mode is titled a heuristic solver, offered for insight into local solutions (p. 6). The explicit sentence SKILL.md quotes for Heuristic 7 is not in this edition.

**Transferable techniques**:
- In a solver's documentation, list each termination status with what it means for the guarantee and what the user should do next (pp. 4-5).
- Give the solver a preprocessing-only mode (maxiter 0), and document which reduction switches to set to zero to sample local minima without range reduction (p. 6).
- Keep a function's argument away from its singularity with an expression bound in the option file (z = x - y with z.lo 0.00001 for log(z)) instead of editing the model (pp. 7-8).
- Read the default relative tolerance before calling a run global: epsr is 0.1 at these defaults (p. 8).
- Compare the option tables of successive manual editions to date when a component became a default (probing: off in S097 v4.0 p. 49, every third level here, pp. 9-10).

**Quotes**:
- "If this step fails, global optimality of the solutions provided is not guaranteed." (p. 2)
- "Maximum number of times range reduction is performed at a node before a new relaxation is constructed." (p. 10)

### S198 · Polynomial-time algorithms for the integer minimal principle for centrosymmetric structures (Acta Crystallographica Section A: Foundations of Crystallography 2005, doi:10.1107/s010876730501648x)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-9: abstract, §§1-6, Tables 1-6, acknowledgments, references).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Anastasia Vaia (first author); Sahinidis corresponding (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: second step of the crystallography line on a joint NSF/NIGMS mathematical-biology award (NIH GM072023, pp. 8-9): from the integer programming formulation of Vaia and Sahinidis 2003 (p. 2) to this reduction, and on to the triplet sieve in SnB 2.3 (S161).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: the integer minimal principle for centrosymmetric structures (Vaia and Sahinidis 2003) yields the true global minimum, but branch-and-bound may need an exponential number of iterations, although the gap between the IP and its LP relaxation is observed to be zero (pp. 2-3). The entry is the properties of the IP's solutions seen in a large number of computations (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: the relaxation can be expected to have its minimum at tau_t = 0 for all t (positive weights, an underdetermined system), and the IP has such a minimum whenever the equations have an integer solution with tau_t = 0 (p. 3). With tau fixed at 0, the IP becomes a homogeneous linear system modulo 2 (models M2, M3), and a global solution is any nonzero vector of its null space (pp. 3-4). Two algorithms find one: binary Gauss-Jordan using only row additions, with complexity O(M^2 T), and sparse Gaussian elimination with Markowitz pivoting to save memory (pp. 4-5). The IP is needed only when the system has no nontrivial solution (p. 5).
  - Transferable: when an IP's LP bound is tight on every instance you run, look at which variables sit at a fixed value in the LP solutions, and check whether fixing them leaves an exact structured system.
- **D3 Assumptions**: centrosymmetric structures, so phases are 0 or pi and triplet cosines are -1 or 1 (p. 2); the ratio N:M:T = 1:10:100, built from the M strongest reflections (p. 3); a nonzero solution of M3 exists, argued from Sayre's equation for strong reflections (p. 5) and found on all 38 structures (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis devices** (brief): the equivalence chain M1 -> M2 -> M3 over F2 (pp. 3-4); the complexity O(M^2 T), which is O(N^3) at the standard ratio and O(N^6) if M grows like N^2 (pp. 4-5, 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: 18 structures against CPLEX 7.0 branch-and-bound give identical global minima, with PA1 and PA2 on average 34 and 45 times faster (Table 2, p. 6). 20 more structures are all solved (Table 4, p. 7). Resolution is truncated from 0.9 to 1.3 Å (Table 5, pp. 7-8). On all 38 structures PA2 gets better crystallographic R values than SHELXS at defaults (Table 6, pp. 7-8). Everything ran on one 1.5 GHz Xeon (p. 5).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: an algorithmic contribution meant to be built into crystallographic software (p. 3). Stated limits: the minimal principle with CRUNCH peak picking fails below about 1.2-1.3 Å (p. 7). SHELXS ran at defaults and could be improved by a trained user; per Sheldrick, SHELXD solves all structures but takes at least ten times as long as PA2 (p. 7). Centrosymmetric structures only; no future-work section.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the cheap inference that shrinks the search is pushed to the end. The structure removes the enumeration altogether, with no branch-and-bound (pp. 2-5), and the IP stays as the fallback when the reduced system has only the trivial solution (p. 5).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the fast method is checked against the global minima an exact solver certifies on the same 18 structures (p. 6). The developer of the domain baseline is consulted, and his assessment is reported (pp. 7, 9).
- Heuristic 4 ⚠ variant: SHELXS runs at its defaults rather than in its strongest mode. The authors say so and show one tuned rerun as an illustration (p. 7).
- new-pattern candidate: empirical regularity turned into an exact reduction (pp. 2-5).
- new-pattern candidate: global optimum as a test of the scientific model. With no false minima, the resolution at which the model fails can be identified unambiguously (p. 7).
- new-pattern candidate: linearize through discreteness of the domain. Centrosymmetry allows the trigonometric terms to be replaced by binary variables (p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Over F2, eliminate with row additions only (no multiplication or division). When rows have at most three nonzeros, switch to a sparse column-major structure with Markowitz pivots (pp. 4-5).
- Validate a fast special-purpose method against an exact general solver on the same instances: first identical optimal values, then time and memory (p. 6).
- Use a global solution to find where a scientific model breaks: degrade the data step by step and record where the certified optimum stops recovering the known answer (pp. 7-8).
- Against a domain baseline run at defaults, say that tuning could help, show one tuned run, and report what the baseline's developer says (p. 7).

**Quotes**:
- "Instead, properties of the solution of the optimization problem, as observed in a large number of computational experiments, are exploited in order to reduce the optimization formulation to a system of linear equations in the number field of two elements (F2)." (p. 2)
- "Since our algorithm provides the global solution to the minimal principle model and there is no false minima issue, we are able to identify unambiguously the resolution …" (p. 7)

### S118 · Selection, acquisition, and allocation of manufacturing technology in a multi-period environment (European Journal of Operational Research 2008, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2006.11.046)

**Read level**: full for the version read. Version read: **preprint**, a manuscript dated June 20, 2000 and titled *Selection, Acquisition, and Allocation of Manufacturing Technology in a Multi-Product Environment* (p. 1), not the EJOR 2008 text. Read: abstract, §§1-6, acknowledgments, appendix, references and Tables 1-5 (pp. 1-28). Figures 1-7 (pp. 29-32) carry no extractable data, so the plotted results were not read.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Shabbir Ahmed (UIUC Mechanical & Industrial Engineering); Sahinidis corresponding (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the capacity-planning line. It follows the LP-based approximation schemes with probabilistic analysis of Liu and Sahinidis 1997 and Ahmed and Sahinidis 1999 (p. 4) and the MILP reformulations of Sahinidis and Grossmann 1992 (p. 3). Funded by IBM Research, Mitsubishi Chemical and NSF (p. 23).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: two gaps. Heuristics for technology selection and capacity expansion are justified only by empirical evidence (p. 4). And LP and Lagrangian relaxation gaps are observed not to grow, and even to fall, as the planning horizon lengthens, although the problem is NP-hard (pp. 11-12). The model is a multiperiod MILP that selects, times and sizes dedicated and flexible technologies with fixed charges and procurement bounds (pp. 5-7).
- **D2 Core idea**: a temporal capacity shifting heuristic. Solve the LP relaxation and keep its allocations; shift each technology's capacity additions to its earlier LP acquisition periods up to the availability bound; round the binaries up (pp. 12-14). It costs O(T_LP + |I|n^2) (p. 14), and unlike naive rounding it adds at most one acquisition per technology over the LP (Lemma 4.3, pp. 14-15). A probabilistic analysis then shows asymptotic optimality, which also explains the observed gap trend (pp. 15-21).
- **D3 Assumptions**: no inventory and no disposal of capacity; discounted costs (p. 7). Assumption 1 (constant procurement bound) is for exposition only and is lifted in the appendix (pp. 14, 24). Random data follow models D1-D3 (i.i.d., independent or bounded demands; i.i.d. or bounded costs), with feasibility ensured by an expensive artificial technology (pp. 15-16). Assumptions 2-3 (unit production cost at least 1, demand at least 1 for some family in each period, by scaling) serve the relative-error result (p. 19).
- **D4 Proof devices** (brief): NP-hardness by an instance mapping from capacitated lot sizing with zero holding costs (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 1; pp. 9-11). Extreme order statistics: the maximum of n i.i.d. nonnegative variables with finite second moment is o(√n) almost surely, and that of independent ones with bounded moments is o(n) (Lemmas 5.1-5.2, pp. 16-17); Lemma 5.1's proof is credited to discussions with Galambos (p. 23). The error sandwich 0 <= z_H - z_IP <= z_H - z_LP (Theorem 3, p. 18). z_IP is Ω(n), shown by an explicit dual-feasible solution (Corollary 3.1, pp. 19-20). LP and Lagrangian relaxations are asymptotically tight (Corollary 3.2, pp. 20-21).
- **D5 Experiments**: random instances of a two-family network with two dedicated and one flexible technology (plus the artificial one), ten samples per point. They cover convergence under D1-D3, sensitivity to demand and cost variance and to the fixed-to-variable cost ratio, and the flexible-capacity share under correlated demands (pp. 21-23; Tables 1-5, p. 28). The plots were not readable (pp. 29-32).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: theory first; the computations are explicitly secondary to the theoretical analysis (p. 21). Stated limits: the guarantee is asymptotic in the horizon length; the heuristic is in its simple form, improvable by cost-aware shifting or by combining it with earlier heuristics (p. 15); no inventory or disposal (p. 7). The preprint has no conclusion section.

**Method links**:
- No link to Methods 1-5: the work has no solver, no benchmark library and no relaxation-speed trade-off.
- Research Taste mark 3 (a heuristic paired with a bound) ✅ evidence: the heuristic's error is bounded by its distance to the LP value and is proven to vanish relative to z_IP (pp. 18-20).
- new-pattern candidate: empirical regularity turned into a theorem. The observed gap trend is explained by the heuristic's analysis (pp. 11-12, 20-21).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove a planning model NP-hard by mapping every instance of a known NP-hard special case (capacitated lot sizing) onto a single-technology instance, and show the feasible sets equivalent in both directions (pp. 9-11).
- Design an LP-rounding heuristic around one counting property (at most one more fixed charge per resource than the LP pays), because that is the property the error bound needs (pp. 14-15, 18).
- For an asymptotic relative-error result, lower-bound the optimum linearly in n with a hand-built dual-feasible solution (pp. 19-20).
- Make random test instances feasible by construction with an expensive artificial resource of unlimited capacity (pp. 16, 21).

**Quotes**:
- "However, the qualitative justification of these heuristics has been based upon empirical evidence." (p. 4)
- "In the next section, we provide a mathematical explanation of this phenomenon using the heuristic strategy developed next." (p. 12)

### S161 · SnB version 2.3: triplet sieve phasing for centrosymmetric structures (Journal of Applied Crystallography 2008, doi:10.1107/s0021889808007966)

**Read level**: full (file pp. 1-10: the PMC rendering of the author manuscript NIHMS48630 with abstract, §§1-3, Tables 1-2, acknowledgments and references; the flow chart, Fig. 1, is an image, p. 4).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Hongliang Xu (first and corresponding author), Alexander B. Smith, Charles M. Weeks (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the crystallography line after S198: the triplet sieve of Smith, Xu and Sahinidis 2007 (pp. 2, 9) built into the SnB direct-methods program, on NIH award GM072023 (p. 8).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: SnB's time to a solution depends on the cost of a refinement cycle and on the share of random trial structures that refine to solutions, and better-than-random starts raise that share (p. 2). The triplet sieve supplies a subset of perfect or nearly perfect initial phases from an integer minimal principle (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: a subroutine generates the trial phase sets for centrosymmetric structures with fewer than 100 atoms in the asymmetric unit. It cuts the number of sieve phases when there are too many degrees of freedom, and the program reverts to standard random-atom SnB when no satisfactory trials result (p. 3). Three new parameters (NSP, SPR, NSPmin) are set as functions of the structure size N (Table 2, p. 8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: centrosymmetric structures. Triplets with large |E| values have phase 0, so NSP must stay small enough to keep 180-degree triplets out, and below a minimum NSP no solution is found (p. 2). Fewer than about 100 atoms in the asymmetric unit (pp. 2-3). The degrees of freedom vary with origin fixing (p. 3).
- **D4**: n/a (program note, no analysis).
- **D5 Experiments**: 15 centrosymmetric data sets (Table 1, pp. 6-7). The sieve parameters were varied on these test jobs, the best values became the defaults, and final jobs were timed on the same sets; solutions were identified by their mean phase errors against the phase sets computed from the known coordinates (pp. 4-5, 7). Time to solution fell 4.1 to 98.5 times for 14 of 15 structures, by an average factor of 29.5, and Table 1 shows one structure slower (ratio 0.47) (pp. 6-7).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: a software contribution: SnB 2.3, with an automatic solution detector that stops a run at the first solution (p. 7), integrated so that users need not see it (p. 1). Stated limits: centrosymmetric structures with fewer than about 100 atoms (pp. 2-3). Not stated: the defaults were tuned and evaluated on the same 15 structures (pp. 4-5, 7), and the slower structure is not discussed (pp. 6-7).

**Method links**:

### S044 · Multiterm polyhedral relaxations for nonconvex, quadratically constrained quadratic programs (Optimization Methods & Software 2009, doi:10.1080/10556780902883184)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1-20: abstract, §§1-6 with proofs, Algorithms MUL and COL, Tables 1-2, conclusions, references). Figures 1-4 are images: only their captions and the text about them were read. The online Supplement cited on p. 16 is not in the file.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xiaowei Bao (UIUC, first author), Mohit Tawarmalani (Purdue); Sahinidis (CMU) corresponding (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the convexification line with Tawarmalani carried to QCQP. Rikun's envelope theorem and the authors' own envelope results (p. 3) are turned into cuts for BARON, which at the time relaxed QCQPs term by term (pp. 2, 16).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: practice, BARON and LINDO included, relaxes quadratic constraints term by term with the bilinear envelope over a rectangle, because termwise relaxations are easy to keep polyhedral in the original variables (p. 2). Relaxations that see whole constraints (RLT, Lagrangian, SDP and SOCP) are in general tighter, and the Lagrangian ones often have nonconvex subproblems (p. 2). The entry: keep polyhedrality and relax the sum of a constraint's bilinear terms jointly (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: the convex envelope of a multilinear function over a box is polyhedral and generated by the vertices. Its nonvertical facets are exactly the basic feasible solutions of an LP whose constraints are indexed by the box vertices (Theorem 2.4, p. 5). The multiterm relaxation of each quadratic function combines this envelope for the bilinear part with termwise envelopes, and with concave envelopes or sandwich-algorithm tangents for the square terms (relaxation MR, pp. 7-10). Since the facets are exponentially many, Algorithm MUL separates them on demand: it takes the function with the largest underestimation gap and solves the separation LP with objective a^T x* + b (pp. 10-11). That LP is solved by column generation whose pricing problem is an unconstrained binary QP (Theorem 4.1, Algorithm COL; pp. 13-15).
- **D3 Assumptions**: the variables lie in a nonempty bounded box (p. 1). The envelope is assumed polyhedral, which holds on boxes; a counterexample shows it fails on other domains (p. 3). Each quadratic is split into a bilinear part and separable squares (p. 7). In the BARON tests the cuts are added at the root only (p. 16).
- **D4 Proof devices** (brief): Rikun's sufficient condition (Lemma 2.2) and an explicit facet formula on a naive simplex (Proposition 2.3) (pp. 4-5). Facets correspond to basic feasible solutions through affine independence (Theorem 2.4, pp. 5-6). The convex envelope of a lower semicontinuous function converges as the box shrinks, which branch-and-bound convergence needs (Theorem 3.1, pp. 7-8). A three-term example shows the multiterm envelope strictly dominating the termwise one (p. 8). A bilinear function separates over the connected components of its interaction graph (pp. 12-13). The reduced cost of a vertex column equals the UBQP objective (Theorem 4.1, p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments**: 135 random QCQPs (n from 10 to 50, m from 1 to 2n, densities 1, 0.5 and 0.25 after earlier generators, three per (n, m)); the yardstick is the best solution of BARON, MINOS, CONOPT and SNOPT at 100 s each (p. 15). At the root (per-density summary, Table 1, p. 15), the multiterm relaxation takes 76-104 s against 0.1-0.5 s for the termwise one and closes 45-88% of the termwise gap. MUL closes more than 90% of the gap on 64% of the 112 instances with a nonzero termwise gap (p. 15). Inside BARON, with the cuts passed as relaxation-only constraints at the root, absolute and relative tolerances of 10^-3 and 10^-6 and 1000 s (p. 16), more instances are solved, with less time and smaller final gaps on the non-trivial ones (Table 2, pp. 17-18). Summed over the Table 2 rows, 98 against 75 of 135 instances are solved (my count, p. 17).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: theory plus algorithm, tested through BARON. Stated limits: the multiterm relaxation takes significantly more CPU time to construct (p. 18). The separation LP is exponential in a function's number of variables, so MUL suits many small bilinear functions and needs decomposition otherwise (p. 12). The reported gaps may overstate the true ones because some instances were not solved to global optimality (p. 16). Trivial instances are defined and set apart (p. 17). Future work: more general problems, and cuts at every node to exploit local bounds (p. 18).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the stronger relaxation is kept polyhedral on purpose (p. 2). Its construction time is reported beside the gap it closes (Table 1, p. 15), its stopping rules trade tightness against time (p. 12), and it is admitted only after BARON is shown to solve more instances faster with it (pp. 16-18). This is Method 5 step 3, with multiterm LP inequalities as the cheaper object.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: the test runs inside the production solver with and without the new cuts, but the cuts are generated outside and injected at the root through BARON's relaxation-only construct, not as a built-in module with a switch (p. 16).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: the yardstick is the best solution of four solvers at 100 s, not a certified optimum, and the instances are random, not a public library. The authors say the reported gaps may be overstated (pp. 15-16).
- Heuristic 4 ✅ evidence: trivial instances are defined by an explicit size-and-density rule and set apart before the effects are reported (p. 17).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To separate a point from the convex envelope of a multilinear function over a box, solve the LP over the vertex constraints with objective a^T x* + b; an optimal basic solution is the most violated facet (Theorem 2.4, pp. 5-6; p. 10).
- Price the vertex columns of that LP's dual with an unconstrained binary QP: run a multistart tabu heuristic first, and go exact only when it finds no negative reduced cost (Theorem 4.1, Algorithm COL; pp. 13-14).
- Split a bilinear function by the connected components of its interaction graph before separating. This keeps the separation problems small and loses nothing when the function is separable (pp. 12-13).
- Prototype a relaxation outside a closed solver, pass its cuts as relaxation-only constraints, and compare solved counts, time and final gap with and without them (pp. 16-18).
- Stop a cut loop on an iteration or time cap, or when the relative bound improvement stays below delta for K_seq consecutive rounds (p. 12).

**Quotes**:
- "Our main goal is to maintain the polyhedrality enjoyed by termwise relaxations, while considerably improving the quality of the relaxation used." (p. 2)
- "Compared with the standard termwise relaxation of bilinear functions, the proposed relaxation requires significantly more CPU time to construct." (p. 18)
