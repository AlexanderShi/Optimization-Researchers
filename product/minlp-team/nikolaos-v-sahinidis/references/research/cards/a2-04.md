### S350 · Optimization of Carbon Capture Systems Using Surrogate Models of Simulated Processes (NETL in-house research 2011, no DOI; OSTI 1036502)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json; source https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1036502 per abstract-sources.json) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. Cozad (first author), D. Miller, Y. J. Chang (works.json)

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: the entry is an application target. Carbon capture raises the cost of electricity of existing pulverized-coal plants by 71-85 percent, and NETL wants the increase below 30 percent. Three simulator defects stand in the way: no accurate algebraic model, costly simulations, and simulations that are not robust (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: take an indirect route. First learn low-complexity algebraic surrogates from data sampled from the detailed simulations, combining derivative-based and derivative-free optimization with machine-learning and statistical techniques. - **D8 (contribution)**: a methodology statement, with no results in the abstract. It is the 2011 form of the chain that S009 published in 2014; the name ALAMO does not appear here (abstract).
- Transferable technique: before choosing a solver class, write down which defect of the simulator rules out which class: no algebra rules out provable derivative-based methods, and cost and crashes rule out direct derivative-free search. Let that table choose the indirect route (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ evidence. The abstract gives the whole chain of Method 3 (low-complexity algebraic surrogate, adaptive derivative-free sampling, superstructure optimization with derivative-based solvers) and the reason against direct DFO, three years before S009 (abstract).
**Quotes**: "The first challenge bars the use of provable derivative-based optimization algorithms." (abstract); "To overcome these difficulties, we take a more indirect method to solving this problem by, first, generating an accurate set of algebraic surrogate models from the simulation then using derivative-based solvers to optimize the surrogate models." (abstract)

### S080 · Scaling linear optimization problems prior to application of the simplex method (Computational Optimization and Applications 2012, doi:10.1007/s10589-011-9420-4)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. M. Elble (first author); Comput. Optim. Appl. 52(2):345-371 (Crossref)

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: scaling is the most common preconditioning in LP solvers, and the abstract calls it "poorly understood". The paper reframes it: scaling also sets the relative reference point for the simplex method's absolute tolerances, such as the test of whether a reduced cost is nonnegative (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: no new scaling method. The existing techniques are measured inside three simplex codes (CPLEX, MINOS, GLPK), and the condition number is computed at every iteration (abstract).
- **D5**: Netlib and Kennington problems, over half a billion simplex computations (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: an empirical verdict. On average no technique beats the simplest one, equilibration, despite its rivals' added complexity and computational cost; some techniques are also more expensive to compute (abstract).
- Transferable technique: to judge a preconditioning heuristic, log a diagnostic of what it is meant to fix (here the condition number at every iteration) in several solvers. Test first whether the elaborate variants beat the simplest one at all (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 4 ✅ evidence. The group enters LP-engine work by measuring every existing technique on public sets (Netlib, Kennington) in three codes, before any method of its own, and the verdict favours the simplest technique (abstract). With the two 2012 reviews S125 and S196, this is the entry into LP-engine internals that 06-trajectory dates to 2012. The group's own LP methods came later: S158 (2021, simplex initialization) and S239 (2026, Fourier-Motzkin presolve) (works.json titles).
**Quotes**: "Most importantly, scaling provides a relative point of reference for absolute tolerances." (abstract); "For the Netlib and Kennington problems considered herein, it is found that on average no scaling technique outperforms the simplest technique (equilibration) despite the added complexity and computational cost." (abstract)
