### H009 · New proofs of exact LP reformulations for binary polynomial optimization with bounded treewidth (Discrete Optimization, submitted 2026, no DOI or arXiv id)
**Read level**: full. Pp. 1-15 (abstract; §1 with Theorem 1; §2 with the reformulation, Claim 1, Algorithm 1 and Observation 1; §3 with both proofs; Examples 1-6; Corollary 2) and the references (pp. 16-17) were read in full. The file is a hand-supplied manuscript without date or journal header (INDEX source: manual); the works.json note records it as submitted in 2026 with no public version found (CV B4.169).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Sourabh K. Choudhary (first author), Santanu S. Dey (Georgia Tech ISyE; p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: Georgia Tech MIP/MINLP theory with Dey around their co-supervised student (06: Choudhary PhD 2026 with Dey); the same author team as S238. No solver, instance or computation appears.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The object is a known result: a binary polynomial optimization (BPO) instance whose associated graph has treewidth d admits an exact LP of size O(n2^d) (abstract, p. 1), stated as Theorem 1 with O(k2^d) variables and constraints for a tree decomposition with k nodes (p. 4). Motivation: BPO is NP-hard even unconstrained, models many combinatorial objectives and logical constraints, and occurs in MINLPLib (low auto-correlation sequences, the ground state of the Bernasconi model) and as QUBO (pp. 1-2). The entry point is the proof technique: the "Previous proofs" of Laurent (2009) and Bienstock and Muñoz (2018) argue by induction on the number of tree-decomposition nodes, with a one-node base case and an inductive step that glues integer points of two subtrees (pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: when a theorem is known only through an inductive proof, look for a direct one (extreme-point perturbation, LP duality) that may yield extra structure, here a TDI corollary (pp. 4-5, 15).
- **D2 Core idea**: A new form of the standard LP, which both proofs use (p. 5). Lift each monomial into a w variable (p. 2); per bag of the tree decomposition, write w[B_i] as a convex combination (multipliers λ^{iP}) of the feasible binary points of the bag's multilinear set (pp. 5-7); project out w, which leaves consistency equations (7) between child and parent bags and convexity rows (8) (p. 9). Elementary row operations (Algorithm 1, p. 11) turn (7)-(8) into (9)-(10), in which each λ variable appears in exactly one equation of each parent-child block and only the root keeps a convexity row (Claim 1, p. 10; formulation LP (11)-(12) and Observation 1, pp. 11-12). Proof 1: a fractional extreme point is the midpoint of two feasible points obtained by ±ε perturbations propagated from the root to the leaves (§3.1, pp. 12-13). Proof 2: the dual of LP is the dynamic program over the tree decomposition; the duals are set by a leaf-to-root min-recursion, the primals by a root-to-leaf choice of active dual constraints, and complementary slackness proves an integral primal optimal for any objective (§3.2, pp. 13-15). Unchanged: the theorem and the LP size (pp. 4, 7).
  - Transferable: before an integrality proof, put the coupling equations into a block normal form in which each variable appears once per block (pp. 10-12).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Binary variables, polynomial objective and constraints (p. 1); the associated graph joins two variables that share a monomial or a constraint (p. 4); a tree decomposition of width d with k nodes is given ("over known tree decompositions", p. 5); how to find one is not discussed (not in the text). Clique containment in some bag, "a standard result in graph theory" (p. 6), makes the bag-wise integer program exact. TDI is stated for integral objectives (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) an exact bag-wise integer program from clique containment (pp. 6-7); (b) Claim 1 by reverse induction over the iterations of the row-operation algorithm (pp. 10-11); (c) midpoint perturbation with ε the smallest fractionality, propagated through Observation 1 (pp. 12-13); (d) LP duality with the dual written as a min-recursion (the DP), an explicit primal-dual construction and complementary slackness (pp. 13-15); (e) Corollary 2, TDI, from integral optimal duals for integral objectives (p. 15). The propagation step of Proof 1 is one sentence ("Propagate the perturbations throughout the tree down to the leaves", p. 13), and a fractional non-root node is handled by "the same argument" applied from the node closest to the root (pp. 12-13).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (no computation). One running example (five binaries, three bags; Figure 1, p. 6) is carried through Examples 1-6 (pp. 5-15).
- **D6 Positioning**: The theorem is credited to Laurent (2009, §8) and Bienstock and Muñoz (2018); Wainwright and Jordan (2004) for RLT up to degree equal to the treewidth; Faenza et al. (2022), Del Pia and Khajavirad (2024) and Cifuentes, Dey and Xu (2024) as related work (p. 4); Martin, Rardin and Campbell (1990) for sensitivity analysis through a polyhedral view of DP (p. 2). The earlier proofs are restated, not criticized (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: A short note: contributions in the abstract and at the end of §1 (pp. 1, 4-5); every step is illustrated on the running example. Slips in the example: the b1b4 coefficient is −5 in Examples 1-2 (pp. 5, 8) but +1 in Examples 3, 4 and 6 (pp. 9, 12, 15), so the computed optimum (Ψ̄ = 0 with λ̄1,{2,3} = 1, p. 15) belongs to the +1 version; Example 3 writes λ1,{1,4} and λ1,{1,5} where Example 4 has λ2,{1,4} and λ3,{1,5} (pp. 9, 12); the root is written j in the perturbation formulas (p. 13); "treedwidth" (p. 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory only: two proofs and the TDI corollary (pp. 5, 15). Uses are claimed, not shown: the LP "can help in the generation of cutting planes for BPO relaxations and potentially conducting sensitivity analysis" (p. 2). There is no conclusion, limitations or future-work section; the text ends after Corollary 2 (p. 15).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: an exact polyhedral (LP) description is valued for its exactness, TDI and O(k2^d) size; nothing is timed or run, so the cost side of the method appears only as a size bound (pp. 2, 4, 7, 15).
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: nothing lands in BARON or any solver and no library is built; on this evidence the Georgia Tech theory work with Dey runs outside the lab-notebook route (pp. 1-15).
- New-pattern candidates: dual-as-DP integrality proof (pp. 5, 13-15); block normal form before integrality proofs (pp. 10-12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove an LP integral by constructing both solutions: duals by a leaf-to-root recursion (the DP), primals by a root-to-leaf choice along active dual constraints; complementary slackness closes the proof, and integral duals for integral objectives give TDI (pp. 13-15).
- Before a midpoint (perturbation) argument, use row operations so that each variable appears in exactly one equation per block; ±ε perturbations then pass from parent to child one block at a time (pp. 10-13).
- Carry one tiny running example through every reformulation, and recheck its data at each appearance (the coefficient here changes between Examples 2 and 3, pp. 8-9).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Two applications, produced-water management in oil and gas and urban water supply design, with one named source of difficulty: frictional pressure loss modelled by the Hazen-Williams equation, q^1.85 (abstract, p. 1; p. 3). The bottleneck is placed on the primal side: "Finding high-quality primal solutions for produced water optimization problems presents a considerable challenge, even for the most advanced Mixed Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINLP) solvers (Li et al., 2024)" (p. 3), while global solvers are credited with good dual bounds (p. 14). The gap in the literature: MILP approximations for water networks "do not present theoretical guarantees on the performance of approximations" (p. 2). For urban design, Bragalli et al. (2012) showed that direct piecewise-linear (PWL) models give MINLP-infeasible solutions unless the breakpoints are dense, and dense ones make the MILPs intractable (pp. 3, 14).
  - Transferable: locate the bottleneck first; if the global solver's lower bounds are good but its upper bounds are not, spend the new method on primal solutions (pp. 14, 17).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: (i) Produced water: a standard convex-combination PWL of q^1.85 with binary interval selectors (20)-(27) and only four intervals (pp. 8-9); McCormick inequalities for the product of binary diameter choices and friction loss, exact because one factor is binary (p. 9); pump electricity overestimated at the upper end of each flow interval (33) (p. 10). Because the secants overestimate friction (p. 11; Figure 2, p. 9, image), every PWL solution becomes MINLP-feasible by recomputing friction and moving the difference into the relief-valve variable, at no higher cost (Proposition 3.1, p. 10), so z_MINLP ≤ z_PL (pp. 10-11). Conversely, an MINLP solution can be turned into a PWL solution at a bounded extra cost, paid in pumps: C2·Qmax·ε|E|T + C2(pmax − pmin)ΔQ|E|T under chordless cycles and slack pressures (Proposition 3.2, p. 11), or C1|E| + C2·ε·T·Qmax|E| for any network (Proposition 3.3, p. 13). (ii) Urban design: continuous-variable diving (CVD). Fit one least-squares line to uniformly sampled points of signpower(q, 1.85) on the current box, solve the MILP for a fixed time, stop if the largest discrepancy is within τ, otherwise shrink each flow's box to [x̄ − l/η, x̄ + l/η] with η > 2 and repeat (pp. 14-15; Algorithm 1, p. 15); at most ⌈log(2D0γ/ε)/log(η/2)⌉ MILPs for univariate Lipschitz nonlinearities (Proposition 4.1, p. 16). Against mesh refinement (Burlacu et al. 2020; Nagarajan et al. 2019) the domain is recentred and shrunk instead of refined; against diving heuristics (Berthold 2008; Bonami and Gonçalves 2012) continuous domains are restricted instead of integers fixed (p. 4).
  - Transferable: when an approximation errs in a known direction, find the slack variable (here the relief valve) that absorbs the error, so every approximate solution maps to a feasible one and the approximation's value is an upper bound (pp. 10-11).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Fixed topology, one diameter per arc, binary flow directions, big-M pressure rules that switch off without flow (pp. 4-7); flows in [0, Qmax] (p. 8); urban instances with fixed demands and an unlimited reservoir (p. 3). Proposition 3.2 assumes chordless cycles and an MINLP optimum with pressures at least 2|L||E|ε above the minimum and |L||E|ε below the maximum (p. 11); the authors write that these "hold true for all instances we solved" (p. 11) and call them "mild" (p. 13), but how a condition on an MINLP optimum was checked where BARON found no upper bound (Permian, Treatment; Table 3, p. 18) is not shown. CVD assumes bounded continuous variables (p. 14); Proposition 4.1 needs univariate Lipschitz nonlinearities (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Constructive maps between the two formulations. PWL → MINLP: recompute friction from (11) and move the difference into the valve variable (Proposition 3.1, p. 10); the nonnegativity of the new valve value rests on secant overestimation, which the proof leaves implicit (stated on p. 11). MINLP → PWL: correct the residual pressure drop cycle by cycle; a cycle with one-way flow must contain a pump (sum (12)-(13) around it), so raise its head by R_l ≤ ε|Lc|; on a mixed cycle raise a valve on a counter-flow arc (pp. 11-12); then reassign pressures from a source and shift all non-source pressures by D (pp. 12-13); the cost of the moves is bounded in pump terms (p. 13), with a Remark reading each term as a pump operating cost (p. 14). Proposition 4.1: a point where the least-squares line meets f (intermediate-value argument), Lemma 1 bounding the fitted slope by the Lipschitz constant through a rotation argument (p. 27), |f − L| ≤ 2γl, and box lengths shrinking by η/2 per iteration (p. 16).
  - Transferable: price an approximation's worst-case loss in the cost of the physical device that would compensate it (pumps here), which makes the bound readable to engineers (Remark, p. 14).
