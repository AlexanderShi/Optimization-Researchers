### S179 · Transfer learning in information criteria-based feature selection (Journal of Machine Learning Research 2022, arXiv:2107.02847)

**Read level**: partial. The file is arXiv:2107.02847v2 of 29 May 2022 (p. 1). Read:
- the abstract and §1 with the contributions (pp. 1-4), §2 (pp. 4-6) and §3 (pp. 6-10);
- the statements and remarks of §4 (pp. 10-19), the opening of §5.1 and §5.3-5.5 (pp. 19-20, 23-28);
- the setups and result text of §6.1, §6.3 and §6.4 (pp. 29-31, 33-40), §7 (pp. 39-58) and §8 (p. 58);
- the proofs of Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 (pp. 72-73).

Not read: §6.2 (pp. 32-33), Appendices A, B, D and E, and the other proofs (pp. 63-106). The figures survive only as axes and captions.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Shaohan Chen (first author) and Chuanhou Gao, both of the School of Mathematical Sciences, Zhejiang University (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: Georgia Tech period (affiliation, p. 1). The paper starts from the ALAMO line: information criteria can be optimized through MIP formulations, ALAMO implements them, and its best-subset core relies on BARON (p. 2). The funding is two NSFC grants and a Zhejiang provincial grant (p. 58). Sahinidis's part in the work is not stated.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: supervised learners need many samples, which industrial applications often cannot collect (pp. 1-2). Tighter error bounds from transfer learning are not enough, because they only make sense for large data (p. 3). The question is when transfer learning provably beats independent learning for feature selection at small sample size, and with which tuning rules. Both accuracy (MSE) and stability (the probability of selecting the relevant features) count (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: the parameter-sharing scheme of Evgeniou and Pontil 2004 has a common w0 and task-specific v1, v2 (eq. 3, p. 5). Embedding it in Mallows' Cp gives TLCp (eq. 8, p. 11).
  - One complexity term counts a shared support, so both tasks pick the same subset, and the ||w0|| regularizer is dropped (p. 11).
  - A diagonal λ3 sets per feature how closely each coefficient is tied across tasks (p. 11).
  - TLCp reduces to Cp when λ2 = 0 (p. 12) and to Cp on the pooled data when λ3 → ∞ with λ1 = λ2. With λ3 = 0 it shares only the sparsity pattern (p. 11).
  - The tuning rule is explicit: λ1* = σ2², λ2* = σ1², λ3,t* = 4σ1²σ2²/δt², plus a formula for λ4* (Corollary 15, p. 16). In practice the variances and dissimilarities are estimated from the training data (pp. 39-41).
  - For correlated designs: orthogonalize, solve the separable problem in closed form, back-transform, and threshold with cutoffs chosen by the Cp statistic (Algorithms 1-2, pp. 23-26).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: a linear model with Gaussian noise. Most results assume an orthogonal design, X'X = nI, and X̃'X̃ = mI for the source (pp. 4, 12), with σ̂1² ≈ σ1² (p. 5). The source model differs from the target by δ (p. 11).
  - The guarantees need a small δ (|δt| ≤ κ in Corollary 15, p. 16; ||δ||2 < κ̃ in Theorem 20, p. 19). For MSE they also need coefficients away from the critical points (Theorem 20, p. 19).
  - The authors call orthogonality the key assumption and say it raises the question of practicality (p. 19). The non-orthogonal results are asymptotic in n (Theorems 23, 28, 31; pp. 20-23).
- **D4 Analysis devices (brief)**:
  - Under orthogonality, the l0-penalized least squares splits into k one-dimensional problems. Each is a choice between 0 and the OLS value, which gives a hard threshold (Proposition 1, p. 7; proof pp. 72-73).
  - Selection probabilities and MSE then become Gaussian integrals (Theorems 3 and 6, pp. 7-9), and the Cp rule equals a z-test at level 2φ(-√λ/σ1) (Remark 2, p. 7).
  - This pins down where Cp fails. With λ = 2σ1², a superfluous feature is kept with probability about 0.16 (p. 8), and a feature at the critical value β² = 2σ1²/n is missed with probability 0.5 (Proposition 5, p. 8).
  - Comparing the TLCp and Cp probabilities gives conditions that need the unknown β (Theorem 12, p. 14; p. 15). Corollary 15 turns them into an explicit rule (p. 16).
  - At n = m, the probability of keeping a critical feature rises to about 0.72 (Proposition 16, p. 17). This is proved for δ = 0 (p. 17).
- **D5 Experiments**: simulations with known β and three real data sets.
  - Simulations: n = m = 20 with 5000 data sets per similarity value, correlated features, and k up to 30000 at n = m = 30.
  - Real data: blast furnaces, London schools and Parkinson's telemonitoring.
  - Baselines: LASSO, stepwise and univariate selection, their pooled versions, and two multi-task methods, all tuned by 5-fold cross-validation. Results carry paired t-tests and CPU time per run.
  - The exact Cp and TLCp problems are solved by enumeration (pp. 29-58; enumeration on pp. 34, 41).
- **D6 Positioning**: earlier work on transfer learning with LASSO and SVM is reviewed without criticism (p. 3). Mallows' own warning that the minimum-Cp rule should not be applied universally is taken as the opening (p. 5).
- **D7 Writing**: each result is followed by a plain-language remark (Remarks 2, 4, 11, 17, 18; pp. 7-18). All proofs go to the appendix (p. 4). A practitioner's guideline box closes the methods (p. 27).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: the contributions are theory (explicit tuning rules with accuracy and stability guarantees under orthogonality, p. 3), extensions to BIC and to the non-orthogonal case (pp. 3-4, 28), and code on GitHub (p. 29). Limits written:
  - TLCp still keeps superfluous features about as often as Cp (Remark 17, pp. 17-18).
  - With low task similarity, TLCp may choose wrong models with high probability (p. 29).
  - Above k = 3000 all methods are almost identical (p. 39).
  - The tuning rules are sub-optimal for non-orthogonal designs. As a result, pooled Cp beats TLCp on one school task (p. 45), and TLCp is slightly worse than Cp on a dissimilar Parkinson's task (p. 51).
  - No dominance over other selection methods is claimed (p. 57).
  - The exact TLCp is slow: 2550.83 s per run against 0.24 s for the approximate cutoff method on the furnace data (Table 8, p. 42). ALAMO is suggested for large k (p. 28).
  - Future work: the BIC versions of the δ = 0 results (p. 28).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: the object is best-subset selection by an information criterion in the small-data regime. It is framed through ALAMO (p. 2) and tested against LASSO and other selectors on real data (pp. 39-57). The paper answers one of Method 3's stated limits, small noisy samples (p. 8), by borrowing a related task's data rather than by shrinkage.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the exact subset problem is solved by complete enumeration (pp. 34, 41) or replaced by a closed-form approximation (pp. 19-26), not by integer programming. ALAMO is only suggested for large k (p. 28). No first-principles constraints or adaptive sampling appear.
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: every baseline has a stated tuning protocol (5-fold cross-validation over a fixed grid, p. 39). Comparisons carry paired t-tests and CPU times (pp. 42, 46, 53), losses are reported (pp. 45, 51, 57) and the scope is stated (p. 57). But the ground truth is a simulated β, not a certified optimum, and there is no portfolio conclusion.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: the code is a stand-alone GitHub repository (p. 29). The paper does not say that TLCp went into ALAMO.
- new-pattern candidates:
  - *Theory-set hyperparameters instead of cross-validation*: the rule of Theorem 20 replaces cross-validation and is the stated reason for the speed of the cutoff method (pp. 44, 52, 58). The ALAMO paper makes the same move for the trade-off between fit and complexity (card S017, p. 7).
  - *Exact analysis on the orthogonal design, then orthogonalize and back-transform* (pp. 7, 19-20).
  - *A computable applicability gate with fallback*: transfer only if the relative dissimilarity is below 3, otherwise run plain Cp (p. 27). The experiments bear this out (pp. 51, 57).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To study an NP-hard l0 criterion, take the orthogonal design first, where it separates into k scalar choices. Compute exact selection probabilities and MSE, and locate the failure (critical coefficients missed with probability 0.5). Then design the fix for that point and measure it there (pp. 7-8, 17).
- Derive hyperparameters from the theorem, plug in estimated variances and dissimilarities, and report CPU time per run against cross-validated baselines (pp. 39-42, 44).
- Put a computable gate in front of a transfer method, with an explicit fallback to the base method (p. 27).
- End a theory paper with a practitioner's decision box. Give the preprocessing, the gate, and which variant to run when n ≥ k, when the design is singular, and when n < k (p. 27).

**Quotes**:
- "Merely providing tighter upper error bounds is not enough to guarantee that a model selection technique coupled with transfer learning will perform better in real industrial applications with limited data, because these error bounds only make sense in large data size cases." (p. 3)
- "However, this does not imply that the proposed TLCp methods are always better than the other feature selection methods in any problem." (p. 57)

### S243 · Optimizing the designs and operations of water networks: A decomposition approach (NETL 2023, no DOI)

**Read level**: full (all 34 file pages: the title, the acknowledgement and disclaimer, 18 numbered slides with their overlay builds, and the closing slide). The file is the slide deck of a talk at the INFORMS Annual Meeting, Phoenix, 18 October 2023 (p. 1), with a DOE/NETL funding acknowledgement and disclaimer (p. 2). Title and authors, with Nick Sahinidis (Georgia Tech) third, checked on p. 1. Formulas survive only as extracted text (pp. 13, 15, 19-20).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Yijiang Li (Argonne; first author, whose address is on the closing slide, p. 34), Santanu Dey (Georgia Tech), Naresh Susarla and Miguel Zamarripa-Perez (NETL and NETL site support contractors) and Markus Drouven (NETL) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: Georgia Tech period, DOE-funded (p. 2). The talk is an early version of the 2025 journal paper S165 (card c08). Its results table (p. 32) has the same numbers as that paper's Table 4 for 53 periods (S165 p. 29), so it is not independent evidence beside S165.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: produced water comes up with oil and gas. Its volume starts high and falls, and the network depends on node elevations; the work belongs to the PARETO produced-water initiative (p. 3). The previous-work slides list design papers (pipe sizing) and operation papers (flowrates, pressures) under separate headings (pp. 5-6). The model puts both in a single problem (p. 7). The entry is a solver observation: BARON and SCIP improve dual bounds but are slow to find feasible points (p. 17).
- **D2 Core idea and design**: two primal methods (p. 18). The solution either produces is handed to SCIP as a starting point, with a 5-hour limit (p. 30).
  - (i) CVXNLP-based decomposition: for a given budget, a master problem built on CVXNLP with binary diameter choices and a cost constraint. The subproblem fixes the diameters and is a more tractable feasibility MINLP. An infeasible subproblem adds a no-good cut, and a budget-initialization step finds a starting budget (pp. 21-24).
  - (ii) Time decomposition: solve a restricted problem over the first T̃ periods to a target gap ε. Fix the diameters and pump locations (and possibly some pump statuses after T̃), then solve the remaining periods. On infeasibility, pick new T̃ and ε from given sets (pp. 26-29). The rationale is physical: designs good for the early high-volume periods are likely feasible later, and pumps are needed most at high flow (p. 25).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: a directed graph. Each pipe has several diameter choices and a relief valve, and pump stations can be installed (p. 8). There are T periods with flows and pressures per period, and reservoir pressures are fixed (p. 9). The objective is the total cost of the chosen diameters (p. 11).
  - Pressure drop follows Hazen-Williams, proportional to |q|^0.852 q, plus elevation terms. Flows are copied per diameter, with direction binaries (pp. 12-13).
  - Pumps add pressure and valves relieve it. There are at most N stations, with minimum up and down times and a cap on pump operation (pp. 14-15).
  - The two named difficulties are the nonconvex pressure constraints and the binaries (p. 16).
  - CVXNLP needs a strictly increasing pressure-drop function with φ(0) = 0 (p. 20). The time decomposition relies on falling volumes (pp. 3, 25).
- **D4 Analysis devices (brief)**: a cited theorem (c.f. Raghunathan 2013; CVXNLP after Cherry 1951): the network analysis equations have a solution if and only if CVXNLP does (pp. 19-20). So a convex program stands in for the nonconvex pressure equations in the master. Integer no-good cuts (p. 24). No proofs.
- **D5 Experiments**: one PARETO case derived from a real US network: 19 sources, 7 sinks, 29 in-nodes and 58 pipes, 4 diameters and 53 periods, at stress factors 0.1-2.0 (p. 31).
  - SCIP alone finds no primal solution in any scenario (p. 32).
  - The time decomposition (5 ≤ T̃ ≤ 8, 5% ≤ ε ≤ 15%) ends with gaps from 0.00% to 13.53% (p. 32).
