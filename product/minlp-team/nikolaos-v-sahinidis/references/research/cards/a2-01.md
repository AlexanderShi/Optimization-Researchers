### S263 · MINLP model for multiproduct scheduling on continuous parallel lines (Carnegie Mellon University (Engineering Design Research Center report) 1989, no DOI; KiltHub doi:10.1184/R1/6467156.v1)
**Read**: abstract (of this CMU report, from its KiltHub record) · **Coauthors**: I. E. Grossmann

- **D1 problem/entry**: Long-range multiproduct scheduling on continuous parallel production lines, the configuration typical of specialty-chemicals manufacturing; the method is applied to a real polymer production plant (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: Write the problem as a large MINLP, reformulate it so that it is linear in the space of the integer variables, then exploit the Kuhn-Tucker conditions so that Generalized Benders Decomposition needs no explicit solution of the very large NLP subproblems; a further scheme strengthens the master problem's bounds (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: A decomposition scheme tailored to one large industrial scheduling MINLP; the 1991 CACE paper S026 (title adds "cyclic") is presumably its journal version (inferred from titles and authors).
  - Transferable: Before decomposing, reformulate so the model is linear in the integer variables, and use the subproblem's Kuhn-Tucker conditions analytically so that no huge NLP subproblem is solved explicitly; then add bound strengthening for the master (abstract).

**Method links**: new-pattern candidate: Linearize through discreteness of the domain (existing candidate (S198, S361); in this 1989 report the MINLP is reformulated to be linear in the space of the integer variables before GBD is applied; the abstract gives no mechanism, so the fit is loose).

**Quotes** (abstract): "The Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions are exploited in the resulting model in order to effectively apply Generalized Benders Decomposition." / "The corresponding MINLP contained 780 binary variables, 23,000 continuous variables and 3,200 constraints."

### S023 · Convergence properties of generalized Benders decomposition (Computers & Chemical Engineering 1991, doi:10.1016/0098-1354(91)85027-r)
**Read**: abstract (of the 1990 CMU Engineering Design Research Center report "Convergence properties of generalized Benders decompositions", per abstract-sources.json; the journal abstract was not seen) · **Coauthors**: I. E. Grossmann

- **D1 problem/entry**: Two open questions about the convergence of GBD, an algorithm for mixed-integer linear and nonlinear programs (abstract).
- **D2 key idea**: (i) A proof that a MINLP formulation with zero NLP relaxation gap needs only one Benders cut, the one at the optimal solution; (ii) a demonstration that GBD on nonconvex problems need not reach the global optimum or even a local minimum, because every local optimum of the NLP gives rise to a local optimum of the projected Benders problem (abstract).
- **D8 contribution**: One positive and one negative convergence property of GBD; the negative one says decomposition gives no global guarantee on nonconvex problems, the gap his 1991-1997 global-optimization work addresses (abstract; link to the later work inferred).
  - Transferable: Pair a positive convergence result with a failure class and name the mechanism of failure (here: local optima of the subproblem become local optima of the projected problem) (abstract).

**Method links**: new-pattern candidate: failure case of the standard decomposition, explained, before the fix (new: GBD shown to stall at non-global, even non-local points on nonconvex problems, with the mechanism stated; S064 shows the same move for Benders on trivial TDTSPs).

**Quotes** (abstract): "First, it is proved that a mixed integer nonlinear programming formulation with zero nonlinear programming relaxation gap requires only one Benders cut in order to converge, namely the cut corresponding to the optimal solution." / "Second, it is demonstrated that the application of generalized Benders decomposition to nonconvex problems does not always lead to the global optimum for these problems; it may not even lead to a local minimum."
