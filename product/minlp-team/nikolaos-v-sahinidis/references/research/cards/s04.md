### S160 · SAS-Pro: Simultaneous residue assignment and structure superposition for protein structure alignment (PloS one 2012, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0037493)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–9: abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion; reference list p. 10 skimmed). First-page check: title and "Shweta B. Shah, Nikolaos V. Sahinidis" match; Sahinidis is the corresponding author (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Shweta B. Shah (CMU Chemical Engineering, p. 1); both authors designed the experiments, analysed the data, wrote the paper and designed the software, Shah ran the experiments (Author Contributions, p. 9) · **Position in trajectory**: Shah's PhD line (S254 is its 2010 abstract); research note 06 lists it as the last bioinformatics journal paper in the CV; it uses the Rios–Sahinidis DFO benchmark, cited as a 2011 working paper (ref. 37, p. 10), as its component chooser.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Existing tools decouple residue assignment (dynamic programming or heuristics) from superposition (convex optimization) and iterate; this is fast but can give suboptimal, initialization-dependent alignments, while exact contact-map tools are expensive (pp. 1–3). Stated as an MINLP minimizing RMSD over binary assignments S and rotation-translation θ with at least r_m matches (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: One bilevel problem: a master over θ (six degrees of freedom) whose objective SRMSD(θ) is the value of an assignment subproblem; for fixed θ the subproblem is a k-cardinality linear assignment problem, solved exactly in polynomial time by SKAP (pp. 3–4). Without sequentiality constraints the alignments may be non-sequential; a dynamic program extracts the largest sequential one afterwards (pp. 4–6). SRMSD has no explicit algebraic form and is multimodal (Fig. 1), so the master goes to DFO: of 28 solvers tested, SNOBFIT did best at small budgets, consistent with the Rios–Sahinidis results, and was run through an interface based on Rios's "mydfo" with 500 evaluations per r_m (p. 4). For similar-sized proteins the translation is fixed by making centroids coincide, leaving three angles, justified by the benchmark finding that problems with three or four variables were almost always solved globally; with all six, 1000 evaluations (p. 5). r_m is chosen automatically by scanning from 100% down to 85% of the smaller protein until SASnseq < 4 Å, a new score based on total fragment length (pp. 4–6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: rigid Cα backbones (p. 2); similar proteins align 85–100% of the smaller one (pp. 4–5); centroid coincidence is an approximation for similar sizes (p. 5); no bond rotations (p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis**: an equivalence argument: every MINLP-feasible assignment and superposition is feasible to the master, and the same objective at both levels (unlike Kolodny & Linial's bilevel scheme) makes a SAS-Pro optimum an MINLP optimum (pp. 3–4); SRMSD(θ) = sqrt(k_θ/r_m) from the k-LAP value (p. 4). Global optimality of the master is not established: dense-sampling DFO could reach it, but for efficiency the paper also evaluates local search, caps SNOBFIT at 500 evaluations and calls the result near-optimal (p. 4).
- **D5 Experiments**: 850 all-to-all pairs (Sokol and Skolnick sets, 222 similar) and 23 non-sequential RIPC problems, about 1 CPU minute per pair (p. 6); 96% agreement with exact CMOS alignments, RMSD better or equal on 59–69% of similar pairs against CE, SSM and STSA, perfect classification at SAS 4 Å (p. 6; Table 2, p. 7); on RIPC, mean and median agreement 62% and 70%, above eight tools but below STSA, with 8 perfect matches against STSA's 4 (pp. 7–8); except for SAS-Pro and CE, competitor figures are taken from [33] (Table 3, Fig. 3, p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: the bilevel formulation, non-sequential capability, released code and data sets (pp. 1, 6, 9). Future work: add bond-rotation degrees of freedom (p. 9). Losses are reported but softened: the three RIPC problems below 50% agreement may have alternative alignments, so performance "may be even better" than shown (p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (step 7, the benchmark picks the components): 28 DFO solvers tested and SNOBFIT chosen at the affordable budget, consistent with the Rios–Sahinidis study, whose result on problems with three or four variables then justifies cutting the master to three angles (pp. 4–5). The SNOBFIT interface is based on Rios's in-house "mydfo" (p. 4), which bears on SKILL.md's note that the promised unified DFO interface was never released.
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (step 7, derivative-free search only for what cannot be made algebraic): DFO handles only the six (or three) continuous degrees of freedom whose objective has no algebraic form; the combinatorial part stays an exact polynomial-time subproblem (pp. 3–4).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: competitor figures are copied from [33] instead of rerun under identical conditions (only CE rerun, p. 8), and the 4 Å cutoff that classifies the Skolnick pairs perfectly was read off those same results and then built into the code's r_m scan and stopping rule (pp. 5–6), so the classification claim is in-sample; the cutoff is not tested on its own elsewhere (the RIPC study measures agreement with reference alignments, pp. 7–9).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the master's space is shrunk before searching (translation fixed by centroids), but by an approximation said to hold very often, not by a valid inference (p. 5).
- New-pattern candidate: "exact inner subproblem, derivative-free outer master": an MINLP split into an exactly solvable combinatorial subproblem and a low-dimensional black-box master over the continuous variables, with one objective at both levels (pp. 3–4).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Split a mixed-integer alignment or registration problem into a polynomial-time exact subproblem for the discrete part (here a k-cardinality assignment) and a low-dimensional continuous master; keep one objective at both levels so a global master optimum is a global optimum of the MINLP (pp. 3–4).
- Before choosing a black-box solver for a new application, test an existing DFO benchmark's candidates at the budget the application allows, and cut the master's dimension into the range where the benchmark shows solvers reliably reach global optima (pp. 4–5).
- Let the code set its own key parameter by a scan with a quality-based stop (r_m from 100% to 85% of the smaller protein, stop at SASnseq < 4 Å), so users need not choose it (pp. 4–6).
- Where an exact method exists for part of the test set, measure the heuristic's agreement with the exact optima (96% with CMOS, p. 6).

**Quotes**:
- "Global optimality cannot be guaranteed unless the MINLP is somehow solved directly." (p. 3)
- "We performed extensive computational analysis with 28 different DFO solvers, based on a variety of techniques that included direct search, pattern search, surrogate management frameworks, domain partitioning methods, local search, global search, deterministic and stochastic algorithms [37]." (p. 4)
