### SW1. Branch-and-reduce and the birth of BARON

*Read: the 1996 abstracts (Springer pages); his group's 2017 account of the reduction theorems (Puranik & Sahinidis, arXiv:1706.08601, §6); the 2002 book preface; the 2026 BARON manual §5. The 1995 and 1996 full texts were **not read** (closed access).*

| Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | [stated, primary: book preface, 2002, pp. xiii–xiv] "We embarked on the journey of developing an efficient global optimization algorithm for MINLPs in the early 1990s when we realized that there was no software that could even solve small-sized MINLPs to global optimality despite indications in the literature that such an algorithm could be easily constructed. Our initial attempts, however, found us struggling with many gaps in the literature in the specifications of such a global optimization algorithm. Therefore, about ten years ago, we decided to concentrate on special classes of mixed-integer nonlinear programs, including separable concave minimization problems and applications in capacity expansion of chemical processes. In the process, we developed the first branch-and-bound framework for MINLPs, the Branch-And-Reduce Optimization Navigator (BARON). Its initial purpose was to facilitate design and experimentation with global optimization algorithms." [practice, primary: CV] The talk record runs from "Global optimization of molecular mechanics formulations" and "Branch-and-bound experiments in global optimization" (1992) to "Accelerating branch-and-bound in continuous global optimization" (1993). His first sole-PI internal grant (1992–93) was "An aid to molecular structure prediction". So the first trigger appears to be **molecular-structure global minimization** [inferred], and the capacity-expansion MILPs of his Ph.D. supplied the special-class structure (concave costs) that the preface names [stated]. Ryoo's M.S. thesis (1995) is titled "Range reduction as a means of performance improvement in global optimization: A branch-and-reduce global optimization algorithm" [stated, primary: CV], so the idea was developed as a student thesis. |
| **Why then** | [inferred, with stated context] (a) He had just moved from a Ph.D. on MILP and decomposition (generalized Benders convergence, 1991) into an Industrial Engineering department, where integer-programming tools such as reduced-cost fixing were standard. Range reduction transfers that tool to continuous nonconvex problems. (b) Resources were small: a $11,550 internal grant (1994–95) carrying the 1995 paper's title, then NSF DMII and ACS-PRF grants (1995–97) for "a global optimization methodology". (c) He spoke of a perceived gap, "no software that could even solve small-sized MINLPs to global optimality" (preface). |
| **Key insight** | [practice, primary: 1996 abstract] "This paper presents valid inequalities and range contraction techniques that can be used to reduce the size of the search space of global optimization problems. To demonstrate the algorithmic usefulness of these techniques, we incorporate them within the branch-and-bound framework. This results in a branch-and-reduce global optimization algorithm." The core reduction, as his group restated it in 2017 and attributed to "Theorem 2 in" the 1995 paper: if the upper bound of x_j is active at the optimum of the convex relaxation, with multiplier lambda_j > 0 and relaxation value L, and an incumbent value U is known, then the constraint x_j >= x_j^u - (U - L)/lambda_j "does not exclude any optimal solutions better than U" [stated, primary: Puranik & Sahinidis 2017, §6]. Shrinking the box also tightens every relaxation built on it, so reduction and relaxation reinforce each other: "Smaller domains lead to tighter relaxations" (Puranik & Sahinidis 2017, §1). |
| **Minimum evidence** | [practice, primary: 1996 abstract] "Specialized algorithms for polynomial and multiplicative programs are developed. Extensive computational results are presented for engineering design problems, standard global optimization test problems, univariate polynomial programs, linear multiplicative programs, mixed-integer nonlinear programs and concave quadratic programs. For the problems solved, the computer implementation of the proposed algorithm provides very accurate solutions in modest computational time." The 1995 paper's applications were process-design MINLPs (title); its contents were **not read**. |
| **Abandoned paths** | (a) [stated] The general-MINLP attempt of the early 1990s was **shelved in favor of special classes** (separable concave, capacity expansion) before being resumed "in the last six years" (preface, written 2002). (b) The molecular-mechanics talks of 1992–93 produced no journal paper in the 1990s [practice, primary: CV]. The topic returned 30 years later, in Lennard-Jones (2025) and Thomson/Tammes (2026) global optimization with Kuznetsov [practice]. Calling it "abandoned, then revived" is **inferred**. |
| **Reception** | [practice, secondary] Scholar: 1995 paper 785, 1996 paper 516, BARON note 1,137 citations; S2 marks 111 of the note's 822 citations as "influential". [stated, primary: CV] The note is "a top ten most frequently cited article in the Journal of Global Optimization". [observed, primary: Neumaier, Shcherbina, Huyer & Vinkó, "A comparison of complete global optimization solvers", *Math. Program.* 103:335–356, 2005, DOI 10.1007/s10107-005-0585-4; author preprint read] They found "Among the currently available global solvers, BARON is the fastest and most robust one, with OQNLP being close", but BARON "lost the global minimum in about 4 percent of the cases". After fixes, "BARON/GAMS had some bugs fixed, which eliminates all wrong claims to infeasibility and reduces the rate of wrong claims of global optimality to 12/675 = 1.8%". Interval-rigorous solvers sometimes "found solutions where BARON failed". **Thirty years on**, the 2026 manual still defines the method by this idea: "What distinguishes branch-and-reduce [41, 42] from plain spatial branch-and-bound is the aggressive use of range reduction." [stated, primary: BARON manual v. 2026.9.10, §5.1] |
| **Method it shows** | **Reduce before you branch, and turn every dual or incumbent signal into a smaller box**: carry integer programming's reduced-cost fixing into continuous nonconvex search, apply it at every node, and rebuild the relaxation on the reduced box. A companion move: **when the general problem is out of reach, retreat to special classes, build the platform there, then generalize** (preface). [inferred; both steps documented in stated sources] |

|---|---|
| **Origin** | [stated, primary: CV] Mohit Tawarmalani took an M.S. in 1997 ("Multistage network optimization and decomposition algorithms") and a Ph.D. in 2001 ("Mixed-integer nonlinear programs: Theory, algorithms, and applications"), both in Industrial Engineering at UIUC. | **Method it shows** | **Convexify the factorable pieces, but keep the subproblem an LP**: build theory (convex extensions, disaggregation, duality-based reduction) whose output a robust LP solver can consume. [inferred, built on stated sources] |

## 4. Cross-cutting patterns (candidate research methods for Phase 2; all [inferred] from the evidence above)

1. **Software as the integrating research artifact.** One solver, developed since about 1992, absorbs almost every theoretical result. Theses become solver modules: Ryoo (range reduction), Ghildyal (system), Tawarmalani (convexification), Mehra (cuts), Bao (convexity identification, multiterm), Khajavirad (hybrid), Zorn (intermediates), Puranik (reduction, presolve), Kılınç (integrality, multicore), Nohra (MIQP), Y. Zhang (presolve, probing). The work is commercialized through a firm (since 2001) and documented in a manual that is cited as a publication (about 787 Scholar citations across manual rows). Evidence: SW1, SW2, SW5, §1.9.
2. **Reduce before you branch, and carry the template across domains.** "Branch-and-reduce" in global optimization (1995–96); "A branch-and-reduce algorithm for the contact map overlap problem" (2006) and "residue-rotamer-reduction" (2006) in protein bioinformatics; deletion presolve and Fourier–Motzkin presolve (2017, 2026); Branch-and-Model in derivative-free optimization (2023). He states the cross-domain premise himself: informatics problems "originate from very disparate fields, they have very similar mathematical structures. In particular, they involve the use of a merit function to evaluate alternatives from very large, often combinatorial, search spaces." [stated, primary: informatics page]
3. **Ablate your own solver on public libraries, reported library by library.** Examples: root-gap and speed effects of cuts (2005); BARON, SCIP and Couenne with domain reduction switched off on 1,740 problems (2017); hybrid relaxations on 1,741 problems (2018); "the benefits from the newly added algorithmic facilities" (2025).
4. **Benchmarking as a contribution, with ground truth and portfolio conclusions.** Eight SDP relaxations (2011); 22 DFO solvers with BARON as the ground-truth oracle (2013); 5 local and 5 global MINLP solvers on symbolic regression instances (2018); 27 DFO solvers tuning BARON (2019); 13 mixed-integer DFO solvers (2022); QPLIB (2019). He prefers conclusions like "no single solver ... dominates" plus a recommended portfolio over a single winner.
5. **Retreat to special classes, then generalize.** The preface states this explicitly (special classes "about ten years ago", general MINLP "in the last six years"). The sequence of classes: separable concave, multiplicative, multilinear, bilinear and polynomial intermediates, convex-transformable, MIQP, and Euclidean-norm compositions (2023–2026).
6. **Keep the node subproblem cheap and robust; buy strength through cheap surrogates of expensive relaxations.** Entirely linear relaxations (2002–05); SDP strength measured as too costly (2011) and then emulated by convex quadratic cuts (2021–22); NLP relaxations admitted only with "fail-safe techniques" (2018).
7. **Close the loop between black-box and algebraic optimization.** BARON certifies the ground truth for DFO benchmarks (2013); DFO tunes BARON (2019); ALAMO and symbolic regression turn simulators into algebra for BARON (2014–2022); BAM covers what remains non-algebraic.
8. **Problems sourced from applications, especially industry and national laboratories.** Refrigerant design (NSF/Lucent), crystallography (NIH/HWI), carbon capture (NETL CCSI), turnaround planning and plant data (Dow), pharmaceutical processes (Lilly), produced water and rare-earth recovery (NETL), perovskites (GT materials). Students are first authors and the partners' staff are co-authors.
9. **Simplicity and certification as values.** "as accurate and as simple as possible" (ALAMO 2014); deterministic symbolic regression because genetic algorithms "lack optimality certifications" (2018); reproducible termination via work units (manual 2026); "Tight bounds based on physical or problem-specific knowledge are often more valuable than any algorithmic option in this manual." (manual §4.1) [stated, primary].

Era and resource notes for these patterns:
- **1990s UIUC**: tiny internal grants, single PI, Industrial Engineering M.S. students, ftp distribution. Pattern 5 belongs to this era.
- **2000s**: GAMS and NEOS distribution, a firm, 6,100 user-submitted models, NIH money. Patterns 1 and 4 were enabled here.
- **2010s CMU**: CAPD, DOE NETL and Dow money, multicore workstations, MATLAB and Linux test rigs. Patterns 7 and 8 belong here.
- **2020s Georgia Tech**: firm research scientists co-author the solver papers, GPU teaching, ML-in-solver. Pattern 3 is now partly done by firm staff.
- Pattern 1 needs a **long-lived code base and a commercialization channel**. It is a senior or institution-level strategy, not one a single early-career researcher can copy wholesale.

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

- **C1. Founder or co-founder.** The CV says "Founder and CEO of The Optimization Firm, LLC."; minlp.com "Our Team" says "Co-Founder/CEO".
- **C2. Khachiyan Prize 2026.** The CV (7 Oct 2026) lists "Khachiyan Prize, INFORMS Optimization Society, 2026". The INFORMS prize page, read 2026-10-10, lists winners only through 2025 and names the 2026 committee. Not yet confirmable; it may be announced at the October 2026 meeting.
- **C3. Page ranges.** CV versus Crossref/DBLP: Ryoo & Sahinidis 1996, 107–139 versus 107–138; Tawarmalani & Sahinidis 2001, 137–158 versus 133–154; Tawarmalani, Ahmed & Sahinidis 2002, 385–417 versus 385–416. Google Scholar carries both page variants of the 2001 paper as separate rows with different titles. This note uses Crossref/DBLP.
- **C4. minlp.com's BARON list** titles *JOGO* 74:611–637 (2019) as "Global optimization of nonconvex problems with convex-transformable intermediates". DBLP, Crossref and Springer give "Tuning BARON using derivative-free optimization algorithms". The same list dates Zhou et al. (*JOGO* 70) as 2017; DBLP says 2018.
- **C5. A CV talk dated 1993** concerns GPU-BLAST, a 2010–2011 development.
- **C6. Year of Kılınç & Sahinidis.** His 2019 editorial cites the *OMS* 33:540–562 paper as "Kılınç and Sahinidis 2019"; DBLP and Crossref give 2018 (online July 2017).
- **C7. Page numbers of the 2025 *ACS Energy Letters* paper.** Scholar has "10 (12), 6190"; Crossref and the CV give 6037–6046.
- **C8. Stance over time on nonlinear relaxations.** 2005: "state-of-the-art nonlinear programming codes are significantly slower and numerically unstable compared to linear programming software" (the motive for polyhedral relaxations). 2018: "we introduce a new relaxation paradigm" that "combines polyhedral and convex nonlinear relaxations". Both are kept; the change is dated, not resolved.
- **C9. Stance across problem classes on general-purpose exact algorithms.** His career rests on a general-purpose exact global solver. Yet for stochastic integer and nonlinear programs he wrote (FOCAPO 2003): "It appears unlikely that general-purpose algorithms will solve such problems exactly. Instead, we expect to see the development of problem-specific approximation schemes". Not reconciled.
- **C10. Own-group benchmark claims versus third-party results.** His group wrote: "Even without reduction, BARON dominates over the other two solvers" (2017) and "the leading performance of BARON over both local and global solvers" (2025). Others found: BARON 7.2 "lost the global minimum in about 4 percent of the cases" (Neumaier et al. 2005); SHOT "has an edge" on convex MINLPs (Kronqvist et al. 2019, as he summarized it); and tailored algorithms "outperform BARON and SCIP" on network classes (the 2019 special issue, as he summarized it). The comparisons differ in time, version and problem class, so they are not directly contradictory. They are kept side by side; note that the solver's author ran the self-comparisons.
- **C11. Self-description of BARON's relaxations.** The undated group page claims "an entirely linear outer-approximation scheme". The 2026 manual says the node subproblem is "an LP, or a MIP when integer variables are present", and the 2018–2025 papers add nonlinear relaxations. The descriptions belong to different eras; the web page was not updated.
- **C12. BARON's original purpose.** The 1995 report and the 1996 note present BARON as an "all-purpose" or "general purpose" package. The 2002 preface recalls that "Its initial purpose was to facilitate design and experimentation with global optimization algorithms." This is a mild tension in self-description.
