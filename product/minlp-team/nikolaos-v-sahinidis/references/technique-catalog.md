## 1. Proof devices (PD)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| PD1 | Value-function argument and one dual master for range reduction | Take any dual vector of the node relaxation: its supporting hyperplane of the perturbation function, cut at the incumbent's level, bounds a variable; for a variable at its upper bound with marginal λ_j > 0, relaxation value L and incumbent U, x_j ≥ x_j^U - (U - L)/λ_j [S077 pp. 12, 15]. Write one homogenized Lagrangian range-reduction problem so that each known rule is a choice of direction and dual, and reuse its cuts across nodes | [S077 pp. 12, 15; S058 preprint pp. 6–7; S162 pp. 12–14; S006 pp. 12–16; S046 arXiv pp. 12–13] |
| PD2 | Exactness through discreteness | Before relaxing, check whether the optimum must sit at vertices (box-constrained bilinear terms) or whether a factor is binary or at a bound (McCormick is then exact); linearize exactly there | [S025 pp. 6–7; S053 pp. 5–6; S168 p. 4] |
| PD3 | Envelope constructions | Derive convex envelopes from a generating set or convex extension; for two faces take the disjunctive hull with a perspective substitution; use separability for sums and the conjugate for composites; prove a piecewise envelope region by region | [S028 pp. 2–14; S032 pp. 6–7; S101 p. 10; S025 p. 8; S032 pp. 19–20; S044 pp. 4–6; S003 pp. 12–14; S190 pp. 12–13] |
| PD4 | Dominance and equivalence | Order relaxations by two-way feasibility maps; show an older rule as the new construction with a weaker bound plugged in; prove containment by sandwich or chain-rule arguments; settle each incomparable pair with one small instance | [S018 pp. 6, 8, 16; S101 p. 16; S006 p. 15; S053 pp. 8, 19; S133 arXiv pp. 6–7; S003 pp. 3–4; S025 p. 10; S018 p. 3; S028 p. 5; S042 preprint p. 21] |
| PD5 | Complexity by restriction, carried across a literature | Prove hardness on the simplest restriction by a reduction from a classical hard problem (3-PARTITION, subset sum, lot sizing, 3-dimensional matching), then show that each published formulation contains the restriction by zeroing weights and deleting the blocks that become redundant | [S024 manuscript pp. 10–12, 14–25; S101 p. 6; S118 preprint pp. 9–11; S090 preprint pp. 8–11] |
| PD6 | Probabilistic and a priori analysis of heuristics | Prove one counting lemma for the heuristic against the LP, bound the LP from below with a dual-feasible solution and extreme order statistics, and conclude asymptotic optimality almost surely; or bound every heuristic at once by two quantities computable from the input | [S029 preprint pp. 14–24; S118 preprint pp. 14–21; S119 preprint p. 18] |
| PD7 | Finiteness | Partition with half-open boxes and prove finite consistency; or show the search space is finite; or combine periodic bisection with branching at the incumbent | [S014 pp. 8–15; S168 p. 5; S058 preprint pp. 8–9; S077 p. 24] |
| PD8 | Smallest decisive counterexamples | Before trusting a cheap device or a folklore target, build the smallest instance where it fails (propagation that reduces nothing while the bound LP does, a non-attained separation infimum, a targeting rule that is no upper bound, chemically infeasible combinations that pass older constraints) | [S046 arXiv pp. 7–9; S133 arXiv p. 14; S119 preprint p. 17; S035 pp. 7–9; S077 pp. 18–19; S162 pp. 16–18] |
| PD9 | Equivalence chains to a tractable object | Reduce the question to an object that is easy to solve: an integer program to a linear system over F2, network equations to the optimality conditions of a convex program, a semi-infinite certification to one global maximization | [S198 pp. 3–4; S128 arXiv pp. 10–13; S165 manuscript p. 14; S039 manuscript pp. 4–5, 8; S017 p. 8] |
| PD10 | Duality and dynamic programming over tree decompositions | Compute duals by a leaf-to-root recursion and primals by root-to-leaf choices; read total dual integrality off the construction; price columns by dynamic programming | [H009 pp. 5, 10–15; S388 p. 1] |
| PD11 | Convergence by a checkable design rule or monotone updates | Keep a positive basis with a cosine-measure floor at every unsuccessful iteration; make relaxation updates monotone so that one proof covers every variant; count the tangents a projective tolerance needs | [S170 pp. 12, 16–22; S189 arXiv pp. 5–14; S211 arXiv pp. 6–10; S006 pp. 9–11] |
| PD12 | A map from the approximation to the exact model, priced in a device | Show that the approximation's solution maps to a feasible solution of the exact model, and bound the loss by the cost of the physical device that absorbs the error (relief valves, compensating pumps) | [S238 manuscript pp. 10–14] |
| PD13 | Domain bounds from the incumbent and from symmetry | Prove distance or coordinate bounds against the putative optimum; count orbits and add fundamental-domain inequalities so that each orbit meets the domain as little as possible | [S190 pp. 4–9; H007 pp. 19–20, 26] |

## 2. Design moves (DM)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| DM1 | Exact once at the root, cheap at every node | Solve the expensive bound problems (the 2n bound LPs, an SDP-quality step) once at the root; at the nodes use propagation or cuts derived from them | [S162 pp. 16–18; S077 pp. 18–19; S133 arXiv pp. 18–19; S090 preprint pp. 16–17] |
| DM2 | Reduce and re-relax with pass caps and a threshold | Alternate reduction and relaxation at a node while the improvement exceeds a threshold, with a cap on the number of passes: in the 2005 GAMS chapter at most maxredpass = 10 reductions per relaxation and maxnodepass = 5 relaxations per node, repeated while the bound improves by redreltol = 0.1, probing every third level (pfreq = 3) [S012 manual pp. 9–10]; these options are absent from the 2021 option tables [S191 pp. 21–30]. Defaults differ by interface: relative gap epsr = 0.1, tied to GAMS optcr, in 2005 [S012 manual p. 8] against EpsR 10^-9 natively in 2021 [S191 p. 21] | [S012 manual pp. 8–10; S077 pp. 21, 50; S191 pp. 21–30] |
| DM3 | Lift, then linearize | Introduce one auxiliary variable per distinct subexpression and linearize composite functions layer by layer in the lifted variables | [S003 pp. 3–7; S006 pp. 4–7] |
| DM4 | Cuts only when violated, kept local, reused in probing | Start each convex term with a few supporting hyperplanes, add a cut at the LP point only when it is violated, keep it local to the node and reuse it in the probing LPs | [S003 p. 15] |
| DM5 | A channel for knowledge the solver cannot infer | Offer declarations the solver cannot derive (relaxation-only and convex equations, solver-only bounds) and use them only where they are safe | [S069 preprint p. 9; S003 pp. 14–15; S012 manual p. 12; S095 p. 6; S044 p. 16] |
| DM6 | A core with user exits | Keep a callable core and let a user replace each search component through an exit | [S077 pp. 55–61; S053 p. 21] |
| DM7 | Time per component in every log | Print time per component (preprocessing, relaxation, local search, reduction, probing) by default, and profile before accelerating | [S077 pp. 51, 65–66; S012 manual p. 3; S159 v15.2 pp. 9–11; S069 preprint pp. 20–21; S015 pp. 2–3, 6] |
| DM8 | An automatic default plus an explicit off value | Let the solver set each component's effort by default and keep one value that switches it off, so that every component can be ablated. BARON 2021: the reduction families TDo, LBTTDo, MDo and OBTTDo are on by default, probing PDo −2 and local searches NumLoc −2 leave the amount to BARON [S191 pp. 24–26]; four options are switched off to sample local minima without reduction [S159 v15.2 p. 18]; probing off was pdo = 0 in 2000 [S077 p. 49] | [S191 pp. 24–26; S112 v17.4.1 pp. 23–24; S159 v15.2 pp. 18, 22–23; S077 p. 49] |
| DM9 | A new component behind an applicability gate | Run the new component only where its applicability test passes and fall back to the old path elsewhere | [S161 manuscript p. 3; S179 arXiv p. 27] |
| DM10 | Per-node switching between relaxation classes | At each node, choose which relaxation class to solve from the bound difference observed so far | [S116 arXiv pp. 22–23; S133 arXiv pp. 21–22] |
| DM11 | Search where the discontinuities are axis-parallel | Branch in the linking (tender) variables, so that the value function's discontinuities are parallel to the axes and rectangular partitions can isolate them | [S014 pp. 6–10] |
| DM12 | K-best or all solutions in one tree | Change the fathoming rule so that one tree returns the K best or all feasible solutions; in BARON the options NumSol and IsolTol, with no integer cuts [S191 pp. 16, 24] | [S035 p. 11; S162 pp. 25–26; S191 pp. 16, 24] |
| DM13 | Constrain the model's output and certify it | Impose bounds and physical relations on the fitted model's response, not on its coefficients, and certify them over the whole domain by globally maximizing the violation | [S039 manuscript pp. 4, 7–9; S017 p. 8] |
| DM14 | Turn the incumbent into a constraint or a cutoff | Add the incumbent's value as a cutoff, or derive from it a valid constraint that prunes and tightens | [S069 preprint p. 5; H007 p. 23; S108 manuscript p. 14; S271 p. 10] |
| DM15 | Implement equivalent encodings and keep the fastest | Encode the same model in the forms different solvers prefer, measure them, and keep the fastest | [S128 arXiv pp. 16–17; S206 manuscript p. 15] |
| DM16 | Exact inner subproblem, low-dimensional DFO master | Solve the structured part exactly and let a derivative-free method search only the few interface variables | [S160 pp. 3–4] |
| DM17 | Restrict a DFO solver to settings a lab can dial | Discretize the search to settings the instrument can realize, and feed back the median of replicates | [S205 p. 4] |

## 3. Experiment and evaluation protocols (EP)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| EP1 | Ablate inside the production code | Compare named builds with the component on and off, print the exact option lists, and admit only competitors that offer the same switch | [S046 arXiv pp. 15–16, 21; S003 pp. 17–23; S090 preprint pp. 22–27] |
| EP2 | Read profile tails and bound progress | If an ablated variant's profile catches up late, the component serves the proof rather than the search; plot scaled primal and dual progress of the solved instances to see which side lags (not to rank solvers) | [S046 arXiv p. 16; H007 pp. 14, 17] |
| EP3 | Count effects in classes, losses included | Report effect classes per metric (2× better to 2× worse), factor bands, wins, ties and losses with stated thresholds ("same" within 10%, "much faster" above 50% [S239 p. 26]), and per-instance ratios that keep the slowdowns | [S003 pp. 22–23; S090 preprint p. 22; S239 pp. 26–27; S161 manuscript pp. 6–7; S190 p. 16] |
| EP4 | Ground truth certified or constructed | Certify optima with a deterministic global solver, enumerate small spaces completely, or use closed-form families, known supports or a perfect-information oracle | [S001 p. 21; S063 p. 13; S273 pp. 10–14; S074 arXiv pp. 10–11; S134 manuscript p. 12; S017 pp. 8–10; S182 arXiv pp. 3, 7–9; S034 arXiv pp. 7, 10, 17–18] |
| EP5 | A mixed-tolerance success test on the median of ten shared starts | Count a problem as solved when the median of ten runs from shared starting points lies within a relative or an absolute tolerance of the certified optimum: f(y) ≤ max(1.01 f(x*), f(x*) + 0.01) in 2013 [S001 p. 21] | [S001 p. 21; S063 p. 13; S270 p. 8; S272 p. 8] |
| EP6 | An unseen set after developer rounds | After developers have seen and tuned on the set, test on problems they never saw; prefer structurally new problems to a transformed copy of the old ones | [S001 p. 41] |
| EP7 | A minimal covering portfolio and a virtual-best envelope | Report the smallest set of solvers that covers the solved problems at each budget, with a virtual-best curve, instead of one ranking | [S001 pp. 30–33; S063 p. 20; S270 pp. 14–16; S272 pp. 14–16; S239 pp. 27–31] |
| EP8 | Budgets and costs tied to use | Tie the evaluation budget to a named application's cost; set tuning hours against the seconds they save; price the whole tuning path | [S001 pp. 20, 22; S084 preprint pp. 9, 15–17; S182 arXiv pp. 6–7] |
| EP9 | Longitudinal reruns | Rerun the same protocol years later, with solvers tagged same, updated or new, and re-test old gains on new versions | [S272 pp. 4–5, 8–9; S270 p. 5; S190 p. 15] |
| EP10 | Test inside a closed solver through its channels | Pass cuts as relaxation-only constraints, try an alternative decomposition at fixed options, or run your module after the solver's presolve and hand the result back | [S044 p. 16; S109 pp. 31–32; S239 p. 21] |
| EP11 | Replicates for order-sensitive choices | Where row or column order can change the result, run permuted copies with distinct seeds | [S239 pp. 17–18; S269 arXiv pp. 11–12] |
| EP12 | Stress tests from the bound or the case | Multiply the parameter that the error bound depends on, or build stress-factor families from one case study, and check that the results move as predicted | [S238 manuscript p. 17; S165 manuscript pp. 23–24; S128 arXiv p. 16] |
| EP13 | Validate a surrogate pipeline once against brute force; judge surrogates by their optimum | Check the surrogate route once against the brute-force answer, and compare surrogates by the optimum they lead to, not only by fit | [S052 manuscript pp. 22–24; S103 pp. 9–13; S206 manuscript p. 16; S271 pp. 13, 16] |
| EP14 | Rehearse a lab campaign in silico | Before an experimental campaign, run the algorithm on a simulated response, counting evaluations as experiments | [S272 p. 17; S273 pp. 24–28] |
| EP15 | Show the search before the score | Plot where each solver samples before reporting scores, so that the reader sees why the solvers rank as they do | [S063 pp. 11–12; S001 pp. 18–19] |

## 4. Writing moves (WM)

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| WM1 | Concede, then sharpen | Grant early that a result may be expected, then stress the strong sense in which it holds and the simple cases that are already hard | [S024 manuscript p. 1] |
| WM2 | Numbered remarks on why the obvious approach fails | Open an algorithm paper with numbered remarks that give the structural reasons, and answer each with one component | [S006 pp. 3–4] |
| WM3 | The smallest computed example for every claim | Give each claim a small computed instance, with its numbers or a plot | [S002 manuscript pp. 19–21; S003 pp. 4–6; S018 p. 3; S046 arXiv pp. 7–9] |
| WM4 | Criticism by subsumption or classification | Show earlier methods as special cases or place them in a classification, instead of attacking them | [S024 manuscript pp. 2–3, 27; S006 pp. 2, 15–16; S003 pp. 9–10] |
| WM5 | Reviews with one lens | Organize a review by one lens or taxonomy, rate algorithms by guarantee and implementation status, state the coverage rule at the start and end with numbered open problems | [S001 pp. 11–13; S018 p. 8; S007 arXiv p. 5; S002 manuscript pp. 5–6, 21–22; S010 preprint pp. 2, 8; S070 manuscript p. 8; H007 pp. 25–27] |
| WM6 | Questions first | Organize a benchmark paper by the users' questions, and answer each by section and again in the conclusion | [S001 pp. 2, 41; S063 p. 2] |
| WM7 | Motivate a benchmark with a dated count | Open with a dated count: solvers then and now, earlier studies and their sizes | [S001 pp. 2, 13; S063 p. 9; S270 p. 3; S272 p. 3; S273 p. 9] |
| WM8 | Calibrated claims | Hedge in both directions; label each claim as proved, observed on one example or conjectured; state conjectures with the observation behind them | [S002 manuscript pp. 19, 22; S025 p. 16; S042 preprint pp. 21–22] |
| WM9 | Credit a speed-up to its developments | Attribute a speed-up to the list of developments that produced it | [S035 p. 12] |
| WM10 | Manuals put the contract first | Put model requirements and bounds before options, and pair every termination status with what it certifies and what the user does next | [S012 manual pp. 1–5; S112 v17.4.1 pp. 12–14; S191 pp. 12–13] |
| WM11 | A tutorial on a deliberately weak relaxation | Walk through a tutorial run on a weak relaxation, switching on one device at a time | [S077 pp. 26, 30–31; S162 pp. 26–33] |
| WM12 | End an application paragraph with the largest certified instance | Close each application paragraph with the largest instance ever certified and the method that certified it | [H007 pp. 5, 9, 10, 12] |
| WM13 | A neutral lesson from the reading notes | Before release, read the abstract and the conclusion against the tables they summarize | — (a general lesson; no work named, by the house rule) |
