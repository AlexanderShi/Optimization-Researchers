# Sven Leyffer · Technique catalog

> Date: 2026-11-19 (stage T3.6). Transferable techniques found in the paper cards, grouped as proof devices, design moves, experiment and evaluation protocols, and writing moves. Each entry has a one-line **How to use** and the cards with pages (`[S016 pp. 61–62]` = card S016 in `research/07-paper-cards.md`, at the `[[page N]]` page of the file read, as listed in `sources/papers/INDEX.md`). Many core works were read in a report, preprint or arXiv version, not the journal: a version word marks some (`[S028 TR p. 14]`, `[S096 arXiv p. 32]`, `[S069 preprint p. 3]`, `[D001 arXiv pp. 4–8]`); others are unmarked, notably S001, S003, S009, S015 (reports, preprints) and S031, S065, S119, S126 (arXiv); each card names its version [08 §1]. Ids: P proof devices, D design moves, V experiment and evaluation protocols (V, not E, so they are not confused with the evidence items E01–E73 of note 03), W writing moves. Source: `research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §7 (technique inventory) and the "transferable" lines of the cards in `research/cards/`. Most works are coauthored, and from about 2013 on Leyffer is often a middle or last author: an entry from a team paper is the team's practice [08 §1].

## 1. Proof devices

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Finiteness by non-repetition | In a decomposition, keep a strict incumbent bound in the master so every visited integer assignment is cut off; with a constraint qualification the fixed-integer KKT point leaves no feasible descent direction, so no assignment repeats | [S016 pp. 61–62; S003 p. 11; S101 pp. 4, 6; S002 pp. 31–34; S147 p. 38]; variants with finitely many binary points [S084 p. 17], pointwise lower bounds [S094 arXiv pp. 10–11], enumerated leader solutions [S120 p. 12] |
| P2 | Cut comparison through the KKT conditions | Aggregate the stronger cuts with KKT multipliers to recover the weaker family (OA cuts → the Benders cut, so Benders is never tighter); substitute first-order conditions to see when two families coincide | [S016 pp. 56–57; S002 pp. 34–35; S147 pp. 39–40; S003 pp. 20–21; S055 p. 9] |
| P3 | Filter and trust-region convergence template | Count the area the filter blocks, chain small-radius lemmas, split on infinitely versus finitely many filter (h-type) iterations, keep the radius window wider than the reduction factor | [S007 pp. 13–22; S026 pp. 9–15; S042 pp. 16–21; S040 pp. 10–15; S110 pp. 11–13; S114 arXiv pp. 14–18; S126 pp. 29–31; S092 p. 9] |
| P4 | Reduction to an analysed case | Show that the new rule implies the old fathoming rule, or that the new method is a parameter setting of an analysed one, and inherit its proof | [S009 p. 7; S101 pp. 4–5; S012 pp. 12–14; S040 p. 12; S114 arXiv p. 11; S002 pp. 24, 37–38; H014 pp. 4–5; S134 arXiv pp. 11–12] |
| P5 | Closed-form iterates of the smallest counterexample, then a run | Give the counterexample's iterates in closed form with limit and rate, then run it and tabulate | [S055 p. 5; S067 pp. 4–6; D001 arXiv pp. 4–8; S020 p. 3; S042 p. 8; S034 p. 15; S003 pp. 12–13] |
| P6 | Multiplier structure under CQ failure | When MFCQ fails (complementarity), work with the minimal element of the multiplier ray and split on/off the manifold; prove superlinear convergence basis by basis | [S057 pp. 16–17; S022 pp. 12, 18–22; H004 p. 8] |
| P7 | Proof-only ruler | Use an exact penalty only inside the analysis of a penalty-free method; the algorithm never evaluates it | [S040 pp. 19–21] |
| P8 | Outcome theorems | Prove that the method ends in one of a fixed list of outcomes (KKT point, Fritz John point, infeasible stationary point, restoration failure) and report the outcome the run reached | [S019 pp. 7–8; S108 p. 19; S110 p. 13; S119 pp. 18–19; S126 pp. 30–31] |
| P9 | Function-space devices for integer control | Existence by the direct method; Γ-convergence of discretized relaxations; a signed-measure derivative on set space; weak-* convergence of sum-up rounding | [S096 arXiv pp. 7, 13–14, 33–34; S162 p. 2; S091 pp. 6–12; S085 p. 15; S065 pp. 3–5] |
| P10 | Relaxation strength with explicit witnesses | Prove one relaxation is at least as strong by set inclusion, then give a witness point that shows it is strictly stronger | [S041 pp. 12–15, 20–21; S087 arXiv pp. 6–11; S120 pp. 8–9; S142 preprint p. 6; S089 p. 8] |
| P11 | Rounding-error bounds | Bound the effect of rounding a relaxed control by Taylor expansion of the propagators, or by a convex-combination contradiction | [S134 arXiv pp. 8–9, 33–36; S088 p. 13; S145 arXiv pp. 14–16] |
| P12 | Operation counts before experiments | Count flops or subproblem solves of the new step (child bounds from the parent tableau, matrix exponentials per decision) before running anything | [S016 pp. 41–42; S010 p. 7; S134 arXiv pp. 7–8; S103 arXiv p. 10; S168 pp. 3–4] |
| P13 | Tolerance chain | Budget the outer termination tolerance from the inner solvers' tolerances: ε0 > ε1 + ε2 + ε3 (NLP accuracy, underestimator gap, MILP accuracy), ε0 non-decreasing, which gives finiteness at an ε0-optimal point; bound ε1 and ε2 below from the solver tolerance, multipliers and gradient norms, and report the final ε0 with the answer | [S016 pp. 95–98, 102–103] |
| P14 | Budget-equivalence bound | State the bound that makes two budgets equivalent, then confront it with one measurement | [S082 p. 7] |
| P15 | Solver-agnostic infeasibility theory | Write the theory against an abstract weighted phase I problem so that the master stays correct for any NLP code; linearize at the point where restoration stopped | [S016 pp. 49–51; S003 pp. 4–5, 8] |
| P16 | A small proof that certifies a local answer | When a local NLP solution matters, build the smallest certificate of its quality (six LPs certify a VaR answer globally optimal) | [S031 pp. 12–15, 18] |
| P17 | Error-charged lower bound for an approximate relaxation | Plug the true minimizer into the approximation as a feasible point and charge the error to a Lipschitz constant times the discretization error | [S096 arXiv pp. 9–10] |

## 2. Design moves

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Reuse what the inner solver already produced | Price children from the parent's final tableau, hot-start from stored factors, read the active set from the last QP | [S010 pp. 4–7; S149 pp. 6–8; S028 TR pp. 11–13; S049 pp. 5–7; S001 p. 10] |
| D2 | Put new information where it cannot break the guarantee | Add curvature to the master's objective, not its constraints, so the finiteness proof survives | [S101 pp. 4–5; S016 pp. 64–70; S003 p. 14] |
| D3 | Replace a schedule or arbitrary parameter by an acceptance test or a radius | Read a penalty parameter, forcing sequence, sampling radius or ADMM penalty as something a filter, funnel or trust region decides | [S048 p. 3; S077 p. 3; S108 pp. 7, 10–11; S110 p. 4; S159 pp. 13, 16; S164 p. 4] |
| D4 | Trust regions on combinatorial or set spaces | Use a Hamming or ℓ1 ball on binaries (knapsack or MILP subproblem), an L1 ball on integer-valued controls (linear IP subproblem), or a measure-space ball on control sets, with an ared/pred acceptance test | [S084 pp. 16–18; S065 pp. 5–6; S092 pp. 3–6; S128 pp. 12–13; S091 pp. 23–26; S144 p. 12; S088 pp. 17–19] |
| D5 | The model picks the branch, the NLP solver picks the point | Use the combinatorial model (LPEC, MILP) only to choose an active set; accept only points the NLP solver returns with decrease; use an LP or LPEC for the active set and an EQP for speed | [S126 pp. 4, 11; S042 pp. 9, 21–22; S108 pp. 6–8] |
| D6 | The subproblem whose trivial solution is the notion to certify | Choose the step subproblem so that a zero step means exactly the stationarity notion you claim (an LPEC with d = 0 is B-stationarity) | [S042 pp. 4, 10; S126 pp. 9, 16–17] |
| D7 | Restoration as the same loop with another filter | Run feasibility restoration as the main loop on the violation, with its own filter, built on the inner solver's phase I | [S199 pp. 8–9; S040 p. 23; S001 pp. 9–12] |
| D8 | Big-M constants you can defend | Bound big-M by the trust region: in MPECopt's LPEC, M = max(x₁, x₂) + ρ, and the trust region keeps the relaxation tight when ρ < M/2; or derive valid constants before any speed-up | [S126 pp. 15–16; S146 pp. 19–21; S064 p. 14; S032 TR p. 23; S002 pp. 41–42] |
| D9 | Slack and bounded-auxiliary reformulations | Move complementarity and domain violations into slacks or bounded auxiliaries so iterates stay evaluable and CQs hold | [H004 pp. 7–9; S034 p. 11; S074 p. 15; S002 p. 15; S124 pp. 2–3] |
| D10 | Exact linearization with stated errors | Linearize binary × bounded continuous terms exactly; use McCormick for continuous × continuous terms and state the error of each | [S036 pp. 18–19; S032 TR p. 24; S060 preprint p. 10; S120 pp. 8–9] |
| D11 | Precompute what does not depend on the decision | Move decision-independent work (propagators, PDE solves for unit sources) out of the loop | [S134 arXiv pp. 7–8, 15; S084 pp. 23, 29, 31; S093 pp. 23–24] |
| D12 | Coarse to fine | Aggregate a nonlinearity over a coarse partition before relaxing it; solve on a coarse grid and round or warm-start on finer ones | [S096 arXiv pp. 8–9; S145 arXiv pp. 11, 16; S150 pp. 11–12, 21–23; S116 pp. 3, 5] |
| D13 | Relax, round with structure, improve locally | The field's standard pipeline for integer control; the distinctive part is the local improvement step, an integer trust region | [S088 pp. 8, 12–19; S143 pp. 18–20] |
| D14 | Fathom by surrogate-QP infeasibility | Add linearized objective ≤ U − ε to each SQP subproblem; under convexity an infeasible QP proves the node cannot improve; fathom only when the trust region is inactive | [S009 pp. 5–6, 8] |
| D15 | Early-branching trigger from the convergence rate | Replace a fixed post-convergence wait by a convergence-rate test: with integrality gap θ and experimental order of convergence p = ln‖d^k‖/ln‖d^{k−1}‖, branch when θ > τ or when p > 1.5 and ‖d^k‖ < τ (τ = 0.1, Borchers and Mitchell's value), because quadratic convergence will not close the gap | [S009 pp. 7, 9] |
| D16 | One exact master, every algorithm a relaxation of it | State the exact but impractical reformulation first and derive each method as its relaxation, so worst cases and strength orders transfer; generate solvers as combinations of ingredients | [S101 pp. 3–6; S016 pp. 56–57; S002 pp. 34–35; S119 pp. 3, 14–15] |
| D17 | Spend the outer proof's slack on inexact inner solves | Read what the outer proof needs (NLP-BB needs global optimality only at pruned nodes and a branching variable elsewhere) and stop inner solves early where it allows | [S002 pp. 24, 29; S126 pp. 29–30, 34–37; S016 pp. 96–103] |

## 3. Experiment and evaluation protocols

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | One solver, one start, one tolerance | Run every method on the same subsolver with identical options, the same start and the same termination test | [S016 p. 78; S010 p. 11; S012 pp. 2, 20–21; S126 pp. 32, 34; S120 p. 14; S169 p. 17] |
| V2 | Failures inside the statistics | Keep failures in tables and profiles, with a per-instance failure taxonomy by exit reason: catastrophic (iteration limit, or step or trust region too small at a non-stationary point) versus soft (locally inconsistent termination); penalize only catastrophic failures in profiles [S020 pp. 9, 12] | [S020 p. 9; S022 pp. 25–27; S199 p. 14; S110 p. 15; S119 pp. 21–24; S048 pp. 11–13; S011 pp. 27–29] |
| V3 | Two meters with stated purposes | Report subproblem counts and time, and say what each isolates and where each misleads | [S016 p. 78; S010 pp. 10–12; S075 pp. 8–9; S028 TR p. 9; S071 pp. 19–20] |
| V4 | Control reruns and factorial failure designs | Rerun only the failing instances with the suspected cause removed (for MPECs, complementarity relaxed to z1ᵀz2 ≤ 10⁻²; failures that persist are not solely due to MFCQ) [S020 pp. 10–12]; or move the suspected factor into the better configuration and check that it degrades | [S020 pp. 10–12; S064 p. 10; S168 pp. 4–5; S114 arXiv p. 20] |
| V5 | Theory quantities and certificates beside performance | Profile a quantity the theory predicts (minimal multiplier size) and add a certificate column next to time and iterations | [S020 p. 13; S022 p. 27; S088 pp. 22–24; S126 pp. 34–37] |
| V6 | Extended or absolute performance profiles | Build a new profile when the standard one cannot answer the question (extended profiles; absolute wall-time profiles beside relative ones) | [S049 pp. 11–12; H014 pp. 14–15; S126 p. 33] |
| V7 | Yardsticks other than competitors | Exact optima on small instances, idealized-oracle variants, or an a posteriori audit of the solution against the nonlinear model; for an integer control, check r-optimality a posteriori: re-solve the MINLP restricted to a small L1 ball around the returned control and report the relative gap, clipped at the solver tolerance | [S073 arXiv pp. 6–7; S102 pp. 4–5; S094 arXiv pp. 16–18; S123 arXiv pp. 17–20; S068 pp. 22–24; S036 pp. 30–32; S065 pp. 23–25] |
| V8 | One certificate for every method; generators for the hard case | Grade all outputs post hoc by the same certificate, protocol in an appendix, check not charged; write a public generator that violates the competitors' key assumption; add library variants when the library is one-sided | [S126 pp. 34–36, 40–43; S012 pp. 21, 23] |
| V9 | Equal-budget protocols | Compare at equal energy, equal evaluations or equal time; report the median of repeated runs | [S082 pp. 3–6; S045 pp. 10–11; S145 arXiv p. 26] |
| V10 | Sweeps that expose dependence | Sweep the mesh or a parameter past the theory's range; give per-component CPU tables | [S115 p. 17; S085 p. 23; S150 pp. 21–23; S084 pp. 21–24; S144 pp. 14, 16; S091 p. 32; S128 pp. 18, 20] |
| V11 | Show the minority where methods differ | When methods differ on few instances, tabulate those instances | [S114 arXiv p. 21; S012 pp. 23–25] |
| V12 | Ablation ladder from a plain baseline | Add one feature at a time to a deliberately simple base solver, confounders off, solver revision recorded | [S015 pp. 9, 12–25; S028 TR p. 5; S032 TR pp. 19, 24–27; S012 p. 20] |
| V13 | A self-built baseline at its best | Give your own implementation of the rival its most favourable parameter and say so; drop instances where solvers reach different local minima and report how many | [S001 p. 18] |
| V14 | Safeguard firing counts | Count how often each safeguard acts and rerun with it switched off | [S001 pp. 23–24] |
| V15 | Profiles with and without a handicapping subset | If a competitor is handicapped on a subset for structural reasons, show the profiles both ways | [S069 preprint p. 21] |
| V16 | Comparisons with published numbers | Publish the instance generator, map problem names, argue comparable work per iteration, and label the comparison tentative | [S020 pp. 14–17; S013 TR pp. 14, 17] |

## 4. Writing moves

| # | Technique | How to use | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | One computed example per technique; a running example | Follow each algorithm or claim with a small computed example and a figure; carry one example through theory, reformulations and code listings | [S016 pp. 49, 62–66; S002 pp. 7, 40–41; S126 pp. 11–14; S021 TR pp. 11–20; S032 TR pp. 5, 10, 14] |
| W2 | Contribution against one named predecessor | List the improvements over one named predecessor, each tied to a section; write contrast paragraphs against named rivals; open chapters by saying what improves on whom | [S009 p. 2; S040 p. 4; S016 pp. 46, 58] |
| W3 | Grade your own assumptions in print | Say which assumptions are restrictive, mild or merely technical | [S002 p. 22; S147 p. 3; S022 p. 14; S096 arXiv p. 6] |
| W4 | Open with the pathologies behind the received verdict | List what the "unsafe" or "intractable" verdict rests on, then show which items are harmless | [S057 p. 15; S126 pp. 2–3; S112 p. 1] |
| W5 | Time-stamp comparisons | Write "at present" and versions into comparative claims; label literature comparisons tentative | [S013 TR pp. 1, 14, 17; S020 pp. 14–15; S022 p. 29] |
| W6 | Keep judgements apart from theorems | Mark first-person judgements as such, outside the theorems | [S016 pp. 13, 75, 105] |
| W7 | A not-the-neighbour remark | Add a remark that separates the method from the class it uses as a tool, and what each one certifies | [S126 pp. 17–18] |
| W8 | Erratum template | Count the mistakes, give the mechanism, print the corrected statements in full, say which steps are unchanged | [S162 pp. 1–2] |
| W9 | Outlook with the maturity gap | Close a topic by naming the maturity gap and the infrastructure to build on | [H004 pp. 7, 9; S021 TR p. 22] |
| W10 | Name the continuous method an algorithm reduces to | After stating an algorithm, say which continuous method it is (OA as SLP, quadratic OA as SQP for integers) and derive usage advice | [S003 p. 15] |
| W11 | Open problems in one line | Print the open problem in a single sentence where the result stops | [S009 p. 11; S072 arXiv p. 23; S126 p. 41] |
