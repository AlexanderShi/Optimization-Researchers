### S025 · DOE Advanced Scientific Computing Advisory Subcommittee (ASCAC) report: top ten exascale research challenges (USDOE Office of Science 2014, DOI 10.2172/1222713)
**Read level**: full for the main text (file pp. 1-75, printed front matter and pp. 1-69, including the charge letter, file p. 86); bibliography (file pp. 76-84) skimmed, with the optimization references [10], [18], [44], [46], [61], [62], [88] checked. First-page check: title matches (file pp. 1-2, "DOE ASCAC Subcommittee Report, February 10, 2014"). **Leyffer is not among the authors.** The subcommittee is chaired by Robert Lucas with 22 members (file p. 2). "Sven Leyffer (ANL)" appears only in the Acknowledgements list of contributors, next to Stefan Wild and Rob Ross (file p. 2). The card is therefore written in the subcommittee's voice. Which section Leyffer contributed to is not stated anywhere in the text. His Acta Numerica MINLP survey is cited only in §10.1.2 Optimization ([10], file pp. 56, 76), so that section is the likely one, but this is **inferred, not confirmed**.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: R. Lucas (chair), J. Ang, K. Bergman, S. Borkar, W. Carlson, L. Carrington, G. Chiu, R. Colwell, W. Dally, J. Dongarra, A. Geist, G. Grider, R. Haring, J. Hittinger, A. Hoisie, D. Klein, P. Kogge, R. Lethin, V. Sarkar, R. Schreiber, J. Shalf, T. Sterling, R. Stevens (file p. 2); Leyffer is an acknowledged contributor · **Position in trajectory**: the 2013-2014 Argonne exascale/HPC side line (SKILL.md Trajectory, "Side lines ... HPC, inexact computing"). Contemporary with his ExaMath13 MIPDECO position paper, which S112 cites as [4] (S112 p. 2). S025 refers to the ExaMath working group: "The Exascale Mathematics Working Group, chartered by ASCR, will soon produce a detailed report" (file p. 53).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is commissioned. On 29 July 2013 the DOE Office of Science charged ASCAC to return "a list of no more than ten technical approaches" for an exascale system, with preliminary comments due by 15 October and a final report by 30 November 2013 (charge letter, file p. 86; restated file pp. 7, 11-12). The problem is framed as barriers: energy consumption, memory performance, resilience, extreme concurrency and big data (file pp. 7, 12). Exascale is defined relatively, as "1000-fold better capability" than the 2009 petascale, not as an HPL peak (file p. 11). Challenge 8, "Algorithms for discovery, design, and decision", is the optimization/UQ entry (file pp. 8, 13).
  - Transferable technique: when a funder asks for "the top N", define the target metric relative to a dated baseline (1000× over 2009) rather than by one benchmark, so the agenda does not collapse onto that benchmark.
- **D2 Core idea and design**: There are ten challenges, explicitly "not rank-ordered by importance, but rather grouped" (file pp. 7, 12). The unifying idea is co-design: "one can't design in isolation", and exascale design is "an optimization problem involving a set of constraints, a set of metrics ... and a methodology" (file pp. 13, 64). For optimization (§10.1.2, file pp. 56-57):
  1. classical optimization is an inherently sequential outer loop around forward and adjoint evaluations;
  2. parallelising the inner linear algebra will saturate, so the key challenge is fewer outer iterations;
  3. the proposed routes are higher-order derivative information, multi-point/concurrent-point methods (derivative-free [62] and decomposition [18, 44] are named as current work), multifidelity optimization, and stochastic and robust optimization;
  4. integer, robust and global problems "allow for algorithms that are less sequential" (file p. 56);
  5. inner-loop codes should expose functionality: fidelity levels, resource-usage information, error propagation, reuse (file p. 57).

  §10 opens with the claim that "heroic" single forward simulations contribute little; ensembles, sensitivities and optimality are what count (file p. 54).
  - Transferable technique: when the inner solve can no longer be sped up, make the outer-iteration count the cost metric of the algorithm design, and write down which capabilities the inner solver must expose (fidelity levels, cost information, reuse) for the outer loop to exploit (file pp. 56-57).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The system targets are 10^18 flop/s, about 20 MW, a mean time between service of at least 24 h, under 15,000 sq. ft. and under 300 cabinets (file p. 65; 20 MW also file pp. 12, 15). A further constraint is commercial viability ("A first-order constraint", file pp. 65-66). Optimization is modelled as an outer loop around simulation with "functions ... evaluated at a single point ... for each iteration" (file p. 56). The report concedes that the optimization and UQ directions "are meant to be illustrative and not exhaustive" (file p. 57; UQ likewise file p. 56).
- **D4 Analysis devices**: n/a for proofs. The report's analytical devices:
  - the performance expression P = S × e(L,O,W) × µ(E) × α(R) and the SLOWER list (Starvation, Latency, Overhead, Waiting, Energy, Reliability) as a checklist of degradation sources (file p. 67);
  - energy-per-operation accounting: about 12 pJ for a double-precision flop on 28 nm against about 200 pJ to execute one instruction on a simple core and about 20 pJ to read a 32-bit instruction from cache (file pp. 17-18);
  - the memory-per-flop trend (Figure 7, file p. 28).
  - Transferable technique: break a slowdown into multiplicative factors (here P = S × e × µ × α, file p. 67) and attribute each observed loss to one factor before proposing a fix.
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (no experiments). Evidence is cited from others: NTV measurements (Figure 1, file p. 17), Tianhe-2 at 17.8 MW for 33.86 PFlop/s HPL (file pp. 15, 72), and an integrated voltage-regulator experiment (Figure 3, file p. 19).
- **D6 Positioning**: The report builds on "Numerous reports over the past five years" and the DARPA exascale report [71] (file pp. 7, 27, 72). For optimization it cites the Acta Numerica MINLP survey [10] and Floudas [46] for integer decisions, and Horst-Pardalos-Thoai [61] and Neumaier [88] for global optimization (file pp. 56, 76-78). It distances itself from HPL/Linpack as the definition of exascale (file p. 11). The compiler section rejects funding duplicate open-source infrastructure: "wasteful and should be discouraged" (file p. 43).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Each challenge section ends with an "Impact" subsection stating what fails without the investment (e.g. file pp. 22, 26, 31, 37, 53, 57). The findings and recommendations are stated twice, in the Executive Summary and in §14 (file pp. 8-10, 72-75). The rhetoric is national-competitiveness advocacy ("U.S. national leadership is at risk", file pp. 8, 72). The report itself flags that section structure "varies a bit, reflecting the personal styles of the experts who contributed them" (file p. 14). Hedging appears mainly as "illustrative and not exhaustive" (file pp. 56, 57).
  - Transferable technique: in a research-agenda document, close each challenge with a short "Impact" paragraph that names the concrete failure if it is not addressed (file pp. 22, 57).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: This is an agenda and policy contribution: ten challenges, five findings and four recommendations, including investment in "exascale mathematics" (file pp. 7-10, 74) and a co-design framework (file p. 75). The optimization section is short (about 1.5 pages, file pp. 56-57) and illustrative. It defers to the forthcoming Exascale Mathematics Working Group report (file p. 53) and warns that exascale algorithm research "must not consume a disproportionate amount" of applied-math resources (footnote 3, file p. 53).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (own the instrument, integrate at the iteration level) ⚠ variant, committee voice: the call for inner-loop solvers to expose fidelity, resource-usage and error information to the optimization outer loop (file p. 57), and for "techniques more tightly coupled to the forward solution strategy" (file p. 54). This is the iteration-level integration of Method 1 stated at the level of simulation codes. Leyffer's authorship of this passage is not established.
- Method 2 (base and extension) ⚠ variant: the report frames optimization, including integer decisions [10], as an outer loop to be carried onto exascale simulation codes (file p. 56). This is the extension move at programme level, not in his own voice.
- Tension noted, not a method link: the report says integer, robust and global problem structures "allow for algorithms that are less sequential" (file p. 56). SKILL.md quotes Leyffer's 2013 slides, "BnB won't scale to a billion-way concurrency" [02 §1.7]. The two may be compatible (more parallelism ≠ enough parallelism), but the report is the more optimistic of the two.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Make the number of outer iterations, not the inner linear algebra, the cost to reduce once the inner solve is saturated; list concurrent-point, multifidelity and higher-order options explicitly (file pp. 56-57).
- Diagnose system-level performance by a multiplicative factor model (SLOWER) before proposing a remedy (file p. 67).
- Close each section of an agenda or proposal with an "Impact" paragraph stating what fails without the work (file pp. 22, 57).

**Quotes**:
- "What is clear is that one-off, “heroic” forward simulations on the scale of a full machine contribute little, in isolation, to scientific knowledge." (file p. 54)
- "A fundamental challenge in optimization for the exascale is thus the reduction of the number of outer loop iterations" (file p. 56)

### H005 · It's to Solve Problems: An Interview with Roger Fletcher (OPTIMA 99, December 2015, no DOI)

The rest of the issue (Powell and Su obituaries pp. 7-9, conference announcements pp. 9-12) was skimmed and is not used for Leyffer. - **D1 Problem and entry point**: The genre is a career interview of one's PhD advisor for the society newsletter (editors' note p. 1). **Transferable techniques**:
- When a method's numbers look good but the globalization rejects steps, ask "why am I throwing this away?" and design the safeguard to interfere less. This is the filter's origin story (p. 4).

- When interviewing or surveying, attach a link list to the primary sources so overlooked work gets picked up (p. 3, pp. 5-6).

### S112 · Mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimization (Oberwolfach Reports 2015 / ANL preprint ANL/MCS-P5429-1015, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-3; p. 3 is the Argonne licence notice). First-page check: the title "Mixed-Integer PDE-Constrained Optimization" and "Sven Leyffer, Argonne National Laboratory (joint work with Pelin Cay, Drew Kouri, and Bart van Bloemen Waanders)" are on p. 1. The footer reads "Argonne National Laboratory, Preprint ANL/MCS-P5429-1015, November 2015." (p. 1). This is an extended abstract. The review of existing approaches and the full benchmark set announced on p. 1 are **not** in this text; only one benchmark example is shown (p. 2).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: P. Cay, D. Kouri, B. van Bloemen Waanders · **Position in trajectory**: early public statement of the MIPDECO line (SKILL.md Trajectory "2013-now Mixed-integer optimal control → MIPDECO"). The class name is credited to the 2013 ExaMath13 position paper by Leyffer, Munson, Wild, van Bloemen Waanders and Ridzal ([4], p. 2). That is the same exascale-mathematics process that S025 mentions (S025 file p. 53).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is an application catalogue. The applications listed are:
  - contaminated-site remediation, oil recovery with wellbore placement and flow rates, and operational schedules [1, 2];
  - shale-gas scheduling [6] and solar-cell design [5];
  - oil-spill [7] and hurricane [3] response;
  - wind farms and gas networks (p. 1).

  The class is named "mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimization (MIPDECO)" [4], combining "the combinatorial challenge of integer decision variables" with "the numerical and computational complexity of PDE-constrained optimization" (p. 1). The received view is stated as the foil: these problems "have been regarded as computationally intractable" (p. 1). The formal statement is problem (1): min F(u,w) s.t. C(u,w) = 0 (PDEs with boundary and initial conditions), G(u,w) ≤ 0, u ∈ D, w ∈ Z^p (p. 1).
  - Transferable technique: introduce a new problem class with three things on page 1: a catalogue of real applications with citations, the received "intractable" verdict, and one abstract formulation that covers them all.
- **D2 Core idea and design**: The abstract promises three components: (i) a review of existing approaches and their challenges, (ii) "a benchmark set for this class", and (iii) early numerical experience with "both mixed-integer nonlinear solvers and nonlinear rounding heuristics" (p. 1). Only (ii)/(iii) are illustrated, by a source-inversion example. Laplace's equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions is used, sources are selected by binaries w_kl with a budget Σ w_kl ≤ S, and the objective is a least-squares fit to observations ū (Figure 1, p. 2). The design choice is stated: binaries are preferred because modelling source locations as continuous variables "results nonlinear (i.e. nonconvex) constraints" (p. 2).
  - Transferable technique: when a location or design variable can be modelled either as a continuous coordinate (nonconvex) or as a selection among candidate sites (binary, convex relaxation), choose the selection model and say why in one sentence (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The problem is infinite-dimensional in general, since (u, w) are functions on Ω, but "we avoid a formal discussion of function spaces in this paper" (p. 1). The example is discretize-first: a five-point stencil, h = 1/32, S = 3 sources, giving a convex MIQP (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis devices**: n/a (no proofs in the abstract).
- **D5 Experiments**: one instance. MINOTAUR's branch-and-bound searches 759 nodes in 69 s of CPU time on an Intel i7 core; Figure 1 shows the selected sources and the state error, of order 10^-3 (p. 2). There is no baseline, no rounding-heuristic result and no scaling with mesh size in this text (p. 2).
- **D6 Positioning**: Seven references, all application papers [1-3, 5-7] plus the self-cited ExaMath13 position paper [4] (p. 2). [7] is Leyffer's own oil-spill MIDO paper with Fengqi You. No algorithmic predecessors are cited in this abstract.
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: The text is compact and declarative. The new acronym is coined in the first paragraph and attributed to [4] (p. 1). Notation conventions are fixed explicitly (x, y, z spatial, t time, u continuous, w integer, p. 1). The contrast "Until recently ... regarded as computationally intractable" carries the motivation (p. 1). Formulation choices are justified with "We prefer" (p. 2).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is naming the class and seeding the benchmark library, with software evidence that a small instance is solvable (pp. 1-2). The limits are clear from the text itself: one 32×32 instance, no function-space analysis (p. 1), and no comparison. The abstract states that it "highlight[s] their computational and mathematical challenges" (p. 1) but does not list them in this text.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 (base and extension; step 4 "name the class publicly with its challenges and a starting library") ✅ evidence: class named and attributed [4], challenges announced, "We introduce a benchmark set for this class" (p. 1).
- Method 4 (test library as research instrument) ✅ evidence: benchmark set introduced with the first instance shown and solved (pp. 1-2).
- Method 6 (fix the formulation first; prefer linear/convex over nonconvex) ✅ evidence: binary source selection preferred because continuous locations give nonconvex constraints; the result is a convex MIQP (p. 2).
- Method 1 (own the instrument) ✅ weak evidence: solved with MINOTAUR, the group's own toolkit (p. 2).
- Heuristic 8 ("function space first, discretize second") ⚠ variant: here the text explicitly avoids function spaces and discretizes first (pp. 1-2). This is consistent with a short abstract, but it is the opposite order in practice.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Seed a new problem class with one tiny, convex, reproducible instance (stencil, mesh size, budget, solver, node count, CPU time all stated) before attempting the hard cases (p. 2).
- Turn a continuous location decision into binary selection over candidate sites to keep the discretized problem a convex MIQP (p. 2).

**Quotes**:
- "Until recently, these grand challenge problems have been regarded as computationally intractable." (p. 1)
- "We prefer the use of binary variables, because an alternative formulation, which models the source location as continuous variables, results nonlinear" (p. 2)

### S082 · Doing Moore with Less – Leapfrogging Moore's Law with Inexactness for Supercomputing (arXiv preprint 2016, arXiv:1610.02606)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-9, including references). First-page check: the title "Doing Moore with Less – Leapfrogging Moore's Law with Inexactness for Supercomputing" and the authors Sven Leyffer, Stefan M. Wild, Mike Fagan, Marc Snir, Krishna Palem, Kazutomo Yoshii and Hal Finkel are on p. 1. "Corresponding author: Sven Leyffer" (p. 1). The version read is arXiv:1610.02606v2 [cs.OH], 12 Oct 2016, marked "Pre-publication draft" (p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: S. M. Wild, K. Yoshii (Argonne MCS), M. Fagan, K. Palem (Rice), M. Snir (UIUC), H. Finkel (ALCF) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the inexact-computing side line (SKILL.md Trajectory "2001-2016 Side lines ... HPC, inexact computing", representative work arXiv:1610.02606). It came ten months after Leyffer asked Fletcher why BQPD was written in single precision (H005 pp. 4-5).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is a technology trend. Moore's law and the energy budget are "running out of steam" (p. 1). The paper reframes the benchmark question. Instead of "how much time or energy it takes to solve a problem", it asks: (1) "What quality of answer can be achieved with a given computation budget?" and (2) "How large a budget is needed to achieve a certain quality threshold?" Prior work addressed (2) with time as the budget; this paper addresses (1) with energy as the budget (p. 1). The critique of Top500/Linpack is that "the notion of a problem having a unique solution is not representative of modern practice" (p. 1).
  - Transferable technique: invert the usual benchmark question (budget-for-accuracy → accuracy-for-budget) and state which of the two your paper answers and why it matches practice (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: The approach has two phases (p. 2):
  1. inexactness: run in reduced precision and save energy;
  2. reinvestment: spend the saved energy on extra double-precision iterations to beat the double-precision baseline's accuracy at equal energy.

  The vehicle is a basic inexact Newton method (Algorithm 1, p. 3). The inner solver is BI-CGSTAB with relative-residual forcing (3) and η_{k+1} = min(‖F(x_{k+1})‖^{1/2}, 1/2); an Armijo line search is used; the implementation is matrix-free with the tridiagonal Jacobian stored in three vectors (pp. 2-3). A design move: a failed line search (non-descent direction) is read as "an indication that the precision level was too low", and precision is switched up (p. 2). Algorithm 2 is a meta-strategy over precision levels p_1 < ... < p_L, with the accuracy target depending on the level (p. 6). The motivation: "most of the work of Newton's method typically occurs before the transition to fast quadratic convergence" (p. 6).
  - Transferable technique: use the globalization's failure (line-search failure) as a cheap online detector that a cheaper inner approximation has run out of accuracy, and escalate only then (pp. 2, 4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - F: R^n → R^n twice continuously differentiable (p. 2).
  - A root exists. No safeguards for convergence to residual minimizers are implemented, with a pointer to Gould-Leyffer-Toint's multidimensional filter [18] for that case (p. 2).
  - Energy model: compute energy, transfer energy and cache-miss rate all linear in precision p (eq. 5), giving E(p) = knE_c p + lnrE_t p² (eq. 6). In practice the linear term dominates because r ∈ [10^-6, 10^-3] (p. 7).
  - Attainability: precision p usable while ε > 2^{-p+s}, with s = 3 lg p − 7 + δ and δ = 8 "for simplicity". This gives single precision down to ≈ 10^-5 and double down to ≈ 3·10^-14 (p. 7).
  - The rate model is linear convergence ε_{k+1} ≤ ε_k/λ, with a quadratic variant (pp. 7-8).
  - Implementation is "somewhat simplistic" (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis devices**: The analysis is an energy-equivalence argument:
  - the baseline energy E(p_2)·lg(1/ε)/lg λ (7) set equal to the hybrid energy (8) bounds the number of high-precision iterations k_2 (9);
  - substituting gives the accuracy bound (10), which decreases in k_1, so "one should choose k1 to be as large as possible";
  - this is capped by the attainability limits at both precisions, giving (11) and the improvement-factor lower bound (12), which relates the gain to ε, p_i − s_i and the energy ratio (p. 7);
  - the quadratic-convergence analogue (13) changes the bound by a modest factor (p. 8).

  The bound is checked against data. For Rosenbrock (Table III), (12) gives ≥ 5.5·10^6, while the measured factor is 10^8, "more than an order of magnitude short", so the bound is "not necessarily tight" (p. 7).
  - Transferable technique: write "same budget" as an equation between the baseline cost and the hybrid cost, solve for the remaining iterations at the expensive level, and turn that into a bound on the achievable accuracy; then compare the bound to one measured case (p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: Setup (p. 3):
  - hardware: Dell T1700, Intel i7-4770 with AVX2, CentOS 7, kernel 4.3, IFORT 16.0.3;
  - energy measured with RAPL through etrace2, a tool written by one of the authors;
  - noise control: the same process pinned to all 8 logical CPUs with taskset, and the median of 31 measurements per data point;
  - problems: Laplace (well-conditioned, linear) and chained Rosenbrock (nonlinear, "notoriously ill-conditioned"), both at n = 100000.

  Gains experiment: four variants, scalar/SIMD × single/double (Figs. 1-2, p. 4), explained by cache doubling and vector width (p. 4). Reinvestment protocol: four numbered steps with an explicit success criterion, "If the energy from this step does not exceed the energy from step 2, then the reinvestment experiment was successful!" (p. 4). Results:
  - Laplace: improvement factor 10^4 at ε = 10^-6; 10^5 was possible but over budget, 10^6 gave line-search errors (Table I, Fig. 3, p. 4);
  - Rosenbrock: 10^11 at ε = 10^-2 (Table II, Figs. 4-5, p. 5) and 10^8 at ε = 10^-5 (Table III, Fig. 6, p. 5);
  - energy versus improvement-factor gradations cluster by number of extra outer iterations (Tables IV-V, Figs. 7-8, p. 6).

  Standard deviations are reported, and measurement error is acknowledged (p. 6). Switching points were chosen by hand: "We have not yet developed an algorithm to decide automatically" (p. 4). Failures (line-search errors) mark the limits of every configuration (pp. 4-5).
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper builds on Palem's inexact computing [2]-[4] ("established the principle of trading application quality for disproportionately large energy savings", p. 2), inexact Newton [13] and JFNK [14] (p. 2), and mixed-precision refinement and tools [9]-[12] (p. 2). Its claimed novelty is the second "reinvestment" phase (p. 2). It cites the exascale study [7] and abstract machine models [8] for "communication becomes the major source of energy consumption" (p. 2). It ties back to weather and climate work [22, 24-26] (p. 8). Self-citation: [18] Gould-Leyffer-Toint filter (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: A boxed "Significance Statement" claims that "smart use of a current machine has leapfrogged a machine generation" (p. 1). A "mini" table of contents explains the section plan (p. 2). The paper names its own paradox: "paradoxically, use reduced precision to improve the quality" (abstract p. 1), and "somewhat paradoxical procedure" (p. 8). It is candid about limits ("somewhat simplistic", p. 2; "not necessarily tight", p. 7; "not yet developed an algorithm", p. 4). The closing outlook moves to societal value, weather and climate (p. 8).
  - Transferable technique: when the main result sounds paradoxical, name the paradox in the abstract and resolve it with one budget picture (the "X"/"Y" energy bars of Figs. 3-6, pp. 5-6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is a demonstration plus a simple model. It shows that reinvested reduced-precision energy can improve accuracy by 10^4-10^11 at equal energy (pp. 4-5), and that energy can be cut "by a factor of 2.x" at equal quality (the "dual" problem, p. 8). The limits are concrete:
  - two tridiagonal test problems (p. 3);
  - manual switching points (p. 4);
  - no nonzero-residual safeguards (p. 2);
  - an energy model with linear scaling assumptions (p. 7) and a loose bound (p. 7).

  Future work: a methodology for jointly choosing many quality-versus-cost "knobs" (model, method, discretization, asynchrony), which "are not independent" (p. 8). Per SKILL.md Trajectory this line was then left (no later statement in this batch).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 (calibrated claims on fairness-stated baselines) ✅ evidence: the fairness rule is explicit (equal energy to the double-precision baseline, p. 2, p. 4), the fair unit is energy measured by RAPL with the median of 31 runs and standard deviations (pp. 3-6), failures (line-search errors) are reported as limits (pp. 4-5), the bound is compared with measurement and admitted loose (p. 7), and the manual switching is disclosed (p. 4).

- Method 6 (read the solver's numbers) ⚠ variant: the solver's failure signal (line-search failure) is used online as evidence about the arithmetic, not about the formulation. Precision is escalated rather than the model repaired (pp. 2, 4).

**Transferable techniques**:
- An equal-budget experimental protocol: measure the baseline's cost to reach ε, then search for the largest improvement factor the hybrid reaches without exceeding that cost; report energy, standard deviation and the outer/inner iteration split per configuration (pp. 3-6).
- Energy-measurement hygiene: pin identical processes to every logical CPU, take the median of 31 runs, and report the standard deviation (p. 3).
- Use globalization failure (a non-descent direction in the line search) as the trigger to raise the inner precision or accuracy (pp. 2, 4).
- Prove a simple budget-equivalence bound, then confront it with one measured case and say by how much it is loose (p. 7).
