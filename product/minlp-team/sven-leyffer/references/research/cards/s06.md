### S116 · Modeling mixed-integer constrained optimal control problems in AMPL (IFAC Proceedings Volumes 2012, doi:10.3182/20120215-3-at-3016.00199)

**Read level**: full (pp. 1–6, all sections and the AMPL listing).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Christian Kirches (first author), Hans Georg Bock (Heidelberg IWR) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: short conference companion of TACO (S076); MINLP modelling infrastructure carried into mixed-integer optimal control.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Modelling systems for ODE/DAE optimization are tied to one or a few discretizations and solvers, while the MINLP community "most often considers problems without dynamics" and uses AMPL, through which NEOS exposes many MI(N)LP codes (abstract, p. 1; §1, p. 1). The entry point is a gap between two communities' tooling.
- **D2 Core idea**: Add three AMPL user functions (`diff`, `eval`, `integral`) and a suffix `type` so that an AMPL model states a mixed-integer optimal control problem (MIOCP) without encoding a discretization; roles of variables (time, states, algebraic states, controls with discretization type u0/u1/u1c/u3/u3c, parameters) are inferred by rules (§2, pp. 2–3). The solver side (TACO, MUSCOD-II, MS-MINTOC) applies its own discretization (§3, pp. 3–4). What is not changed: the AMPL language and implementation (abstract, p. 1).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MIOCP class (1) with ODE/DAE dynamics, integer controls from a finite set, free end time, path and point constraints (pp. 1–2); DAE index 1, with regularity left to the modeller and checked at runtime by the integrator (§2.1, p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a (no theorems).
- **D5 Experiments**: none; one heavy-duty truck model (4 states, 16 binary gear selectors with a sum-to-one constraint) written in the extended AMPL as a showcase, no solve times or solutions reported (§4, pp. 4–5).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: software/modelling contribution; MUSCOD-II and MS-MINTOC made available on NEOS through AMPL and TACO (§5, p. 5). Future: fully implicit DAEs, implicitly switched systems, nonsmooth constraints, multistage problems, collocation schemes so that more NEOS (MI)NLP solvers can be used (p. 5). Library contribution through mintoc.de with best known solutions (§4.4, p. 5).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (evidence ✅): structure exposed to the solver through the modelling layer rather than hidden in a hand-coded discretization; extension done through existing hooks (user functions and suffixes) with no intrusive change (abstract, p. 1; §2.5, p. 3).
- Method 2 (evidence ✅): the home MINLP infrastructure (AMPL, NEOS, MINLP solvers listed in §1.2, p. 2) carried into mixed-integer optimal control.
- Method 4 (evidence ✅, weak): models deposited in the wiki library mintoc.de "together with best known optimal solutions" and an open call for contributions (§4.4, p. 5).
- New-pattern candidate: separate the model from the discretization (state the infinite-dimensional problem in the modelling language; let the solver choose and refine the discretization) (§2.5, p. 3; §5, p. 5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Extend a modelling language through its existing extension points (user functions as markers, suffixes as type tags) so the extension runs anywhere the language runs (§2, pp. 2–3).
- Infer variable roles from where a variable appears (e.g. inside `diff`) instead of asking the user to declare everything (§2.2, pp. 2–3).

**Quotes**:
- "These extensions are easily realized and do not require intrusive changes to the AMPL language standard or implementation itself." (p. 1)
- "Uncluttered by any discretization code, the resulting AMPL model is easy to read, maintain, and extend." (p. 5)

### S076 · TACO: a toolkit for AMPL control optimization (Mathematical Programming Computation 2013, doi:10.1007/s12532-013-0054-7; read as ANL preprint P1948-0911)

**Read level**: partial. Read version: ANL/MCS preprint P1948-0911 of 22 Nov 2011 (pp. 1, 3), not the MPC typeset version; cite as [S076 preprint pp. …]. Read: abstract, §1–§4, §5.2–§5.4, §6, §7 (pp. 3–24). Skimmed: §5.1 multiple-shooting description (pp. 18–19). Not read: appendices A–C (pp. 24–35, data structures, functions, error codes).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Christian Kirches (first author, Heidelberg) (p. 3) · **Position in trajectory**: full journal version of the S116 idea, with toolkit, checks and solver coupling.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: AMPL was designed for finite-dimensional problems; modelling ODE/DAE optimal control in it forces an explicit discretization into the model; existing dynamic-optimization environments (gPROMS, PROPT, Dymola, Modelica, JModelica) are "closely tied to one or a few selected discretization schemes and solvers only" (§1.3, p. 5; §2, p. 6).
- **D2 Core idea**: TACO reads AMPL `stub.nl` files, infers the optimal-control structure (via the DAG of each expression), checks it and builds a database for an optimal control solver; demonstrated by coupling to MUSCOD-II and MS-MINTOC (abstract, p. 3; §4, pp. 13–17; §5, pp. 18–22). AMPL's automatic sparse derivatives replace a separate AD tool and finite differences inside the IVP sensitivity computation, where "easily in excess of 80%" of runtime goes (§5.2, p. 20).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MIOCP (1) including a point least-squares term (p. 4); explicit ODEs only (§2.2, p. 8); twice continuously differentiable functions, checked by rejecting logical, relational and nonsmooth operators (§4.2, pp. 15–16); index-1 DAEs, regularity left to the modeller (§2.2, p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a; the analogue is a list of automatic consistency checks (one `diff` per state, explicit ODEs, matching DAE counts, Mayer term independent of controls, no nesting) run before the solver is invoked, with human-readable errors naming the offending item (§4.2, pp. 15–16) and a `verbose` diagnostic table (§4.3, p. 16).
- **D5 Experiments**: demonstration only: COPS fluid-flow BVP and a Lotka–Volterra fishing MIOCP solved inside AMPL (Figs. 5, 7, pp. 12–13); 18 control and parameter-estimation models (10 from COPS) contributed to mintoc.de (Table 1, pp. 12–14); no timings or comparisons. The abstract and §1.4 promise three examples (pp. 3, 6), §3 gives two (p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: software (open-source TACO, NEOS availability) (§1.4, p. 6). Limits stated concretely: fully implicit DAEs need DAG manipulation the AMPL solver library does not support; multistage problems lack contextual information in AMPL syntax; nonsmooth operators could open complementarity- and vanishing-constrained control (§6, pp. 22–23). Future: collocation schemes so that NLP and MINLP solvers can be used directly (§7, p. 24).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (evidence ✅): pass structure to the solver ("the nature of the differential equation, which is lost in the COPS model", p. 10); reuse what the modelling system already computes (sparse derivatives) inside the integrator (§5.2–5.3, pp. 20–21).
- Method 4 (evidence ✅): 18 models added to a public library with known solutions; COPS models re-expressed side by side with the original encoding (Figs. 3–4, pp. 10–11; Table 1, p. 14).
- Method 6 (evidence ✅, weak): formulation checks (smoothness, structural consistency) before any solver run, errors reported per named constraint (§4.2, pp. 15–16).
- New-pattern candidate: separate the model from the discretization (same as S116; §7, p. 23).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before handing a model to a solver, walk each expression DAG and reject what the method's assumptions exclude (nonsmooth operators, wrong constraint types), naming the offending constraint (§4.2, pp. 15–16).
- Provide a verbose dump of the detected problem structure so users can see how their model was interpreted (§4.3, p. 16).
- When presenting a modelling extension, show the new model next to the old hand-discretized one (COPS) to make the gain visible (Figs. 3–4, pp. 10–11).

**Quotes**:
- "In addition, our extension allows us to pass more structural information to the solver, such as the nature of the differential equation, which is lost in the COPS model, which is just interpreted as a large NLP." (p. 10)
- "Contrary to prior approaches at modeling such problems in AMPL by explicitly encoding a discretization scheme for the dynamic parts of the model, our approach separates model equations and the discretization scheme." (p. 23)

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: as S037: system-level design and dispatch of a combined heat and power DG system for a commercial building, posed as a large nonconvex MINLP because of variable fuel-cell efficiency and tank temperature (abstract, p. 3; §1, pp. 4–5).
- **D2 Core idea**: as S037: McCormick/αBB envelopes give an MILP lower bound (U); fixing efficiencies and tank temperature gives an MILP heuristic (H) for upper bounds (§3.2, p. 19; contents p. 2). Bound tightening raises the lower bound by 1.6 % on average (p. 21).

- **D8 Contribution and limits**: as S037; future uncertainty in demand, output and prices (p. 29). Revision contrast with S037 recorded in the S037 card.

**Method links**:
- Method 5 (variant ⚠): competitors run with a warm start and a stated gap/time rule (p. 26), and the MINOTAUR gaps are flagged as not valid global bounds (Table 4 note, p. 27); but the solver set is limited to AMPL solvers, some on a different machine (NEOS), and the instance costs were chosen so the technique has something to show (p. 25).
- Method 6 (evidence ✅): see S037.
- New-pattern candidate: custom bounding pair for instances no solver can handle (problem-specific relaxation for the lower bound, fix-and-linearize heuristic for the upper bound, reported gap) (pp. 19, 27).

**Transferable techniques**:
- State openly in the preprint why instance parameters were chosen, so a later revision can replace the choice with a justified scenario (p. 25; compare S037 p. 23).

**Quotes**:
- "we find that higher capital costs result in the “no DG” solution for most instances, which limits the ability to demonstrate our bounding techniques." (p. 25)
- "In all instances, we provide the solvers the “no DG” solution as an initial feasible solution, set an optimality gap of 1%, and enforce a time limit of 36,000 seconds." (p. 26)

### S073 · Fast response to infection spread and cyber attacks on large-scale networks (Journal of Complex Networks 2013, doi:10.1093/comnet/cnt009; read as arXiv:1207.0133v1)

**Read level**: full. Read version: arXiv v1 of 30 Jun 2012 (p. 1), not the JCN typeset version; cite as [S073 arXiv pp. …]. All prose read (pp. 1–11); the graph-label dump inside Fig. 2 on p. 6 is extraction noise.

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ilya Safro (Argonne MCS) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: follow-on to the Open Science Grid response work [1] and the Goldberg–Leyffer–Safro response model [17] (pp. 3, 7, 11–12).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: optimal response to infection spread (SIS model) as a binary program: keep a weighted number of links alive subject to per-node infection-probability thresholds, a nonconvex integer nonlinear program, NP-complete [17] (§2, pp. 2–3). Entry point: BARON and Couenne "cannot handle problems with tens of thousands of nodes" because the tree explodes (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: an algebraic-multigrid-style multiscale heuristic: coarsen by algebraic distance (5), traversing nodes in ascending infection level; coarse problem (6) with an added linear term for internal edges; uncoarsen by C- and F-node interpolation, Gauss–Seidel-like flips and localized refinement subproblems (7) on small connected subsets, parallelizable in red-black order (§3, pp. 3–5). MINOTAUR solves the coarsest (40 variables) and local (15 variables) problems exactly (§6.4, p. 11).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: independence of infection events between neighbours; infection probabilities and thresholds aggregated as normalized sums, "according to the application" (p. 2; §6.3, pp. 10–11); one nonzero per row of the restriction (p. 10).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a; no bounds or guarantees; rigorous upper and lower bounds named as future work (§5, p. 9).
- **D5 Experiments**: 200 small Erdős–Rényi graphs with known optima (about half solved optimally, the rest within 90–100 %, Fig. 3, pp. 6–7); HIV network (25,090 nodes) and Gnutella P2P (30 seeds) against iterated local search (ILS): equal or up to 20 % better, 100–200× faster (pp. 7–9); 100 diverse large graphs, ratio up to 132 at average degree 240 (p. 9). Only ILS as large-scale baseline; no lower/upper bounds at scale.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic/heuristic; code released [27] (p. 2). Future: theory for bounds; SIR model with PDE-based constraints (§5, p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 (variant ⚠): his own MINLP toolkit (MINOTAUR) used as an exact building block on tiny subproblems inside a heuristic (§6.4, p. 11), not integrated at iteration level.
- Method 5 (variant ⚠): calibration against exact optima on small instances first (p. 6), but the large-scale claims rest on one heuristic baseline (ILS) and objective ratios, with no bound (pp. 7–9); the massive set includes graphs whose link to the application "is not straightforward (if at all)" (p. 9).
- Heuristic 8 (evidence ✅): size arithmetic decides the method: exact global solvers handle a few hundred variables, the instances have tens of thousands of nodes (p. 3).
- New-pattern candidate: calibrate a heuristic on small exactly-solvable instances before scaling up (p. 6).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before reporting large-scale heuristic results, generate a few hundred small instances, solve them exactly, and plot heuristic/optimal ratios (Fig. 3, pp. 6–7).
- Coarsen a graph optimization problem by aggregating variables and carrying intra-aggregate interactions as a new linear objective term (6) (p. 5).
- Refine a projected solution by solving many tiny exact subproblems with boundary values fixed, in a parallel colouring order (p. 5).

**Quotes**:
- "Such methods are often more useful in practice than are optimal ones that take a long time to converge even for small instances." (p. 3)
- "Theoretical work involves rigorous analysis in order to identify upper and lower bounds." (p. 9)

### S079 · Regularizing bilevel nonlinear programs by lifting (Argonne National Laboratory 2013, preprint ANL/MCS-P4076-0613)

**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 2–3), motivating example §2.1 (p. 5), lifting §2.2–2.4 with Theorem 2.1 statement and proof skeleton (pp. 5–9), Propositions 2.1–2.2 and Corollary 2.1 (pp. 10–11), §3 (pp. 11–13), §4 text and summary (pp. 13–16), conclusion (pp. 19–20). Skimmed: §2 definitions (pp. 3–4), proof algebra (pp. 6–7). Not read: Tables 4.1–4.2 row by row (pp. 16–19).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Kathrin Hatz (first author), Johannes P. Schlöder, Hans Georg Bock (Heidelberg IWR) (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: continues his MPEC-as-NLP line (SQP for MPCCs, Fletcher et al. [11], MacMPEC [13]).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: the SQP convergence theory for MPCCs [11] assumes MPCC-LICQ; one would expect a bilevel NLP whose lower level satisfies LICQ and SSOC to give an MPCC with MPCC-LICQ, and a two-variable example (2.6) shows it does not (abstract, p. 2; §1, p. 3; §2.1, p. 5). Equivalence of the MPCC with the bilevel problem without convexity is set aside explicitly (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: lift each lower-level inequality, h(x,y) ≥ w, and penalize w in the upper objective (ℓ1 exact with w ≥ 0, or squared ℓ2) (2.11)–(2.12), (2.27) (pp. 5–6, 9). The extra |D| variables regularize the degenerate rows of the Jacobian. Generalized to any MPCC by lifting h(x,y) ≥ 0 (§3, pp. 11–13). Unchanged: the solver (standard SQP, filterSQP) (p. 16).
- **D3 Assumptions**: C² functions; lower-level LICQ and SSOC; π > ‖ν*‖∞ for the exact penalty (dropped for the smooth w ≥ 0 version); the smooth version loses MPCC-LICQ where h_i = z_i = w_i = 0 (pp. 2, 10–11).
- **D4 Analysis**: Theorem 2.1 (lifted MPCC satisfies MPCC-LICQ) by writing the active-constraint Jacobian in blocks (2.14)–(2.15) and a Schur-complement rank argument (pp. 6–7); Remark 2.1: without lifting, MPCC-LICQ needs |D| = ∅ (strict complementarity) or assumptions on the number of upper-level variables (pp. 6–8); exact-penalty equivalence of KKT points (Props. 2.1–2.2, Cor. 2.1, pp. 10–11).
- **D5 Experiments**: filterSQP in AMPL on MacMPEC, iterations as the measure "given that all problems have been solved with filterSQP", performance profiles; bilevel set 273 → 222 (ℓ1) / 205 (ℓ2) iterations, general MPCCs 1288 → 871 / 1090; failures labelled (I), (ERR), (no conv), (w ≠ 0), (fail QP); six bilevel and fourteen general problems infeasible unlifted but solved lifted; two general problems solved only unlifted; π chosen per problem (pp. 13–15). The bilevel set is called 47 problems on p. 13 and 48 on p. 14.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory plus a reformulation usable with any SQP solver (p. 16); one example (incidset1-8) shows linear vs superlinear convergence (Fig. 4.3, p. 15). Limits: π needs tuning, the smooth exact variant loses the CQ at triply degenerate points (pp. 11, 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 (evidence ✅): smallest breaking example (2.6) against an expected inheritance property, Jacobian rank computed (rank 5, p. 5), then the remedy checked on the same example (2.26) and run (12 → 7/8 iterations, p. 13).
- Method 6 (evidence ✅): repair the formulation, keep the method: "does not require a tailored solver" (p. 16).
- Method 2 (evidence ✅): filter SQP and the MPEC-as-NLP stance carried into bilevel programs (p. 3).
- Method 4 (evidence ✅): the whole MacMPEC set used, problems unchanged by lifting excluded with a stated rule (pp. 13–14).
- Method 5 (variant ⚠): same-solver rule and failures in the tables (pp. 13–15), but penalty parameters tuned per problem on the test set with a rule of thumb given afterwards (p. 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To restore a constraint qualification, add one slack-like lifting variable per degenerate constraint and drive it to zero with an exact penalty; check rank on the block Jacobian (pp. 5–7, 9–11).
- Prove the lifted and original problems share KKT points by comparing dual-feasibility systems with the multiplier bound π > ‖ν*‖∞ (Props. 2.1–2.2, p. 10).
- Show the mechanism on one instance by plotting constraint violation per iteration on a log scale (linear vs superlinear) (Fig. 4.3, p. 15).

**Quotes**:
- "One would expect the resulting mathematical program with complementarity constraints (MPCC), whose constraints are the first-order optimality conditions of the lower-level NLP, to satisfy an MPEC-LICQ. We provide an example which demonstrates that this is not the case." (p. 2)
- "Furthermore, the lifting proposed in this paper does not require a tailored solver, standard SQP-solver can be used after adapting the problem formulation." (p. 16)

### S142 · A new perspective on convex relaxations of sparse SVM (Proceedings of the 2013 SIAM International Conference on Data Mining, doi:10.1137/1.9781611972832.50; read as ANL preprint P2049-0212)

**Read level**: full. Read version: ANL/MCS preprint P2049-0212 of 11 Jun 2012 (pp. 1–2), not the SDM typeset version; cite as [S142 preprint pp. …]. All sections read (pp. 2–12).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Noam Goldberg (first author), Todd Munson (Argonne MCS) (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: relaxes the MINLP sparse SVM of Guan, Gray and Leyffer (2009) (p. 3; refs p. 11).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: sparse SVM with a zero-norm penalty; the MINLP (3) of Guan et al. uses w_j² ≤ z_j u_j, which is nonconvex and, in the paper's words, makes continuous relaxations "violate constraint qualification", so Guan et al. fell back on big-M constraints; existing convex relaxations (Chan et al. QCQP/SDP) rest on a norm inequality, LP-SVM on big-M (§§2–3, pp. 3–4).
- **D2 Core idea**: read (3c) as a perspective constraint and rewrite it as a rotated second-order cone ‖(2w_j, u_j − z_j)‖ ≤ u_j + z_j; relax z ∈ [0,1] and replace the zero-norm penalty by Σz_j ≤ r (CQ-SSVM (5), pp. 4–5); apply the same device to the slack variables for a zero-one loss (CQ01-SSVM (8), pp. 5–6). Unchanged: the hinge-loss SVM core; r = n gives the standard SVM (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions**: integer data with |A_ij| ≤ U for the exactness result; penalty parameters c, d large (Prop. 1, p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis**: Prop. 1 (MINLP (7) solves the zero-one-loss problem (6) for c, d above an explicit bound) by contradiction through a basic feasible solution, Cramer's rule and the Hadamard inequality (pp. 5–6); integrality of z whenever ‖w‖₀ ≤ r at the relaxation optimum (§6, p. 6); perspective vs big-M picture (Fig. 1, p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: SDPT3 4.0; relaxation quality on Ionosphere by plotting ‖w‖₀ vs r (points on or below the diagonal are integral) (pp. 7–8); SDP dropped after 40.99 s vs 1.87 s (p. 7); 9 UCI sets, 5×5-fold CV, c by inner 3-fold CV, own r = 2 because it "seemed to provide a reasonable tradeoff", competitor r = 0.1 as recommended by its authors (p. 8); CQ01-SSVM best average accuracy 86.10 % vs 85.36–85.76 %, QCQP sparsest (Table 2, p. 10); LP-SVM failures on 2/10 KDD runs reported (p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: a convex conic relaxation without big-M, faster than QCQP-SSVM, tighter in the tests (pp. 7, 9); CQ01-SSVM more expensive than QP alternatives (p. 10). Future: dynamic constraint generation and first-order conic solvers for large or online data; adversarial label noise (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 (evidence ✅): a CQ failure in the natural MINLP formulation is named (p. 4) and the formulation is changed (perspective → SOC) before any algorithm work; big-M avoided (p. 3).
- Method 2 (evidence ✅): MINLP perspective-reformulation machinery (Günlük–Linderoth) carried into machine learning (p. 2).
- Method 5 (variant ⚠): competitor run at its authors' recommended setting and c tuned by CV for all methods (p. 8), but the own parameter r is set by inspection on the data (p. 8) and the SDP baseline is dropped after one dataset (p. 7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn a bilinear indicator constraint x² ≤ z·u with u, z ≥ 0 into a three-dimensional rotated second-order cone to get a convex relaxation without big-M (p. 4).
- Judge a relaxation by how often its indicator variables come out integral, plotting achieved support size against the cardinality budget (pp. 6–8).

**Quotes**:
- "Further, even general nonlinear programming solvers have difficulty in computing local minima for continuous relaxations of (3) because the constraints (3c) violate constraint qualification." (p. 4)
- "In all cases CQ-SSVM produced significantly sparser classifiers than LP-SVM." (p. 9)

### S037 · A mixed-integer nonlinear program for the optimal design and dispatch of distributed generation systems (Optimization and Engineering 2014, doi:10.1007/s11081-013-9226-6)

**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1–3), §2 opening and model description (pp. 4–5), §3 in full (pp. 15–21), §4 in full (pp. 21–29), §5 (pp. 29–30). Not read: the full formulation §2.2–2.3 (pp. 5–15).

**Role**: supplement (published version of S129) · **Coauthors**: Kristopher A. Pruitt (first author), Alexandra M. Newman (corresponding), Robert J. Braun (Colorado School of Mines) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: application MINLP in energy systems; Leyffer's role is not stated in the text.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: DG design and dispatch studies either use simulation/evolutionary methods without optimality guarantees, or branch-and-bound models that drop operating detail; the paper wants global optimality with realistic operation (on/off status, ramping, variable efficiency, temperature-dependent thermal storage) (§1, pp. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea**: all nonlinearities are bilinear and trilinear equalities (p. 15); replace each by its convex envelope to get an MILP lower bound (U) (§3.2, p. 16), tighten variable bounds by optimization-based bound tightening on the three bounds that respond (pp. 17–18), and get upper bounds from an MILP (H) obtained by fixing fuel-cell efficiencies and tank temperature, with the fixed values derived from the (U) solution and a temperature fixed-point recursion ("power DG" and "CHP DG" variants, best of three kept) (§3.3, pp. 18–21).
- **D3 Assumptions**: deterministic data (p. 4); simulated EnergyPlus hourly loads with a justification paragraph (p. 21); capital costs at about 70 % of pre-commercial values, presented as projected future costs (p. 23).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a (bounds valid by construction; no convergence result for the bounding scheme inside B&B, which is proposed but not implemented, pp. 3, 29).
