### S127 · State elimination for mixed‐integer optimal control of partial differential equations by semigroup theory (Optimal Control Applications and Methods 2022, doi:10.1002/oca.2861)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. Thönen, S. Sager · **Note**: open-access PDF found (Wayback copy of the author-hosted wiki file; ANL/MCS-P9481-0521 on p. 1) and saved as NEW-PDF for the next round; not read here.

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: MIPDECOs are entered as a class that is "powerful" for modelling but "challenging in terms of theory and computation"; the targeted subclass is time-dependent PDEs that have the structure of an abstract ODE in function space (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: eliminate the states once, before optimization, using that abstract-ODE (semigroup) structure, so the optimizer never recomputes the states for every time step (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: a state-elimination reformulation with theoretical results plus numerical experiments (abstract); the abstract states no limitation or future direction (not read).
  - Transferable technique: when the PDE is an abstract ODE in function space, precompute the solution operator (semigroup) once and substitute it for the states, turning the MIPDECO into a reduced problem in the controls only (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ evidence — the PDE-constrained mixed-integer class (MIPDECO) is one of his extension areas, and here the work is a reformulation that makes it tractable for mixed-integer machinery (abstract).
**Quotes**: "We propose a highly efficient state elimination approach for MIPDECOs that are governed by partial differential equations that have the structure of an abstract ordinary differential equation in function space." (abstract) · "This allows us to avoid repeated calculations of the states for all time steps, and our approach is applied only once before starting the optimization." (abstract)

### S166 · A Bilevel Optimization Framework for Dynamically Correcting Imbalance in Two-class Classification Problems (IJCNN 2025, doi:10.1109/ijcnn64981.2025.11227944)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core (per INDEX.md; no open full text found) · **Coauthors**: K. Medlin (first author), K. Raghavan

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: entry by criticising a standard practice: indiscriminate oversampling enlarges the data set without regard to the effect of the added samples, which in the presence of noise lowers accuracy and introduces underfitting (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: a bilevel formulation: after the usual optimization over model parameters, optimize over samples of the majority training data, accepting only data points that improve the loss, so the method selects an optimal subset of the majority class (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: a bilevel data-selection framework for imbalanced two-class classification; reported F1 scores "up to 10%" above state-of-the-art methods (abstract). Limitations and baselines not stated in the abstract (not read).

(abstract) · "Experimental results show our proposed technique with F1 scores up to 10% higher than state-of-the-art methods." (abstract)

### S172 · maestro (US DOE Software 169, 2022, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (DOE software record description) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: M. Krishnamoorthy, T. Peterka, O. Yildiz, S. Mrenna

- **D1 (problem/entry)**: fitting Monte Carlo event-generator simulations to experimental data: choose simulation parameters that minimise a least-squares misfit when the simulation is expensive and noisy (abstract).
- **D2 (key idea)**: MÆSTRO (Multi-fidelity Adaptive Ensemble Stochastic Trust Region Optimization): a derivative-free stochastic trust-region method that, each iteration, builds local approximations of both the simulation signal and the simulation noise from runs at design points inside the trust region, solves the resulting closed-form subproblem, then moves the trust region (abstract).
- **D8 (contribution)**: software (a "plug n play" solver) rather than a paper; convergence theory and test results are not described in the record (not read).
  - Transferable technique: model the noise as well as the signal of a stochastic simulator over trust-region design points, and optimize the surrogate in closed form inside the region (abstract).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ evidence — the trust-region NLP toolkit carried into noisy, expensive simulation calibration (abstract). Method 1 ✅ evidence (software level) — the method is released as a stand-alone solver (abstract; DOE software record).
**Quotes**: "This problem is solved using a stochastic trust-region optimization algorithm where in each iteration, a local approximation of the simulation signal and of the simulation noise is constructed over data, which is obtained by running the simulation at strategically placed design points within the trust-region around the current iterate." (abstract)
