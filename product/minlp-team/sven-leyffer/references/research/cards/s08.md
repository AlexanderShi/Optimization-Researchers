### H001 · Design, Optimization, and Control of Complex Interconnected Systems (DocSis) (DOE-ASCR Applied Mathematics PI Meeting White Paper 2017, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.5307067.v1)
**Read level**: full. All 4 pages (2 pages of text, 2 of references). Title, the four authors and "Sven Leyffer (corresponding author)" confirmed on p. 1; dated August 14, 2017 (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Mihai Anitescu, Todd Munson, Stefan Wild · **Place in trajectory**: Argonne agenda-setting document for the DOE applied-math program; lists the problem classes the optimization group wants funded.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The move "from forward simulation to design, optimization, and control" produces problems that "do not fit into traditional optimization paradigms" (p. 1). Four classes are named with their challenges: mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimization (nuclear core design, well placement, oil-spill and wildfire response, p. 1); time-dependent systems and dynamic programming (no compression of the time dimension besides checkpointing, p. 1); nonsmooth constraints and discrete optimization in gene networks and switched systems (p. 2); and design and scheduling of DOE facilities, where the problems are linear but too large for commercial solvers (p. 2).
  - Transferable: in a white paper, give each new class one paragraph: applications with citations, why the standard algorithm fails, what is unknown.
- **D2 Core idea**: An agenda, not a method. It places optimization as both a user and a provider of paradigms for UQ (stochastic programming, optimal experimental design), AD (gradient computation as a discrete optimization problem; lighter AD requirements without slower convergence) and linear solvers (structure-exploiting kernels) (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions**: n/a.
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a.
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: a programmatic contribution. For MIPDECO it states that "traditional algorithms such as nonlinear branch-and-bound are computationally prohibitive" (p. 1). It cites Leyffer's own oil-spill MINLP papers as instances (refs 21-22, p. 4) and his in-situ analysis scheduling paper (ref 16, p. 3). No concrete research plan beyond the class descriptions.

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: step 4, naming new classes publicly with their challenges (MIPDECO, nonsmooth/discrete gene networks, facility scheduling) (pp. 1-2). Heuristic 8 evidence ✅ (weak): nonlinear B&B called computationally prohibitive for MIPDECO (p. 1), stated without the arithmetic.
**Transferable techniques**:
- In an agenda document, pair each problem class with what the field's standard solver cannot do (nonlinear B&B for MIPDECO, commercial MIP for facility scheduling) (pp. 1-2).
**Quotes**:
- "Very little is known about this challenging class of problems" (p. 1)
- "huge discrete optimization problems that though linear are well beyond the capabilities of even the best commercial solvers" (p. 2)

### S036 · Optimal design and dispatch of a system of diesel generators, photovoltaics and batteries for remote locations (Optimization and Engineering 2017, doi:10.1007/s11081-017-9355-4)
**Read level**: partial. File is the author draft "DRAFT as of November 27, 2016" (p. 1), 41 pages; Leyffer last author (p. 1). Read: abstract and introduction (pp. 1-4), linearization §2.4 (pp. 15-19), heuristic overview §3 (p. 19), §4.2-4.4 numerical results and solution quality (pp. 25-32), conclusions (pp. 33-34). Skimmed the full formulation (pp. 5-15) and the instance data (pp. 21-25); not read: Appendix Algorithm 3 (pp. 39-41) and the references.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Michael S. Scioletti, Alexandra M. Newman, Johanna K. Goodman, Alexander J. Zolan (Colorado School of Mines, Georgia Tech, UT Austin, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: applied MINLP for energy systems with Newman's group, ONR-funded (p. 33); Leyffer's share not stated.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Joint design (which generators, PV and batteries to buy) and hourly dispatch for a remote site such as a forward operating base, over a year (8,760 hours) (pp. 1-2). The entry is the common split into design-then-dispatch, which "does not guarantee global optimality"; designing for maximum load alone can use 50% more fuel (p. 2). HOMER, the standard tool, simulates fixed dispatch strategies (p. 2); earlier MIPs shorten the horizon or omit battery detail (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: A nonconvex MINLP (P) with a detailed battery model (one quadratic term, bilinear terms in power and lifecycle constraints) is replaced by a MIP (U) (p. 15). The quadratic term is dropped because manufacturer data are linear (p. 15). Binary-times-continuous products are linearized exactly with bound constraints (24a-e, p. 18). Continuous bilinear products get convex-envelope (McCormick) under-estimators (25a-h, pp. 18-19). A heuristic H restricts candidate technologies (Algorithms 1-2) and builds a myopic dispatch as a warm start that is also feasible for (P) (p. 19).
  - Transferable: split the bilinear terms by type: binary × continuous gets an exact big-M reformulation, continuous × continuous gets McCormick under-estimators; then say which error each introduces.
- **D3 Assumptions**: deterministic inputs; the year-long load variability serves as a proxy for robustness to stochastic loads; two- or multi-stage models are called intractable (p. 2). Battery banks are modeled as one unit; the bus and converter only through efficiencies (p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis**: no theorems. The quality argument is an a posteriori audit: the nonlinear power and lifecycle quantities are recomputed from (U)'s solution and compared (eqs. 26-29, Table 10, pp. 30-32).
- **D5 Experiments**: (P) on 24-hour instances with BARON 12.5, Couenne 0.4 and BONMIN 1.7 at default settings (5% gap or 3 h): BARON solves 4 of 14, BONMIN 2, Couenne none within the gap; (U) solves all 14 in under a second (pp. 25-27). Year-long (U) in CPLEX 12.5.1 (up to 1.78M constraints, 263k binaries), all within 5% in 3 h or less with the heuristic warm start (p. 28). Fuel savings of hybrids are 30% on average and up to 50% (p. 30).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: modelling plus computation. Limits stated concretely: (U) overestimates battery discharge by up to 2.8 kW per period (less than 0.02% of total demand) and over-approximates lifecycles by about 25%, defended as conservative (pp. 31-32). Seeding (P) with (U)'s procurement did not help (p. 26). Aggregating to 3- and 12-hour periods gave designs with too little battery capacity, so the hourly model was kept (p. 27). The error comes from the SOC bounds [0, 1]; partitioning on SOC could reduce it at some cost (p. 33). Future work: minute-level dispatch with the design fixed, rolling-horizon simulation with DFO, and a stochastic program (pp. 33-34).

**Method links**: Method 6 evidence ✅: the nonconvex MINLP is rewritten as a MIP after the MINLP solvers fail on small instances (pp. 15-19, 26), with exact linearization where possible and convex under-estimation elsewhere. Method 5 evidence ✅: three MINLP solvers at default settings with a common gap and time limit, failures kept in Table 6 (pp. 26-27); a negative result (time aggregation) reported (p. 27). New-pattern candidate: a posteriori audit of a relaxation against the original nonlinear model (pp. 30-32).
**Transferable techniques**:
- After solving a linearized surrogate, recompute the original nonlinear quantities at its solution, tabulate the worst-case error per instance, and express it as a share of a physical total (here demand) (Table 10, pp. 31-32).
- Check that the surrogate's discrete decisions admit a feasible completion in the nonlinear model on a short horizon (p. 32).
- Before shrinking a time-indexed model by aggregation, test whether the aggregated design is robust at full resolution; report it when it is not (p. 27).
**Quotes**:
- "The poor performance of these MINLP solvers on the majority of these small instances suggests that (P) is not a tractable formulation" (p. 26)
- "our model over-approximates lifecycles consumed by roughly 25%. This is acceptable because lifecycles provide only a small contribution to the objective function value" (p. 32)

### S056 · Mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimal control of gas networks (Preprint ANL/MCS-P7095-0817, 2017)
**Read level**: partial. File is the preprint dated December 18, 2017 (p. 1). Read: abstract, introduction and outline (pp. 1-2), flow model and smoothing (pp. 3-4), Remark 2.1 (p. 6), junction/compressor model opening (p. 7), assumptions and the full problem (pp. 10-12), §3 B&B customization (pp. 12-14), §4 experiments and results (pp. 14-20), conclusions (p. 20). Skimmed the finite-volume derivation (pp. 4-6) and the compressor-mode constraints (pp. 7-10). From the references, only [8] (Linderoth and Savelsbergh) and [15] (Zavala 2014, the base model) were checked (p. 21).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Mirko Hahn (Argonne), Victor M. Zavala (Wisconsin) (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: a MIPDECO application solved with a modified Minotaur; part of the mixed-integer PDE-constrained line.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Transient (non-stationary) gas-network control with binary compressor controls, aimed at extreme transitions such as flow reversal (p. 1). Prior work fixes the flow direction, simplifies compressor operation, or solves only approximately; here the aim is integer optimality with few restrictions on the controls (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: Simplified 1D Euler equations, discretized by a finite-volume scheme that is symmetric in pipe orientation and therefore needs no flow direction fixed in advance. Upwinding would need it, or artificial flow splitting with many integers (Remark 2.1, p. 6). The friction term |q|q/p is smoothed with a scaled arctangent so the discretized solution is twice differentiable (p. 3). Since combinatorial integral approximation cannot handle constraints that tie controls to the PDE state, a general MINLP solver is used (p. 12). The new node selection rule RLAD keeps a stack and a min-heap, dives below an artificial cutoff f_co = min{α f_ub + (1−α) f_lb, β f_lb}, defers nodes above it, and "rebases" to the best deferred node (Algorithm 1, pp. 12-13).
  - Transferable: when the solver abandons good subtrees, keep the dive but allow lateral moves restricted to a cutoff between the bounds, so the search stays in a shrinking sublevel set (pp. 12-14).
- **D3 Assumptions**: valves only inside compressor stations at junctions; no antiparallel arcs; global bounds on pressure and flow (pp. 2-3); for the tests, one mode per compressor and no compressor pushing against the flow (p. 10). Existence and uniqueness of C¹ PDE solutions are cited for suitable boundary conditions (p. 4). Minotaur is a convex-MINLP solver, so global optimality is not guaranteed for this nonconvex model (p. 12).
- **D4 Analysis**: no theorems. RLAD is argued geometrically: enforcing the cutoff restricts the search to relaxed solutions in a sublevel set that shrinks as the upper bound falls (Fig. 4, p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: two networks ("Inversion", 3 pipes; "Triangle"), six randomized instances each; 1-hour horizon, 180 simulation intervals, 10 integer control intervals; 13,486 and 19,694 variables with 60 and 100 integers (pp. 14, 17). Modified Minotaur 0.2.0 with IPOPT 3.12/MA57, single thread; six variants (best-then-dive or RLAD × forward-time, reverse-time or reliability branching); stop at 0.1% gap or 48 h; wall time, called unreliable on shared nodes (pp. 17-18).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: modelling plus algorithmic. Claimed as the first model that allows simulating flow reversal, shown on a small network (p. 20). RLAD with reliability branching beats best-then-dive in almost all instances; inversion-random02 is the exception, likely from nonconvexity (pp. 18-19). Time-ordered branching does not help (p. 18). One run was omitted because IPOPT hit its iteration limit, which would have shown misleadingly few nodes (p. 18). Inconsistency: the results say the time-branching failure "is not due to a negative interaction between RLAD and time-based branching orders" (p. 18), but the conclusion says RLAD "appears to negatively interact" with them (p. 20). Future work: dynamic cutoffs, dedicated simulators and PETSc parallelism (p. 20).

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅: the default Minotaur node selection was watched by hand, found to abandon near-optimal subtrees, and redesigned as RLAD (p. 12); this is the Workflow D step 4 behaviour. Method 6 evidence ✅: the discretization is chosen so that flow direction need not be fixed and no flow-splitting integers are needed (p. 6); the friction term is smoothed (p. 3). Method 2 evidence ✅: MINLP B&B carried into mixed-integer PDE-constrained control (pp. 12, 20). Method 5 variant ⚠: one omitted run is disclosed and wall time is flagged as unreliable (pp. 17-18), but the conclusion contradicts the results paragraph on branching interaction (pp. 18, 20).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Choose a discretization that is indifferent to the sign of an unknown flow, so the sign does not become a binary variable (Remark 2.1, p. 6).
- When dropping an instance from a plot, say why (here the subsolver hit its iteration limit, so the node count would mislead) (p. 18).
- Build test networks that each test one capability (flow inversion) and randomize demands around a scripted mean profile (pp. 14-16).
**Quotes**:
- "In preliminary experiments, we frequently observed Minotaur abandoning subtrees that could, through manual exploration, be shown to contain nearly optimal solutions, in favor of exploring other subtrees that branch off near the root of the branch-and-bound tree." (p. 12)
- "Although Minotaur is designed to solve convex MINLPs and is not guaranteed to globally solve problems using our gas network model, we believe the application of a theoretically correct optimization method (e.g., spatial branch-and-bound) to be computationally intractible." (p. 12)

### S066 · Joint reconstruction of x-ray fluorescence and transmission tomography (Optics Express 2017, doi:10.1364/oe.25.013107)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract (p. 1), introduction (pp. 3-4), opening of §2 (p. 4) and of §3 (p. 5), §4 formulation and algorithms (pp. 8-9), §5 experiments and results prose (pp. 10-16), conclusion (pp. 16-17), start of the appendix (p. 17). Skimmed: the XRT/XRF imaging models (pp. 5-8). Figures known from captions only.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Zichao Wendy Di (corresponding, p. 1), Si Chen, Young Pyo Hong, Chris Jacobsen, Stefan M. Wild · **Place in trajectory**: an inverse-problem application at the Advanced Photon Source; extends Di, Leyffer and Wild, SIAM J. Imaging Sci. 2016 (ref 30, p. 2; p. 5). Continuous NLP, not MINLP.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: XRF tomography suffers from finite detector energy resolution, which mixes elemental signals, and from self-absorption in dense samples (abstract, pp. 3-4). Existing self-absorption corrections had not been combined with full-spectrum analysis (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Couple a second modality (transmission, XRT) to XRF by making both models share the unknown elemental concentrations W, then maximize a joint Poisson likelihood with W ≥ 0 and weights β1, β2 (eq. 8, p. 8). Solve by an outer loop that freezes the self-absorption terms A(W^i), P(W^i), with an inner truncated-Newton solve (bound-constrained PCG plus backtracking) of the linearized-XRF subproblem (Algorithms 1-2, p. 9).
  - Transferable: when a forward model has a hard nonlinear coupling term, freeze it at the outer iterate, solve the easier inner problem inexactly with a fixed iteration budget, and update the term.
- **D3 Assumptions**: independent Poisson noise in photon counts (p. 8, appendix p. 17); pencil-beam geometry (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis**: none; convergence analysis of Algorithm 1 is explicitly deferred (p. 9). The maximum-likelihood objective is derived in the appendix (p. 17).
- **D5 Experiments**: one real dataset (borosilicate rod with W and Au wires, APS beamline 2-ID-E, 73 angles); one 2D slice; 800 of 2,000 energy channels; baselines MAPS+TomoPy MLEM and XRF-only inversion; β1 chosen by the L-curve's maximum curvature (β1 = 1), robust for β1 in [0.25, 10]; 4,301 s plus hours to build the forward map (pp. 10-11, 16).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: an application of a joint-inversion framework. It reduces "elemental crosstalk" and fills in the self-absorbed Si core (pp. 11-12, 16-17). The simulated spectrum is checked against the measured one (Fig. 12, p. 16). Limits: memory and the cost of the self-absorption tensor; only a prototype code (pp. 16-17). Future work: parallel TN, multilevel schemes, other acquisition designs (p. 17).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅ (weak): an NLP formulation with a reliable bound-constrained Newton-type solver carried into tomographic inversion (pp. 8-9). Taste 3 variant ⚠: the algorithm is published without a convergence guarantee, which is deferred to future work (p. 9).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Choose a regularization-type weight by the maximum curvature of the L-curve between the two misfit terms, then show the reconstruction over a range of weights to demonstrate insensitivity (pp. 11, 15).
- Validate a reconstruction by pushing it through the forward model and overlaying the simulated and measured spectra (p. 16).
- Balance two data sources of different magnitude with a fixed scaling so both have maxima near the same value, before tuning the trade-off weight (p. 11).
**Quotes**:
- "Since the focus of this work is to show the potential of joint inversion with multimodal data, future work will address convergence analysis of Alg. 1." (p. 9)
- "This lack of sensitivity to precise balancing parameter values indicates robustness for practical utilization." (p. 11)

### S095 · A mathematical programming- and simulation-based framework to evaluate cyberinfrastructure design choices (IEEE e-Science 2017, doi:10.1109/escience.2017.27)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §I-III opening (pp. 1-3), MIP sets, objective and the slowdown and full-model constraints (pp. 2-4), simulator overview (pp. 4-5), case study §IV (pp. 5-8), related work and conclusion (pp. 8-9). Skimmed: job-scheduling and network constraints (pp. 3-4) and simulator internals (p. 5).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Zhengchun Liu, Rajkumar Kettimuthu, Prashant Palkar, Ian Foster (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: DOE "superfacility" design work, the facility-scheduling class of H001 (p. 2 there). Leyffer's role is probably the MIP model, but the paper does not say.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Light sources need on-demand HPC for near-real-time analysis. Should there be a computer per facility, one national computer, or a distributed superfacility (abstract, pp. 1-2)? A full optimization is NP-complete and relies on simplified transfer and ideal scheduling models (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Combine a MIP (job-to-resource assignment by time interval, path selection, a binary per job for slowdown ≤ S, a percentile constraint, big-M, objective = aggregate slowdown, pp. 2-4) solved on small representative subsets with a discrete-event simulator (ns-3) that evaluates the top-k MIP designs on the full problem (pp. 2, 4, 8).
  - Transferable: use exact optimization on representative slices to produce a top-k list of candidate designs, then rank them by a higher-fidelity simulator on the full case.
- **D3 Assumptions**: discretized time; fixed paths per site-resource pair; new resources only at the three DOE HPC sites (pp. 2, 7). Facility demand data are approximate estimates (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a.
- **D5 Experiments**: case study of five DOE light sources in 2017. Utilization is 20.7% with a computer per facility, 28.7% with one central computer, 34% after an exhaustive search of whole-day schedule shifts (about 1,000 core-hours), and 61% if 5% of jobs may exceed slowdown 1 (pp. 6-7). The MIP is solved in AMPL/CPLEX on six 3-hour periods and the top 20 solutions simulated; they differ by only 1.6% in objective but distribute resources differently (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: a framework plus open code (MIP on GitHub, p. 4; simulator, p. 4). Limits: commercial clouds are not considered (p. 9). Future: couple the simulator with derivative-free optimization (p. 9).

**Method links**: Method 6 evidence ✅ (weak): the full-year model is not solved; the problem is cut to representative periods that a MIP can handle, and the rest is left to simulation (pp. 2, 8). Taste mark 5 evidence ✅: model and simulator released as open code (p. 4). New-pattern candidate: optimize on representative subsets, validate the top-k in a simulator (pp. 2, 8).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Output the top-k solutions rather than one optimum when near-optimal designs differ structurally, and say why (the model misses constraints) (p. 8).
- Rule out simple design points by back-of-envelope utilization arithmetic before building the optimization model (pp. 6-7).
**Quotes**:
- "Second, we do not expect that even a good model will capture all design constraints." (p. 8)
- "Thus an apparently suboptimal design choice (i.e., one with a slightly different objective function value) may be subjectively more feasible in practice." (p. 8)

### S211 · Exposing Latent Hierarchies for Large-Scale Design and Discovery (DOE-ASCR Applied Mathematics PI Meeting white paper 2017, doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.5339416)
**Read level**: full. Both pages. Title and authors (Di, Leyffer, Otten, Wild) confirmed on p. 1, which calls it a whitepaper submitted to the 2017 DOE ASCR Applied Mathematics PI Meeting.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Zichao (Wendy) Di (first author), Matthew Otten (Cornell), Stefan Wild (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: companion to S066's tomography work; multilevel optimization.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Data volume (APS upgrade) and model dimension (nanoscale materials) grow faster than computing (p. 1). Multilevel optimization can exploit hierarchies, but "exploitable hierarchies are often hidden and/or difficult to construct" (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two cases. In emission/transmission tomography, hierarchies exist in both sample and data spaces; the question is restriction/interpolation operators that keep the two spaces consistent. Multilevel was not robust there because the solver must separate high and low Hessian frequencies (p. 2). In nonorthogonal configuration interaction, the ResHF parameters' hierarchy could support a multilevel method; the challenges are building a coarse model when the hierarchy is not obvious and when derivatives are unavailable (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions**: n/a.
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a.
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a.
