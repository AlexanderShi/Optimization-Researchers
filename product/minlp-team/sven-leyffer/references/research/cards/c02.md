# Batch c02 · Sven Leyffer · core (Dundee years, 1994-2001)

Batch intent: see how Leyffer, first with Fletcher and then alone, entered MINLP in the Dundee years. The questions are how OA was corrected and generalized, how the MIQP subproblem became its own line, how the filterSQP / MINLP_BB tool chain was built, and how owning the SQP solver made integrated SQP + branch-and-bound possible.
Focus dimensions: D1-D8, the standard mathematical/computational meanings (team.json "card_dimensions" is empty). D4 and D5 are "n/a" for the two user manuals.
Material roles: S003 core (theory paper), S010 core (algorithm + numerics), S014 core (software manual), S023 core (software manual, 4 pp.), S009 core (algorithm + numerics).

### S003 · Solving mixed integer nonlinear programs by outer approximation (Mathematical Programming 1994, doi:10.1007/bf01581153)
**Read level**: full, pp. 1-22 including references; version read is the authors' preprint dated 8 October 1996 (p. 1). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher · **Position in trajectory**: the founding MINLP paper, grown out of the 1993 Dundee thesis. It is the source of the MIQP line (S010) and of the SQP-as-integer-method view (S009).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Duran and Grossmann's OA covers only MINLPs linear in the integer variables (p. 2). Their master program ignores infeasible NLP subproblems. Their fix, integer cuts, is "only pra ti al for binary variables" and misreads the master program (p. 6). Their numerical evidence is limited to problems linear in y (p. 12). The paper enters through these three gaps. It also asks a worst-case question: is there a problem on which OA "performs badly"? (p. 12).
  - Transferable: read the competitor's test class as a statement of scope. "superior ... although the test problems are limited to the ase where f and g are both linear fun tions in y" (p. 12) turns directly into the question of a worst case outside that class.
- **D2 Core idea**: (i) Project P onto the integer variables and replace each NLP(y^j) by its linearization at the optimum (exact under a CQ). This gives an equivalent MILP master M that allows y to appear nonlinearly, including pure INLP (pp. 6-9). (ii) Represent y ∈ V (the feasible assignments) by cuts from a *general* Phase I feasibility problem F(y^k) whose weights cover l1, l∞ and constraint-by-constraint restoration (pp. 4-5, 8). (iii) A strict upper bound η < UBD prevents a repeated assignment (p. 10, Algorithm 1). (iv) A worst-case example motivates quadratic OA: add the Lagrangian Hessian term to the master objective, giving an MIQP master (pp. 13-14). (v) Section 5 replaces feasible/infeasible case analysis by an exact-penalty, convex-composite formulation, so that one nonsmooth OA covers both cases (pp. 15-20). What stays the same: the alternating NLP/MILP structure owed to Duran and Grossmann (p. 9). What changes: the derivation, the treatment of infeasibility, and nonlinear y.
  - Transferable: when an algorithm has a case split (feasible vs infeasible subproblem), look for a reformulation that removes it. Here exact penalty functions "make a distin tion between feasible and infeasible subproblems unne essary" (p. 15). Then prove the main result for the unified case.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: A1: X is a compact polyhedron and f, g are convex. A2: f, g are C^1. A3: a CQ holds at the solution of every fixed-y NLP subproblem (p. 2). The authors call convexity "the most serious restri tion". In the nonconvex case the algorithm applies without a global guarantee (p. 2). |Y| < ∞ is required for finiteness (p. 11). Section 5 needs h convex *and monotone* to keep P_ns convex (p. 16). Theorem 3 needs no CQ (p. 19). The penalty equivalence needs A4 (penalty parameter small relative to the dual norms of the multipliers and to the objective gaps of infeasible assignments) and A5 (second-order sufficiency) (p. 19). A3 is defended as equivalent to what OA and NLP-B&B already need (p. 2).
  - Transferable: give each theorem its own minimal assumption list. Here Theorem 3 drops the CQ, and the CQ comes back only in the penalty-equivalence theorem (p. 19). The reader then sees which assumption buys which conclusion.
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Lemma 1: cuts from an infeasible point of F(y^k) exclude y^k. Proof: multiply the linearized inequalities by the weights and the KKT multipliers of F, add the normal-cone inequality for X, and contradict positive infeasibility (pp. 5-6). A referee's strengthening (only strongly active constraints are needed) is acknowledged (p. 6). Theorem 1 is master equivalence (p. 9). Theorem 2 is finite termination: (a) no assignment repeats, because at an optimal x^l under the CQ there is "no feasible des ent dire tion", which contradicts η < UBD (p. 11); (b) optimality, because M relaxes P (p. 11). The authors claim this proof is "mu h more simple" than Duran and Grossmann's polyhedral argument (p. 12). The worst-case construction is f(y) = (y − δ)^2 on {0, δ, ..., 1/2, 1} with δ = 2^{-p}: OA visits every point from a start adjacent to the optimum (p. 12, Fig. 1 p. 13). A one-line counterexample (−ln(x+1) on [0,2]) shows that the quadratic master value is not a lower bound (p. 14). Theorem 3 (nonsmooth OA) uses the subgradient inequality, with X folded in as a polyhedral composite t(r(x)) ≤ 0 (pp. 18-19). Theorem 4 (penalty equivalence) is proved by comparing objective values at infeasible assignments (p. 20). The single OA cut is compared with the Benders cut through first-order conditions; the two coincide when x^j is interior (pp. 20-21).
  - Transferable: to prove no cycling in a decomposition method, combine optimality of the iterate for its subproblem plus a CQ (hence no feasible descent direction) with a strict incumbent bound in the master (p. 11).
  - Transferable: build a worst case as a one-dimensional convex function on a geometrically spaced grid (δ = 2^{-p}). The visit count then grows with p while the start stays adjacent to the optimum (p. 12).
  - Transferable: check what a proposed modification breaks. Quadratic OA keeps finite convergence because the feasible region of the master is unchanged, but it loses the lower bound; the paper shows the loss with a two-line example (p. 14).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: none in this version. The only computational statements are verbal and come without tables: "pra ti al performan e ... similar to that of the Duran and Grossmann algorithm" (p. 9), and adding only strongly active linearizations "results in better overall CPU times" according to "Preliminary omputational experien e" (p. 12). Results for quadratic OA are deferred: "It is hoped to present results ... in a later paper" (p. 15). Worst-case behaviour is analysed by hand (pp. 12-14).
- **D6 Positioning**: extends Duran and Grossmann and openly credits them ("owes mu h to the innovative work of Duran and Grossmann", p. 9). It also corrects them directly: their argument that the linearizations represent y ∈ V is "erroneous", shown with a two-point counterexample (p. 8). The paper simplifies Yuan et al. (p. 6) and notes that Viswanathan and Grossmann give no proof against repeated assignments (p. 6). GBD (Geoffrion; Flippo et al.) is the recurring foil. OA bounds are tighter, but the paper says no conclusion on final efficiency follows from that alone, because GBD adds fewer constraints per iteration (p. 3). The worst case also hits GBD, while NLP-B&B solves it "in only one step" (p. 13). GBD for MIQP masters (Lazimy) is called "inadequate" (pp. 14-15). Kocis and Grossmann's nonconvex heuristics are said to carry over (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when you correct a founding paper, give the smallest instance on which its claim fails and also show the tempting wrong repair. Here linearizing at x = 1/2 also fails to exclude y = 1 (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: the abstract lists four contributions in order: generalization, a simpler proof, a rigorous treatment of infeasibility, and a worst case leading to QOA (p. 1). The outline gives every section's purpose (p. 3). Each theorem is followed by interpretation: QOA is an SQP method generalized to integer programming and linear OA is SLP (p. 15), which leads to usage advice on when QOA pays: many discrete values and nonlinear y, rarely for 0-1 problems (p. 15). Hedging is light and concentrated on performance claims ("Preliminary", p. 12; "hoped", p. 15). Referees are thanked for a strengthened lemma (p. 6, p. 21).
  - Transferable: after an algorithm, add a paragraph that names the continuous-optimization method it reduces to (OA = SLP, QOA = SQP; p. 15) and draw usage advice from that analogy.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory and algorithm: a generalized OA, a corrected master, a simpler finiteness proof, quadratic OA, nonsmooth/penalty OA. Stated limits: convexity is needed for global guarantees (p. 2); QOA loses the lower bound (p. 14); "little or no software" exists for the MIQP masters (p. 14); adding fewer cuts weakens the master (p. 12); including composite constraints brings back separate infeasibility handling (p. 21). Future work is MIQP B&B with improved lower bounds (p. 14), carried out in S010. Not in this version: any numerical validation of QOA (deferred, p. 15).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence. The smallest breaking instance for the received OA master (two points y ∈ {−1, 1}, p. 8) and a worst-case family against OA's reputation (pp. 12-13) lead to the redesign, quadratic OA (pp. 13-14). The paper also gives a one-line counterexample to its own QOA lower bound (p. 14). The hypothesis check comes first: D&G's evidence is "limited to the ase where f and g are both linear fun tions in y" (p. 12).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence. Fletcher's NLP toolkit is carried into MINLP: Phase I feasibility problems (pp. 4-5), exact penalty functions and convex-composite subdifferentials ([3] Fletcher 1987, pp. 16-21). QOA is read as SQP for integers (p. 15).
- Method 6 ⚠ variant. Formulation enters as a warning in the reverse direction: "nonlinearities an be hidden away by using linearization te hniques" (p. 15), which defeats QOA's advantage. Here linearizing is not always preferred.
- New-pattern candidate: *inherit convergence by preserving the classical invariant*. QOA keeps the finiteness proof because the master's feasible region and its supporting hyperplanes are unchanged (p. 14). S009 uses the same move (p. 7).
- New-pattern candidate: *one weighted feasibility problem for all Phase I variants*. The l1, l∞ and constraint-by-constraint restoration schemes are covered by a single parametrized problem F, so that one lemma covers every NLP solver (pp. 4-5).

**Transferable techniques**:
- A no-cycling proof for decomposition: KKT at the subproblem optimum plus a CQ gives no feasible descent direction, which contradicts the strict incumbent bound η < UBD (p. 11).
- A worst case on a geometric grid, δ = 2^{-p}, with the start adjacent to the optimum, and a perturbed variant in which the start *is* the optimum (pp. 12-13).
- Phase I cuts that work for any NLP solver: write whatever the solver's restoration terminated on as min Σ w_i g_i^+ subject to the currently satisfied constraints, and linearize at that point (pp. 4-5, 8).
- Comparing cuts through first-order conditions: substitute the stationarity condition in y into the Benders cut to show when it equals the OA cut (pp. 20-21).

**Quotes**:
- "This argument is erroneous as the following example indi ates." (p. 8)
- "Therefore quadrati outer approximation an be interpreted as a Sequential Quadrati Programming method generalized to integer programming." (p. 15)

### S010 · Numerical experience with lower bounds for MIQP branch-and-bound (SIAM Journal on Optimization 1998, doi:10.1137/s1052623494268455)
**Read level**: full, pp. 1-14 including references; version read is the preprint dated 3 October 1995 (p. 1). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher · **Position in trajectory**: the subproblem created by quadratic OA (S003) becomes its own paper. It is the first statement of the view that B&B beats decomposition when node solves are cheap, that S009 builds on.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: quadratic OA needs MIQP masters, and the authors say this motivated a detailed study of MIQP (p. 1). MIQPs also matter in their own right (index tracking, substation siting; p. 1). The gap: no one had derived lower bounds for MIQP branch-and-bound (p. 1). The parent's optimal value is a weak child bound that "rarely give rise to any fathoming" (p. 4).
  - Transferable: when your algorithm needs a subproblem class that has no good solver, make the subproblem class the next paper (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: after the parent QP is solved, its final active-set (LCP) tableau is available. One step of the Goldfarb-Idnani dual active-set method, moving the new branching bound parametrically and stopping before a pivot would be needed, gives a dual-feasible point and hence a lower bound for each child (pp. 4-6). The bound has two parts: a second-order change in the objective and a Lagrangian penalty for the remaining bound violation (p. 6). If the dual ratio test is unbounded, the child is infeasible and is fathomed before it is solved (p. 5). Three branching rules use the bound in different ways: BR1 most-fractional with no bounds, BR2 branches on max_j min(L+_j, L−_j), BR3 is BR1 plus bounds for pruning only (p. 9). What stays the same: Dakin/Beale B&B, depth-first with backtracking to the most promising node (p. 3).
  - Transferable: get a child bound from the parent's final factorization with one pivot-free step of a dual method instead of a fresh solve (pp. 4-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: a convex QP objective (positive definite G for the stated tableau properties), linear constraints, a bounded feasible region (p. 2), and an active-set QP solver whose final tableau can be recovered (p. 4). A null-space implementation with LU factors of [A_A : V] and LDL^T factors of the reduced Hessian (pp. 6-7). An extension to general discrete values is claimed to be straightforward (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: tableau algebra. The expressions for A', G', H' come from the inverse of the KKT matrix (Fletcher's "Resolving degeneracy" paper, Theorem 2.1) (pp. 6-7). The dual ratio test gives the steplength (p. 5). Closed-form L+ and L− (p. 6), with a simplification when n constraints are active, re-derivable from marginal costs (p. 6). An operation count of 5n² + mn + k² + O(n) flops, with sparse savings noted (p. 7). A referee's point that range-space factors would be cheaper is answered with a practical reason (bounds handling; G may not be factorable) (p. 7). A worked two-variable example closes 80% of the child gap (p. 8). A structural ceiling is stated: pruning by bounds cannot remove more than 50% of nodes (p. 10).
  - Transferable: give a flop count for a bounding procedure and place it between the cheap rule it competes with and a full solve ("considerably cheaper than a QP solve, but an order of magnitude more expensive" than most-fractional, p. 7). Then let experiments decide.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: 15 instances (p. 9, Table 1): literature problems (Beale, HS76), LPs (AVGAS, AFIRO, SHARE2B) made into MIQPs by adding integrality and a positive-definite quadratic objective, QAP(6,4), and two index-tracking portfolios. A SUN SPARC SLC, single-precision Fortran 77, mixed accuracy 10^-6, and a 2,000 s cap with abandoned runs kept in the tables (pp. 9-10). Metrics: QPs generated, QPs solved and % solved (Table 2, p. 10); CPU (Table 4, p. 12). BR3 isolates the bound's pruning effect and gives a modest gain (p. 10). BR2 gives an order-of-magnitude CPU gain on AFIRO/SHARE2B, explained by finding an incumbent early (15 QPs vs 2,348/2,143 on AFIRO(1a), pp. 10-11). BR2 loses on 0-1 problems, about doubling CPU on QAP and portfolio (p. 11). The explanation offered is that "neighbourhood" has no meaning for 0-1 problems. Baselines GBD, OA and LP/QP-B&B are all self-implemented in the same Fortran with the same QP solver bqpd and the authors' own MILP B&B for the masters, with the best branching rule per problem "to gain a fair comparison" (p. 11). B&B is an order of magnitude faster and never slower; the relaxation methods solve fewer QPs but "the number of LP problems grows dramatically" (p. 12, Table 3).
  - Transferable: to measure one feature, add a hybrid arm that differs from the baseline in only that feature (BR3 = BR1 + bounds, "designed to expose the e ect of the new bounds", p. 9).
  - Transferable: report both meters, subproblem counts (QPs, LPs) and CPU, so the reader can see *why* a method wins. Here the LP counts explain the failure of decomposition (pp. 11-12).
- **D6 Positioning**: builds on Beale (MILP bounds) and Goldfarb-Idnani (dual ASM) (pp. 4-5). Rejects Korner's branching rule because its tree structure "can lead to wide trees" (p. 4). Cites Gupta-Ravindran against pseudo-costs (p. 4). Groups Lazimy/Flippo-Rinnooy Kan GBD, Duran-Grossmann OA and Quesada-Grossmann LP/QP as methods based on linearizations that "ignore higher order terms" (p. 2). The verdict is limited to MIQP: for MINLP, the thesis shows "important classes of MINLP problems for which branch{and{bound is not the best method" (p. 12).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: a short abstract with one-sentence claims (p. 1). The section plan is given in the introduction (p. 2). Results are hedged ("As one would expect, the lower bounds result only in a modest improvement", p. 10). The method's failure class is named and explained (0-1 QPs, p. 11). A "should not come as a surprise" paragraph turns the ranking into a mechanism, cheap child QPs at 1-2 pivots (p. 12). The conclusions restate three results in three sentences (pp. 12-13).
  - Transferable: end a comparison with the mechanism that makes the ranking hold (cost per node) and the class where it stops holding (MINLP nodes need several QPs, p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic (cheap child bounds, the BR2 rule) and empirical (a B&B vs decomposition comparison for MIQP). Stated limits: modest gains for pruning alone (p. 10); the 50% ceiling (p. 10); BR2 is worse on 0-1 problems (p. 11); the result does not transfer to MINLP (p. 12). Not discussed: small instances built from LPs, and a single machine in single precision.

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence. Self-built competitors in the same code base with the same QP solver and the best branching rule per problem "to gain a fair comparison" (p. 11). Time-outs kept in the tables (pp. 10-12). The class where the new rule loses is reported (0-1 QPs, p. 11). A rule of thumb by class rather than a universal winner (MIQP vs MINLP, p. 12).
- Method 1 ✅ evidence. Reuses what the inner solver already produces: the parent's final LCP tableau and factors give the child bounds with no extra factorization (pp. 4-7). Warm starts in bqpd come in "six di erent degrees" (p. 3).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence. Fletcher's QP technology (bqpd, degeneracy resolution with guaranteed termination, p. 3; the tableau theorem, p. 6) is carried into integer programming.
- New-pattern candidate: *a cost-per-node mechanism for method rankings*. The ranking of B&B vs decomposition is explained by subproblem cost (1-2 pivots per child QP) and explicitly predicted to change when node cost rises (MINLP) (p. 12). This is a prediction of where the result stops, not just a result.

**Transferable techniques**:
- A one-step dual-active-set bound for B&B children from the parent's final tableau. Fathom the child with no solve if the dual ratio test is unbounded (pp. 4-6).
- A three-arm branching study: baseline, feature-only hybrid and full rule, with generated/solved/% solved counts plus CPU (pp. 9-10).
- A test-set construction move: turn standard LP test problems into MIQPs by adding integrality and a positive-definite quadratic objective, alongside application instances (p. 9).

**Quotes**:
- "In order to gain a fair comparison the best branching rule for each problem was used in the tests." (p. 11)
- "Note that one cannot expect to prune more than 50% of the nodes through lower bounds, which limits the scope for improvement through pruning for the hybrid branching rule BR3." (p. 10)

### S014 · User manual for filterSQP (Numerical Analysis Report NA/181, University of Dundee 1998, no DOI)
**Read level**: full, pp. 1-17; version read is dated April 1998, Version 1 June 1998, updated March 1999 (p. 1). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher · **Position in trajectory**: documents the NLP solver that the Dundee MINLP work runs on (MINLP_BB S023, integrated SQP-B&B S009). The filter algorithm itself is in NA/171 (cited p. 17).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: a software description: solve NLPs of the form min f s.t. bound and two-sided general constraints, with linear constraints flagged so that their structure is exploited (p. 1). The filter is motivated only as a way to promote global convergence (p. 1). The algorithmic case is left to NA/171 (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: trust-region SQP with an l∞ box. A step is accepted if it improves either f or h compared with every filter entry (p. 2). The radius is halved on rejection and doubled on acceptance (p. 2). QPs are solved by bqpd, a null-space active-set method; the user caps the reduced-Hessian dimension through kmax (p. 2). A Phase I restoration minimizes the violation of constraints the QP cannot satisfy while keeping the others (p. 3). Design choices for MINLP use: warm start through ifail = −1, for use when solving MINLP problems by branch-and-bound (p. 9); arithmetic-exception flags in every user routine (pp. 11-15); after a bqpd crash the radius is reduced and the QP re-solved cold (p. 11).
  - Transferable: if your solver will be called inside a tree search, expose a warm-start entry and a per-call statistics vector from the start (istat/rstat, p. 9).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: smooth f and c with user-supplied gradients and Hessian of the Lagrangian, through a user-defined Hessian-vector product Wdotd (pp. 13-16). Nonconvexity is acknowledged: the method may fail to find a feasible point "due to the nonconvex nature of (P)" (p. 3). Termination at non-KKT points is allowed explicitly (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a (a manual, no proofs). One design argument: the normalized KT residual, which divides by the largest summed vector length µ_max, "removes scaling anomalies from the termination criterion" (p. 3).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a. One example problem is shipped: Duran and Grossmann's TP1, given with its relaxed NLP solution (p. 16).
- **D6 Positioning**: cites only CUTE, Duran-Grossmann (for the example) and the authors' own NA/171 (p. 17). No comparison with other SQP codes.
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: reference-manual style. Problem, algorithm, termination, infeasibility, scaling, system requirements, parameter list, outputs, user routines, example, interfaces, change log (pp. 1-17). It gives diagnostic guidance as well as definitions: a large maximum multiplier at termination suggests "the active constraints are nearly dependent" (p. 3); re-entering restoration repeatedly suggests a tighter upper bound on violation, ubd = 10, tt = 0.0001 (p. 10); the per-iteration code ±POXX records phase, step order and which heuristics fired (p. 11). The change log is dated, June 1998 to March 1999 (p. 17).
  - Transferable: design the solver log so the user can diagnose from it. One line per iteration with accept/reject, phase, step type and heuristic flags (p. 11), plus documented readings of the end state (p. 3).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: software. Stated limits: kmax storage is O(kmax²) (p. 2); double precision is recommended for large or ill-conditioned problems (p. 5); CUTE cannot pass scale factors, so scaling must be done in the SIF file (p. 17); AMPL/CUTE interfaces only "upon request" (p. 16). Future direction not stated.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence. This is the owned instrument. Warm starts for B&B (p. 9), solve statistics returned to the caller (p. 9) and modular dense/sparse linear algebra (pp. 4-5) are the hooks that S023 and S009 use.
- Method 6 ✅ evidence. The solver tells the user what to fix in the model: the Phase I solution "indicates which constraints are causing the NLP (P) to be (locally) infeasible and the user can modify the problem accordingly" (p. 3). Evaluation failures are first-class: IEEE flags in user routines (pp. 12-15) and ifail = 7 "crash in user routine" (p. 8).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant. The dated public change log (p. 17) is the software counterpart of the test library's dated correction log.

**Transferable techniques**:
- A scale-free stopping test: the KT residual divided by max{µ_max, 1}, where µ_max is the longest vector in the stationarity sum (p. 3).
- An exit-code taxonomy that separates local infeasibility with a solved feasibility problem from inconsistent linear constraints and from evaluation crashes (ifail 2, 3, 7; p. 8). Infeasibility then becomes information, not a failure.

**Quotes**:
- "A new step is accepted whenever it improves the objective or the constraints compared to the filter." (p. 2)
- "The use of the normalized Kuhn-Tucker residual removes scaling anomalies from the termination criterion." (p. 3)

### S023 · User manual for MINLP BB (University of Dundee Numerical Analysis Report, 1999, no DOI)
**Read level**: full, pp. 1-4; version read is dated April 1998, revised March 1999 (p. 1). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: the B&B layer on top of filterSQP (S014), and the default solver that S009's Algorithm 2 is compared against.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: software for MINLP by B&B. The NLP part is specified exactly as for filterSQP (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: depth-first B&B with filterSQP solving the NLP relaxations. User priorities decide branching, with maximal fractional part as the tie-break (p. 1). Discrete non-integer variables are handled by reformulation into binaries with an SOS1 constraint, z = 0.2y1 + 7.4y2 + 18.7y3 (p. 1); the interface takes SOS1 sets with their own priorities (pp. 2-3).
  - Transferable: give the MINLP layer the same problem interface as the NLP solver it calls, so that every NLP model and user routine carries over unchanged (p. 1, p. 4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: global optimality is guaranteed only for convex problems (p. 1). Nonconvex problems are accepted without a guarantee (p. 1). Tree memory is fixed by nstackmax; stack overflow is an exit state that may still return an incumbent (ifail 3/4, p. 4).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: n/a.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: n/a. Only a makefile for the small MINLP example of the filterSQP manual (p. 2).
- **D6 Positioning**: cites its own MIQP B&B paper (S010) for the B&B scheme (p. 1, ref [1] p. 4) and Williams for SOS1 (p. 1). Claims more robustness than OA and Benders on nonconvex problems, with no evidence in the document (p. 1).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: a terse manual. Parameter list, an ifail taxonomy that includes "integer infeasible" and stack-overflow states (p. 4), and a forward reference to the filterSQP manual for user routines (p. 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: software. Limits stated: no global guarantee for nonconvex problems (p. 1); CUTE/AMPL interfaces "upon request" (p. 2). Future work: none stated.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence. A thin B&B layer over the owned NLP solver and QP solver, distributed together as one file suite (p. 2).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence. A modelling reformulation inside a user manual: discrete-valued variables become binary SOS1 combinations (p. 1).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant. The comparative robustness claim against OA and Benders ("usually cut away large parts of the feasible region", p. 1) is made without a fairness rule, a test set or a date. This is uncalibrated, in contrast to S010 p. 11.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Exit states that keep partial value: "stack over ow some i.f.s. obtained" vs "stack over ow, no i.f.s. obtained" (p. 4). A resource-limited tree search should always say whether an incumbent exists.

**Quotes**:
- "Even though no guarantee can be given that a global solution is found in this case, the solver is more robust than outer approximation or Benders Decomposition which usually cut away large parts of the feasible region." (p. 1)

### S009 · Integrating SQP and branch-and-bound for mixed integer nonlinear programming (Computational Optimization and Applications 2001, doi:10.1023/a:1011241421041)
**Read level**: full, pp. 1-13 including references; version read is Dundee NA/182, dated 6 August 1998 (p. 1), the report version of the 2001 paper. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: the payoff of owning filterSQP (S014). It is the integrated idea in which "the nonlinear part of the MINLP problem is solved whilst searching the tree" (p. 1) that later leads to FilMINT and Minotaur.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: the diagnosis is that classical MINLP methods "decompose" the problem into nonlinear and integer parts, and that this "is largely due to the existence of packaged software" (p. 1, p. 2). It applies to NLP-B&B, OA, GBD and even Stubbs-Mehrotra branch-and-cut (p. 2). Standard B&B also solves "a large number of NLP problems ... which have often no physical meaning" (p. 4). The entry is a restriction in prior work: Borchers and Mitchell solve two QPs before branching only because their NAG SQP code cannot be interrupted (p. 2, footnote p. 4).
  - Transferable: for each restriction in the best prior method, ask whether it comes from the mathematics or from the tool. Here the restriction to two QPs before branching came from the NAG library (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: interleave SQP iterations with the tree search and branch after as little as one QP (p. 2). The lower bound comes without any dual evaluation: under convexity the QP linearizations are outer approximations. Adding the surrogate constraint f + ∇f^T d ≤ U − ε turns fathoming rule FR3 into a QP-infeasibility test, which a primal active-set QP solver performs "at no extra cost" (pp. 5-6, Lemma 1). Algorithm 1 is the basic version (pp. 6-7) and Algorithm 2 the trust-region one. With a trust region, an infeasible QP fathoms the node only if the trust region is inactive; otherwise restoration decides (p. 8). A parameter-free early-branching trigger uses the experimental order of convergence p = ln‖d^k‖/ln‖d^{k−1}‖: branch when the gap exceeds τ, or when p > 1.5 and ‖d‖ < τ (p. 9). A nonconvex heuristic (step 2 double-prime) fathoms only when NLP-B&B would (pp. 9-10). Compared with Quesada-Grossmann: they still solve NLPs at integer nodes, while here "usually ... one quadratic programming (QP) problem at each node" (p. 2).
  - Transferable: replace an explicit lower-bound computation by a feasibility test. Add the constraint model value ≤ incumbent − ε to the subproblem you already solve; if it becomes infeasible, prune (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: f and g smooth and convex, X and Y convex, Y ∩ integers finite, and the underlying trust-region SQP globally convergent (Theorem 1, p. 8). In the nonconvex case linearizations do not underestimate and nodes can be wrongly fathomed (p. 9). The rigorous fix, convex underestimators, is set aside because "it is not clear what e ect this would have on the convergence properties of the SQP method" (p. 9). τ = 0.1 is taken from Borchers-Mitchell (p. 7).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Lemma 1. An infeasible surrogate QP means no point beats U − ε, because the linearized constraints outer-approximate and the linearized objective underestimates (p. 6). The convergence argument is a reduction to B&B: the children still partition the parent even after a single QP, and the new fathoming rule implies FR3, so "convergence follows from the convergence of branch-and-bound" (p. 7). Theorem 1 combines tree finiteness with the global convergence of trust-region SQP (p. 8). The trust-region case analysis separates a binding radius from genuine infeasibility (p. 8).
  - Transferable: prove a modified tree search correct by showing (a) that branching after a partial solve still partitions the feasible set and (b) that every new pruning rule implies a classical one. The classical convergence proof then applies (p. 7).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: 8 problems from process synthesis, product location, batch design, distillation and trim loss, two of them nonconvex (Table 2, p. 11). All are in SIF and posted on the public MINLP_TP page (p. 10). NLP-B&B and Algorithm 2 are both built on filterSQP; Algorithm 2 is attached through a callback after each SQP step (p. 10). A SUN SPARCstation 4 (p. 10). Metrics: NLPs or nodes, QPs and CPU (Table 3, p. 11). The paper reports B&B at 2-6 QPs per node; Algorithm 2 visits more nodes and solves 20% to 80% fewer QPs, and is 3× faster on the largest problem (p. 11). It reports failures: in convex mode Algorithm 2 found no integer-feasible point for nonconvex FEEDLOC, while nonconvex mode found B&B's minimum at 65 nodes, 159 QPs and 55.9 s (p. 11). The new branching trigger gave about 5% fewer QPs (p. 9). Reader's check of Table 3 (p. 11): on BATCH, Algorithm 2 solved *more* QPs (391 vs 371) and took longer (14.8 s vs 8.9 s). The text's 20%-80% reduction and "dramatic reduction" in CPU do not mention this. The FEEDLOC row (29.0 s) is the convex-mode run that found no integer-feasible point.
  - Transferable: compare the old and new tree search inside the same NLP solver, attached through a callback, so that the only difference is the integration (p. 10).
- **D6 Positioning**: extends Borchers-Mitchell with three numbered improvements (p. 2): free bounds instead of Lagrangian-dual evaluations every sixth QP (p. 4, p. 7), no two-QP restriction, and a convergence-aware trigger. Related to Quesada-Grossmann's LP/NLP-B&B by "similar motivation" (p. 2). Classical decomposition is criticized as an artefact of packaged software, not of the mathematics (p. 2). Builds on the authors' own OA paper and filter report (refs [8], [9], p. 12).
  - Transferable: frame the contribution as a numbered list of improvements over one named predecessor, each tied to a section (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: the contribution sentence is "In contrast, an integrated approach to solving MINLP problems is considered here" (p. 1). The thesis about packaged software appears in the first abstract paragraph (p. 1). Enthusiasm is moderate ("very encouraging", p. 3; "dramatic reduction", p. 11). The observed adverse behaviour (the gap stays near 0.02 while SQP converges quadratically) is reported as the reason for a design change (p. 9). The open problem is stated in one line (p. 11). A typo: "weaker lower bound than FR2" should presumably read FR3 (p. 11).
  - Transferable: document an observed pathology of the first prototype (here, early branching that never fires during quadratic convergence) and turn it into a heuristic based on a measurable quantity (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithm plus convergence theory plus implementation. Stated limits: lower bounds are invalid for nonconvex problems (p. 9); the nonconvex mode is less competitive (p. 11); "better heuristics for nonconvex MINLP problems remain an open problem" (p. 11). Not stated: 8 small instances, no comparison with OA or other solvers, and the BATCH regression.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence, the core case. Restrictions traced to packaged software (pp. 1-2, footnote p. 4) are removed by "using our own solver" (p. 2). SQP linearizations are reinterpreted as cutting planes (pp. 5-6). The callback after every SQP step keeps the changes to filterSQP "to a minimum" (p. 10).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence. Trust-region SQP, the filter and feasibility restoration are carried into B&B, and the integer-side decisions are rebuilt around them (pp. 3, 7-8).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence. The test problems are made public at the MINLP_TP URL in a standard format (SIF plus an integer-variable file) (p. 10).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant. Failures are reported (FEEDLOC convex mode, nonconvex mode slower, p. 11), but the headline claim (20%-80% fewer QPs, p. 11; "a factor of about 3 improvement", p. 1) passes over BATCH, where Algorithm 2 is worse (Table 3, p. 11). The only baseline is the author's own NLP-B&B.
- Method 6 ⚠ variant. Reading the solver's numbers, applied to the *algorithm*, not the formulation: the iterates' integrality gap and step norms (p. 9) are diagnosed and lead to a new trigger.
- New-pattern candidate: *inherit convergence by preserving the classical invariant*. The new fathoming rule implies FR3 and children partition the parent, so B&B convergence carries over (p. 7). This is the same move as S003's QOA finiteness argument (S003 p. 14).
- New-pattern candidate: *self-tuning triggers from the convergence rate*. The experimental order of convergence replaces a fixed tolerance in the branching decision (p. 9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Fathoming by surrogate-QP infeasibility: add the linearized objective ≤ U − ε to each SQP subproblem. Under convexity, infeasibility proves the node cannot improve (pp. 5-6).
- A trust-region guard: fathom on QP infeasibility only if the trust region is inactive; otherwise go through restoration (p. 8).
- An early-branching trigger with p = ln‖d^k‖/ln‖d^{k−1}‖: branch when p > 1.5 and ‖d‖ < τ, because quadratic convergence will not close the integrality gap (p. 9).
- A nonconvex safe mode: fathom only when the classical method would have fathomed (p. 10).

**Quotes**:
