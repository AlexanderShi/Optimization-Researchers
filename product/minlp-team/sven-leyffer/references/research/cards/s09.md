### S147 · Convex Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Optimization, I (Proceedings of the Summer School on Optimization of Dynamical Systems 2018, no DOI)

**Read level**: full (all 51 frames: problem and assumptions pp. 3-11, nonlinear B&B pp. 13-24, multi-tree methods pp. 26-41, single-tree methods pp. 43-50, references p. 51). Slides, not prose; claims below are slide bullets.

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jeff Linderoth (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: teaching restatement (Argonne summer school, 3-7 Sept 2018, p. 1) of the convex-MINLP algorithm family he helped build (OA 1994, FilMINT 2010).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Convex MINLP min f(x) s.t. c(x) ≤ 0, x ∈ X, x_i ∈ Z under three stated assumptions A1 bounded polyhedral X, A2 convex C² f and c, A3 a CQ; A2 is called "most restrictive", A3 "technical" (p. 3). Entry for multi-tree methods is software maturity: "MILP solvers much better developed than MINLP", LPs easy to hot-start, no CQs needed for MILP (p. 27); entry for single-tree methods is the cost of repeated MILP trees ("No warm-starts for MILP ... expensive tree-search", p. 41).
- **D2 Core idea**: All MINLP solvers are presented as three components: relaxation, constraint enforcement (refinement, branching, spatial branching), upper bounds (pp. 5, 9). NLP-BB, OA, Benders, ECP and LP/NLP-BB are then placed in that grid; OA as alternating NLP(x_I) and MILP master M(F^k) with the bound η ≤ U^k − ε (pp. 34, 37); LP/NLP-BB as interrupting the MILP tree at integer points to add linearizations (p. 43), software FilMINT and BONMIN (p. 43). Refinements: drop old OA cuts, ECP cuts at fractional points, "balance OA accuracy with LP solvability" (p. 50).
  - Transferable: teach (or design) an algorithm family through a fixed component grid (relaxation / enforcement / upper bound) so that each method is a choice per component rather than a separate story (pp. 5, 9).
- **D3 Assumptions**: B&B "It Works Theorem" assumes X bounded polyhedral and an NLP solver that "returns global min" (p. 19); OA convergence under A1-A3 (p. 38). The slide states that A3 is stronger than needed (p. 3).
- **D4 Proof devices**: OA convergence sketched in two lines: NLP optimality gives η ≥ f^(j) on the master (finiteness, compact X), convexity makes linearizations supporting hyperplanes (optimality) (p. 38). Benders cut derived from OA by summing the OA cuts with the NLP multipliers λ^(j) and eliminating x_C through the KKT conditions in x_C (p. 39), giving "Benders cuts are linear combination of OA" and so weaker (p. 40). Feasibility-cut lemma left as an exercise (p. 31).
  - Transferable: derive a Benders cut as a multiplier-weighted sum of OA cuts with the continuous variables eliminated by stationarity; this proves in one line that Benders is never tighter than OA (pp. 39-40).
- **D5 Experiments**: none (one motivating figure: synthesis B&B tree with 10000+ nodes after 360 s, p. 26).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: teaching survey; no new result. Limits stated on slides: worst-case example for OA "also applies for Benders and ECP"; multi-tree methods lack MILP warm starts (p. 41). ECP not discussed ("did not discuss ECP", p. 41; "Exercise ... add ECP???", p. 39, a draft marker left in the slides).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (the component decomposition "relaxation and constraint enforcement" as building blocks, pp. 5, 9; LP/NLP-BB as interleaving NLP solves inside the MILP tree, p. 43). Method 6 ✅ (MILP preferred because "no need for constraint qualifications", p. 27). Method 2 ✅ (carried-in assumptions listed explicitly as A1-A3 with a note on which is strongest, p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Component grid for an algorithm family (pp. 5, 9).
- Benders-from-OA derivation by multiplier aggregation (pp. 39-40).

**Quotes**:
- "A3 is technical (MFCQ would have been sufficient)" (p. 3)
- "MILPs much easier; e.g. no need for constraint qualifications" (p. 27)

### S045 · Joint ptycho-tomography reconstruction through alternating direction method of multipliers (Optics Express 2019, doi:10.1364/oe.27.009128)

**Read level**: full (abstract, §1-§5 pp. 1-14; references skimmed pp. 14-16).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Selin Aslan (first), Viktor Nikitin, Daniel J. Ching, Tekin Bicer, Doğa Gürsoy (corresponding, X-ray Science Division) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: Argonne x-ray imaging collaboration; Leyffer (MCS) fifth author.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: 3D ptychography is done in two steps (phase retrieval per angle, then tomography), which "imposes rigid constraints on data collection, such as lateral probe overlap" (p. 1). Entry: previous joint work [2] alternates single minimization steps "without directly connecting the two subproblems" (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: write the amplitude misfit in consensus form ψ_θ = h_θ(x) (eqs. (2)-(3), p. 3) and apply ADMM; the dual variable couples phase retrieval (eq. (8)) and tomography (eq. (9)) (pp. 3-4). The phase-retrieval subproblem gains a proximal term toward the current tomographic estimate, absent in [2] (p. 4). Nonlinear tomography step replaced by a log change of variables (eq. (28), p. 6). Both subproblems solved by a few gradient steps (Algorithm 1, p. 8).
  - Transferable: when two inverse problems share an unknown, write the coupling as a consensus constraint and let a multiplier carry the mismatch, instead of alternating independent solves (pp. 3-4).
- **D3 Assumptions**: probe Q_i known (p. 6); phase in [0, 2π), phase unwrapping ignored (p. 7); Lemma 2.1 needs full column rank of R and exact subproblem solves (p. 7), and the authors state that inexact solves void it (p. 7).
- **D4 Proof devices**: stopping test derived from the KKT conditions of the consensus problem: primal residual (18), dual residual (19) and two extra residuals (20)-(21) for inexact subproblems (pp. 4-5); residual-balancing ρ update with μ = 10, τ = 2 from Boyd et al. (p. 5). Lemma 2.1 equivalence by comparing first-order conditions (p. 7). No convergence proof; deferred to "a forthcoming paper" (p. 7).
  - Transferable: when subproblems of a splitting method are solved inexactly, add the subproblem stationarity residuals to the primal/dual residual test (pp. 5, 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: synthetic 64³ phantom (Au, Pt, ZnO, TiO2), 70% and 30% probe overlap, 100 and 400 angles, noise-free and Poisson noise (p. 8); inner/outer iteration sweep in Table 1 (p. 9) showing few inner iterations suffice; baselines sequential approach with 200 iterations each "for a fair comparison" (p. 10) and coupled approach [2] with the same solvers and iteration counts (p. 11); coupled needs 1.2-1.5 times more iterations (p. 11). GPU time ~65 min for 200×500 iterations (p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: framework-level contribution (generic ADMM, swap in other optimizers, p. 13); limits: no convergence proof for inexact solves (p. 7), computational performance not yet addressed (p. 13), probe assumed known (pp. 6, 13). Future: probe retrieval inside eq. (5), log-polar tomography, TomoPy release (p. 13).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (augmented-Lagrangian/KKT toolkit carried into ptycho-tomography: KKT-derived stopping test, pp. 4-5). Method 5 ✅ (equal-iteration "fair comparison" rule stated for both baselines, pp. 10-11). Taste 3 variant ⚠ (method published with observed convergence only, proof deferred, p. 7; recorded under Method 2 step 5 as an unchecked carried-in assumption). New-pattern candidate: couple, don't alternate (joint inversion with explicit coupling instead of alternating solves).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Consensus-form splitting with a multiplier linking the two physics models (pp. 3-4).
- KKT-residual stopping tests extended by subproblem residuals for inexact inner solves (pp. 5, 7).
- Sweep inner versus outer iteration counts before claiming the method's cost (Table 1, p. 9).

**Quotes**:
- "We are investigating the application of global convergence results for augmented Lagrangian methods to develop a formal mathematical proof of convergence for the case when the subproblems are approximately minimized, which we leave to a forthcoming paper." (p. 7)
- "In our numerical results below, however, we still observe convergence of the overall scheme." (p. 7)

### S090 · Optimization-based simultaneous alignment and reconstruction in multi-element tomography (Optics Letters 2019, doi:10.1364/ol.44.004331)

**Read level**: full (accepted manuscript, all text pp. 2-5; reference list p. 6 skimmed).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Zichao (Wendy) Di (first, corresponding), Si Chen, Doga Gursoy, Tatjana Paunesku, Stefan M. Wild, Stefan Vogt (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: follow-up of S103 (it builds on "the numerical approach in [24]", Austin, Di, Leyffer, Wild, p. 3), carried from single-channel CoR drift to multi-element x-ray fluorescence data.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: nanoscale XRF tomography is "highly vulnerable to geometrical errors" (p. 2); current practice aligns before reconstruction (cross-correlation, fiducials, hotspots), and alternating reprojection methods "can fall short for the cases when the drift is significant" (p. 2); aligning on one high-SNR channel fails for poor SNR (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: recover per-angle shifts P and concentrations W jointly in one Poisson maximum-likelihood problem summed over elemental channels with weights α_e ∝ channel standard deviation (eqs. (3)-(4), p. 3); the Dirac shift is smoothed by a Gaussian with FWHM of one beam width (eq. (2), p. 3) so that derivatives in P exist; solved by truncated Newton with projected CG for W ≥ 0 (p. 3).
  - Transferable: replace a nonsmooth shift operator (Dirac convolution) by a Gaussian whose width is tied to the physical resolution, so calibration parameters can enter a derivative-based solver (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions**: same experimental error in all channels (p. 2); center of rotation assumed at the detector center (p. 3); only horizontal drift (p. 3, Fig. 1); 2D slice only (p. 3).
- **D4 Proof devices**: n/a (no theory; analytic gradients only, p. 3).
- **D5 Experiments**: one real dataset (SK-N-DZ cell, 48 projections over 141°, Bionanoprobe at APS, six elements) (p. 3); baselines manual hotspot alignment and the alternating method of TomoPy v1.2.1 (pp. 3-4); channel ablations (drop the good Ti channel, drop the bad P and S channels, separate per-element) (p. 4); no ground truth, so the median of five automated methods serves as reference (Fig. 3, p. 4), then SNR synthetically reduced to 30%-3% (Figs. 4-5, pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: without ground truth, take the consensus (median) of several independent automated methods as reference, then stress all methods by degrading the data (pp. 4-5).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: modelling contribution (joint multi-channel inversion) validated on one dataset; future: 3D extension "is natural", faster computing by in-plane parallelization (p. 5).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (smooth-NLP reformulation plus truncated Newton carried into XRF alignment, p. 3). Method 5 ✅ (baseline version stamped, TomoPy v1.2.1, p. 4). New-pattern candidate: couple, don't alternate (joint alignment and reconstruction instead of iterative reprojection, pp. 2-3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Gaussian smoothing of the shift operator with width fixed by beam FWHM (p. 3).
- Consensus-median reference plus synthetic SNR degradation for evaluation without ground truth (pp. 4-5).
- Channel ablation: remove the best channel and the worst channels to show robustness does not hinge on one signal (p. 4).

**Quotes**:
- "Overall, the contribution of each elemental channel to the final inversion should be proportional to its SNR." (p. 3)
- "This result indicates that the shifts recovered for the raw data are reliable and can be considered as ground truth." (p. 4)

### S103 · Simultaneous sensing error recovery and tomographic inversion using an optimization-based approach (SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 2019, doi:10.1137/18m121993x; read as arXiv:1902.02027v2)

**Read level**: full for the main text (arXiv pp. 1-19: §1-§6); appendices A and C partial (statements and error split, pp. 20-23); references not read. Read version: arXiv v2 preprint; cite as [S103 arXiv pp. …].

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Anthony P. Austin (first), Zichao (Wendy) Di, Stefan M. Wild (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the methods paper behind S090; problem brought by Vogt, Chen, Gursoy and De Carlo (acknowledgments, p. 19).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: drift of the center of rotation (CoR) during acquisition; "even a small error in the CoR can yield a large error in the reconstruction" (Fig. 1, p. 2). Generic remedies (Bayesian priors, regularizers) are claimed insufficient, shown in §5 (p. 2); mirrored-pair and cross-correlation methods are sensitive or limited (pp. 2-3); iterative reprojection does not fully couple alignment and reconstruction (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: the CoR shift becomes a translation P_θ = x*_θ(1 − cos θ) + y*_θ sin θ of the measured Radon data (eqs. (2.3)-(2.4), p. 4), so the projection matrix is computed once at CoR = 0 (p. 5); translation implemented by convolution with a Gaussian-regularized delta via DFT (eq. (2.5), p. 5). Two formulations: explicit in CoR coordinates (3.1) and implicit in shifts P (3.4), with half as many extra variables (pp. 8-9). Solver: truncated Newton with projected PCG and finite-difference Hessian-vector products (Algorithm 4.1, p. 10).
  - Transferable: when an unknown experimental parameter would force recomputing an expensive operator, move it to the data side as a cheap differentiable transformation and keep the operator fixed (pp. 4-5).
  - Transferable: keep two formulations of the same nuisance model (explicit coordinates vs implicit shifts) and report where each wins (pp. 12-17).
- **D3 Assumptions**: object attenuation C² for the error estimate (p. 6); W ≥ 0 as the only regularizer, by design (p. 11); 2D only, claimed to extend to 3D (pp. 3, 19); non-unique solutions up to translation and rotation acknowledged (p. 9).
- **D4 Proof devices**: error of the regularized translation O(σ²) + O(1/(σN_τ)) (eq. (2.6), p. 6), proved by splitting the error into regularization, truncation and discretization terms (eq. (C.2), p. 22) and bounding the trapezoid error with a bounded-variation result (Proposition C.1, p. 23); the trade-off fixes σ = 1/2.355 (FWHM = one beamlet), checked numerically (Fig. 3, p. 6). Flop count per TN iteration 52(|V| + N_θ) plus 7 function-gradient evaluations (p. 10).
  - Transferable: choose a smoothing parameter where two error terms of an a-priori bound cross, and confirm the crossover on simulated data (p. 6).
- **D5 Experiments**: synthetic only (Shepp-Logan, MRI image; N = 128, 30 angles) (p. 10); SSIM as metric because of non-uniqueness (p. 10); baselines: no recovery, L2 and TV regularization with λ chosen by best SSIM, mirror alignment (pp. 11-13), and the alternating method of TomoPy 1.1.3 under 4%-22% Gaussian noise (pp. 17-19); initialization study over five CoR starts (Fig. 9, p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: model plus formulations plus solver; limits named: computational complexity, future parallel or multigrid implementation, priors on CoR dynamics as regularizer (p. 19). No real data.

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ (baseline given its best setting: regularization λ chosen by an oracle SSIM, explicitly called impractical, p. 12; competitor version stated, TomoPy 1.1.3, p. 17). Method 6 ✅ (the formulation, explicit vs implicit and data-side translation, is the main design lever; pp. 8-9). Method 2 ✅ (truncated-Newton NLP machinery carried into tomography, p. 10). New-pattern candidate: couple, don't alternate (p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Data-side differentiable nuisance transform with a fixed forward operator (pp. 4-5).
- Oracle-tuned baseline: give the competing regularizer its idealized best parameter, say so, and still beat it (p. 12).
- Error-term crossover to pick a smoothing width (p. 6).

**Quotes**:
- "In the case of CoR drift, we show (in Sec. 5) that standard regularization approaches fail even when the regularization parameter value is chosen in an ideal way." (p. 2)
- "This is an idealistic choice of λ that is impractical." (p. 12)

### S155 · A Lagrangian dual approach for identifying the worst contingencies in power systems (Optimization Online 2019, no DOI)

**Read level**: partial (abstract and §1 pp. 1-3; §2.2 Lagrangian relaxation p. 5 and Proposition 1 p. 7; §3 opening and Propositions 3-5 pp. 7, 11; §4.1 algorithm, Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 pp. 11-14; §5 setup and results pp. 15-19; §6 conclusions pp. 19-20). The ACOPF model details of §2.1, the SDP/SOC derivations of §3.1-§3.3 and Propositions 6-7 were skimmed only.

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Brian Dandurand (first), Kibaek Kim (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: Argonne power-grid project; a 2019 ANL preprint distinct from the later SIAM J. Optim. paper (batch note).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: worst-K line contingencies as a max-min (Stackelberg) problem with a nonconvex ACOPF lower level (eq. (1), p. 1). Entry by excluding alternatives: DC/linear models "oversimplify" (p. 2); KKT reformulations violate CQs everywhere and their MILP versions give weak bounds (p. 2); the SOC approach of Wu et al. is less accurate than SDP (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: dualize the lower-level ACOPF to get a single-level mixed-binary concave maximization with one nonsmooth constraint η(π) ≥ 0, η a minimum-eigenvalue (trust-region) subproblem (eqs. (23)-(24), p. 7); solve it with a single-tree ECP (1T-ECP): cuts added as CPLEX lazy constraints at integer-feasible nodes within one tree (§4.1, pp. 11-12; p. 15). A new "structured relaxation" of line flows reduces dimension by 2|L| (pp. 9, 18).
  - Transferable: when a bilevel lower level is nonconvex, dualize it to get a valid single-level bound whose only nonlinear piece is an eigenvalue function, then generate its cuts lazily in one MILP tree (pp. 7, 11-12, 15).
- **D3 Assumptions**: feasible problem, bounded affine region, g_N convex nonsmooth and locally Lipschitz (pp. 12, 14); duality claims under "mild constraint qualifications" (p. 19); line flow bounds relaxed in all formulations (p. 15).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Proposition 1 turns the inner infimum into an eigenvalue indicator (p. 7). Lemma 1 (finite inner loop at each node) by contradiction: accumulation point, local Lipschitz ball of radius min{ε/(3L), δ}, and the earlier cut evaluated at a later iterate force g_N(ȳ) < ε (eq. (43), p. 14). Theorem 1 finite global termination from finitely many nodes plus Lemma 1 (p. 14). Chain of bounds Φ^SOC ≤ Φ^SDP = Φ^AC_D ≤ Φ^AC (Proposition 5, p. 11).
  - Transferable: for a cutting-plane loop on a Lipschitz nonsmooth constraint, prove finite ε-termination by putting two iterates in a small ball around an accumulation point and using the cut from the earlier one (p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: IEEE 30/57/118/300 and Pegase 1354/2869, budgets K = 1-4, 24 h limit, single core each, Julia/JuMP, CPLEX 12.7, Mosek (pp. 15-16); own baselines miSDP (simple B&B with a physics-informed branching rule) and miSOC (Mosek) (pp. 2-3, 15-16); losses reported: miSDP finds better solutions within time (p. 16), 1T-ECP-R tends to underestimate attack values (p. 17); miSOC fails on Pegase by memory (NA entries, p. 18).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: four listed contributions (p. 2); limits: exponentially many dense cuts, master-update time; future cut aggregation, spectral bundle/proximal methods, hybrid with mixed-integer SDP branching (pp. 19-20). The preprint still shows unresolved "Tables ??" references (p. 16).

**Method links**: Method 6 ✅ (formulation chosen by CQ reasoning: KKT reformulation rejected because it violates CQs at every feasible point, p. 2; master kept linear so MILP presolve helps, p. 19). Method 1 ✅ (single-tree integration via solver callbacks, the LP/NLP-BB idea of [QG92] carried to ECP, pp. 11, 15). Method 5 ✅ (self-built baselines, stated limits and threads, losses reported, pp. 15-18). Method 2 ✅ (MINLP cutting-plane toolkit carried into power-grid bilevel problems).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Dualize a nonconvex lower level into an eigenvalue constraint and cut it lazily in one tree (pp. 7, 11-15).
- Lipschitz-ball contradiction for finite termination of a cutting-plane inner loop (p. 14).
- Physics-informed pseudocost branching when strong branching is too expensive per node (pp. 2-3, 16).

**Quotes**:
- "However, such KKT-reformulated problems are well known to violate constraint qualification at every feasible point." (p. 2)
- "Note that if we ignore numerical error, both methods should produce the same optimal values when termination due to convergence is achieved." (p. 16)

### H014 · Compact Representations of BFGS Matrices (Argonne National Laboratory, MCS Division Preprint ANL/MCS-P9279-0120, January 2020, no DOI)

**Read level**: partial (cover p. 1; abstract, §1 and contributions pp. 3-5; Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 statements pp. 5-6 with proof openings pp. 5, 7; §3.1, §3.3 and §3.4 openings pp. 9-12; §4 setup and Experiments II-IV pp. 14-24; conclusions p. 24). The algebra of the proofs (pp. 5-9) and the figures were not read in detail. The inner title reads "Compact Representations of Structured BFGS Matrices" (p. 3).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Johannes J. Brust (first), Cosmin G. Petra (LLNL) (p. 3) · **Position in trajectory**: preprint of the later COAP paper (S121, batch note); Leyffer's home NLP line (quasi-Newton, large-scale NLP), funded by the "Multifaceted Mathematics for Complex Energy Systems" project (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: structured objectives f = k̂ + û with known Hessian for k̂ and unknown for û (eq. (1.4), p. 4); Petra et al.'s structured BFGS updates S-BFGS-M/P (eqs. (1.5)-(1.6), p. 4) have no compact representation, so no limited-memory version (p. 5).
- **D2 Core idea**: derive compact (low-rank factored) representations of both structured updates (Theorems 2.1 and 2.3, pp. 5-6), giving limited-memory versions, Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury search directions and four initialization strategies, embedded in strong-Wolfe line-search algorithms L-S-BFGS-M/P (Algorithms 3.1-3.2, pp. 10-12).
- **D3 Assumptions**: f twice continuously differentiable (p. 3); positive definiteness kept when s_k^T u_k > 0 (p. 4); efficient solves with K_k assumed for large n (p. 12).
- **D4 Proof devices**: reduce to the known case: adding K_{k+1} to both sides turns S-BFGS-M into the standard BFGS recursion in u_k, so Byrd-Nocedal-Schnabel applies (proof of Theorem 2.1, p. 5; p. 4); Theorem 2.3 by induction on k (p. 7).
  - Transferable: before proving a new compact formula, look for a shift of the matrix that maps the new recursion onto a classical one whose compact form is known (pp. 4-5).
- **D5 Experiments**: Experiment I tunes initializations on synthetic quadratics with controlled spectra (p. 15); II compares limited vs full memory on the CUTEst set of [18] (stated as 61 problems on p. 14 and 62 on p. 19); III on 10 LIBSVM logistic regressions and a PDE control problem up to n = 9604 (pp. 21-23); IV against L-BFGS and IPOPT 3.12.12 with limited-memory option on a structured quartic, averages of 5 runs (p. 24); extended performance profiles of [15] in iterations and time (pp. 14-15).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic plus implementation contribution; conclusion is calibrated by class: initializations 1 and 2 best for M, 4 and 2 robust for P (p. 24). No convergence theory in the paper; no future-work paragraph.

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ (competitor version stamped, IPOPT 3.12.12 with stated options, p. 24; conclusion as a rule of thumb per variant, p. 24; extended performance profiles, pp. 14-15, Heuristic 10). Method 2 ✅ (home large-scale NLP line).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Reduce a new quasi-Newton recursion to a classical one by a matrix shift, then inherit the compact form (pp. 4-5).
- Tune algorithmic defaults on synthetic problems with controlled spectra before running a library (p. 15).

**Quotes**:
- "The structured methods typically require fewer total iterations than do the unstructured approaches." (p. 24)
- "L-BFGS converges on about 28% of the problems." (p. 24)

### S034 · MPEC methods for bilevel optimization problems (Bilevel optimization: advances and next challenges 2020, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-52119-6_12)

**Read level**: full for the text (pp. 1-18: §1 introduction and reformulation, §2 stationarity and CQs, §3 solution approaches, §4 software, §5 extensions); reference list skimmed (pp. 19-24). Read version: the chapter preprint ANL/MCS-P9195-0719 (p. 1).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Youngdae Kim (first), Todd Munson (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: survey consolidation of his MPEC line (FilterMPEC, MPECs as NLPs, local SQP convergence with Fletcher, Ralph, Scholtes; SLPEC filter with Munson), cited as [46]-[49], [79]-[83] (pp. 20-22).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: optimistic bilevel problems with convex lower level, compact feasible region and a CQ for every upper-level decision (abstract; p. 3), replaced by the lower-level KKT system to get an MPEC (eq. (2), p. 3). Illustrated by the moral-hazard contract problem (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: survey organized by solution class: NLP reformulations (relaxation, penalty, elastic mode, augmented Lagrangian), combinatorial methods (pivoting, branch-and-bound/cut, SQPEC, SLPEC-EQP), implicit methods excluded (pp. 10-16); software table (p. 18).
- **D3 Assumptions**: all functions at least C¹ (p. 2); the bilevel-MPEC correspondence holds for global solutions under Slater, not always for local ones (p. 3); Fritz-John reformulation discouraged on computational grounds (p. 3).
- **D4 Proof devices**: survey of proof results, not new proofs: tangent-cone decomposition over NLP pieces (eqs. (8)-(9), p. 6); quadratic SQP convergence for the slack reformulation (15) near strongly stationary points under MPEC-LICQ and RNLP-SOSC, with a counterexample showing slacks are needed (p. 11); SQPEC counterexample (19) converging quadratically to an M-stationary point with descent direction (1, 0) (p. 15).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: survey plus pointers; states that no robust large-scale bilevel solver exists (p. 17) and that NLP methods may reach only C-stationary points (Remark 1, p. 14); extensions not covered listed (pessimistic, nonconvex lower level, SOCP lower level, stochastic, discrete, multiobjective, p. 18).
