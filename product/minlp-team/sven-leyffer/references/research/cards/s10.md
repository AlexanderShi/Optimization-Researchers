### S110 · An augmented Lagrangian filter method (Mathematical Methods of Operations Research 2020, doi:10.1007/s00186-020-00713-x)
**Read level**: full for the main text. Pp. 1-17 read (abstract, introduction, §§2-6, all lemmas and proofs); references and appendix tables of per-problem results (pp. 18-33) not read. The file is the Argonne preprint version with "Changes to previous version are marked in red" (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Charlie Vanaret (Fraunhofer ITWM, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the filter carried from SQP to augmented Lagrangian (AL) methods, extending the AL filter for QPs of [44] to general NLPs (p. 4); a predecessor of the Uno framework with Vanaret.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is downstream use. Interior-point methods lead for large NLPs but cannot warm-start, which matters for MINLP where thousands of related NLPs are solved; active-set methods warm-start but pivot and do not scale to multicore (p. 2). AL methods promise both, but classical AL needs forcing sequences ηk, ωk to control infeasibility and first-order error (p. 3, Algorithm 1). Infeasible NLPs are frequent inside MINLP, so infeasibility detection must be fast (p. 7).
  - Transferable: motivate an NLP method by what the outer (integer) algorithm needs from it, warm starts and fast infeasibility detection, and say so in the first page.
- **D2 Core idea**: Replace the forcing sequences by a filter on (η, ω0), the constraint violation and the first-order error of the Lagrangian (not of the augmented Lagrangian), Definition 2.1 (p. 4). Choosing the Lagrangian is deliberate: after the first-order multiplier update its gradient equals that of the AL, so acceptance can be checked while minimizing the AL for fixed multipliers (p. 5, (2.8)). Inner loop: approximately minimize the bound-constrained AL until a filter-acceptable point appears; switch to a restoration phase (and double ρ) when η ≥ βU or when the AL stalls at a stationary point with η ≥ βηmin (pp. 7-8, (3.15)-(3.16)). Optional pieces: a penalty estimate from norms of the reduced Hessian and Jacobian (pp. 6-7, (3.14)) and an EQP step on the active bounds with a backtracking search (p. 9). Unchanged from classical AL: the BCL subproblem and the first-order multiplier update (pp. 3, 10).
  - Transferable: when a globalization device needs a measure that survives the multiplier update, pick the one whose gradient identity makes it checkable inside the inner solve (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions**: A1 f, c twice continuously differentiable; A2 ||c(x)|| → ∞ as ||x|| → ∞, replaceable by finite bounds (pp. 10-11). No bounded-multiplier or compact-iterate assumption (p. 5). LICQ only at limits where ρk → ∞ (p. 13). The analysis covers the plain method without the EQP step (p. 10).
- **D4 Proof devices**: compactness from the filter's upper bound U on η (Lemma 4.1, p. 11); a filter-free neighbourhood of the origin from the sloping envelope (Lemma 4.2, p. 11); finite inner loop because the sufficient reduction (3.18) would make the AL unbounded below (Lemma 4.3, pp. 11-12); feasibility of limits borrowed from the SLP filter proof [26, Lemma 1] (Lemma 4.4, p. 12); the inner sufficient reduction turned into an outer one via the multiplier update, with the term c(x)ᵀy bounded by a geometric series in β from filter acceptance (Lemma 4.5, (4.21)-(4.22), pp. 12-13); the unbounded-penalty case through Theorem 2.5 of [43] (Lemma 4.6, p. 13). Three outcomes: KKT, FJ without LICQ, or a minimizer of infeasibility (Theorem 4.1, p. 13).
  - Transferable: to turn an inner sufficient-decrease condition into outer progress, write the outer decrease as inner decrease minus ρ||c||², and absorb the second term once feasibility is shown (p. 12).
- **D5 Experiments**: preliminary C++ filter-al (L-BFGS-B for the AL, BQPD for the EQP) on 429 CUTEst problems with at most 100 variables/constraints, against filterSQP, SNOPT, MINOS, IPOPT, LANCELOT, metered by function evaluations, performance profiles; 34 failures listed by cause (pp. 14-17).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic plus convergence theory. Claims are bounded: "competitive" with SQP solvers in evaluations, better than the AL solvers (pp. 15, 17). Limits stated: implementation preliminary, L-BFGS-B iteration counts inflate evaluations (p. 15); no second-order convergence proof, expected via second-order corrections or a nonmonotone filter (p. 17); Remark 4.1 conjectures bounded penalties for strongly stationary MPECs, untested (p. 14).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: the filter carried into AL, with MINLP warm starts as the reason (pp. 2, 4). Method 5 evidence ✅: evaluation count chosen and justified as the meter for small problems and a preliminary code (p. 15); failures kept and classified, 14 IEEE evaluation errors versus 20 L-BFGS-B failures (p. 15, Table 1). Heuristic 7 (downstream use chooses the upstream research) is the stated motivation (pp. 2, 7). New-pattern candidate: "prove the plain core, keep accelerators optional and show they cannot hurt" (pp. 5-6, 10).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Analyse the plain algorithm only, and for each optional heuristic (penalty estimate, EQP step) give the one property that keeps the theory intact: the estimate is bounded, the line search can fall back to the already acceptable point (pp. 6, 9).
- In a failure table, separate failures outside the solver's control (IEEE errors in evaluating f or its gradient) from failures of the subproblem solver (p. 15).
**Quotes**:
- "The Achilles’ heel of interior-point methods is the lack of efficient warm-start strategies." (p. 2)
- "In practice, many NLPs are not feasible; this situation happens frequently, for example during the resolution of MINLPs." (p. 7)

### S175 · Solving combinatorial optimization problems on quantum computers (SIAM News 53(06), July/August 2020, no DOI)
**Read level**: full. The whole article read (file p. 1 = SIAM News p. 3; file p. 2 = p. 6). The file also contains the end of an unrelated article on population recovery and a book review; they were not used.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Yuri Alexeev, Jeffrey Larson, Ruslan Shaydulin (p. 2) · **Place in trajectory**: an early written trace of the quantum-optimization extension at Argonne, years before the 2026 quantum Hamiltonian descent work named in SKILL.md Method 2.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: No quantum algorithm is demonstrably faster for combinatorial optimization; QAOA is the candidate for gate-model hardware. The aim is "to disentangle the hype from reality" (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: An expository recasting in optimization terms: a function on the Boolean hypercube becomes a diagonal operator on a 2^n-dimensional space via indicator (projector) expansion (p. 1, (1)); QAOA's parameterized state turns the combinatorial problem into a nonconvex continuous problem over (β, γ) solved by a classical optimizer (p. 2). Two obstacle lists follow: understanding (no analysis beyond p = 1 or p = ∞, no scaling experiments) and realization (parameter optimization, hardware) (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions**: n/a (no theorem). Gate counts assume full qubit connectivity and CNOT as the native 2-qubit gate (p. 2).
- **D4 Proof devices**: n/a. Cited bounds only: Hastings on p = 1, the symmetry argument that Goemans-Williamson beats constant-depth QAOA on MAXCUT (p. 2).
- **D5 Experiments**: none; one landscape figure for MAXCUT on an 8-node graph, p = 1 (p. 1, Fig. 1).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: a calibrated overview. The single-local-run default in QAOA packages is criticized, multistart and learning are named as partial fixes, and the best parameter optimizer is called "largely an open question" (p. 2). Classical methods win under a few hundred variables, so QAOA cannot compete until hardware with hundreds of high-fidelity qubits exists (p. 2).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: the quantum object is recast into the home class, combinatorial optimization replaced by "a nonconvex optimization over" the circuit parameters (p. 2). Method 5 evidence ✅ (calibrated claims transferred to a field with hype): a stated condition, hundreds of qubits, under which the new method could compete, and the classical baseline named (p. 2).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When writing about a hyped method, split the obstacles into "what prevents us from understanding its potential" and "what prevents us from realizing it", and give a concrete threshold (problem size, hardware) for when it could compete (p. 2).
**Quotes**:
- "To disentangle the hype from reality, we first recap the mathematics of quantum computation and QAOA." (p. 1)
- "Therefore, until quantum hardware with hundreds of qubits is accessible, QAOA will not be able to compete with classical state-of-the-art methods under the current approach." (p. 2)

### S044 · Apprentice for event generator tuning (EPJ Web of Conferences 251, CHEP 2021, doi:10.1051/epjconf/202125103060, arXiv:2103.05748)
**Read level**: full. All 9 pages read (arXiv v1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Mohan Krishnamoorthy, Holger Schulz, Xiangyang Ju, Wenjing Wang, Zachary Marshall, Stephen Mrenna, Juliane Müller, James B. Kowalkowski (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the software paper of the HEP surrogate line (SciDAC "HEP Data Analytics on HPC", p. 8); companion to S052.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Monte Carlo event generators have O(10-100) parameters tuned by minimizing a weighted χ² over observables (p. 1, (1)). The predecessor tool Professor has three gaps: polynomial surrogates fail on ~1/x behaviour, the minimizer suite is limited and its influence unexplored, and observable weights are set by hand in a costly loop (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Three surrogate builders: the Stieltjes-based rational fit, a constrained pole-free formulation, and a linear-denominator special case in which LP duality turns the "denominator > 0 on a box" constraint into finite constraints, so one optimization suffices (p. 2). Weight selection is automated in two ways: bilevel optimization over weights with a portfolio or scoring-rule merit function approximated by an RBF (p. 3), and a single-level robust formulation in which the worst deviation over an interval uncertainty set sits at an endpoint, plus a coverage constraint (at least µ% of observables) to rule out all-zero weights (p. 3).
  - Transferable: when a robust counterpart has a convex square inside a 1-D interval, replace the inner max by the two endpoints and get a single-level problem (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions**: experiment and simulation modelled as independent random variables with mean Rb (p. 3); pole-free guarantee only for box-shaped parameter domains (p. 2).
- **D4 Proof devices**: n/a (tool paper).
- **D5 Experiments**: dark-matter limit setting, 3 parameters: rational (4,4) vs polynomial degree 7 with matched coefficient counts, about 30,000 likelihood evaluations, factor ~50 faster than full simulation (p. 6); ATLAS A14 tune re-done, χ² per-bin distributions by observable class against the expert tune and equal weights (p. 7, Fig. 2).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: software with three formulations; no single method wins: each weighting performs better on some observable classes, and the result is offered as guidance for the physicist (p. 7). Future: derivative-free tuning on generator data directly, χ² minimizers robust to local minima, ML for dimension reduction (p. 7).

**Method links**: Method 6 evidence ✅: the surrogate is constrained to be pole-free because "the guaranteed absence of poles ensures that subsequent optimization problems involving our rational approximations are well-defined" (p. 2). Method 5 evidence ✅: trade-offs by observable class instead of a winner (p. 7). Method 2 evidence ✅ (weak): robust and bilevel optimization carried into generator tuning (p. 3).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When comparing surrogate types, match the number of coefficients, not the degree (p. 6, footnote 3).
**Quotes**:
- "The dependence on minimizer, if any, needs to be explored." (p. 2)
- "Clearly, the rational approximation faithfully represents the full simulation, while the polynomial approximation is only valid over a limited range." (p. 7)

### S052 · Practical algorithms for multivariate rational approximation (Computer Physics Communications 2021, doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107663; read as arXiv:1912.02272v1 "Multivariate Rational Approximation")
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-3), §2 and §3.1 (pp. 4-6), degree reduction §3.3 (pp. 9-11), §4 and Algorithm 4.1 (pp. 11-13), §5.1-5.2 (pp. 13-18), §5.4-5.5 (pp. 22-23), §6 HEP application (pp. 24-27), §7 conclusions (pp. 26-28), Appendix B (p. 33). Not read: the Stieltjes recurrences in detail (pp. 6-8), the noise and pole-count results §5.3 (pp. 18-21, only p. 19 skimmed), tables of Appendix A.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Anthony P. Austin, Mohan Krishnamoorthy (corresponding), Stephen Mrenna, Juliane Müller, Holger Schulz (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: the method paper behind apprentice (S044): outer approximation reused for a semi-infinite constraint outside MINLP.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Expensive HEP simulations need cheap surrogates; polynomials extrapolate poorly and cannot represent poles, rational functions can, but are fragile and prone to spurious poles; inside an optimization a pole makes the objective unbounded (pp. 2-3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two methods for the linearized least-squares fit min Σ(p(xk) − fk q(xk))² (p. 4, (2)). (i) Linear algebra: an orthonormal discrete basis by a multivariate Stieltjes process, the denominator as the smallest right singular vector, and degree reduction while the smallest singular value is below a threshold (pp. 4-5, 9-10, Algorithm 3.3). (ii) Optimization: "no poles in D" is an open condition, so it is replaced by q(x) ≥ τ on D, a linear semi-infinite constraint, handled by Polyak's outer approximation: solve the finite relaxation, globally minimize q on D, add the violating point, repeat (pp. 11-13, Algorithm 4.1). The global check is done by multistart, tested against BARON (p. 33).
  - Transferable: turn an open "never zero" requirement into a closed "bounded away from zero with a fixed sign" constraint before optimizing (p. 11).
- **D3 Assumptions**: sample points form a set of linear independence for the polynomial space (p. 5, footnote 1); D a box; degrees fixed in advance ("beyond the scope", p. 14); the pole-free claims assume no true pole in the sampled domain (p. 23).
- **D4 Proof devices**: brief. Rank count for the interpolation case: (VMV⁺M − I) has rank at most α(N) − 1, so W has a zero singular value (p. 5). Otherwise algorithmic, no convergence theorem for Algorithm 4.1.
- **D5 Experiments**: analytic test functions, numerator and denominator degree 5, K = 2(α(M)+α(N)) points, five seeds for random designs, noise levels 0, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻², held-out L2 error and counts of pole-like points (pp. 13-14, 19); sparse grids vs LHS vs a new face-plus-interior design d-LHD (pp. 14-18); CPU time split into fit and multistart (p. 22, Table 3); HEP dark-matter likelihood with 500 LHS training points, 29,612 vs 29,459 evaluations, 288 s vs 14,594 s (pp. 25-26, Table 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: algorithmic, with an application. Limits stated: choosing degrees is open, noise makes it harder (p. 27); the multistart cost grows exponentially with degrees of freedom (p. 23); tested up to 7 parameters while HEP has dozens, so scalability is open (p. 28). Future: other structural constraints such as monotonicity for CDFs in chance constraints (p. 28).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: outer approximation, the home MINLP device, carried into surrogate fitting as a cutting-point scheme for a semi-infinite constraint (pp. 11-13). Method 6 evidence ✅: the formulation is changed (pole-free constraint) so that the downstream optimization is well posed, before any algorithm is tuned (pp. 3, 11). Method 5 evidence ✅: the expensive method's cost is reported and decomposed (p. 22), multistart vs BARON given equal time "to ensure a fair comparison" (p. 33), and the pole-free claims bounded by an explicit assumption (p. 23). New-pattern candidate: "structural constraints that make a surrogate safe for the optimizer that will use it" (pp. 2-3, 11; also S044 p. 2).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When samples are collinear (sparse grids) and the fit is singular, choose the ridge weight by an L-curve, and report that regularization can hurt accuracy even when it speeds convergence (pp. 14-16).
- If the separation oracle of an outer-approximation loop only needs a violated point, stop the global search at the first violation instead of solving to optimality, and budget it by the time the exact global solver would take (p. 33).
**Quotes**:
- "The key idea is this: If there are many approximations that will work, one should use the approximation with the lowest-degree denominator." (p. 9)
- "We note here that these claims are based on the assumption that the data are sampled over a domain that does not include any true poles." (p. 23)

### S080 · Joint ptycho-tomography with deep generative priors (Machine Learning: Science and Technology 2021, doi:10.1088/2632-2153/ac1d35; read as arXiv:2009.09498v2)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-4), §2.2-2.3 (pp. 5-6), §3 (p. 7), Algorithm 1 (p. 10), §5.1-5.3 (pp. 10-16), §6 (pp. 16-17). Not read: §2.1 forward model details, §2.4 subproblem solvers, §4 network and training details (pp. 8-9), references.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Selin Aslan, Zhengchun Liu, Viktor Nikitin, Tekin Bicer, Doğa Gürsoy (p. 1); Leyffer is fifth of six, so the voice is mainly the imaging group's · **Place in trajectory**: augmented-Lagrangian/ADMM machinery used in an Argonne X-ray imaging collaboration.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Sequential ptychography-then-tomography needs ≥60% probe overlap; joint reconstruction relaxes it (p. 2). Plug-and-play (PnP) denoisers were built for additive Gaussian noise in linear problems, while ptycho-tomography is nonconvex with Poisson noise that blurs high frequencies (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: An AL/ADMM splitting into ptychographic phase retrieval, tomography and regularization subproblems with two multiplier updates (p. 6, (6)-(10)); the regularization step is replaced by a GAN denoiser (TomoGAN) via PnP (p. 7, (20)); a denoiser parameter αk blends denoiser output and input, ηk+1 = αk Denoiser(x̃) + (1−αk) x̃ (p. 10, Algorithm 1).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Poisson MAP model (p. 5, (2)-(3)); the PnP prior has no explicit objective, so "a formal optimization strategy is not applicable" (p. 3).
- **D4 Proof devices**: n/a (no convergence analysis; ADMM convergence monitored through primal and dual residuals, p. 15, (22)-(23)).
- **D5 Experiments**: two simulated 3D objects (64×512×512 chip; 180×512×512 Au/Hg phantom), Poisson noise at three photon counts, well-sampled (50% overlap) vs under-sampled (25% overlap, 3N/8 angles), 250 ADMM iterations with 4 inner CG iterations; ML vs TV-MAP vs PnP-GAN by PSNR; six αk schedules with residual decay (pp. 10-16).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: method plus empirical study; claims a 75% reduction in projections at good quality (p. 4). Limits stated: the α schedule is empirical, not optimal, because of nonconvexity (pp. 14, 16); code not yet optimized; only synthetic data because no dedicated ptycho-tomography beamline is available for validation (p. 17).

**Method links**: Method 2 variant ⚠: the AL/ADMM toolkit enters imaging, but with an implicit prior and no convergence guarantee, the opposite of Taste 3 ("A result is only as good as the guarantee behind it"); given his authorship position this is weak evidence of his own method (pp. 3, 7, 14).
**Transferable techniques**:
- With a learned denoiser inside ADMM, ramp its weight from small to full over the outer iterations and judge schedules by both image quality and primal/dual residual decay; oscillating weights give oscillating residuals (pp. 15-16).
**Quotes**:
- "Our results show that our optimizations can decrease the total number of required projections (with significantly fewer overlapped regions) by 75% compared with using adequately sampled data (based on Nyquist) while maintaining good image quality." (p. 4)
- "The goal of this section is not to provide an optimal denoiser parameter, but to share valuable observations to decide on an effective one." (p. 14)

### S084 · Inversion of convection-diffusion equation with discrete sources (Optimization and Engineering 2021, doi:10.1007/s11081-020-09536-5)
**Read level**: full for the main text. File pp. 3-31 read (abstract to conclusions), except the instance-generation part of §5 (pp. 18-19, skimmed); appendices 1-3 and references (pp. 31-41) not read. Displayed equations and algorithm symbols are partly lost in extraction; the prose around them is intact.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Meenarli Sharma, Mirko Hahn, Lars Ruthotto, Bart van Bloemen Waanders; Leyffer is the corresponding author (p. 3) · **Place in trajectory**: a MIPDECO test case from the SAMSI 2016-2017 optimization program (p. 31); a sibling of the Hahn-Leyffer-Manns trust-region work and the cloaking paper (S085).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Find the number and location of pollutant sources in a convection-diffusion model from sparse sensor data, with a binary source function (pp. 3-4). The entry is an experiment with general solvers: "one motivation of this paper is to experiment with state-of-the-art mixed-integer solvers for solving this class of problems" (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Discretize-then-optimize (optimize-then-discretize is ruled out because it would imply first-order conditions for global integer optimality, p. 8), eliminate the states with the factorized stiffness matrix to get a binary program with a convex objective (p. 12, (6)). Two-phase heuristic: (i) rounding of the relaxation that uses its value (objective-gap-reduction: search the cut-off t) or its physics (mass-preserving: set the largest S̄ entries to 1, S̄ the rounded total mass), pp. 13-15; (ii) a trust-region improvement on binaries with an ℓ1 radius, i.e. a Hamming-distance limit on flips, optionally restricted to a θ-neighbourhood of current sources (pp. 15-17, Algorithm 3); the linear subproblem with one knapsack constraint is solved by sign case analysis, variable complementing and sorting (pp. 17-18).
  - Transferable: an ℓ1 trust region around a binary point is a cardinality constraint on flips, so a linearized step is a sorted knapsack, solvable exactly (pp. 16-18).
- **D3 Assumptions**: linear steady-state PDE with nonsingular stiffness matrix, so the states can be eliminated (p. 12); iid Gaussian measurement noise with known σ (p. 10); TV regularization, with α chosen by the L-curve on the relaxation (pp. 10, 20).
- **D4 Proof devices**: brief. Finite termination of the trust-region heuristic: objective bounded below, finitely many binary points, integer radius halved by floor (p. 17). Mass-preserving rounding keeps the mass difference below 1 (p. 15).
- **D5 Experiments**: AMPL finite-difference 2D instances from 16×8 to 256×128 (up to 32,768 binaries) on SCIP, Bonmin (B&B, hybrid, OA) and Minotaur (B&B, QG) with 10 CPU-hours: only B&B solves the two smallest meshes, gaps up to infinity elsewhere (pp. 20-24, Table 2); rounding schemes and the NLP penalty heuristic compared by objective, time and PDE solves (pp. 24-25, Table 3); trust region from each rounding in 2D and 3D, with IoU against the ground truth (pp. 25-30, Table 4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: application-driven heuristic with open Julia and AMPL code (pp. 5, 18, 31). Limits: heuristics with no optimality guarantee; the neighbourhood variant cannot find a source missed by the starting point (p. 27); whether the approach generalizes to MINLPs with convex objective and linear constraints is left open, the obstacle being a tractable subproblem when states cannot be eliminated (p. 31, a referee's question).

**Method links**: Method 6 evidence ✅: solver logs read before blaming the problem: SCIP presolve adds 8,002 quadratic constraints at 128×64 although the problem "can be solved as a bound-constrained NLP" after eliminating states, Bonmin-OA returns only W = 0, Minotaur stops on an IPOPT restoration failure (pp. 23-24). Method 4 evidence ✅: a self-contained finite-difference model written for reproducibility, AMPL instances released "to promote experimentation with existing mixed-integer programming (MIP) solvers" (pp. 5, 12). Method 5 evidence ✅: bounds and gaps reported per solver and mesh, Bonmin-OA's exclusion explained, and the time limit raised from 2 to 10 hours with no change in bound quality (pp. 21-24). Method 1 evidence ✅ (as diagnosis): off-the-shelf solvers refactor the stiffness matrix every iteration; reusing one factorization is what makes the heuristic cheap (pp. 23, 29, 31). New-pattern candidate: "Hamming-distance trust region on binaries solved as a knapsack" (pp. 16-18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Before proposing a heuristic for a new MINLP class, run the standard solvers on a mesh ladder with a generous limit, tabulate nodes, bounds and gaps, then raise the limit once to show the bounds do not move (pp. 21-24).
- Score inverse-problem reconstructions with a task metric (intersection-over-union against the ground truth) beside the objective value (p. 21).
**Quotes**:
- "We show empirically that current state-of-the-art mixed-integer solvers cannot solve this problem and that applying simple rounding heuristics to solutions of the relaxed problem can fail to identify the correct number and location of the sources." (p. 3)
- "None of the off-the-shelf solvers exploit the special structure that is inherent in the discretized PDEs" (p. 23)

### S085 · Convergence of sum-up rounding schemes for cloaking problems governed by the Helmholtz equation (Computational Optimization and Applications 2021, doi:10.1007/s10589-020-00262-3; read as preprint ANL/MCS-P9268-1019)
**Read level**: partial. The file is the Argonne preprint (cover dated December 10, 2019; title page dated December 23, 2020), the same text as S177's preprint row. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 3-5), Assumptions 2.1/2.3 (p. 6), problem (P) (pp. 9-10), §4 complete except the proofs of Lemmas 4.6 and 4.9 and of Theorem 4.10 (pp. 12-20), §5 (pp. 20-23) and §6 (pp. 23-24). Not read: the Helmholtz solution theory, Propositions 2.4-2.7 (pp. 6-9), the first-order optimality system §3.1 (pp. 10-12).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Paul Manns, Malte Winckler (p. 3) · **Place in trajectory**: the MIPDECO line with Manns, between the 2019 preprint and the McCormick-envelope paper in SKILL.md's anatomy; the convergence theory comes from Manns and Kirches [28] (p. 12).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Design a scatterer with finitely many material values so that an object is hidden from an incident Helmholtz wave (pp. 3-4). Prior work treats it as topology optimization with a level-set method that leaves some fractional material [18] (p. 4); the MIPDECO view asks for discrete-valued controls, and the problem may only have an infimum (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Never solve the discretized integer program. Algorithm 1 refines and locally solves the continuous relaxation, refines a rounding grid, applies multidimensional sum-up rounding (SUR) along an order-conserving cell ordering such as a space-filling curve, then median-filters (pp. 12-15, Algorithms 1-2). SUR runs in O(N) (p. 5).
  - Transferable: a rounding scheme for one-dimensional (time) controls carries to spatial controls once the cells are ordered by a space-filling curve whose refinements keep the order (pp. 13-14, Remark 4.2).
- **D3 Assumptions**: wave number k0 "sufficiently small" for unique solvability and the needed compactness (pp. 16-17, 19); domain regularity of Assumption 2.1 (p. 6); rounding grids form an order-conserving domain dissection with regular shrinkage (Definition 4.1, pp. 13-14); a consistent choice of relaxed controls αn (p. 14).
- **D4 Proof devices**: weak* convergence of SUR controls in L∞ from [28] (Proposition 4.4, p. 15); complete continuity of the control-to-state operator (weak* to norm) from compactness of the Helmholtz solution operator (Lemmas 4.6, 4.9, pp. 16, 18); hence inf of (P) = min of (R) and a minimizing sequence of discrete controls (Theorem 4.7, p. 17); the algorithm's objective values converge to those of the relaxation's local minimizers (Theorem 4.11, p. 19). Remark 4.8: if the relaxation only has fractional minimizers, (P) has no minimizer (p. 17).
- **D5 Experiments**: FEniCS/dolfin-adjoint/PETSc-Tao, Robin boundary instead of Sommerfeld, nine refinements up to 2⁹×2⁹ cells, about 12 hours on a laptop; state errors and objective gaps between relaxed, SUR and filtered controls (pp. 20-22, Tables 1-2); a wave-number sweep 0.24π to 750π showing convergence only for small k0 (p. 23).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: theory plus validation. Limits stated: designs chatter, which is unavoidable when the limit is fractional (p. 5); median filtering is a heuristic without guarantee, and a counterexample can destroy the approximation (pp. 5, 19); results are not one-to-one comparable with [18] because of the boundary condition (pp. 20-21); proofs hold only for small k0 (p. 23). Future: regularization that promotes discrete values (L1, multi-bang, BV), Remark 4.12 (pp. 19-20).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: the combinatorial-integral-approximation toolkit from mixed-integer optimal control carried into a PDE design problem, "the MIPDECO point of view" against the topology-optimization one (pp. 3-4, 12). Method 3 evidence ✅: the authors' own post-processing gets a counterexample, chattering SUR controls whose median is zero everywhere (p. 19). Method 5 evidence ✅: the competitor's published design was digitized and evaluated in their own setup to show why the numbers are not comparable (pp. 20-21). Method 2 limitation (carried-in assumption) confirmed: the theory needs k0 small, and the sweep shows the method failing beyond it (p. 23). New-pattern candidate: "sweep the theorem's smallness parameter past its range and report where convergence stops" (p. 23).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Validate an approximation theorem in two ways: self-convergence of the relaxed states across refinements, and the gap between relaxed and rounded states on a fixed relaxed control as only the rounding grid is refined (pp. 20-22).
- When a competitor's numbers come from a different boundary model, digitize their design and evaluate it in your model before comparing objective values (pp. 20-21).
**Quotes**:
- "We emphasize that the runtime complexity of SUR is O(N), where N is the number of cells that discretize the domain of the control input function." (p. 5)
- "However, the filtering step is a heuristic, and we cannot guarantee that the approximation properties of the SUR approximation are conserved." (p. 5)
