### S088 · Binary control pulse optimization for quantum systems (Quantum 2023, doi:10.22331/q-2023-01-04-892)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-3), §2 generic model (pp. 3-4), §3 opening and Table 1 (p. 8), §3.2-§3.3 penalty, SUR and CIA with the theorem statements (pp. 10-15), §4 TV model and ADMM (pp. 15-17), §5 approximate local branching (pp. 17-19), §6 all numerical results (pp. 19-26), §7 conclusion (pp. 26-28), proof of Theorem 3 and the opening of the proof of Theorem 4 (pp. 34-35), reference list entries [57]-[85]. Not read: the four application formulations in §2.1-§2.4 beyond headings, Theorem 1 (p. 7), the GRAPE gradient derivations (pp. 8-10), the remaining appendix proofs and Appendix B tables (pp. 35-43).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xinyu Fei, Lucas T. Brady, Jeffrey Larson, Siqian Shen (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: Argonne quantum-computing work (DOE Accelerated Research for Quantum Computing, p. 28); his mixed-integer optimal-control toolkit (sum-up rounding, CIA, trust-region local branching) carried into quantum pulse design.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Application-driven. Quantum optimal-control methods (GRAPE, K-GRAPE, CRAB) are "designed for unconstrained continuous quantum control problems" (p. 2), while gate-based and QAOA-like settings need binary bang-bang controls with one active control per time step (SOS1) and side constraints (pp. 2, 4). Binary-control methods either fail in quality (genetic algorithms) or are too slow (branch-and-bound); the Vogt–Petersson trust-region method is extended to constraints such as min-up-time and max-switching (pp. 2-3). Four test problems: energy minimization, CNOT gate, NOT gate with leakage, circuit compilation (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: A relax → round → improve pipeline (Table 1, p. 8). (i) Keep GRAPE's adjoint gradient but move the SOS1 constraint into a squared L2 penalty so the bound-constrained GRAPE still applies (pGRAPE, §3.2, pp. 10-11). (ii) Add a total-variation regularizer against chattering; "Because of the L1 term, we cannot use GRAPE" so ADMM with a soft-threshold v-update is used (p. 16, Algorithm 2 p. 17). (iii) Round by sum-up rounding (SUR) or by CIA with min-up-time / max-switching constraints (§3.3, pp. 12-15). (iv) Improve the binary point by "approximate local branching": a trust-region MILP subproblem with an L1 radius, accepted by an actual/predicted decrease test; the radius is halved above a threshold, then decreased by one (§5, pp. 17-19, Algorithm 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: F differentiable in the final operator (p. 4); uniform piecewise-constant time discretization with explicit matrix exponentials (p. 4); Theorem 3 assumes F bounded by C_F and (DQCP) feasible (p. 11); penalty exactness (Theorem 2) holds only for binary controls and is taken from [57] (Sinclair 1986) (pp. 11-12).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Proposition 1 is a negative result: without SOS1 in the relaxed control, SUR's integral error is bounded below by ε(Δt)/N (p. 13). Theorem 4 extends Sager's SUR bound by an additive term (2N−1)/N·ε(Δt) (p. 13). Corollary 1 links that term to the penalty residual, ε(Δt) ≤ sqrt(t_f·l·Δt), so convergence of the rounded control follows (p. 13). Theorem 3 (l ≤ 2C_F/ρ) is a short contradiction against a feasible point (p. 34).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: NEOS baselines (SNOPT 7.6.1, IPOPT 3.13.4, MUSCOD-II, MINLP, Bonmin 1.8.8, Couenne 0.5.8, SCIP 7.0.3.5) on energy minimization with q = 2, 6, with time limits and OOM marks (Table 2, p. 20); 12 instances in four classes (Table 3, p. 22); theory checked by plots (penalty O(1/ρ), Fig. 6 p. 22; SUR error under the bound, Figs. 8-9 p. 24); objective vs TV scatter before/after ALB (Fig. 12, p. 27); code public on GitHub ([85], p. 22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Four contributions listed (model, exact penalty + GRAPE/rounding framework, TV + ADMM, modified trust region) (p. 3). The conclusion gives a choice rule by goal (low infidelity → SUR; few switches → min-up-time; ALB in both cases) and admits parameter tuning (p. 27). ADMM gives no global optimality (p. 17). Future work: switching-time optimization (p. 28); running the time evolution on quantum hardware (pp. 25, 28).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (the SKILL already names "quantum pulses as TV-regularized binary control"; here the carried-in pieces are SUR/CIA rounding and the trust-region local-branching step of [69] Leyffer–Manns, pp. 16, 18). Method 5 ⚠ variant: baselines are named with versions and limits (p. 20), but no baseline tuning is reported, they are run on one problem class, and the paper concludes that they "cannot solve" discretized control problems (p. 20); the closing advice is a rule by goal, not a winner (p. 27), as in Method 5 step 5. Method 3 ✅ (weak): Proposition 1 shows that the known SUR guarantee fails once its SOS1 hypothesis is dropped, before an extended bound is proved (pp. 12-13). New-pattern candidates: "Relax, round with structure, then improve locally"; "theory-quantity profile beside performance profiles" (Figs. 6, 8, 9).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When the inner solver only handles bounds, move a side constraint into an exact squared penalty, bound the residual violation (O(1/ρ)), then extend the rounding theorem by an additive term in that residual (pp. 11-13).
- Plot each theoretical bound against the measured quantity as Δt or ρ varies (Figs. 6, 8, 9, pp. 22-24).
- Report a two-objective scatter (objective vs switching/TV) before and after the improvement step, and recommend a method per user goal (pp. 26-27).
- Integer trust-region radius schedule: halve above a threshold, then decrease by one (Algorithm 3, p. 19).

**Quotes**:
- "We show that as long as the violation of the SOS1 constraint is small, the solution that is constructed via SUR still satisfies the strong convergence properties of SUR." (p. 12)
- "Both cases require the use of the approximate local branching algorithm to improve final solutions." (p. 27)

### S115 · On convergence of binary trust-region steepest descent (Journal of Nonsmooth Analysis and Optimization 2023, doi:10.46298/jnsao-2023-10164; arXiv 2202.07934)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract, §1 with Assumption 1.1 and contributions (pp. 1-3), §2 CIA and Proposition 2.1 (pp. 3-5), §3 BTR algorithm (pp. 5-7), §4 criticality measure, Theorem 4.4, Corollary 4.5 (pp. 7-9), the proof of Theorem 4.4 (pp. 11-13), §6 all experiments with Tables 1-3 (pp. 13-18), Appendix A.1 (pp. 19-21) and the opening of Appendix B.1 (pp. 22-23). Not read in detail: Lemmas 5.1-5.4 (pp. 9-11), the rest of Appendix A and B (pp. 21-24). The title matches the journal version; the work-list note gives the arXiv first title.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Paul Manns, Mirko Hahn, Christian Kirches, Sebastian Sager (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: follow-up to the Hahn–Leyffer–Sager BTR paper ([10], Math. Program. 197, 2023) and his MIPDECO line (refs [33], [36]).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A theory gap. BTR was known only to reach ε-stationarity under a regularity assumption; "it is not known whether the iterates generated by BTR converge to a limit point that satisfies a first-order optimality condition of (R)" (p. 2), whereas CIA has stationary limits under a compactness assumption (p. 2). Entry: import CIA's compactness assumption into the BTR analysis (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Under Assumption 1.1(d) (∇J weak-norm continuous), BTR's binary iterates converge weakly-* to points that are feasible and stationary for the relaxation (R) (Theorem 4.4, Corollary 4.5, pp. 8-9). The algorithm is unchanged: a special case of [10, Algorithm 2] with ε = 0 (p. 5). The link is a criticality measure C (the primal gap function) that equals the L1 norm of min{g, 0} at characteristic functions (Proposition 4.3, p. 8). Practical addition: a hybrid of SUR on a coarse mesh followed by BTR (§6.4, pp. 17-18).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 1.1 (a)-(d): J bounded below; Fréchet differentiable on L1; ∇J Lipschitz from L1 to L2; ∇J weak-norm continuous (p. 3). Restriction to {0,1} values and no L1 regularization is stated as chosen "in the interest of a concise presentation" (pp. 1-2). The test problem's PDE is shown to satisfy (a), (c), (d) through Sobolev embeddings (pp. 13-14). Appendix B maps Assumption 1.1 onto the set-based assumptions of [10] (pp. 22-23).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: First liminf C = 0 by contradiction: if C stayed positive the radius would go to zero, yet the remainder r^n becomes nonnegative and steps are accepted (pp. 11-12). The key device is that for {−1, 0, 1}-valued steps the L2 norm is the square root of the L1 norm (p. 11). Then lim C = 0 by the proof strategy of [35, Theorem 6] over successful iterations (p. 12). Last, weak lower semicontinuity of C under (d) (p. 13). Proposition 2.1 (weak continuity of J) uses the mean value theorem and compactness of ∇J (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: One 2-D elliptic tracking problem on a 256×256 mesh; continuous relaxation vs CIA (SUR, COR, SHG) vs BTR from three starts, reporting objective, gap, time, BTR iterations and interface length (Table 1, p. 17); a mesh-refinement table (Table 2, p. 17) and a coarse-SUR hybrid table (Table 3, p. 18).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Theory contribution closes the BTR–CIA gap (p. 3). Reported losses: BTR runs longer than CIA (p. 15); the implementation is not mesh-independent (p. 17); the exact SHG rounding is intractable, so a heuristic is used (p. 14); the observed regularizing effect (shorter interfaces) is unexplained: "we cannot explain our consistent observation" (p. 15). Future: the objective/interface trade-off (p. 15) and adaptive mesh refinement inside FindStep (p. 14).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (trust-region acceptance and radius rules carried from NLP to binary control functions in L∞, Algorithm 2 p. 6; the analysis follows a standard trust-region proof strategy, p. 12). Method 5 ✅ (losses reported: slower BTR, mesh dependence, no control dominates on both objective and interface length, pp. 15, 17). New-pattern candidates: "borrow the sibling method's assumption to close a convergence gap" (pp. 2-3, 8); "Relax, round with structure, then improve locally (with coarse-to-fine warm start)" (§6.4, pp. 17-18).
**Transferable techniques**:
- To close a convergence gap for method A, adopt the assumption under which sibling method B already converges and prove that A reaches B's stationarity notion; the two methods then become directly comparable (pp. 2-3, 8).
- Show that the algorithm's termination quantity equals a standard criticality measure (Proposition 4.3, p. 8); then the stopping test becomes a first-order optimality condition.
- On uniform meshes, solve the binary trust-region subproblem as an equal-weight knapsack: sort the cell averages and fill the radius greedily (p. 14).
- Add a mesh-refinement table (gap, iterations, a structural metric) to expose mesh dependence instead of hiding it (Table 2, p. 17).

**Quotes**:
- "This lack of a convergence result is in contrast to CIA, which has stationary limits under a suitable compactness assumption." (p. 2)
- "In particular, our implementation of the algorithm is clearly not mesh-independent." (p. 17)

### S169 · Polytopic Superset Algorithm for Nonlinear Robust Optimization (arXiv preprint 2023, arXiv:2302.12306)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 with Assumption 1 (pp. 1-4), §2 background and Polak's algorithm (pp. 4-6), §3.1-§3.3 superset NRO, Proposition 1, Theorem 2 statement and start of proof, cutting planes and example (pp. 6-10), §3.4 termination criteria (p. 11), §3.5-§3.6 feasibility restoration and full algorithm (pp. 12-15), §4 convergence analysis (pp. 15-17), §5 numerics (pp. 17-22), §6 conclusion (p. 23). Not read: the rest of the Theorem 2 proof (pp. 8-9), the iteration table of the example (p. 12), the asset-allocation model details (p. 18).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Bowen Li, Kibaek Kim (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: Argonne robust-optimization work with a postdoc; filterSQP still his NLP engine (p. 17).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Polak's outer-approximation (exchange) method for nonlinear robust optimization yields iterates that are infeasible for the robust problem and only a lower bound (abstract; pp. 3, 5). Entry through structure: with constraints affine in the uncertainty u, a polytopic superset of U can be dualized by LP duality into a finite NLP (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Replace the sample set by a polytopic superset S_k ⊇ U (an inner approximation of the feasible set), reformulate by LP duality (Proposition 1, pp. 7-8), and shrink S_k by cuts: Kelley cuts at the violated point, supporting cuts at its Euclidean projection, or gradient-free projection cuts (§3.3, pp. 9-10). Iterates are feasible and give upper bounds; projected points give a lower-bound problem like Polak's (p. 14). A phase-I problem with scalar slack p separates an infeasible problem from an overly conservative initial box (§3.5, pp. 12-13). Kept from Polak: the iterative structure (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Assumption 1: g_j convex and C1, f, h, b C1, U compact and nonempty, F bounded (p. 2); constraint affine in u (p. 3); MFCQ for the first-order condition (Theorem 1, p. 4). Convexity of U is the extra assumption compared with Polak, argued to be no stronger than Polak's global-solve assumption (pp. 15, 17).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 2 shows that KKT points of the nonconvex reformulation satisfy the first-order condition of the semi-infinite subproblem (p. 8). Proposition 3 uses one contradiction argument with a case per cut type, each giving a uniform separation of iterates (r/L or r/2R) and so no Cauchy subsequence in a compact set (pp. 15-16). Theorem 3 follows (p. 17). The authors say the unified proof covers hybrid cut strategies (p. 15). The abstract says "converges to the optimal solution", but Theorem 3 gives convergence to a point with vanishing error measure, read as stationarity (p. 17).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: Julia, filterSQP for every NLP and Mosek for the SDP (p. 17); robust asset allocation (T ∈ {7, 14, 21}, four transaction costs, three random asset groups averaged, Table 2 p. 19) and 27 production-cost instances on PJM load data (Tables 3-4, pp. 21-22); performance profiles of CPU time (pp. 19-22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Algorithm with feasible iterates, two-sided bounds and a convergence proof (p. 23). Limits: convex U and affine-in-u structure (pp. 15, 23); in Table 2 the superset method loses some short-horizon runs (T = 7, cost 0.05: 1.55 s vs 1.23 s, p. 19). Future: relax the affine structure (p. 23).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ (same NLP solver, filterSQP, under both algorithms, p. 17; per-instance tables include the cases it loses, p. 19) and ⚠ variant (headline "outperforms Polak's algorithm in 92% of the instances", p. 19, framed as a winner, though tied to the regime of many robust constraints, p. 23). Method 2 ✅ (cutting-plane / outer-approximation machinery and a phase-I feasibility restoration carried into robust optimization, pp. 9-13). New-pattern candidates: "inner approximation for feasible iterates, outer problem for the bound" (pp. 7, 14); "one convergence proof with a case per cut type" (pp. 15-16).
**Transferable techniques**:
- When an exchange or outer-approximation method returns infeasible iterates, approximate the uncertainty set from outside by a polytope and dualize, so every iterate is feasible and gives an upper bound; reuse the projected points for a lower bound (pp. 7, 14).
- Prefer supporting cuts at the projection over Kelley cuts at the violated point, and show the difference on a 2-D example over three iterations (pp. 9-11, Fig. 1).
- Write one convergence proof with a case per cut type so that mixed cut strategies inherit convergence (p. 15).
- Use a phase-I problem with one scalar slack to tell "problem infeasible" from "initial approximation too conservative" (pp. 12-13).

**Quotes**:
- "In other words, the NRO subproblem (8) can be seen as an inner approximation of (2), which is distinct from the classical outer approximation methods [7–9]." (p. 7)
- "Note that convexity is a sufficient condition for global optimality, which means that the two sets of assumptions are not significantly different." (p. 17)

### S215 · Mixed-Integer PDE-Constrained Optimization (Oberwolfach extended abstract, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (all 4 pages). The work-list note dates it as Oberwolfach Report 2019-26, June 2019; the text itself carries no date or report number.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Leyffer presents joint work with Bart van Bloemen Waanders, Mirko Hahn, Todd Munson, Lars Ruthotto, Meenarli Sharma, Ryan Vogt (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: MIPDECO line, between the source-inversion and cloaking papers and the later BTR analysis (S115).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: MIPDECO with mesh-dependent binary indicators (source location, cloaking material) (p. 1). Entry by cost: commercial branch-and-cut is prohibitively expensive and impractical in 3D (p. 1). Two motivating applications: source inversion with an advection–diffusion PDE and electromagnetic cloak design with a 2D Helmholtz equation (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: An improvement heuristic modelled on NLP trust-region methods and related to local branching (p. 1). It works in reduced space (state eliminated), linearizes J at w^(k), and imposes an integer L1 trust region, which on binaries is one affine constraint; the subproblem is a knapsack. Steps are accepted by the ratio ActRed/PredRed, the radius is halved on rejection, and the method stops when the radius reaches zero (p. 2). Each successful iteration costs two PDE solves (forward and adjoint) (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: "given w, we can uniquely solve the PDE" (reduced-space formulation) (p. 1); finite-element discretization on quadrilaterals (p. 1).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a. Formal convergence is listed as an open problem (p. 3).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: Qualitative only: source inversion reaches IoU 82.3% (p. 2); the cloak solution and wave difference are shown as figures (pp. 2-3); no baselines.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Heuristic plus two applications (p. 3). Three open problems are named: a stopping criterion from lower and upper bounds, multigrid refinement, and convergence analysis with behaviour under mesh refinement (p. 3). The gaps are stated, and the closing sentence still claims the scheme works well in practice (p. 3).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (NLP trust-region globalization carried into MIPDECO, p. 2; the unique-PDE-solvability assumption that the SKILL lists as a carried-in risk appears explicitly, p. 1). New-pattern candidate: "NLP globalization acceptance tests transplanted to non-NLP outer loops" (p. 2).
**Transferable techniques**:
- With binary variables, write the L1 trust region around the current point as a single affine constraint, so the linearized subproblem becomes a knapsack (p. 2).
- End a short report with numbered open problems that say which guarantee is missing (bounds-based stopping, convergence, mesh refinement) (p. 3).

**Quotes**:
- "The presence of mesh-dependent integer variables in these models makes the use of commercial branch-and-cut (see, e.g., [1]) prohibitively expensive (and impractical for 3D extensions of our models)." (p. 1)
- "Despite these gaps in its theoretical justification, the proposed trust-region scheme performs well in practice, and we are working on other applications that could benefit from this approach." (p. 3)

### S216 · Teaching PROFESSOR new math (CHEP 2018 proceedings, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (all 7 pages). Venue and year from the work-list note (CHEP 2018); the text carries neither.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Anthony Austin, Steven Mrenna, Juliane Müller, Holger Schulz (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: SciDAC collaboration with high-energy physics (p. 7); optimization methodology contributed to a physics tool.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: In Monte-Carlo event-generator tuning, observable weights are adjusted by hand "in a trial-and-error fashion" when the model cannot fit all data equally (p. 2). The polynomial surrogates in PROFESSOR fail on data with rational-function traits (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: (i) A bilevel problem: weights on the simplex at the upper level, a weighted χ² fit at the lower level; the upper objective is mean + λ·variance of per-observable goodness of fit (pp. 2-3). The upper level is solved by an RBF surrogate optimizer with multistart (p. 3). (ii) Rational approximation by linear algebra: pad numerator and denominator to order K, form Z = V⁻¹FV, and take the denominator coefficients from the null space of the trailing block by SVD (pp. 5-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Surrogates f_b trained on MC runs stand in for the generator (p. 2); V invertible, i.e. "nicely" chosen points (p. 5); 1-D presentation, multi-D called "straight forward" (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a. The only argument is an equation/unknown count (n equations, n+1 unknowns) (p. 6).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: ATLAS underlying-event data with Pythia8: portfolio vs standard tune vs random guess, shown as plots only (Fig. 2, p. 4); RBF convergence from two starts (Fig. 1, p. 4); a 1-D toy comparing rational and polynomial fits with six coefficients each (Fig. 3, p. 7).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Hedged claims ("seems promising", "looks promising", p. 7); multistart called "not ideal" (p. 3); multi-D rational fitting deferred to "an upcoming publication" (p. 7). No quantitative fit metrics are reported (pp. 3-7).

**Method links**: Method 2 ⚠ variant (a manual tuning practice is recast as a bilevel optimization problem, pp. 2-3, but the upper level is solved by an RBF surrogate method with multistart rather than his NLP toolkit, p. 3). Method 6 ✅ (weak: the surrogate's functional form is changed, rational instead of polynomial, to remove oscillation and nonphysical negative predictions, p. 6).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace manual reweighting of data groups by a bilevel problem whose upper objective is mean + λ·variance of the per-group misfit, so the result is balanced across groups (pp. 2-3).
- Fit a rational approximant without nonlinear least squares: square Vandermonde system, then the denominator from the null space of a small block via SVD (pp. 5-6).

**Quotes**:
- "We notice that the results have a dependence on the initial guess, which is why we apply a multi-start strategy." (p. 3)
- "We would argue that in the presence of mis-modelling, the bi-level optimization is able to yield a balanced prediction without human intervention." (p. 3)

### S222 · The Heuristic Static Load-Balancing Algorithm Applied to CESM (SuperComputing 2013 poster extended abstract, no DOI)
**Read level**: full (both pages). Venue from the work-list note; the reference list (Accessed January 16, 2013; [14] SC 2013 "submitted", p. 2) is consistent with it. Not the IPDPS Workshops 2014 paper S107.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Yuri Alexeev, Sheri Mickelson, Robert Jacob, Anthony Craig (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: Argonne HPC application of Minotaur, after the FMO load-balancing paper (ref. [1], SC 2012, p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Codes with a few large tasks of diverse size: dynamic load balancing is inappropriate, optimal static allocation is NP-hard, and practice is "human optimization" (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: Formulate node allocation as an MINLP (nonlinear runtime vs processor counts, integer node counts) and solve it with MINOTAUR called from AMPL (p. 1). For CESM the model adds layout constraints that encode sequencing and partitioning of components (p. 1).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The prediction needs "accurate benchmarking data and a correct mathematical model" (p. 1).
- **D4 Analysis**: n/a.
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**: 24% speed-up of CESM at 1/8° resolution on 32768 nodes of Intrepid against a baseline guess (p. 1); no other data.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Plans: a black-box tool, conversion of the AMPL model to free C++ code, inclusion in the NCAR pipeline (p. 1). Inconsistency: the same page says HSLB predicts the allocation "By using a linear optimization technique" after formulating an MINLP (p. 1).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ (Minotaur used as an engine callable "from different interfaces, for example AMPL scripts or C++ code", p. 1). Method 2 ✅ (weak: MINLP modelling carried into HPC load balancing through a facility collaborator, p. 1).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Model the processor allocation for a few large heterogeneous tasks as an MINLP whose runtime functions are fitted to benchmark timings, with code-structure requirements (sequencing, partitioning) as constraints (p. 1).

**Quotes**:
- "The MINLP approach provides a great flexibility in modeling the allocation problem realistically." (p. 1)
- "By using HSLB, we improved the speed of CESM on 32768 nodes for 1/8°-degree resolution simulations by 24% compared to a baseline guess on intrepid." (p. 1)
