### S089 · MINLP formulations for continuous piecewise linear function fitting (Computational Optimization and Applications 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10589-021-00268-5)
**Read level**: full (all 11 pages: abstract, §1, counterexamples §2, Theorems 1-2 with proofs, §3 experiments, §4 conclusions, references).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Noam Goldberg (first), Steffen Rebennack (corresponding), Youngdae Kim, Vitaliy Krasko (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: a correction of the MINLP model in Goldberg, Kim, Leyffer, Veselka (COAP 2014), reference [5], a paper Leyffer coauthored (p. 11).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is an error in the authors' own earlier model: MINLP (1) from [5] for linear-spline regression with free breakpoints "is incomplete and can result in a piecewise linear curve that is not the graph of a function" (abstract; p. 2). The defect was hidden because [5] applied (1) only to concave/convex fitting, where slope ordering implies the missing inequalities (p. 3), and because on all datasets of [5] and [9] the relaxed model happens to return a correct function (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two counterexamples, then three repairs. Example 1 is synthetic and tiny: five points, m = 3, zero objective with breakpoints b = (1.04, 1.00, 1.03) and one segment assigned no data; the correct fit has value 0.167 (p. 4, Fig. 1). Example 2 is real (scaled Titanium, n = 49): objective 1.003 vs. 2.129 for the ordered fit (pp. 4-5, Fig. 2). Repairs: (2) assignment-type constraints (each segment gets ≥1 point), (3) explicit breakpoint ordering b_j ≤ b_{j+1}, (4) the chained φ inequalities from the convex MILP of [9] (p. 5).
- **D3 Assumptions**: sufficiently large finite big-M constants M_i, M̂_i, with data-dependent bounds (pp. 3, 7); m ≤ n for the equal-value result (p. 8); p = 2 in the experiments, p = 1 possible (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1 (correctness of (1)+(3)) is a two-way correspondence proof: every feasible point is a continuous PL function with objective = sum of pth-power errors, and every optimal continuous PL function maps to a feasible point via explicit constructions (6a)-(8) (pp. 6-7). Theorem 2 orders the feasible regions X_R ⊃ X_3, X_4 ⊃ X_S and proves z_R ≤ z_3 = z_4 = z_S by moving an unassigned segment's breakpoint to the nearest data point without changing the objective (p. 8). Corollary 1: all three repaired models are correct (p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: Julia/JuMP + Couenne 0.5.6, 2-h limit, Titanium/Concrete/NHTemp, m = 2-4, nodes and CPU seconds for none/(2)/(3)/(4)/all, LIMIT entries kept in Table 2; ordering (3) usually fastest, (4) a close second, (2) and the unrepaired relaxation (1) often slower (pp. 9-10).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: error report plus corrected formulations; conclusion recommends ordering-based over assignment-type constraints and suggests the lesson for larger models such as [9] (p. 10). Limits: three small datasets, one solver, m ≤ 4 (p. 9); no explanation offered for why the weakest model is slowest beyond the observation (p. 10).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: smallest breaking example plus a real-data instance, applied to the team's own published model, then a redesign (pp. 4-5, 10).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the fix is in the formulation, and the variant comparison shows the formulation choice decides solvability (Table 2, p. 9).
- Heuristic 9 / Workflow F ✅ evidence: a published correction in print of an earlier coauthored model (p. 10).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (minor): time-outs kept as LIMIT in the table (p. 9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a model was validated only on a special case (here concave/convex data), check which constraints the special case implied for free; those are the candidates for what is missing in the general case (p. 3).
- Pair a 5-point synthetic counterexample with one classic real dataset that also breaks, so the error is shown to matter in practice (pp. 4-5).
- For several repairs, prove a feasible-region chain and an optimal-value equality theorem, then benchmark all variants including the unrepaired relaxation (pp. 8-9).

**Quotes**:
- "We have pointed out an error in the nonconvex MINLP for fitting piecewise linear functions proposed in [5]." (p. 10)
- "In many real datasets including all of those experimented with in [5] and [9], the relaxed formulation (1) coincidentally computes a correct solution" (p. 4)

### S093 · A solution framework for linear PDE-constrained mixed-integer problems (Mathematical Programming 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10107-021-01626-1)
**Read level**: partial. Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-4), §2 framework (pp. 4-6), §3.5 numerical considerations (pp. 15-16), start of §4 (pp. 16-17), §5 solution methods (pp. 21-24), §6 implementation and experiments (pp. 25-29), §7 conclusions (pp. 29-32). Not read: §3.1-3.4 background (MILP, finite differences, FEM, dynamic flows) and the detailed model derivations of §4.1-4.2 (pp. 17-21).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Fabian Gnegel (first), Armin Fügenschuh, Michael Hagel, Marcus Stiemer; Leyffer fourth of five (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: a MIPDECO paper (Series B), an extension of proceedings papers [16, 17] (p. 1); linear PDEs only, discretize-then-optimize into one MILP.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: An empirical bottleneck: a naive discretize-then-optimize MILP built from finite-difference equations "leads to systems that are too large to be solved with state-of-the-art solvers" when accuracy is wanted (abstract, p. 1; p. 3). The class has a two-way coupling: the PDE state constrains the network decisions and the decisions control the PDE (p. 3). They argue optimize-then-discretize is unlikely for MIPDECO because it would need algebraic optimality conditions for general MILPs (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two techniques (abstract; pp. 3-4, 23-26). (i) Superposition: precompute PDE states for a basis of the control space (|N|+1 PDE solves after exploiting time invariance by shifting), so the state becomes an affine expression in the controls (31)-(35) and the state variables disappear from the MILP (pp. 23-24). The basis size grows with the number of controls, not the mesh (p. 4). (ii) The dense state constraints (29) are imposed lazily via a CPLEX lazy-constraint callback that adds at most one most-violated grid constraint per time step at each incumbent (Algorithm 1, pp. 25-26). FEM is used for the preprocessing because its solution can be evaluated anywhere and refined adaptively (pp. 23-24).
- **D3 Assumptions**: linear PDE on a square domain with time interval, binary and L²-continuous controls, linear bounded side-constraint operator H (pp. 5-6); well-posedness required (pp. 5-6); time-invariant systems for the shift trick, which "is not essential" (p. 4). Checkerboarding is avoided by convergent discretizations; absence of chattering is observed, and a proof "is beyond the scope" (pp. 15-16).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: no convergence theorems. Correctness of the reformulation rests on the superposition principle and the time-shift identity (pp. 23-24); lazy constraints keep optimality because an incumbent that violates none of them is optimal for the full model (p. 25).
- **D5 Experiments**: CPLEX 12.8 + oFEM on a 2018 Mac mini, 20,000 s limit, wildfire and river-contamination models; FD direct model fails beyond 20×20 (21×21) grids, FEM basis model solves up to 160×160 (90×90); callback gives speedups up to a factor 200; mesh-dependence tables against the finest grid (pp. 25-29). CPLEX presolve cut off all feasible solutions on many FD instances, so bound strengthening and aggregation were switched off (p. 27).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: framework plus two applications (p. 29). Stated limits: poor scaling in the time resolution "cannot be avoided by our methodology" (pp. 28-29); no convergence analysis, no regularization studied (p. 31); mesh-dependence data too sparse to conclude convergence (pp. 27-28). Future work: goal-oriented adaptive FEM / dual weighted residuals (pp. 31-32).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the MILP solver is used through its callbacks rather than as a black box (p. 25; Algorithm 1).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the fix is a reformulation (eliminate the mesh-scaling state variables) before any algorithmic change (pp. 23-24, 31).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: MIPDECO as a named class entered from discrete optimization plus numerical PDEs (pp. 2-3).
- Taste 3 ⚠ variant: the paper is explicitly empirical; convergence of the discretized MILPs and the chattering claim are left open (pp. 16, 31).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (minor): solver-setting changes forced by numerical trouble are reported (p. 27).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For linear PDE constraints, precompute states for a control basis and substitute the affine superposition into the MILP; if the system is time-invariant, one PDE solve per control suffices by time shifting (pp. 23-24).
- Treat dense, largely redundant discretized state bounds as lazy constraints and add only the single most-violated grid point per time step at each incumbent (p. 26).
- Report a mesh-dependence table (differing binaries and state distance against the finest grid) next to run times (pp. 27-29).

**Quotes**:
- "This poor scaling with the resolution cannot be avoided by our methodology." (p. 28)
- "Our approach demonstrates that eliminating the PDE constraints and variables whose number scales with the discretization size seems to be a viable method for solving MIPDECO approximations with high accuracy." (p. 31)

### S094 · A method for convex black-box integer global optimization (Journal of Global Optimization 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10898-020-00978-w; arXiv 1903.11366)
**Read level**: partial (arXiv v2). Read: abstract, §1-2 (pp. 1-4), §3.1 definitions and Lemma 1 (pp. 4-5), §4 Algorithm 1 and Theorem 1 with proof (pp. 9-11), §5.1.1 MILP model (pp. 11-12), §5.3 SUCIL and Theorem 2 (pp. 16-17), §6 experiments (pp. 16-18), §7 discussion (pp. 18-21). Not read: Lemmas 2-6 proofs (pp. 5-9, 14-15), §5.1.2-5.2 details, appendices A-D (pp. 22-31).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jeffrey Larson (first), Prashant Palkar, Stefan M. Wild (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: integer derivative-free optimization; the algorithm is framed as an outer-approximation analogue (p. 9).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min f(x) over a finite Ω ⊂ Zⁿ where f is convex on Ω but cannot be evaluated at non-integer points (unrelaxable integers) and no (sub)gradients exist (pp. 1-2). The entry is a gap: B&B, Benders and OA all need relaxations or gradients, and "we know of no potential algorithm" besides enumeration (p. 2). Existing integer DFO methods only reach mesh-isolated or local points, "arbitrarily far" from global even for convex f (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Secants interpolating f at n+1 affinely independent ("poised") points underestimate f only on a union of n+1 polyhedral cones U^i (Lemma 1, p. 5); the maximum of these conditional cuts is a nonconvex piecewise-linear underestimator (PLP), and Algorithm 1 alternates lower-bound update and evaluation like OA (pp. 9-10). The MILP encoding of (PLP) (CPF) blew up (p. 12), so the final method SUCIL uses an enumerative look-up table of lower bounds per point, a reduced point set G^k and a trust region (Algorithm 3, p. 17).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Assumption 1: f convex on Ω (Definition 1), Ω nonempty and bounded, no evaluation outside Ω (p. 1); the authors concede that knowing convexity of a black box is rare (p. 1). Mixed-integer extension via f(x) = min_y F(x,y) if F is jointly convex (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1 (finite termination at a global minimizer) by finiteness of Ω plus a contradiction: a better unevaluated x* would keep l_k ≤ f(x*) < u_k (pp. 10-11). Theorem 2 extends it to SUCIL provided W(G^k) gives at least one cut per remaining point (p. 16). A 2-D counterexample shows unconditional cuts fail: f = x₁² − x₁x₂ + x₂² with points (1,1), (0,1), (1,0) gives m ≡ 1 > f(0,0) = 0 (p. 19). Worst case: binary domains force full enumeration (p. 19).
- **D5 Experiments**: 24 instances ([−4,4]ⁿ, n = 3-5), four SUCIL variants including two idealized ones that know true f as a best case, against DFLINT(-M), NOMAD(-dm) 3.9.0 and MATSuMoTo (20 replications, given the optimal value so it can stop); performance profiles on evaluations to termination and to first hitting a global minimizer; SUCIL fewest evaluations on >65% and certifies, DFLINT-M finds the optimum first on >85% (pp. 16-18).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: new underestimator, convergent algorithm, and evidence that certification is hard (p. 2). Stated limits: combinatorial growth of secant combinations, most cuts useless (Fig. 9), finding a pruning cut is posed as an open problem (Problem 1, p. 21), storage over 200 GB for [−10,10]¹⁰ (pp. 19-21).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: the outer-approximation lower/upper-bound loop carried into derivative-free integer optimization (p. 9).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: a minimal 2-D counterexample kills the natural "unconditional cut" idea (p. 19).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the comparison is hedged ("merely demonstrate"), baselines given favourable settings (MATSuMoTo told the optimum), and the metric where SUCIL loses is reported (p. 18).
- Taste 3 ✅ evidence: the selling point is a certificate of global optimality, not speed to a good point (pp. 2, 18).

**New-pattern candidates**:
- Idealized-oracle variant as a yardstick: run the method with an impossible oracle (true f) to bound what better point selection could gain (Table 2, pp. 16-18).
- Report the abandoned formulation and its blow-up data in the paper, and say the order of sections retells how the method was reached (pp. 12, 18-19).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When cuts are valid only on a region, store the lower bound pointwise in a look-up table instead of encoding validity with big-M binaries (pp. 11-13, 17).
- Plot evaluations-to-first-optimum and additional evaluations-to-certificate separately (Fig. 8, p. 20).
- State a hard subproblem you could not solve as a formal open "Problem" (Problem 1, p. 21).

**Quotes**:
- "Hence, our results merely demonstrate that SUCIL provably converges to a global optimum and uses fewer function evaluations" (S094 arXiv p. 18)
- "The order of results in this paper tells the story of how we arrived at the implementation of SUCIL." (S094 arXiv p. 18)

### S099 · A bilevel approach for identifying the worst contingencies for nonconvex alternating current power systems (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2021, DOI 10.1137/19m127611x)
**Read level**: partial (preprint version). Read: abstract and §1 (pp. 1-4), assumptions A1-A2 and Proposition 2.1 with proof and remarks (pp. 7-8), start of §2.2 (p. 8), §3 algorithm, Propositions 3.1-3.2 statements, Algorithm 3.1, Theorem 3.3 with proof (pp. 10-14), §4 experiments (pp. 14-21), §5 conclusions (pp. 20-22). Not read: the ACOPF model details of §2.1 (pp. 4-7), the SOC/Lagrangian-dual details of §2.2-2.3 (pp. 8-10), the proof of Proposition 3.2 (pp. 11-12).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Brian Dandurand (first), Kibaek Kim (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: Argonne power-grid line; MINLP/bilevel with a nonconvex lower level.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Attacker removes ≤K lines to maximize the defender's minimal infeasibility under AC power flow (maximin, pp. 1-2). Standard single-level reformulations (KKT or Lagrangian dual) need lower-level convexity and CQs, so the literature relaxes AC flow (SDP, QC, SOC), which yields "false negatives" (pp. 2-3). The question asked: how much vulnerability do relaxations miss (p. 3)?
- **D2 Core idea**: A custom B&B (Algorithm 3.1, p. 13) on attacks. Upper bounds: swap max and min, giving a minimax problem that bounds the maximin node problem from above (Prop. 3.1, minimax inequality) and that can be rewritten as a single-level problem that keeps the nonconvex AC equations (Prop. 3.2, pp. 10-11). Lower bounds: SDP relaxation of the lower level at the node's attack (p. 13). Branching on the line with the largest dual multiplier ξ of the x_l ≤ 1 bound, because integrality-based rules do not apply (p. 12).
- **D3 Assumptions**: A1 consistent physical bounds, A2 W ⊇ W_AC (p. 7); slacks make the lower level always feasible and φ_W concave in x on [0,1]^|L| (Prop. 2.1, pp. 7-8). Node upper bounds come from Ipopt local solutions, so global optimality of the nonconvex subproblems is not verified (pp. 12, 18).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 3.3 (pp. 12-14) gives a graded guarantee: finite termination; ε-optimality for the SDP-relaxed bilevel problem; ε-optimality for the AC problem if every optimality-fathomed node closes its gap; otherwise a vetted candidate list containing a global AC solution. Proof by case analysis over fathoming reasons and Prop. 3.1 (pp. 13-14). Concavity of φ_W follows from being an infimum of affine functions (p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: Julia (MaximinOPF.jl on PowerModels.jl/JuMP), Ipopt+MA57 and Mosek, IEEE 30/57/118/300, K = 1-16, single core, 24 h; enumeration for K ≤ 3 shows SOC (and sometimes SDP) values of zero for attacks with positive AC value (pp. 14-16); against the MISOC baseline of [53], ACBnB is always slower, but finds different or additional candidate attacks on cases 57 and 300 (pp. 16-20, Tables 3-6).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: shows AC equations can sit inside a rigorous global B&B for contingency analysis (p. 3). Limits stated: global optimality of node subproblems not fully verified; Pegase 1354/2869 runs processed few nodes in 24 h; branching rules primitive; many PSVA extensions left (pp. 21-22).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: the paper states in advance that the relaxation baseline will be faster, reports the usual B&B measures "for future reference", and states its solver choices are not advocacy (pp. 16-17).
- Taste 2 ✅ evidence: tests a received tractable shortcut (convex-relaxed lower level) against the exact nonconvex model and shows what it misses (pp. 3, 15-16).
- Taste 3 ✅ evidence: the guarantee is stated precisely, including the weaker "candidate list" outcome when subproblem optimality cannot be certified (Theorem 3.3, pp. 12-14).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (weak): carries the slack-for-feasibility and exact-penalty reasoning into the lower level (Remarks 2.3-2.4, p. 8).

**New-pattern candidates**:
- Graded guarantee with a vetted candidate list: when inner global optimality cannot be certified, state exactly which weaker certificate the algorithm still delivers (Theorem 3.3, pp. 12-14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a maximin with a nonconvex inner problem, swap max and min to get a valid upper bound at each B&B node, then dualize the now-inner linear max over the attack polytope to get a single-level problem (pp. 10-11).
- Branch on the largest dual multiplier of the binary upper bounds when node solutions are not integer-valued relaxations (p. 12).
- Before the algorithmic comparison, enumerate small instances under exact and relaxed lower levels to show which answers change (Table 2, pp. 14-16).

**Quotes**:
- "The solution approaches based on convex relaxations of the lower-level defender problem can be expected to yield “false negatives” in terms of of identifying power system vulnerabilities." (S099 preprint p. 3)
- "It must be emphasized that we do not expect—at this stage of development—for ACBnB to be competitive with MISOC with respect to computational performance" (S099 preprint p. 16)

### S121 · Compact representations of structured BFGS matrices (Computational Optimization and Applications 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10589-021-00297-0; arXiv 2208.00057)
**Read level**: partial (ANL preprint / arXiv v1). Read: cover pages, abstract, §1 (pp. 3-6), Theorem 1 with proof (p. 7), Theorem 2 statement and start of its induction proof (pp. 8-9), §3.1 and §3.3 (pp. 11-14), §4 setup and Experiments II, IV, V and part of III (pp. 17-30), §5 conclusions and Appendix A (pp. 30-31). Not read: Corollaries' full algebra, §2.3 limited-memory update formulas, §3.4 complexity details (pp. 10-17), Experiment I results.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Johannes J. Brust (first), Zichao (Wendy) Di, Cosmin G. Petra (p. 3) · **Place in trajectory**: continuous NLP side work (quasi-Newton), outside the MINLP core; builds on Petra et al. [20] structured BFGS.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: f = known-Hessian part + unknown-Hessian part (4); the structured BFGS updates S-BFGS-M (5) and S-BFGS-P (6) of [20] are dense and lack compact representations, so they do not scale (pp. 5-6).
- **D2 Core idea**: derive compact (low-rank factored) forms of both updates (Theorems 1-2), then limited-memory versions with four non-constant initializations and Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury solves inside two strong-Wolfe line-search algorithms L-S-BFGS-M/P (pp. 6, 11-15). The line search adds the curvature condition s_kᵀu_k > 0 to Moré-Thuente (p. 13).
- **D3 Assumptions**: f twice continuously differentiable (p. 4); positive definiteness of the M-update needs B₀ SPD and s_kᵀu_k > 0 (p. 7); the P-update need not be SPD, so Algorithm 2 tests K_k + A_k (p. 13).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Theorem 1 by relabeling: adding K_{k+1} turns (5) into plain BFGS with y_k → u_k, so the Byrd-Nocedal-Schnabel compact form applies and K_k is subtracted (p. 7). Theorem 2 needs an induction on k with a 2×2 block middle matrix (pp. 8-9).
- **D5 Experiments**: MATLAB; extended performance profiles (iterations, time) on structured quadratics, 61/62 CUTEst problems against full-memory S-BFGS, LIBSVM logistic regression, a Poisson control problem, IPOPT L-BFGS on a structured quartic, and a ptychography problem with n = 5,000 (pp. 17-30).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: compact representations and limited-memory algorithms; structured methods need fewer iterations; initialization advice by variant (pp. 30-31). No convergence analysis of the limited-memory algorithms is given in the parts read; initialization rankings shift with spectrum (Appendix A, p. 31).

**Method links**:
- Heuristic 10 ✅ evidence: uses the extended performance profiles introduced in Mahajan-Leyffer-Kirches [17] (p. 18).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (weak): continued work in the home continuous-NLP toolkit (quasi-Newton, Wolfe line search) at Argonne (pp. 4, 13).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: baselines are the authors' own full-memory methods and IPOPT's L-BFGS; the fairness rule is not stated beyond equal memory and tolerances (pp. 21, 27-28).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To get a compact form for a modified quasi-Newton update, first look for a shift (here adding the known Hessian) that maps it onto the classical update, and reuse the known representation (p. 7).
- Extend a line search with the one extra curvature condition your update needs and cite an existence result for such a step (p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "Unwinding the update formula in (6) is challenging, however by using an induction technique we are able to derive the explicit expression of the compact representation." (S121 arXiv p. 6)
- "The structured methods typically require fewer total iterations than do the unstructured approaches." (S121 arXiv p. 31)
