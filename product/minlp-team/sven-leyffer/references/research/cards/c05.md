### S057 · Mathematical programs with complementarity constraints (SIAG/OPT Views-and-News 2003, no DOI)
**Read level**: full for Leyffer's article (file pp. 15-18) and the guest-editor introduction he co-signed with Nocedal (pp. 1-2); the other articles of the issue (Gould, Sachs, Biegler-Wächter, Bussieck-Pruessner, columns, pp. 2-14, 19-25) were not read, only searched for his name. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none for the article; introduction co-signed with Jorge Nocedal (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: Argonne, one year after the Dundee NA/208-210 reports; a short public statement of the "MPECs as NLPs" turn (SKILL Signature Work Anatomy 4).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: Enters against a received verdict: the NLP form (2) with X1x2 ≤ 0 violates MFCQ at every feasible point, multipliers are unbounded, the central path does not exist, linearizations can be inconsistent near a solution, and early numerics were disappointing, so solving MPCCs as NLPs "has been commonly regarded as numerically unsafe" (p. 15). The field's answer was special-purpose methods (B&B, implicit nonsmooth, piecewise SQP, penalization), "All of these techniques, however, require significantly more work than a standard NLP approach" (p. 16).
  - Transferable: open a survey by listing exactly which pathologies the received verdict rests on (MFCQ failure → four consequences, p. 15), so that each later section can show which consequence is harmless.
- **D2 Core idea**: Scholtes's observation that strong stationarity is equivalent to the KKT conditions of the NLP (2) (p. 16); setting ν̂ = ν − Xξ makes the two systems identical, and there is a minimal ξ (eq. 5), so the unbounded multiplier set is a ray with a bounded base (p. 16). Everything else is "small modifications" that perturb or penalize the complementarity constraint (pp. 17-18).
  - Transferable: when a constraint qualification fails, look for a structured description of the multiplier set (here a ray with minimal element) and design the algorithm to converge to the minimal element.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MPCC (1) with 0 ≤ x1 ⊥ x2 ≥ 0 (p. 15); the SQP local result assumes all QP approximations are consistent, which he names as an undesirable assumption and notes holds under a mixed-P property of the lower level (p. 17); PIPA's perturbation X1x2 = τ is only suitable without bi-active constraints (p. 17).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) equivalence of strong stationarity and KKT of (2) via the multiplier substitution (3)-(4) and explicit minimal ξ (5) (p. 16); (b) the superlinear SQP proof of [8] summarized as a two-case split: if x1ᵀx2 = 0 at some iterate the QP equals the relaxed NLP's QP, otherwise each QP basis stays bounded away from singularity (p. 17); (c) penalty/relaxation equivalence: for every τ a ρ gives the same solution (p. 17).
  - Transferable: the two-case proof split (iterate on the complementarity manifold vs off it) for local analysis of methods on degenerate problems (p. 17).
- **D5 Experiments**: none new; reports others' and his own numbers: over 150 problems solved, quadratic convergence for all but two (p. 16, citing [7]); IPMs with default settings solve about 80% (p. 17).
- **D6 Positioning**: credits Scholtes for the key observation (p. 16), Anitescu for elastic-mode SQP [1] (p. 16), Raghunathan-Biegler and Liu-Sun for relaxation, Benson-Shanno-Vanderbei for the active-set heuristic, with an honest "It is not clear at present, however, what convergence properties this approach possesses" (p. 17); his own PIPA counterexample [10] is presented as one more data point (p. 17).
- **D7 Writing**: section titles as slogans ("The NLP Revolution", "SQP Lead the Way", pp. 16); lively hedging-light prose ("the gloomy prognosis about the use of (2) may have been premature", p. 16; C-stationary points "are really a misnomer!", p. 18); the core claim appears in §1 and is restated as the "underlying theme" in the conclusion (pp. 17-18). The guest introduction (with Nocedal) frames general-purpose NLP algorithms as "having a profound effect" on MPCC and asks NLP researchers to "branch out" into new areas (pp. 1-2).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: survey-level contribution (organizes SQP and IPM work around one equivalence, p. 18). Limits stated concretely: global convergence open; convergence to B-stationary points open; examples converge to C-stationary points; B-stationary but not strongly stationary limits seen with ξ diverging; global solutions needed for brittle-fracture identification where "Local minima" are meaningless (p. 18).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (filter/SQP base carried into MPCCs; the conclusion states that small modifications let NLP solvers work for MPCCs, pp. 17-18; the co-signed call to "branch out", p. 2). Method 6 ✅ (the MFCQ failure "can be traced to the formulation of the complementarity constraint", p. 15; penalty preferred to relaxation because it controls the multipliers directly, p. 17). Method 3 ✅ (PIPA counterexample cited as a reason PIPA's perturbation is limited, and a simple example where the central path fails to exist, p. 17). New-pattern candidate: "close with the missing convergence notion" (open questions phrased as which stationarity concept is not yet guaranteed, p. 18; compare Taste 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Survey design: one pivotal equivalence (strong stationarity ⇔ KKT of the NLP) as the organizing lemma, with every method section reduced to how it perturbs or penalizes the complementarity constraint (pp. 16-18).
- When two regularizations are equivalent (relaxation τ ↔ penalty ρ), choose the one whose parameter controls the quantity that hurts your algorithm (multipliers), and say why (p. 17).

**Quotes**:
- "As a consequence, solving MPCCs via NLPs such as (2) has been commonly regarded as numerically unsafe." (p. 15)
- "The key to proving convergence in both cases is the equivalence between strong stationarity (1) and the KKT conditions (2)." (p. 18)

### S013 · Solving mathematical programs with complementarity constraints as nonlinear programs (Optimization Methods and Software 2004, doi:10.1080/10556780410001654241)
**Read level**: full, in the TR version: Fletcher and Leyffer, "Numerical experience with solving MPECs as NLPs", Dundee Numerical Analysis Report NA/210, 20 August 2002 (p. 1), pp. 1-20 read fully; appendices A (problem characteristics, pp. 21-23) and B (iteration counts and detailed results, pp. 24-29) skimmed. The journal text was not read; page references are to the TR. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher · **Position in trajectory**: the numbers paper of the MPEC turn, companion of the NA/209 local-convergence report [19] and the NA/208 PIPA counterexample [23] (refs, pp. 19-20).

- **D1 Problem and entry**: MPECs violate MFCQ, so the multiplier set is unbounded, which has been taken as an argument against using NLP techniques for MPECs (TR p. 2); past numerics were "rather disappointing" (Bard: failure on 50-70% of bilevel problems) (TR p. 3). Entry: test the received view empirically with off-the-shelf NLP solvers on a large new library (TR pp. 1-2).
  - Transferable: when a class is declared unsuitable for a standard tool on theoretical grounds, run the standard tool on a large public library before inventing a special method.
- **D2 Core idea**: using off-the-shelf NLP solvers on large MPEC instances (abstract, TR p. 1), with two design choices: write complementarity as z1ᵀz2 ≤ 0, not = 0 (shown necessary for quadratic convergence by a two-variable example, TR p. 3), and relax the linearization of complementarity (heuristic (3.4)) plus a restoration phase to handle inconsistent QPs (TR pp. 3, 6). Implemented as filtermpec, an AMPL interface that passes the MPEC to filterSQP as NLP (1.2), adding slacks only when needed (TR pp. 5-6).
  - Transferable: show by the smallest example why a seemingly equivalent constraint form (z1z2 = 0 vs ≤ 0) changes the convergence rate (linear with z(k) = z(0)/2^k vs one iteration, TR p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MPEC (1.1) with 0 ≤ z1 ⊥ z2 ≥ 0; general complementarity reformulated by slacks with no loss of CQs or second-order conditions (TR p. 1); quadratic convergence of SQP to the basic multiplier needs all QPs consistent, shown for a class with vertical complementarity (TR p. 3). Interface limitation: double-bounded F with unequal bounds not handled (TR p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: no new theorem; uses (a) the minimal (basic) multiplier (2.2) and the ray structure of the multiplier set (TR pp. 2-3); (b) explicit SQP iterate formula on the 2-variable example to show linear rate and diverging multiplier (TR p. 3); (c) the dempe example: lower-level solution z(x) computed in closed form to show two local minima and that the x = 0 limit is "not even a B-stationary point" (TR pp. 11-12).
  - Transferable: compute the lower-level solution map in closed form for a small bilevel test problem to classify each solver's limit (TR pp. 11-12).
- **D5 Experiments**: 137 MacMPEC problems (83 LCPs, 15 NCPs, 39 KKT lower levels; size quantiles in Table 1) (TR pp. 7-8); four NEOS solvers: filtermpec, KNITRO, LOQO, SNOPT (TR p. 9). Failure taxonomy: catastrophic failures (iteration limit or vanishing step at a non-stationary point, likely due to missing MFCQ) vs soft failures (locally infeasible, most likely unrelated to MFCQ); only catastrophic counted as problematic (Table 2: filtermpec 0, KNITRO 19, LOQO 20, SNOPT 1) (TR p. 9). Attribution test: rerun every IPM failure with complementarity replaced by z1ᵀz2 ≤ 10⁻²; LOQO still fails 11 of 20, KNITRO solves 13 of 19, so failures are not solely due to MFCQ (TR pp. 10-12). Dolan-Moré profiles on iterations and CPU, each meter's purpose stated; catastrophic failures set to 1E6 iterations / 1E8 CPU so they show as an asymptote, infeasibility exits not penalized (TR p. 12). Multiplier-size profile shows IPM multipliers orders of magnitude larger and tracking the iteration profile (TR pp. 13-15). Comparison with published MPEC solvers (Fukushima-Luo-Pang, Facchinei-Jiang-Qi, Dirkse-Ferris) on reconstructed or renamed instances, called "tentative" because instances and environments differ; the random generator is put online (TR pp. 14-17).
  - Transferable: separate algorithmic failures from model infeasibility before scoring, and score only the former as failures (TR p. 9).
  - Transferable: test a causal attribution (here: failures blamed on the MFCQ violation) by removing the suspected structure and rerunning only the failed instances (TR pp. 10-12).
- **D6 Positioning**: corrects earlier literature by name: Conn-Gould-Toint and Ferris-Pang attributed LANCELOT's LUBRIF failure to complementarity although the model minimizes complementarity; they suggest the failure may instead be due to LUBRIF being (locally) inconsistent (TR pp. 3, 10). Reviews smoothing, penalty, min-function, PIPA, implicit programming and Fisher-Burmeister SQP approaches neutrally (TR pp. 3-4). Credits Scholtes for the inverse-QP idea and Dirkse's mpeclib as a source (TR pp. 8, 18).
- **D7 Writing**: claim stamped "at present" in the abstract (TR p. 1); comparisons explicitly "tentative by design" (TR p. 17); one exclamation mark for the surprise that SQP appears more robust than the specially designed MPEC solvers (TR p. 17); closing claim that SQP solvers provide a benchmark against which MPEC solvers should be measured (TR p. 18).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: contributions: new library with a classification scheme XXX-XX-CCC-n-m-p (TR p. 7), the filtermpec interface (TR pp. 5-6), the empirical result. Limits stated concretely: IPMs fail on about 15% (TR p. 17); properties of the perturbation (3.4) are stated to be under investigation (TR p. 6); slack initialization showed no significant advantage either way (TR p. 6); for ralph1 and scholtes5 (no strongly stationary point) convergence is to a B-stationary point at linear rate (TR p. 14). Future: small IPM modifications, stated as under investigation (TR pp. 13, 18).

**Method links**: Method 4 ✅ (MacMPEC: 137 problems in AMPL with comments, references, solution plots, a classification string, novel models, credits to contributors, generator online; motivation "testing our own ideas" and motivating research, TR pp. 6-8, 15, 18). Method 5 ✅ (catastrophic/soft taxonomy, failures kept inside the profiles, two meters with stated purposes, "at present", "tentative" literature comparison, TR pp. 1, 9, 12, 14, 17). Method 6 ✅ (complementarity as ≤ 0 not = 0, slacks introduced by the interface, LUBRIF and dempe failures blamed on the formulation not the method, TR pp. 3, 5, 10, 12). Method 1 ✅ (own interface to own solver, "relatively minor" modifications, "negligible" overhead; works as NLP and MPEC solver, TR pp. 5-6). Method 2 ✅ (SQP/filter base carried into MPECs, TR p. 5). Method 3 ⚠ variant (smallest example used to justify a design choice, not to refute a theorem, TR p. 3). New-pattern candidates: "attribution test by removing the suspected structure" (TR pp. 10-12); "multiplier-size profile as a diagnostic for IPM slowness" (TR pp. 12-15).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Two-class failure taxonomy (algorithmic vs local infeasibility) with only the first penalized in performance profiles; set penalized failures to a large sentinel so they appear as the right-hand asymptote (TR pp. 9, 12).
- Remove-and-rerun attribution test for claimed failure causes (TR pp. 10-12).
- A profile of a secondary quantity (multiplier size relative to the best solver, restricted to instances where all solvers agree) to explain a speed gap (TR pp. 13-15).
- When comparing with published results you cannot rerun, publish the instance generator and call the comparison tentative (TR pp. 14-15).

**Quotes**:
- "It is shown that SQP methods are very well suited to solving MPECs and at present outperform Interior Point solvers both in terms of speed and reliability." (p. 1)
- "The failure of knitro is a failure of the problem formulation, rather than of the method." (p. 12)

### D001 · The penalty interior-point method fails to converge (Optim. Methods Softw. 20(4-5): 559-568 2005, doi:10.1080/10556780500140078; arXiv math/0310357)
**Read level**: full, in the arXiv version (math/0310357v1, preprint ANL/MCS-P1091-0903, dated September 22, 2003), pp. 1-11 including references; the OMS journal text was not read. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (single author; thanks Fletcher, Nocedal, Pang and a referee, p. 10) · **Position in trajectory**: journal version of Dundee NA/208 (2002), the counterexample strand of the MPEC turn; cited in S057 and S013.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: PIPA [Luo-Pang-Ralph, Ch. 6.1] is called a popular method, with applications in finance, target classification and electricity markets (abstract p. 1; p. 2); its published convergence theorem (Theorem 2.1 = [16, Thm 6.1.17]) is the target. Entry: "a small example for which PIPA converges to a nonstationary point, providing a counterexample to the established theory" (abstract, p. 1).
  - Transferable: target the convergence theorem of a method that is in active applied use, where a counterexample has consequences.
- **D2 Core idea**: the PIPA direction-finding problem (2.2) contains a trust-region-type bound ‖dx‖² ≤ c(‖F‖ + yᵀw) tied to infeasibility (p. 2); on a 3-variable LP-like example where the linear constraint is satisfied from the start, the radius shrinks with yᵀw faster than x can move, so the control x stalls (pp. 4-8). Remedy: let the algorithm, not the iterates, control the radius with a standard ared/pred update (4.13), giving a mixed trust-region/line-search method (p. 9).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: MPCC (1.1) with polyhedral X and C² f, F (p. 1); the theorem's hypotheses [SC] strict complementarity and [NS] nonsingularity, plus bounded Hessians, σ > 0 and bounded α (pp. 3-4). The simplified PIPA omits the iteration-dependent σ of [16] (p. 3). Qk = 0 "for simplicity", with an argument that the example survives bounded positive semidefinite Qk (p. 5). The remedy's convergence is only sketched "along the lines of" Dennis-Heinkenschloss-Vicente, under nonsingularity of the Jacobian with bounded inverse (p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (a) a minimal example (3.1): min x + w s.t. −1 ≤ x ≤ 1, −1 + x + y = 0, 0 ≤ y ⊥ w ≥ 0, solution (−1, 2, 0) (p. 4); (b) explicit verification that the solution satisfies [SC] and [NS] (p. 4) and later that the spurious limit also satisfies them (p. 8), so the theorem's hypotheses hold; (c) Lemma 3.1, five invariants (3.2)-(3.6) proved by induction from a fixed start (0, 1, 0.02) and parameters c = 1, σ = 0.1, γ = 0.01, ρ = 0.9, α = 2 (pp. 4-7); (d) explicit optimal basis inverse and multipliers of the LP subproblem to certify the active set (p. 5); (e) series lower bound on the line-search root τ ≥ 5/9 (pp. 6-7); (f) penalty update never triggered and Armijo condition verified (pp. 6-7); (g) limits x ≥ −2/(10(√2 − 1)) ≈ −0.4828 and y ≤ 1.4828 from geometric sums, then compactness and w∞ = 0 (pp. 7-8).
  - Transferable: build the counterexample so that the linear constraints hold from iteration 0 (they then hold forever), which reduces the induction to a few scalar invariants (p. 5).
  - Transferable: after proving the counterexample, check that the bad limit itself satisfies every hypothesis of the theorem you contradict (p. 8).
- **D5 Experiments**: the example and PIPA implemented in AMPL with its "looping extension"; Table 1 lists 10 iterates with ared and pred, confirming the bounds; ared and pred agree almost perfectly near the end (pp. 7-9).
  - Transferable: tabulate actual vs predicted reduction in the counterexample run; perfect agreement while the radius shrinks is the diagnostic that the radius rule, not the model, is at fault (pp. 8-9).
- **D6 Positioning**: places the note among MPCC approaches (B&B, implicit, piecewise SQP, penalization, PIPA) and the "renewed interest" in standard NLP solvers, citing his own numbers [8] and the SQP theory [1, 9] (pp. 1-2). States the contradiction directly: "This result contradicts the convergence results of [16]" (p. 4) and the example "contradicts [16, Theorem 6.1.17]" (p. 8); this arXiv version does not locate the faulty step of that proof (pp. 4-8).
- **D7 Writing**: short note, four sections; claim in the title and abstract (p. 1); anticipates the objection that the example is "contrived" and answers it with a claim that for any starting point parameters can be constructed so that PIPA fails, stated without proof (p. 8); explains the mechanism in plain words ("whenever the iterates approach feasibility “faster” than optimality", p. 8).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: contribution: a counterexample, its cause, a detectable symptom (the multiplier of the trust-region constraint, which converges to 1 in the example, p. 9) and a remedy (p. 9). Limits: the remedy is proposed with typical constants but no proof or numerics (p. 9); the general-start claim is unproved here (p. 8).

**Method links**: Method 3 ✅ (smallest breaking example, hypotheses [SC]/[NS] checked as satisfied, run in AMPL and tabulated, cause named, symptom and remedy proposed; pp. 4-9; this is the E37 practice cited in SKILL.md). Method 3 ⚠ variant (here the theorem is declared contradicted without pointing to the wrong proof step, pp. 4, 8; the SKILL's step 3 quote about the faulty proof part comes from elsewhere). Method 2 ✅ (trust-region ared/pred machinery from his NLP base proposed as the repair, p. 9). New-pattern candidate: "ared/pred agreement as a diagnostic of a mis-controlled radius" (pp. 8-9).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Counterexample recipe: tiny problem whose solution satisfies the theorem's hypotheses; fixed start and parameters; invariants by induction; bound the limit away from the solution; verify the limit satisfies the hypotheses; run it and print the iterates (pp. 4-8).
- Pre-empt the "contrived" objection explicitly in the discussion section (p. 8).
- When a radius or step bound is tied to the iterates rather than to model quality, check the multiplier of that bound: a nonzero limit means it is blocking progress (p. 9).

**Quotes**:
- "The trust-region radius ∆k converges to zero as the iterates approach feasibility, thereby limiting the progress toward optimality in the controls x." (p. 8)
- "Linking the trust-region radius to the feasibility of the problem is counterintuitive." (p. 9)

### S077 · The return of the active set method (Oberwolfach Reports 2005, no DOI)
**Read level**: full; read in the ANL preprint form (ANL/MCS-P1277-0805, February 17, 2005, revised August 5, 2005, "to appear in Oberwolfach Reports"), file pp. 1-4 (note pp. 1-3 plus cover). **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none · **Position in trajectory**: first Argonne years; links the MINLP need (warm starts) to new NLP/QP active-set research.

- **D1 Problem and entry**: interior methods are often faster, but "active set methods are more robust and better suited for warm starts, which are important for solving integer optimization problems" (p. 2); that downstream need is the stated reason for the interest (p. 2). Second entry: SLP-EQP methods with an ℓ∞ trust region suffer because active trust-region bounds do not settle, causing wasteful LP pivots near the solution (p. 3).
  - Transferable: justify a new inner-solver line by what the outer (integer) algorithm needs from it, in the first paragraph.
- **D2 Core idea**: (QP) two-phase step: approximately minimize the augmented Lagrangian in the box to get a Cauchy point, multiplier estimate and active-set estimate (LANCELOT-like), then solve an equality-constrained QP on the inactive variables with a backtracking line search; with α = 1 the two steps equal a Newton step (p. 2); globalization by a filter on (constraint violation, first-order error) that "removes the need for any forcing sequence" (p. 3). (NLP) replace the ℓ∞ trust-region LP by a penalized ℓ2 subproblem whose dual is a bound-constrained QP in the multipliers (p. 3).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: QP with A of full rank and H symmetric, not necessarily positive definite (p. 2); NLP min f(x) s.t. c(x) ≥ 0 (p. 3). Otherwise not stated.
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: claims only: Newton-step equivalence for α = 1 (p. 2); filter convergence "along the lines of" Chin-Fletcher for the ℓ2 scheme, with the proof said to exploit a piecewise quadratic relationship between the penalty parameter µ and the ℓ2 trust-region radius (p. 3). No proofs given in the note.
- **D5 Experiments**: "Preliminary numerical experience is encouraging", active set detected in a modest number of iterations (p. 3); no tables. Effectively n/a.
- **D6 Positioning**: builds on LANCELOT, Dostál-Friedlander-Santos (one forcing sequence suffices), Fletcher-Sainz de la Maza, Chin-Fletcher and Byrd-Gould-Nocedal-Waltz SLP-EQP (pp. 2-3); cites Floudas and Grossmann for the integer-optimization motivation (pp. 2, 4).
- **D7 Writing**: extended abstract, two method sketches and a three-sentence conclusion; hedged with "We believe" for the matrix-free claim (p. 4).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: two active-set identification strategies, both implementable matrix-free with only matrix-vector products and iterative solves (p. 4); limits implicit: preliminary numerics only, no proofs in the note (pp. 3-4).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ (filter globalization carried into augmented-Lagrangian QP and SLP active-set methods, p. 3). Method 1 ✅ (redesign of the inner solver for the needs of B&B, warm starts, p. 2; also SKILL Heuristic 7, "let the downstream use choose the upstream research"). New-pattern candidate: "replace tuning sequences by a filter" (the filter "removes the need for any forcing sequence whose choice may be problematic in practice", p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace forcing sequences (ηk, ωk) of an augmented Lagrangian method by a filter on (feasibility, first-order error) to remove problematic tuning (p. 3).
- If ℓ∞ trust-region bounds keep changing near a solution, penalize an ℓ2 trust region instead and solve the bound-constrained dual QP in the multipliers (p. 3).

**Quotes**:
- "On the other hand, active set methods are more robust and better suited for warm starts, which are important for solving integer optimization problems [8, 9]." (p. 2)
- "We believe that this is an important ingredient for a successful large-scale active set strategy." (p. 4)
