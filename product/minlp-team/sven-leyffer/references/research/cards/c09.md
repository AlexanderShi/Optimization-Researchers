### S002 · Mixed-integer nonlinear optimization (Acta Numerica 2013, doi:10.1017/s0962492913000032)
**Read level**: full. All of §§1-8 and Acknowledgments read (file pp. 1-100); the reference list (file pp. 100-123) skimmed, used only to identify self-citations. Text read = Argonne preprint ANL/MCS-P3060-1121 of 22 Nov 2012 (p. 1), not the journal typeset version.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Pietro Belotti, Christian Kirches, Jeff Linderoth, James Luedtke, Ashutosh Mahajan (p. 4) · **Place in trajectory**: consolidation of the "MINLP as team science" period (FilMINT, MINOTAUR, IMA volume) and the entry point of mixed-integer optimal control (§7). A six-author survey: individual sections cannot be attributed to Leyffer from the text; first-person passages tied to his software (MINOTAUR p. 40; FilMINT p. 39; MINLPBB p. 96) are flagged where used.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is by modelling generality plus difficulty: MINLP problems "combine the combinatorial difficulty of optimizing over discrete variable sets with the challenges of handling nonlinear functions" and MINLP contains NLP and MILP as subproblems (p. 5). It immediately bounds the question: MINLP is NP-hard and nonconvex integer problems are undecidable (Jeroslow's quadratically constrained example), so the survey restricts itself to the decidable case, X compact or functions convex (p. 5). The organising question is "relaxation and constraint enforcement" (p. 7), with the convex/nonconvex split (Definition 1.1, p. 6) and the single-tree/multitree split (abstract p. 4; §3 p. 22) as the two axes. Applications are used to motivate algorithms, not for their own sake: the batch plant (convexifiable, p. 15-16), water networks (nonconvex, p. 16-18) and the subway (time-dependent integers, p. 18-20) are "chosen to provide insight into the interactions of the two main modeling paradigms" (p. 13).
  - Transferable: open a survey by stating where the problem class stops being decidable or tractable and restricting the scope to the part where theory holds (p. 5), then pick two orthogonal axes (convex/nonconvex x single-/multitree) on which every method is placed.
- **D2 Core idea and design**: The survey's unifying idea is that every MINLP algorithm is a combination of a relaxation (integrality, convex constraints by supporting hyperplanes, nonconvex constraints by convex underestimators, pp. 8-9) and constraint enforcement (relaxation refinement by cuts, or branching, incl. spatial branching, pp. 9-11). Convex MINLP methods are then derived from one another rather than listed: NLP-BB (Algorithm 1, p. 24) as the base single-tree method; OA (Algorithm 5, p. 33) as the base multitree method; Benders cuts derived "given outer approximation" by aggregating OA cuts with multipliers (pp. 34-35); ECP as OA without NLP solves (p. 35); LP/NLP-BB as a hybrid of NLP-BB and OA, i.e., branch-and-cut on the MILP (3.6) with delayed constraint generation (pp. 36-38). The stated verdict: "The most efficient class of methods for convex MINLP are hybrid methods that combine the strengths of both classes" (abstract, p. 4), with FilMINT's lesson that LP/NLP-BB improves "dramatically by exploiting MILP domain knowledge, such as strong branching, adaptive node selection, and, most important, cut management" and that weaker ECP cuts at LP solutions help (p. 39). Inexact NLP solves are justified by the slack in the convergence argument (only pruned nodes must be solved to global optimality, p. 24; non-leaf nodes only need a branching variable, p. 29), covering Borchers-Mitchell, Leyffer (2001) implicit bounds and QP-diving (Mahajan et al. 2012), which "can reduce the CPU time of branch-and-bound by several orders of magnitude for some problems" (p. 29). Nonconvex MINLP: factorable reformulation into auxiliaries and operator-wise polyhedral relaxations (pp. 64-67), spatial B&B with bounds tightening (pp. 68-75), structured relaxations (QCQP via RLT/SDP/completely positive, bilinear covering sets, pp. 75-81). §7 carries the Sager partial-outer-convexification plus sum-up-rounding approach to MIOCPs, presented as an NLP-MILP decomposition with epsilon-optimality certificates (pp. 87-93).
  - Transferable: derive the methods of a family from one master method (OA cuts, then Benders cuts by multiplier aggregation, then ECP by dropping NLP solves), so that a worst case or a convergence proof for the master transfers to all of them (pp. 34-35).
  - Transferable: before designing an inexact or cheaper variant, write down the minimal property the convergence proof actually uses (global optimality only at pruned nodes, a branching variable at non-leaf nodes) and spend the remaining freedom (pp. 24, 29).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Problem (1.1): f, c twice continuously differentiable, X bounded polyhedral (p. 5). Convex methods use Assumption 3.1: A1 X bounded polyhedral, A2 f and c twice continuously differentiable and convex, A3 a constraint qualification "for every point in the convex hull of the feasible set"; the authors call A2 "the most restrictive", A1 "rather mild" and A3 "technical", needed "merely to ensure the existence of multipliers and the convergence of the NLP solvers", following from Slater under a strictly interior point (p. 22). The NLP-BB proof assumes every node is solved to global optimality; convexity is "one convenient sufficient condition ... but clearly not the only one!" (p. 24). OA assumes a finite number of integer points (Theorem 3.2, p. 34) and exact NLP or feasibility-problem solutions (Lemma 3.1, p. 32). Nonconvex part: functions factorable, i.e., symbolic expressions are known; black-box and non-closed-form integral functions are excluded (p. 64). MIOCP: path constraints independent of the integer control (p. 88); Theorem 7.1 needs essential boundedness, a.e. t-differentiability and Lipschitz bounds (7.5) (pp. 91-92); constraints that depend on the integer control are "not immediately covered" (p. 94).
  - Transferable: grade your own assumptions in print (most restrictive / mild / technical, p. 22) so a reader can see which one carries the theory; then check the "technical" one on your instances.
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: Mostly cited, with short arguments given in-line. (i) NLP-BB finiteness by reduction to MILP branch-and-bound (Schrijver Thm 24.1) once each node is solved globally (Prop. 3.1, p. 24). (ii) OA finiteness: the cutoff eta <= U^k - eps plus optimality of x^(j) make a visited integer assignment infeasible in the master; convexity makes linearizations supporting hyperplanes (Thm 3.2, p. 34); infeasible subproblems handled by the feasibility problem F(x_I^(j)) and Lemma 3.1 (pp. 31-32). (iii) The OA equivalence statement is given in its corrected form: (3.6) and (1.2) have the same value and every solution of (1.2) solves (3.6), "However, the converse is not true", shown with the Bonami et al. (2008) example in which x2 has no coefficient in the MILP (p. 32); Theorem 3.1's proof is credited to "Bonami et al. (2008) and Fletcher and Leyffer (1994)" (p. 32). (iv) Worst case: the Hijazi et al. ball-in-hypercube instance (3.7), with the distance argument sqrt(n)/2 > (n-1)/4 and "any outer approximation cut cannot cut more than one vertex", so OA visits all 2^n vertices; transferred to Benders and ECP "because these methods are relaxations of outer approximation" (p. 34). (v) Benders cut derivation: weight OA cuts by (1, lambda), use complementary slackness and stationarity in the continuous variables to obtain a cut in x_I only, then identify its gradient with the bound multipliers mu_I (pp. 34-35). (vi) LP/NLP-BB convergence obtained as a corollary of the OA argument (Thm 3.3, pp. 37-38). (vii) Disaggregation: projection argument that the OA of S_d is tighter than the OA of S, then applied to the worst case: two linearization points x^(1) and its complement make every master infeasible (pp. 43-44). (viii) sBB convergence via finite consistency (Thm 5.1, McCormick; Horst-Tuy, p. 70). (ix) Small counterexamples to procedures: FBBT on x1 = alpha x2, x2 = alpha x1 never terminates (p. 74); the factorable relaxation of x1^2 + x2^2 >= 1 on [0,2]^2 yields x1 + x2 >= 1/2 against the hull x1 + x2 >= 1 (pp. 75-76). (x) MIOCP: state-deviation bound (7.7) and sum-up-rounding bounds (7.9), (7.11), cited from Sager et al. 2012 (pp. 91-92). (xi) Convexification of binary QPs by the eigenvalue shift W = Q - lambda I using x_i^2 = x_i (p. 14).
  - Transferable: test a relaxation recipe against the convex hull on a two-variable instance where the hull is known (pp. 75-76).
  - Transferable: when a worst-case instance exists for your method, show which reformulation defeats it and with how many cuts (Hijazi instance killed by two linearization points after disaggregation, p. 44).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: No systematic study; illustrative computations only. (a) Coefficient tightening: on Syn20M04M MINOTAUR's tree passes 264,000 nodes without solving in 2 hours, BONMIN-BB and MINLPBB about 150,000 nodes, while a hybrid OA solves it in 9 s; with presolve and coefficient tightening MINOTAUR solves it in 2.3 s (pp. 39-42, Figs. 1, 10). (b) Performance profile (Dolan-More) on the Syn* and Rsyn* IBM/CMU instances: MINOTAUR with and without presolve plus Bonmin; presolve lets MINOTAUR solve 20% more instances (p. 42, Fig. 12). Machine, time limit and solver versions are not stated in the text read (p. 42). (c) Uncapacitated facility location, n = m = 40 random instance: 53,000 s aggregated vs 2 s disaggregated (p. 43). (d) Rocket-car MIOCP with MUSCOD-II on NEOS, N = 80 intervals: naive relaxation gives the weak bound x2(32) ~ 37; sum-up rounding gives 64 and the convexified relaxed bound "is exact in this case" (pp. 90-92, Figs. 26-27). (e) Cited results only: strong branching approximated by limited QP pivots (p. 26), QP-diving speedups (p. 29), Kilinc et al. on 207 instances (p. 50), Vielma et al. on piecewise-linear formulations (p. 60).
  - Transferable: report one hard instance with tree size and time for your own solver, the competitors and the reformulated model side by side (pp. 40-42), then a performance profile over the instance family (p. 42); add the machine, time limit and versions, which this text omits.
- **D6 Positioning**: Places itself among earlier surveys (Grossmann 2002; Grossmann and Kravanja 1997; Bonami et al. 2012; Floudas 1995; Lee and Leyffer 2011, p. 13) and explicitly excludes the MILP and NLP subproblems (textbooks and Gould and Leyffer 2003 cited), polynomial-time fixed-dimension algorithms, closures of convex integer sets and mixed-integer derivative-free optimization (pp. 12-13). OA is credited to Duran and Grossmann (1986), with Fletcher and Leyffer (1994) and Bonami et al. (2008) listed beside it (p. 31); the 1994 statement is corrected by citing Bonami et al. (p. 32) and the worst case is taken from Hijazi et al. (p. 34) without defence. Generalized Benders is re-presented as a weaker derivative of OA although "developed before outer approximation" (pp. 34-35). For nonconvex MINLP it defers to Tawarmalani and Sahinidis (2002) and Burer and Letchford (2012a) (p. 57) and treats global optimization as outside scope (p. 57). Own-group software and libraries are cited throughout: FilMINT (p. 39, p. 96), MINOTAUR (pp. 40-42, p. 96), MINLPBB (p. 96), MacMINLP (p. 16, p. 99), Kirches and Leyffer TACO (p. 99); the solver tables are neutral in format and admit "we do not know the language the solver is written in" (pp. 94-95).
  - Transferable: when your own early theorem has been corrected, state the corrected version in your survey with the corrector's counterexample and give joint credit for the proof (p. 32).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contribution sentence in survey form: "This survey presents a broad overview of deterministic methodologies for solving mixed-integer nonlinear programs" (p. 11). The organising concept appears early (relaxation and constraint enforcement, p. 7) and every technique comes with a tiny worked example and figure: the objective-variable trick (Fig. 2, p. 7), coefficient tightening (Fig. 11, pp. 40-41), a disjunctive cut computed numerically (x1 + 0.3x2 >= -0.166, pp. 48-49), the MISOCP Gomory cut (p. 54), the DAG of a small MINLP (pp. 64-65), the convex-hull gap (p. 76), the Undercover graph (p. 84). Algorithms are boxed as pseudocode (Algorithms 1-6). Theorems are mostly quoted with provenance ("Theorem 2.2.6 in Drewes (2009)", p. 53). Hedging is moderate ("arguably more critical", p. 14; "Arguably the most important factor", p. 99) and there is one exclamation (p. 24). First-person plural reports own experience ("We observed very large search trees", p. 39; "We have shown that LP/NLP-BB can be improved dramatically", p. 39). The preprint has rough edges: "Algorithm 1" cited for LP/NLP-BB (p. 44), an undefined |Y| in Theorem 3.3 (p. 38), z_i for y_i in the worst-case argument (p. 44), typos such as "contiuous" (p. 6). No conclusion section: the text ends with software and online resources (pp. 94-100).
  - Transferable: pair each technique in a tutorial or survey with the smallest example on which it can be computed by hand and a figure of before and after (pp. 7, 40-41, 48-49, 76).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution is expository and taxonomic (a two-axis map of methods, modelling tricks, cuts, nonconvex relaxations, heuristics, MIOCP and software, pp. 11-12), with small original computations (D5). Limits and open directions are stated locally and concretely rather than in a closing section: split disjunctions "have not been used in MINLP" (p. 27); updating pseudocosts on infeasible NLPs with the l1 exact penalty is suggested as "a fruitful alternative motivated by NLP" (pp. 26-27); NLP warm starts "generally have not been as successful in MINLP" because Jacobian and Hessian change between nodes (p. 27); separation problems for disjunctive cuts are hard and tested on "only four instances with no more than 30 unknowns" in the original work (p. 50); nonlinear cuts do not benefit branch-and-cut "in general" (p. 50); piecewise-linear approaches are "limited to functions with at most a few arguments" (p. 64); RLT/SDP hierarchies grow "dramatically" (p. 79); gamma(n_omega) is conjectured to be O(log n_omega) (p. 92); constraints depending on the integer control are not covered (p. 94). MINOTAUR is described as implementing only NLP-BB and QP-Diving "Currently" (p. 96).
  - Transferable: attach each open problem to the section where the gap shows, with the evidence that makes it a gap (e.g., the size of the test set of the original paper, p. 50), instead of a generic future-work paragraph.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Move the argument of an undefined-outside-domain function into a new bounded variable (x2 = sin(x1), x2 >= 0) so NLP solvers that keep simple bounds never evaluate it there (p. 15).
- For big-M on/off constraints whose binary also switches the variable bounds (0 <= x_i <= u_i y), tighten M by evaluating c(0) instead of solving a nonconvex maximization (pp. 41-42).
- Disaggregate h(g(x)) and sum_j h_j(a_j^T x) into auxiliary variables before any OA-based algorithm; check on the Hijazi worst case that two linearization points now suffice (pp. 43-44).
- Write down what your convergence proof needs (global solves only at pruned nodes) and use the slack for inexact or surrogate solves at non-leaf nodes (pp. 24, 29).
- Derive weaker method variants from the strongest one (Benders from OA by multiplier aggregation) and state the strength order explicitly (pp. 34-35).
- Test any generic relaxation scheme against the known convex hull of a two-variable set before trusting it in a solver (pp. 75-76).
- Show one hard instance with tree sizes and times for own solver, competitors and the reformulated model, then a performance profile over the family (pp. 40-42).
