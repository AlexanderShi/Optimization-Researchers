### S096 · McCormick envelopes in mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimization (Mathematical Programming 2026, doi:10.1007/s10107-024-02181-1; arXiv:2406.07891)
**Read level**: full, arXiv version. All of pp. 1-34 was read (abstract, §1-§6, Appendix A Lemma A.1); the reference list (pp. 34-38) was skimmed. Title and authors (Sven Leyffer, Paul Manns) confirmed on p. 1. This text is arXiv v5 (January 2026). It postdates the online-first journal version (2025) and may already contain the changes of the 2026 Correction (S162), for example the box constraint wℓ ≤ w ≤ wu in (McCh) on p. 9 and the unique-solvability item Assumption 2.7.2 on p. 9. I did not compare it with the typeset article or with the Correction, so which version each statement belongs to is not verified.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Paul Manns (TU Dortmund) · **Place in trajectory**: the MIPDECO class he named earlier (SKILL.md anatomy 5) receives the global-optimization half (lower bounds), complementing the SLIP primal heuristic of Leyffer and Manns 2022 (ref. [30], used here for upper bounds, pp. 22, 24-25).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry point is a missing tool. McCormick relaxations are standard for lower bounds in MINLP branch and bound but "have not gained much attention in PDE-constrained optimization so far" (abstract, p. 1). The paper names two reasons. A pointwise formulation gives infinitely many linear (state) constraints, and optimization-based bound tightening (OBBT) becomes intractable (p. 1; pp. 2, 8). The goal is lower bounds for solving MIPDECO instances to global optimality (pp. 1-3). The problem class is narrowed on purpose to an elliptic operator, R = TV and the bilinearity N(u,w) = uw (p. 2), and the restrictions are defended on p. 3.
  - Transferable: to enter a new class, take one standard tool from your home area, name the specific obstacle that has kept it out (here infinitely many constraints and an intractable OBBT), and design the paper around removing that obstacle.
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: The averaging happens before the relaxation. The product uw is replaced by (P_h u)(P_h w), the product of cell averages over a coarse partition Q_h (eq. (2), p. 8). This leaves exactly four McCormick inequalities per cell, a finite number (McCh), while u and w stay in function space (p. 9). In a second stage w is also discretized piecewise constant, with a grid-consistent TV_h (McChh, pp. 10-11). Fig. 1 (p. 8) gives the roadmap: (OCP) → (McC) → (McCh) → (McChh). OBBT is then run on the per-cell bounds of P_h u (Algorithm 1, p. 16), on a grid coarser than the state discretization (p. 8). What did not change: the McCormick inequalities themselves (p. 6). Remark 2.4 shows that for binary × continuous products they coincide with the big-M linearization (p. 7).
  - Transferable: when a finite-dimensional relaxation has one constraint per discretization node, aggregate the nonlinearity over a coarse partition first. This reduces the number of constraints and of bound-tightening subproblems to the number of cells, and leaves an approximation error that can be estimated.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: U is reflexive and compactly embedded in H = L²; A is linear, bounded and boundedly invertible; j is Lipschitz on bounded sets; C is convex, closed and bounded; the control space is BV (p. 5). Assumption 2.1 asks for a priori state bounds uℓ ≤ u ≤ uu, which the authors call "quite strong". They mitigate it by noting that the bounds are needed only where N acts, and that L∞ bounds can be obtained for elliptic PDEs (p. 6). Assumption 2.7 adds partitions, unique solvability of the averaged equation (2) and valid per-cell bounds (p. 9). Assumption 2.9 asks for a TV_h consistent with averaging (p. 11), and Assumption 2.12 for bounded eccentricity, compactness, Γ-convergence of TV_h∘P_h and convergence of the bounds and states (pp. 12-13). The authors admit that some regularity is assumed "in the interest of a cleaner presentation" (p. 6), and that higher-order terms u^k w would complicate the PDE analysis (p. 3). The 1D example needs −π² < wℓ < wu < π² (p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) Existence by the direct method of the calculus of variations, through minimizing sequences, compact embedding and weak-* compactness of TV (Prop. 2.5, p. 7). (ii) Lemma A.1: a pointwise inequality between a strongly and a weakly L¹-convergent sequence passes to the limit, proved by contradiction with Egorov's theorem (pp. 33-34). It is used to keep the McCormick inequalities in the limits (pp. 7, 14). (iii) Approximate lower bound m(OCP) ≥ m(McCh) − L_u‖ū − ū_h‖_U, obtained by building a feasible point of the relaxation from the minimizer of (OCP) (Thm. 2.8, pp. 9-10). (iv) The extra error of the control discretization is of order h^{1/2}, through ‖w − P_h w‖_{L¹} ≤ h TV(w) (Thm. 2.11, pp. 11-12; eq. (18), p. 18). (v) Γ-convergence of minimizers of (McChh) to those of (McC) through liminf/limsup inequalities (Prop. 2.14, pp. 13-14), with Lemma 2.15 (TV(P_h f) ≤ TV(f) in 1D) supplying the Γ-limit without modifying TV (p. 14). (vi) OBBT validity: tightened bounds preserve Assumption 2.7.3, so the error term is unchanged while the infimum increases monotonically (Lemmas 2.16-2.17, Thms. 2.18-2.19, pp. 15-16). (vii) An h² estimate on ‖u − u_h‖_H via an Aubin-Nitsche duality argument, using the Galerkin-type orthogonality (23) of cell averaging and switching the Hölder exponents from (2,2) to (1,∞) (Lemma 3.1, pp. 19-21), which gives the computable bound (28) (Lemma 3.2, pp. 21-22). (viii) All constants are made explicit with sharp embedding constants from the literature: the optimal Poincaré constant π² and the Sobolev constant 4 (eqs. (11)-(12), p. 17). (ix) Lipschitz continuity and first and second Fréchet differentiability of the control-to-state map are proved to verify the assumptions of the SLIP method [30] (Lemmas 3.4-3.5, Prop. 3.6, pp. 22-24).
  - Transferable: to bound the price of an approximate relaxation, plug the true minimizer into the approximate problem as a feasible point and charge the difference to a Lipschitz constant times a discretization error (pp. 9-10). Lower bound = relaxed optimum minus an a priori error term.
  - Transferable: when an approximation is a local average, look for Galerkin-type orthogonality (the mean of φ − P_h φ is zero on each cell) and use a duality argument to gain an extra power of h (pp. 19-21).
- **D5 Experiments**: 1D: −Δu + uw = f on (0,1), with f = 6, w ∈ [−4, 4], α = 2.5·10⁻⁴, and a piecewise target u_d (p. 25). The baseline discretization is first-order Lagrange elements on 2,048 intervals (pp. 25-26). Upper bounds come from L-BFGS-B with a Huber-smoothed TV (8.381·10⁻²) and from SLIP with integrality (8.555·10⁻²). Lower bounds from Gurobi QPs are 6.770·10⁻² (α = 0), 6.865·10⁻² (McC) and 8.368·10⁻² for the "tightest" McC. The tightest bound uses state bounds umin/umax from monotonicity of the PDE, information not available in practice and used only a posteriori (pp. 25, 27, Table 1). OBBT time grows from 8.2 s (h = 2⁻³) to 1.08·10⁴ s (h = 2⁻¹⁰), against 2.14·10⁴ s for pointwise OBBT (Table 3, p. 29). The a priori constant c_quad is reported as a range, conservative 5.6·10⁴ and tight 1.1·10³ (Table 2, p. 28). With the conservative constant, only h = 2⁻¹⁰ beats the trivial bound 0. With OBBT and the tight constant, h ≤ 2⁻⁷ does (Table 4, p. 29). A posteriori gaps |m(McChh) − m(McC)| are about 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁵ (Table 5, p. 29). Numerical safeguarding is reported: bounds are relaxed by 10⁻⁷ after each OBBT solve because bounds could otherwise cross (p. 26). 2D: a convection-diffusion problem with binary w on a 48 × 48 grid, run on the LiDO3 cluster, with 8 parallel OBBT rounds, a safety tolerance of 10⁻² and Gurobi BarHomogeneous = 1 to avoid false infeasibility (pp. 30-31). A similar bound quality is reached in 127 s as in 95,522 s and 400,780 s (p. 32, Table 7). The a priori analysis is omitted in 2D (p. 31).
  - Transferable: judge a relaxation against an "oracle" baseline that uses information no practical method has (here exact state bounds from monotonicity), and say explicitly that it is a posteriori (pp. 25, 27).
  - Transferable: report the a priori error constant as a conservative-to-tight range, and show at which mesh sizes the certified bound beats the trivial bound (Tables 2, 4, pp. 28-29).
- **D6 Positioning**: Continues finite-dimensional McCormick [39] and its ODE generalizations (Scott, Barton, Chachuat, Houska and others, refs. [28], [40], [48]-[49], [52]-[55], [57], [62]-[63]; pp. 2, 6). It is separated from Buchheim et al.'s lower bounds for linear PDEs with d = 1 (p. 2). The OBBT literature (Gleixner et al., Puranik-Sahinidis) is used for the claim that OBBT is "one of the most expensive bound tightening procedures" (pp. 2, 14). Prior work is restated, not criticized. The paper ties itself to the authors' own SLIP line [30], [35]-[37], whose assumptions are verified for the example (p. 22).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: A contribution section appears before the structure section (p. 3), and Fig. 1 is a roadmap of the approximation chain (p. 8). The paper frames itself as "a recipe" (p. 3). Restrictions are justified in the first person ("We believe that our restrictions on the setting are sensible", p. 3). The abstract already promises "both the potential of the methodology and the gaps in the research that need to be closed" (p. 1), and the conclusion quantifies the gap (p. 32). Each theorem comes with remarks on practicality (Remarks 2.3, 2.10, 2.13, 3.3). Small slip: the proof of Thm. 2.19 says that it "parallels the one of Theorem 2.19" (p. 16), presumably meaning Thm. 2.18.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory: convex relaxations in function space, approximate lower bounds with a priori estimates, Γ-convergence, and OBBT validity. Computation: 1D and 2D demonstrations; the conclusion reports two to three orders of magnitude less effort in 2D (p. 32). Specific limits: the a priori bound beats trivial bounds only on fine meshes and with well-estimated constants (p. 32). Higher-order estimates of ‖u − u_h‖_H and a better L_u are needed (p. 32). L∞ state bounds may be unavailable in several dimensions (p. 33). The 2D a priori analysis is out of scope (p. 29). Future work: a posteriori error estimates "is key" (pp. 32-33); a function-space branch and bound with adaptive mesh refinement (p. 33); general nonlinearities through generalized McCormick with nested intervals [55] (p. 33).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ evidence: a home MINLP tool (McCormick plus OBBT, with the big-M equivalence of Remark 2.4) is carried into PDE-constrained control, and the new class's objects (an integer control in BV with a TV penalty) are fitted to it (pp. 2-3, 7). Its Limitations entry also applies: the carried-in "quite strong" state-bound assumption and unique solvability are explicit assumptions here (pp. 6, 9). Heuristic 8 ✅ evidence: function space first, discretize second. Pointwise OBBT cost (Remark 2.3, p. 7; p. 8) motivates analysis instead of brute force, and the paper reports the measured pointwise cost of 2.14·10⁴ s and 4.0·10⁵ s (pp. 29, 32). Taste 3 ✅ evidence: every bound carries an a priori certificate, and the paper says where the certificate is too weak (pp. 29, 32). Method 5 ✅ evidence: calibrated claims; the "tightest" baseline is flagged as unavailable in practice, a conservative-to-tight range is given, and failures to beat the trivial bound are kept in Table 4 (pp. 27-29). New-pattern candidates: "certify every assumption on a worked example with explicit constants" (§3, pp. 16-19); "oracle-information baseline used only a posteriori" (pp. 25, 27).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Two-stage approximation chain (averaged nonlinearity, then averaged control), with each stage's error proved separately and then chained (Fig. 1, p. 8; Thms. 2.8, 2.11).
- OBBT soundness argument: shrinking the feasible set preserves the assumption that underlies the error bound, so the certified lower bound can only increase (pp. 15-16).
- Numerical safeguarding of OBBT: relax each computed bound by a small tolerance so that solver inaccuracy cannot make the bounds cross (pp. 26, 31).
- Verify a whole assumption list on one concrete PDE with sharp, cited embedding constants before running experiments (pp. 16-19).
**Quotes**:
- "Consequently, this article will be read as a recipe to first obtain a pointwise generalization of underestimators for nonconvexities as are known from finite-dimensional nonconvex optimization and then approximate this pointwise generalization with finitely many linear inequalities using approximation properties that are due to the PDE setting." (p. 3)
- "the a priori approximation quality of the lower bound only improves over trivial bounds when the mesh size is already quite fine and the involved constants can be estimated well." (p. 32)

### S114 · A unified funnel restoration SQP algorithm (Mathematical Programming 2026, doi:10.1007/s10107-025-02284-3; arXiv:2409.09208)
**Read level**: full, arXiv version. All of pp. 1-24 was read (abstract, §1-§6), plus the references (pp. 24-26). Appendices A and B (pp. 27-37, per-instance evaluation counts for trust-region and line-search methods) were skimmed for status columns only. Title and authors (David Kiessling, Sven Leyffer, Charlie Vanaret) confirmed on p. 1. The text is arXiv:2409.09208v1 (13 Sep 2024), not the 2026 journal version; changes made in revision are unknown.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: David Kiessling (KU Leuven, first author), Charlie Vanaret (Argonne/ZIB) · **Place in trajectory**: rebuilding the filter base inside Uno (SKILL.md Method 2 step 6 lists "funnel SQP"). The funnel is analysed through the 2002 filter convergence proofs of Fletcher, Leyffer and Toint (refs. [10], [11], [14]).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: There are two entries. The first is a framework claim: most NCO solvers can be assembled from four ingredients inside a double loop (pp. 2, 4). The second is a method gap. Funnel methods existed for equality constraints with Byrd-Omojokun steps (Gould-Toint [18], [19]), as an interior-point trust funnel (Curtis et al. [6]), and as a "tolerance tube" SQP in Zoppke-Donaldson's 1995 Dundee thesis "but without any convergence analysis" (p. 2). The stated aim is to show that a funnel performs like a filter "while being simpler to implement" (p. 2). The problem form is min f(x) s.t. c(x) = 0, x ≥ 0 (pp. 1-2).
  - Transferable: when a rival globalization exists in a narrower setting (equalities only, interior point only), restate it inside your own general framework and prove it there.
- **D2 Key idea and algorithm design**: The key observation is that a funnel is a filter with a single entry. A capacity-limited filter that replaces its hmax upper bound when full behaves like a funnel when N_F = 1, apart from the update rule (13) (p. 11). This is what lets the filter proofs carry over. Design choices: the funnel condition h ≤ τ, the switching condition Δm_f ≥ δh², an Armijo test for f-type steps, and a funnel sufficient decrease h ≤ βτ for h-type steps, with τ updated only on h-type steps (pp. 8-9). The update (13) is the second term of Gould-Toint's max-rule (14), because "all had similar performance" (p. 9). Restoration uses a smooth elastic ℓ1 QP whose trust region bounds only the original variables, so it is always feasible and satisfies LICQ (p. 7). The funnel was implemented "by modifying the existing filter implementation within Uno" (p. 2). Both trust-region and line-search mechanisms are given (Algorithms 3-4, pp. 12-13), and the paper claims the first line-search funnel methods (p. 3). Unchanged: the SQP subproblem, the restoration phase and the ingredient interfaces (§2).
  - Transferable: before inventing a proof for a new acceptance test, look for a parameter setting of an analysed test that reduces to yours (here a filter of capacity one). The new proof then becomes a list of adjustments to the old one.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The standard assumptions [11] are iterates in a compact set X, f and c of class C², and uniformly bounded Hessians (Assumption 1, p. 12). MFCQ is used for KKT (pp. 3-4). Convergence to infeasible stationary points is not analysed; a convergent restoration is assumed "without loss of generality" (p. 7). Lemma 5 needs the global QP solution; Remark 1 substitutes a Cauchy-point condition when the Hessian is indefinite (p. 17). Theorem 1 states its sequences under the hypothesis h^(k+n) ≤ βτ^(k) for all k, n (p. 14). The line-search global convergence (beyond feasibility) is not proved (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) Theorem 1 (feasibility) splits into two cases. With infinitely many h-type steps the funnel contracts geometrically, τ^(k+1) ≤ θτ^(k) with θ = 1 − (1−β)(1−κ). With finitely many, the switching condition is summed telescopically against f bounded below, giving Σ(h^(k))² < ∞ (p. 14). For line search the sum carries an α_min factor (p. 14). (ii) Lemmas 1, 2 and 4 are taken from the filter-SQP convergence paper [14], with an adjusted proof for Lemma 2 because the problem format differs (pp. 15-16). (iii) Lemma 3: a trust-region radius bound Δ² ≤ 2βτ/(mnM) makes the step funnel-acceptable (p. 15). (iv) Lemma 5: the inner loop terminates finitely, by a case split on h^(k) > 0 (the QP becomes infeasible for a small radius) and h^(k) = 0 (a descent direction plus the quadratic-minimizer Lemma 1) (pp. 16-17). (v) Theorem 2, three outcomes, by contradiction: near a feasible non-KKT point with MFCQ, the interval [μh, √(2βh/(mnM))] of radii giving f-type steps has an upper end more than twice its lower end, so "given the update rule" the inner loop finds a radius inside it (pp. 17-18). The paper does not spell out the reduction factor that this needs; the decrease rule on p. 6 only says "smaller than min(Δ, ‖d‖∞)".
  - Transferable: show that the window of trust-region radii that forces the desired step type is wider than the radius-reduction factor; the update rule then cannot skip it (pp. 17-18).
  - Transferable: to prove that constraint violation goes to zero, split on whether infinitely many violation-reducing steps occur (geometric contraction of the tolerance) or not (telescoping the switching condition against a bounded objective) (p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: Four configurations (funnel or filter × trust region or line search) are run in Uno on 278 small CUTEst instances. Unconstrained problems are excluded because funnel and filter coincide on them (p. 18). Fairness rule: both strategies start with the same parameters (Table 1, p. 19) and use the same QP solver (BQPD). The trust region uses the exact Hessian; the line search convexifies by adding identity multiples, with inertia from MA57 (p. 19). κ = 0.5 was "picked" because it "worked well for our simulations" (p. 19). The filter capacity is 50 and the iteration limit 4,000 (p. 19). Five termination outcomes and a "solved correctly" rule are defined (pp. 19-20), and log files are public (p. 20). The paper uses performance profiles on constraint evaluations (Fig. 3, p. 20) and scatter plots (Fig. 4, p. 21), noting that only 49 instances differ per mechanism, with bars of f-, h- and restoration-step counts for them (Figs. 5-6, pp. 21-22). The line-search underperformance is attributed to convexification and checked by convexifying the trust-region QPs ("the performance similarly degrades", p. 20). Two illustrative cases follow: on powellbs the funnel's non-monotonicity allows quadratic convergence where the filter blocks (pp. 21-23, Fig. 7); on the Fletcher-Leyffer-Toint Maratos example the funnel also rejects the full step (pp. 23-24, Tables 2-3). Appendices list per-instance counts including error and infeasible statuses for both strategies (pp. 27-37, skimmed).
  - Transferable: when two methods differ only on a minority of instances, say how many (here 49 of 278) and show detailed diagnostics for just those, instead of letting identical runs dilute the profile (p. 21).
  - Transferable: test a suspected cause of underperformance by moving the suspected factor into the better configuration and checking that it degrades too (p. 20).
- **D6 Positioning**: The funnel is examined "through the lens of filter methods" (p. 2). Gould-Toint, Curtis et al. and Samadi are credited as the funnel line (p. 2). The filter papers of Fletcher, Leyffer and Toint and the line-search filter of Wächter and Biegler are "a baseline for proving global convergence results" (p. 3). Rivals are restated rather than criticized; differences are noted factually (for example why (14) cannot keep iterates inside the funnel when both measures may increase, p. 9). The tolerance-tube precursor cited is a Dundee thesis (ref. [32], p. 2).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**: Contributions are numbered (i)-(iv) with one priority claim, "To our knowledge, this is the first time that line-search funnel methods have been considered" (p. 3). The performance claim is "slightly outperforms" (pp. 20, 24). A negative result about the authors' own method is stated plainly (Maratos effect, p. 23). The ingredient labels (constraint relaxation, globalization strategy, globalization mechanism) are typeset beside the pseudocode lines to show the modularity (Algorithms 2-5, pp. 10-13), and the text says the components are deliberately split "to emphasize the modularity of the Uno framework" (p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contributions are algorithmic (trust-region and line-search restoration funnel SQP), theoretical (global convergence for the trust-region version) and software (the Uno funnel branch, public logs) (pp. 3, 18, 20). Limits as stated: no global convergence proof for line search (p. 15); convergence to infeasible points not analysed (p. 7); fast local convergence needs second-order corrections, a watchdog or non-monotone techniques (p. 23); only small CUTEst instances. Not stated by the authors: the only baseline is Uno's own filter, and κ was chosen on the same test set as the comparison (p. 19). Outlook: Uno is "meant to be extended" to MPECs and robust optimization (p. 2); the conclusion lists no further future work (p. 24).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence: four interchangeable ingredients in a double loop, Uno presets that mimic filterSQP, IPOPT and Byrd's Sℓ1QP, and the funnel built by modifying the filter component (pp. 2, 4, 12). Method 2 ✅ evidence: the filter base is rebuilt, and the new globalization is read as a special filter (p. 11). Heuristic 5 ✅ evidence: proofs are transferred lemma by lemma from [14] with adjustments marked (pp. 15-16). Method 3 ✅ evidence: the authors' own new method is run on the Fletcher-Leyffer-Toint Maratos counterexample, and the failure is reported with a remedy list (pp. 23-24). Method 5 ⚠ variant: there is a same-solver, same-initial-parameter fairness rule and per-instance tables with failures, and the claim is "slightly" (pp. 19-20, 27-37). However, the only baseline is the authors' own filter inside the same framework, and κ was tuned on the comparison set (p. 19); this repeats the FilMINT gap noted in SKILL.md. New-pattern candidate: "reduce the new method to a degenerate case of an analysed one (funnel = single-entry filter) to reuse its proof" (p. 11).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Funnel acceptance test as a filter with one entry: switching condition Δm_f ≥ δh², Armijo on f-type steps, h ≤ βτ plus the convex-combination update τ ← (1−κ)h + κτ on h-type steps (pp. 8-11).
- An elastic ℓ1 restoration QP with the trust region only on the original variables, so that it is always feasible and the elastic variables give LICQ (p. 7).
- Radius-window argument for trust-region global convergence (pp. 17-18).
- Restrict performance diagnostics to the instances where methods actually diverge, and break those down by step type (pp. 21-22).
**Quotes**:
- "We can now interpret the funnel as a filter with a single entry, NF = 1, that corresponds to the upper bound on the constraint violation, and we update this upper bound on h-type iterations." (p. 11)
- "This demonstrates that the funnel method also suffers from the Maratos effect." (p. 23)
