### S101 · Generalized outer approximation (Encyclopedia of Optimization 2001, doi:10.1007/0-306-48332-7_161)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-7, including the reference list). The version read is the author file dated August 24, 2006 (p. 1 header); its reference list stops at 1997 (refs [5], [7], [10], pp. 7), so the content is the first-edition entry or a lightly updated copy of it. Whether the 2008/2009 second-edition printing differs is not known from this file.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (S. Leyffer, Department of Mathematics, University of Dundee, p. 7) · **Place in trajectory**: a condensed restatement of his 1993 thesis (ref. [11]) and Fletcher-Leyffer 1994 (ref. [6]) for an encyclopedia, with the LP/NLP-based branch-and-bound generalisation that later becomes the core of FilMINT and Minotaur.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Convex MINLP min f(x,y) s.t. g(x,y) ≤ 0, x ∈ X, y ∈ Y integer, under (A1) twice continuously differentiable convex f, g, (A2) compact convex polyhedral X, Y, (A3) a constraint qualification at the solution of every fixed-y NLP (p. 1). The entry is twofold: present an alternative to nonlinear branch-and-bound built on an MILP master problem (p. 1), and correct the master problem of Duran-Grossmann [4] and Yuan et al. [14], whose treatment of infeasible subproblems "could cause the algorithm to cycle" (p. 1). The predecessor's justification (that a representation of y ∈ V is already contained in the linearisations) is rejected with a counterexample pointer: an MINLP "where this would lead to an incorrect master problem [6], [11, p. 79]" (p. 2).
  - Transferable: open a methods article by naming the one step in the predecessor's derivation that fails, and point to the instance that breaks it.
- **D2 Core idea**: Reformulate P exactly as one MILP master problem M by projection onto the integer variables (value function v(y)), replacing each fixed-y NLP by its linearisation at its solution (valid because of (A3) and convexity), and representing y ∈ V (feasible assignments) by linearisations taken at the solution of a general feasibility problem F(y^k) that minimises a weighted violation of the constraints in J⊥ subject to those in J (pp. 1-3). M is "not practical to solve ... directly" (p. 3); algorithms are relaxations of M: linear OA (Algorithm 1, with the constraint η < UBD^i that excludes every visited assignment, pp. 3-4), quadratic OA (curvature term q^i added to the master objective only, so the feasible region stays an outer approximation and finiteness survives, pp. 4-5), and LP/NLP-based branch-and-bound that updates one MILP tree instead of re-solving masters (Algorithm 2, pp. 5-6), generalised from Quesada-Grossmann's linear-in-y case to nonlinear-in-y problems, plus a new QP/NLP variant (p. 5, p. 7). What changes from Duran-Grossmann: the infeasible-subproblem cuts and the nonlinear-in-y scope; what stays: the NLP/MILP alternation.
  - Transferable: when adding second-order information to a cutting-plane method, put it in the master objective, never in the constraints, so the outer-approximation property and finite termination are untouched (pp. 4-5).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: (A1)-(A3) above (p. 1); |Y| < ∞ for termination (Theorem 3, p. 4; Corollary 4, p. 6). The simplifying assumption that every integer assignment gives a feasible subproblem is used first "to improve the readability", then removed (p. 1). The CQ assumption is what makes the LP linearisation (3) have the same optimal value as the NLP (pp. 1-2). The claim that only strongly active constraints need linearising (pp. 1-2) is stated without proof here.
- **D4 Proof devices**: Equivalence of P and M (Theorem 2, p. 3), proved by the preceding subsections (projection + supporting hyperplanes + Lemma 1 on infeasible assignments, whose proof is deferred to [6, Lemma 1], p. 3). Note: Theorem 2 states that (x*, y*) "solves P if and only if it solves M" (p. 3), the "if and only if" that Bonami et al. later showed too strong (05-peer-critique §1.1); the file dated 2006 still carries it. Finite termination (Theorem 3, p. 4): the bound η < f^i makes the visited y^i infeasible in later masters, so no assignment repeats; optimality from convexity (linearisations are supporting hyperplanes); full proof deferred to [6]. Corollary 4 transfers the same no-repeat argument to the tree search (p. 6).
  - Transferable: prove finiteness of a decomposition method by showing each iteration cuts off the current integer assignment with a strict upper-bound constraint, so the count of iterations is bounded by |Y| (p. 4).
- **D5 Experiments**: None new. Reported from the thesis [11, Ch. 6]: the curvature term reduces OA iterations when general integers are present but not for binary-only problems, so quadratic OA pays off in time only for general-integer problems since MIQPs cost more (p. 5). From [11, Ch. 6] and [5]: LP and QP versions of Algorithm 2 are "usually faster" than Algorithm 1, "often beating the latter by a factor of 2" (p. 7).
- **D6 Positioning**: Explicitly a generalisation of Duran-Grossmann OA and of Quesada-Grossmann LP/NLP-BB (p. 1, p. 5); the predecessor is corrected directly but in a neutral tone ("an inaccuracy", p. 1). Branch-and-bound terminology is outsourced to five textbooks (p. 5). The feasibility-filter of his own NA/171 (= S001) is named as one admissible feasibility problem (pp. 2-3), tying the MINLP line to the NLP line.
- **D7 Writing**: Encyclopedia register: each section opens with what it will show and closes with what it motivates (e.g. the worst-case example "motivates the introduction of a second order term", p. 3). Proofs are outlined in a paragraph and deferred to the 1994 paper (p. 4). Two pictures (OA flow, LP/NLP tree updates) carry the algorithms (pp. 4, 7). Evaluative claims are hedged as "preliminary" (p. 5, p. 7).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: an expository unification of OA, quadratic OA, LP/NLP-BB and QP/NLP-BB under one master problem, with the infeasibility correction. Limits stated concretely: QP/NLP-BB loses the pruning step because QPs give no lower bounds (p. 7); quadratic OA helps only with general integers (p. 5); and "A detailed numerical comparison of the two approaches is still outstanding." (p. 7). Not stated: what happens if (A3) fails or NLPs are solved inexactly (the gap Tamm-Kronqvist 2026 raise, 05 §1.3).

**Method links**: Method 3 evidence ✅: the predecessor's master problem is tested against a breaking instance before being replaced (p. 2), and the worst-case example in which linear OA visits all integer assignments drives the redesign to quadratic OA (pp. 3-5). Method 1 evidence ✅: LP/NLP-BB integrates NLP solves into one MILP tree instead of alternating separate packaged solves (the master relaxations are not re-solved; the branch-and-bound tree is updated, p. 5). Method 5 evidence ✅: rule of thumb by problem class (general integers vs binaries, p. 5) and an explicit "still outstanding" for the comparison (p. 7). Method 2 evidence ✅ (weak): the NLP base enters the MINLP master through the feasibility-filter problem (pp. 2-3). New-pattern candidates: "exact but impractical reformulation as the design scaffold" (p. 3); "curvature only where it cannot break the guarantee" (pp. 4-5).
**Transferable techniques**:
- State the exact (impractical) reformulation first, then derive every algorithm as a relaxation of it; finiteness and optimality proofs then reduce to showing that the relaxations only tighten and never revisit an assignment (pp. 3-4).
- Model infeasible subproblems through the feasibility problem the NLP solver actually solves (a J/J⊥ partition covering l1, l∞ and filter phase I), and take the cuts from its solution (pp. 2-3).
- In a tree-based decomposition, do not fathom an integer-feasible node: re-solve it after adding the new cuts, since the cuts remove the previous solution (p. 6).
**Quotes**:
- "However, it is not difficult to derive an MINLP problem where this would lead to an incorrect master problem" (p. 2)
- "A detailed numerical comparison of the two approaches is still outstanding." (p. 7)

### S001 · Nonlinear programming without a penalty function (Mathematical Programming 2002, doi:10.1007/s101070100244)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-26 read closely; result tables pp. 27-32 skimmed for the cases discussed in the text). Version read: Dundee NA/171 report, revision of 24 August 2000 (p. 1), not the journal typesetting; report pages are cited.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher (Dundee; Leyffer's PhD adviser) · **Place in trajectory**: the origin paper of the filter, the method that becomes the fixed "base" of Method 2; work "started in 1996" (p. 7); its convergence theory came later (S007, S005, and the SLP-filter report NA/183 cited as [10], p. 7).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is a practical complaint: penalty functions force "diÆult deisions from the user in regard to the hoie of penalty parameters" (p. 1); too low a parameter gives infeasible points or unbounded penalty growth, too high damps the objective and slows progress along curved boundaries (p. 2). A second, rarely stressed need: rapid, ultimately second-order convergence to a "best" point when the constraints are inconsistent, which occur often in practice, "for example due to inadequate modelling" (p. 2). Encouragement comes from a Dundee thesis (Zoppke-Donaldson) that worked well without a penalty function and without a proof (p. 2).
  - Transferable: frame a new globalization by the user decision it removes (here, choosing a penalty parameter) and by the failure mode users actually meet (inconsistent constraints), not by a theoretical gap.
- **D2 Core idea**: Treat objective f and constraint violation h (l1 norm) as two aims, akin to a multi-objective problem, with priority on h (p. 4); accept an SQP trust-region step if its (f, h) pair is not dominated by any pair in a "filter" (Definitions 1-2, p. 4; Algorithm 1, pp. 5-6). Only scalars are stored, no vectors, so backtracking is impossible by design (p. 4). Infeasible QPs send the method to a restoration phase that minimises h (p. 5). The stated design principle is a minimalist approach to SQP: interfere as little as possible with SQP (p. 2). Production extensions (Section 3): second-order correction steps (pp. 7-8), an upper bound u on h acting as a tolerance "tube" (pp. 8-9), a restoration phase built on the phase I partition J/J⊥ of the authors' QP solver bqpd with second-order terms and its own phase I filter (pp. 9-12), unblocking of blocking entries with a decreasing u to prevent cycling (pp. 12-13), a sufficient-reduction envelope (pp. 13-14), and North-West/South-East corner rules (pp. 14-15). Unchanged relative to SQP: the QP step, the trust region (l∞, so the subproblem stays a QP, p. 5).
  - Transferable: build the restoration phase from what the inner solver's phase I already returns (its partition of satisfiable and unsatisfiable constraints), and hot-start the next QP from it, so restoration costs about one QP (p. 10).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: f and c twice continuously differentiable with exact first and second derivatives, justified by the ready availability of automatic differentiation software (p. 1). Linear constraints are kept feasible throughout by an initial LP (p. 17, detail 4). No global convergence assumption set: "At present the question of global onvergene is open" (p. 3, text-layer spelling). Known problematic situations are admitted: indefinite Hessians giving non-global QP solutions with negligible or negative predicted reduction, and unblocking (p. 7), "very rare" so far (p. 7).
- **D4 Proof devices**: No theorem is proved. The heuristics are framed as proof scaffolding: "something akin to what we suggest is likely to be needed if a global onvergene proof is to be established" (p. 7, text-layer spelling); "We regard them largely as an indiation of what might be required to produe a global onvergene proof" (p. 13). Cycling is excluded informally by the upper bound acting like a filter entry with f = -∞ plus sufficient reduction (p. 12). Byrd's scenario (f increasing, h decreasing along the filter without converging) is named and patched by the NW corner rule (pp. 14-15), and the authors note that later work (the SLP-filter proof [10]) shows the corner rules can be dropped (p. 15).
  - Transferable: when a proof is not yet available, design each safeguard against a named failure scenario and label it as a conjectured proof ingredient, so that the later proof tells you which safeguards to delete.
- **D5 Experiments**: CUTE problems, exact second derivatives, Fortran 77 on a SPARCstation 4, QP solver bqpd (pp. 17-18). Baselines: LANCELOT (defaults) and the authors' own implementation of Fletcher's Sl1QP with the penalty parameter set from the optimal multipliers, described as the most favourable circumstances for Sl1QP (p. 18). Small set: all 186 smooth CUTE problems with n, m ≤ 25 and nonlinear constraints; problems with different local minima removed, leaving 127; crashes 0 / 2 / 12 and CPU 37.95 / 107.45 / 82.47 s for filter SQP / Sl1QP / LANCELOT (Table 1, p. 18); relative rankings (Figure 6, p. 19); gradient-evaluation histograms (Figure 7, p. 20). Large set: 159 problems up to n, m ≤ 3100 after removing 8 with stated reasons (Table 2, p. 19); filter SQP more than 100x faster on 23 problems, LANCELOT on 5 (p. 20). Failures are reported by name with causes (6 of 159 for filter SQP; LANCELOT fails on 24), with the parameter changes that rescue four of them (pp. 21-22). The safeguards are instrumented: occurrence tables per heuristic and phase (upper bound active in 11% / 21% of iterations, unblocking 1.4% / 2.3%, corner rules ≈ 0.3%) and a switch-off run of the corner rules (15 problems change significantly) (pp. 23-24); filter length ≤ 10 entries on 227 of 247 problems, maximum 43 (p. 24).
  - Transferable: report, for every safeguard, how often it fires and what happens when it is switched off; this is the evidence the authors give that the safeguards do not overly restrict the method (pp. 23-24).
- **D6 Positioning**: SQP lineage Wilson, Han, Powell (p. 2); penalty and augmented-Lagrangian globalization and the watchdog technique as the foil (p. 2); Zoppke-Donaldson's tolerance tube as the direct inspiration (p. 2, p. 9). LANCELOT is not dismissed: the authors explain where it wins (BRATU discretised PDEs where conjugate gradients make iterations cheap; DTOC/ORTHR problems with null spaces of dimension ≈ 1000 that are bad for a dense reduced-Hessian QP solver, p. 20) and where it loses (linearly infeasible FLOSP problems, many-inequality OET problems, many-equation problems, p. 21), pointing to a published remedy for the slack issue ([3], p. 21).
- **D7 Writing**: The basic idea is stated in two definitions and a ten-line algorithm (pp. 4-6), then a frank transition: going from the concept to production software for large-scale NLP is called a very large step involving "many messy details" (p. 6). The revision history is visible and self-corrective: the large-problem results "are not quite as enouraging as our earlier results suggested" because an earlier version used a favourable subset of variable-sized problems (p. 20, text-layer spelling). Hedges are frequent and concrete ("Hopefully", p. 15; "we onjeture" in the text layer, p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: a new globalization concept (filter), a production code (filterSQP) interfaced to CUTE, AMPL and NEOS (p. 17), and a large comparison. Limits stated concretely: no global convergence proof (pp. 3, 7); no scaling used (pp. 22-23); EIGENC and QR3DBD crash because successive QPs are ill-conditioned (p. 22); the NW/SE rule may be violated during unblocking, so an f → -∞, h → 0 sequence remains possible in principle (p. 15). Future: simplify the heuristics guided by the convergence proof of [10] (p. 7, p. 15). Relative-performance verdicts depend on test-set composition: LANCELOT could be made to look better or worse by changing the mix (p. 21).

**Method links**: Method 5 evidence ✅ (strong): self-built competitor at its best setting with the fairness rule written down (Sl1QP, p. 18); failures and crashes kept in the statistics (Table 1, p. 18; pp. 21-22); removed problems listed with reasons (Table 2, p. 19); losses explained by problem class (p. 20); a claim narrowed between report revisions (p. 20); the test-set-composition caveat (p. 21). Method 1 evidence ✅: the restoration phase and SOC steps exploit the phase I partition and hot starts of their own QP solver bqpd (pp. 8-10), while the basic algorithm "works with any (indenite) QP solver" (p. 6). Method 6 evidence ✅: inconsistent constraints are traced to "inadequate modelling" (p. 2); termination advice reads the largest multiplier as a sign of nearly dependent active constraints (p. 17); per-instance diagnoses (HS99EXP poorly scaled, MESH unbounded, COSHFUN objective conjectured unbounded near the feasible region, p. 22). Method 3 evidence ✅: the method is checked against Maratos-type examples, and an example from Fletcher's book in which the unit step increases both f and h justifies the SOC step (pp. 7-8). Heuristic 3 ("numbers first, theory second") evidence ✅: a working, tested method with the convergence question openly left to later work (pp. 3, 7). Taste 4 evidence ✅: the minimalist principle (p. 2). New-pattern candidates: "safeguard firing statistics" (pp. 23-24); "heuristics as provisional proof ingredients, trimmed once the proof exists" (pp. 7, 13, 15).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Give a self-built baseline the most favourable parameter it could have (penalty parameter from the optimal multipliers) and say so; a win against that baseline is robust (p. 18).
- Remove from a comparison the instances where solvers reach different local minima, report how many, and keep crashes in the summary table (p. 18).
- When a competitor wins on a class, identify the structural reason (conjugate gradients on PDE discretisations; large null spaces) rather than averaging it away (p. 20).
- Store only scalars in the acceptance mechanism and accept the consequence (no backtracking), then design a dedicated escape (unblocking with a shrinking bound) for the one scenario that needs it (pp. 4, 12).
**Quotes** (text layer drops "c" and fi/ff/fl; reproduced as extracted):
- "The aim is to interfere as little as possible with the SQP iteration but to do enough to give a bias towards onvergene." (p. 2)
- "Thus any heuristis that might be suggested to enable a global onvergene result to be proved should be heap to apply and rarely invoked." (p. 3)

### S007 · Global convergence of a trust-region SQP-filter algorithm for general nonlinear programming (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2002, doi:10.1137/s1052623499357258)
**Read level**: full (pp. 2-26: body, all lemma proofs, the appendix flowchart p. 24 and the reference list; p. 1 is the repository cover sheet). Publisher's version of record (cover sheet, p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Roger Fletcher and Sven Leyffer (Dundee), Nicholas I. M. Gould (Rutherford Appleton Laboratory), Philippe L. Toint (Namur), Andreas Wächter (IBM Watson, then CMU) (p. 2) · **Place in trajectory**: received June 14, 1999 (p. 2), i.e. while S001 was still in revision; the third step of the filter theory after the SLP-filter proof [19] and the filter-SQP proof [20] (= S005), and the one that brings in the composite-step trust-region machinery of Gould, Toint and colleagues.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Filter SQP works well (S001) but its theory so far needs either SLP subproblems ([19]) or global solutions of nonconvex QPs ([20]), which is NP-hard (Murty-Kabadi) (pp. 2-3). The entry is to remove that requirement: allow an approximate subproblem solution and use a different compatibility test (p. 3). Problem class: general NLP with equality and inequality constraints, "No convexity assumption is made" (p. 2).
  - Transferable: target the assumption in your own earlier proof that no implementation can meet (here, a global QP solve) and make removing it the paper's contribution.
- **D2 Core idea**: Split the step into a normal component n_k (towards linearised feasibility) and a tangential component t_k (model decrease), following the composite-step tradition (Vardi; Byrd-Schnabel-Shultz; Omojokun) (p. 4). Declare TRQP(x_k, Δ_k) "compatible" only if n_k exists and ‖n_k‖ ≤ κ_Δ Δ_k min[1, κ_μ Δ_k^μ] (2.12), leaving room for the tangential step (p. 5); require only a Cauchy-point type decrease (2.15) of the tangential step (p. 6). Otherwise add x_k to the filter and run a restoration procedure on θ (the l∞ violation) (pp. 6-7). The filter uses margins (2.17) and removes only entries whose envelope is dominated (2.18), so "the margin of the filter never decreases" (p. 8). An f-type/θ-type step switch (2.19)-(2.20) decides whether to demand actual-versus-predicted agreement or to add the point to the filter (pp. 9-10). What stays from S001: the filter idea, trust regions, restoration; what changes: inexact subproblems, the normal/tangential split, the switching condition, and no corner rules or unblocking.
  - Transferable: when a global QP solve is unrealistic, replace the requirement to solve the subproblem by the requirement to achieve at least a Cauchy-point fraction of model decrease, which an implementation can verify (p. 6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: AS1 (f and c twice continuously differentiable), AS2 (uniformly bounded Hessian approximations), AS3 (iterates in a closed bounded domain) (p. 12); the normal-step assumption (2.10): n_k exists and ‖n_k‖ ≤ κ_usc θ_k when θ_k ≤ δ_n (p. 4), argued to be not "unduly restrictive" by a continuity argument under a nonempty relative interior of the linearised constraints and a settled active set at limit points (p. 5). The Euclidean norm is "purely for ease of exposition" (p. 4). The restoration procedure is assumed either to fail (converge to an infeasible stationary point of θ) or to reach any desired θ (p. 7), justified by the observation that finding a feasible point is itself a global optimization problem (p. 7). At the end the authors ask "Can we dispense with AS3?" and replace AS1 and AS3 by AS4 on the set C(θ_max), noting what is lost (existence of a limit point) (p. 22).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) Area counting (Lemma 3.3, pp. 13-14): every pair added to the filter blocks a square of side γ_θ ϱ that stays blocked even if the entry is later removed (envelope never shrinks), so only finitely many such squares fit in the bounded part of the (θ, f) rectangle; hence θ → 0 along filter iterations. (ii) A chain of small-radius lemmas (3.4-3.9, pp. 14-18): θ(x_k + s_k) ≤ κ Δ_k² (Lemma 3.4, mean-value theorem), model decrease ≥ ½ κ_tmd ϱ Δ_k (Lemma 3.5), ρ_k ≥ η_2 (Lemma 3.6), the switching condition holds (Lemma 3.7), the trial point is acceptable with respect to x_k (Lemma 3.8), and TRQP is compatible (Lemma 3.9, by contradiction over two cases of iteration k-1). (iii) A two-case split on whether infinitely many iterates enter the filter (Lemma 3.10, pp. 18-19) or not (Lemmas 3.11-3.13, pp. 19-21), joined in Theorem 3.14 (pp. 21-22). (iv) Finite termination of restoration because feasible iterates never enter the filter (2.21), so θ_min over the filter is positive (pp. 10-11). (v) Criticality measured by θ together with χ_k, a convex linearised-stationarity measure, with limits taken through classical perturbation theory (Lemma 3.2, p. 13).
  - Transferable: to show a bi-criteria acceptance test cannot accept infinitely many bad points, give each accepted entry a fixed-size blocked box and count boxes in a bounded region (pp. 13-14).
  - Transferable: split the convergence proof on |Z| = ∞ versus |Z| < ∞ (infinitely or finitely many filter additions); in the second case the method behaves like an unconstrained trust-region method on a monotone objective (pp. 18-21).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a. The only numerical statement: "preliminary numerical experiments indicate that its practical performance is similar to that reported in [18]" (p. 22), with no data shown. Suggested constants are given with the caveat that "it is too early to know" whether they are good (p. 9).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on [18] (= S001), [19] and [20] (= S005), and on the composite-step and Cauchy-point literature (Conn, Gould, Sartenaer, Toint; Byrd, Nocedal and coauthors; Dennis, El-Alem, Maciel, Vicente) (pp. 4-6). The line-search filter of Wächter and Biegler is acknowledged in a footnote as a variant of these ideas (p. 3). The penalty-function approach is the foil but described neutrally (p. 3).
- **D7 Writing**: Classical SIAM theory style: algorithm, then a page of commentary on design choices (restoration radius, f-step vs θ-step, why feasible iterates never enter the filter, pp. 10-11), then lemmas, with dry asides ("as the reader has grown to expect", p. 15). Practical gaps are named inside the analysis: (2.15) "may be difficult to verify in practice" (p. 11); radius choice after restoration lacks experience (p. 10). The conclusion sells the theory as reassurance for an already good method (p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: first-order global convergence of a trust-region SQP-filter method with inexact subproblems (Theorem 3.14, pp. 21-22). Limits stated concretely: local convergence not discussed; because the SQP step can increase both f and the violation (Fletcher's Fig. 9.3.1 example), second-order corrections will "very likely" be needed against the Maratos effect; second-order criticality open; a modified ρ_k (3.15) may be needed (pp. 22-23). The theory assumes restoration succeeds (pp. 11-12).

**Method links**: Method 1 variant ⚠: the restriction removed is not a packaged-software restriction but an analysis-induced one (the global QP solve required by the earlier proof [20], p. 3); the move (ask whether the mathematics needs the restriction) is the same. Method 2 variant ⚠: here the home method (filter) imports outside machinery (composite steps, Cauchy points) with the owners of that machinery as coauthors (pp. 2, 4-6), rather than the home toolkit being carried into a new class.
