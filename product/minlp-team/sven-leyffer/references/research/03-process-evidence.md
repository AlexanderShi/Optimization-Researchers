## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Numbers before theory, or theory before code.** The filter method put experiments first: a 1997 report with numbers, then the first proof (NA/183) in 1998 (E66). The 2026 `conDFO` project produced a full convergence analysis in one day and lists the "Julia prototype" as a next step (E73). The MPEC work fits neither order cleanly: the numerical report NA/210 (August 2002) came three months after the theory report NA/209 (May 2002) and calls its numbers support for "recent theoretical developments", although the test library behind it had been growing since August 2000 (E52, E15).
2. **Fairness rules alongside self-favouring choices.** He writes fairness rules into papers (E01–E05). Yet FilMINT's defaults were tuned on the same suite used for the final comparison, and instances were graded by his own solver's times (E22). The 2020 Minotaur revision still compares against 2015-era BARON, Couenne and SCIP (E55). The thesis dropped the quadratic-OA GTD runs because they would "distort the results" (E23), while from 2005 failures are kept in the statistics as large numbers (E10).
3. **His own position on MPECs moved.** 2002: SQP methods "at present outperform Interior Point solvers", in the same report that expects interior-point codes can be repaired "with small modifications" (E52). 2004–06: interior methods developed with López-Calva and Nocedal (R14). 2005: "Solving MPCCs as NLPs is surprisingly robust." (R13, p. 25). 2007: "the NLP approaches are doomed to converge to spurious stationary points" without modification (E46).
4. **Uno's performance claim.** v1: "outperform most state-of-the-art solvers" and "a strong new contender". v2: "perform well with respect to all the state-of-the-art solvers" (E53). Both versions carry his name.
