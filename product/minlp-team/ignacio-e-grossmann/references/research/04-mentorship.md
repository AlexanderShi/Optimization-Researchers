## B. Layer 7: research organisation (supervision, collaboration, lab culture)

### B.1 How he himself was mentored (the model he inherited)

**Roger Sargent, Imperial College, 1974–77 [stated, caption; P]**

- **From MSc to PhD.** Sargent turned a planned one-year MSc into a PhD: "my own PhD adviser Professor Royer sarant encouraged me to you know continue to do a PhD" *(caption, 0:23:58; "Royer sarant" = Roger Sargent)*.
- **Contact with numerical optimisation.** Sargent took his student into the numerical-optimisation community: "when I was a student of his I was able to attend several meetings with people like Mike Powell Roger Fletcher" *(caption, 0:27:38)*.
- **The meeting rule he remembers.** Sargent, then department head, met students after 5 p.m., and "if you didn't have something very interesting to tell him he would almost start to fall asleep but when you had something exciting he would you know really be wake up and be very excited about it" *(caption, 0:29:12)*.
- **An earlier tribute.** The host reads out an earlier tribute by Grossmann that calls Sargent "a Visionary scholar with very high standards who believed in the power of mathematical optimization" *(caption, 0:25:34)*. The original source of that tribute was not identified. The two 2016 *AIChE J.* tributes he co-wrote (10.1002/aic.15423; 10.1002/aic.15425) are closed access and were **not read**.
- **The academic tree.** He stresses Sargent's academic tree: "a lot of the professors in the area Pro System engineering are somehow descendants of him" *(caption, 0:27:06)*.
- **Communication style.** He learned the Imperial way of presenting. At his 1979 CMU job talk "my first slide already had you know the formulation of with with equations of a nonlinear programming problem" *(caption, 0:39:41)*. He adds that faculty later told him this made them wonder whether he could communicate. In the UK one would "go straight to the point not using so much you know introduction and motivation" *(caption, 0:40:12)*.

**CMU's PSE group was seeded by Sargent students [institutional, S].** Art Westerberg, "former Ph.D. student of Roger Sargent", joined in 1976 and recruited Grossmann. Erik Ydstie, "former Ph.D. student of Roger Sargent", joined in 1992 (CAPD history pages).

**Egon Balas, CMU business school, integer programming [stated, caption; P]**

- **Sitting in on the course.** "after I joined car Millan I actually sat into his course on inter programming I still have his notes in my office" *(caption, 0:41:45)*.
- **Students sent to Balas.** He sent his students through Balas: "my students would actually take courses with him" *(caption, 0:43:20)*. Balas later asked for an adjunct appointment in chemical engineering because "he said look I've had all these wonderful students from chemical engineering in my course" *(caption, 0:43:20)*. He then taught a disjunctive-programming course there.
- **GDP from joint discussions.** Generalized disjunctive programming came out of three-way discussions: "with him we had some discussion with some of my PhD students where we were looking at so for example some reformulations of disjunctive programming problems" *(caption, 0:43:51)*. He also says the egon.cheme.cmu.edu host name honours Egon Balas (*caption, ≈0:44:22*, summarised because the ASR text is badly garbled).
- **Balas in the group's record [practice].** Balas sat on Trespalacios's (2015) and Bernal's (2021) committees. Bernal thanks him "for trusting me as his Teaching Assistant and book reviewer" (Bernal 2021, Acknowledgments p. i).

**[inferred] Three features of the inherited model reappear in his own group:**
1. **Place the student next to the mathematical-optimisation community.** Sargent sent him to Powell and Fletcher; he sends his students to the courses of Balas, Hooker and Cornuéjols and puts them on their committees.
2. **Judge a meeting by whether the student brings something exciting.** This is the Sargent anecdote. No student text describes his own meeting rule, so this part is unverified for his group.
3. **Build a lineage.** Sargent's tree has a counterpart in his "academic family tree with over 500 PhDs" (CEE 2019 profile, p. 3) and in his claim that CMU is "the major source of Faculty members in this area of process systems engineering" *(caption, 1:22:54)*.

## F. Tacit knowledge: what his students know that the papers do not say

Each item gives the evidence and a confidence level. All of it is secondary unless marked [stated].

1. **A thesis starts from a problem that someone in industry owns.** The company names the problem, the data, and often a co-supervisor. The student's job is to turn it into original, publishable method work. Evidence: 14 of 15 acknowledgements; CEE 2018 pp. 34–35; caption 1:14:04. *Confidence: high.*
2. **Build a well-posed deterministic model before adding uncertainty.** [stated, caption 1:05:08.] *Confidence: high as a stated rule; its practice was not checked chapter by chapter.*
3. **Model with logic (GDP) on a superstructure, so that unselected units drop out instead of making the equations singular.** This is the reason he gives for GDP in the interview *(caption, ≈0:58:57–1:02:32; summarised, the ASR text is long)*. Later students use Pyomo.GDP (Cho, p. ii). *Confidence: medium (stated rationale plus tool use).*
4. **Leave a usable artefact in a modelling system.** DICOPT in GAMS, LogMIP, Pyomo.GDP, web interfaces; the next student builds on it (§B.7). *Confidence: medium-high.*
5. **Learn the OR side from OR people.** Take Balas's, Hooker's or Cornuéjols's courses, and expect them on your committee (§B.1, §A.3). *Confidence: high for the 1980s–2010s; Balas died before Bernal's acknowledgement was written ("you will be greatly missed").*
6. **Expect to inherit and to hand over.** Projects, algorithms, sponsor relationships and even the thesis template pass from student to student (§B.5). *Confidence: high.*
7. **Senior students mentor juniors and visitors.** It is part of the job and is acknowledged as training to become an advisor (Zhang p. II). *Confidence: high.*
8. **Present to the sponsor's users, not only to reviewers.** Posters and final-year talks at the CAPD annual review; user evaluation of results (§B.4, §C.4). *Confidence: high.*
9. **Choose the venue by emphasis.** Application papers go to chemical-engineering journals, method papers to mathematical-programming journals (caption 0:54:44). *Confidence: medium (stated; practice not counted here).*
10. **Bring something exciting to the meeting.** This comes from Sargent's habit as Grossmann recounts it (caption 0:29:12). That he applies it to his own students is **not documented**. *Confidence: low; [inferred].*
11. **The working climate is long hours alongside personal care and patience.** The dean's flag about hours (caption 1:36:03) sits next to students' accounts of patience and care (§B.2). *Confidence: medium.*

2. **How many went into academia.** "twelve" (CEE 2019), 16 (his 2024 count), 13 roster lines (2026, by this agent's keyword count). The definitions and dates differ.
3. **When the CAPD was founded.**
   - "In 1985, we formed the Center…" (CEE 2018, which he co-wrote, p. 33).
   - "Since the CAPD's founding in 1985" (CEE 2019).
   - "CAPD started in 1986 as the Computer-Aided Process Design (CAPD) Consortium" (CAPD About page). The archive history says the consortium was "established in 1986" to obtain industrial matching funds.
   - The About-page timeline lists under 1984: "Art Westerberg becomes part of CAPD, starting with five companies".
4. **His directorship.** The 2015 entry says "Nick Sahinidis succeeds Ignacio Grossmann as director of the CAPD." The 2021 entry says "Carl Laird succeeds Ignacio Grossmann as director of the CAPD." (both CAPD About page). This implies a second Grossmann term, around 2020–2021, that no other source mentions. CEE 2018 and Alicante give 2005–2015.
5. **CAPD size.** "more than 70 graduate students and researchers" (CEE 2018) against "about 40 graduate students, postdocs and visitors" (CAPD pages, current).
6. **How long the OA paper took.** "three years" from submission to acceptance (caption 0:55:47) against Received 28 May 1984 and issue October 1986, that is 29 months to print (Springer). The acceptance date is not shown.
7. **Freedom or an industrial agenda?** Zhang reports "unprecedented academic freedom" and Drouven "my very own research path". Garcia-Herreros says the companies "have been instrumental to guide the direction of my research", and Perez calls Wassick's vision "the driver for my research". Both kinds of statement come from the same group and period.
8. **Demanding or patient?** A dean flagged his course for excessive student hours (caption 1:36:03, his own account). Four students describe him as notably patient, and he reports a student-voted teaching award for the same course (unverified).
9. **Roster against repository.** The group page lists Hubbs with no degree ("Christian Hubbs, 2021; RedSynth"); KiltHub records a PhD dissertation co-advised by Sahinidis and Grossmann. KiltHub misspells his name "Grossman" in three advisor fields.
10. **Stated values against what students observe.** His stated advice is persistence, passion and excellence. The most frequent traits in the students' accounts are care and ethics. This is a difference of emphasis, not a contradiction, and it is kept as such.
