### SW1. Outer approximation (Duran & Grossmann, *Math. Program.* 36(3):307–339, 1986; DOI 10.1007/BF02592064), with the 1983 position paper and the 1987 erratum

- **Origin** [stated, primary: DRC-06-59-83, 1983]. The algorithm grew out of a defense of optimization-based synthesis at a time when the field had turned away from it: "In the recent past it is the first two approaches that have received most of the attention due to apparent limitations and failures with optimization techniques." (PDF p. 4; the first two approaches are heuristics and thermodynamic targets). "It is the purpose of this paper to show that algorithmic methods can indeed play a very useful role in process synthesis." (PDF p. 4). His MILP superstructure models (Papoulias & Grossmann 1983) handled nonlinearity by discretization, and the report names that limit as the motive for MINLP: "it would be desirable to handle explicitly nonlinearities that are due to operating conditions such as pressures, temperature, reactor conversions and split fractions, rather than having to discretize these conditions at few selected points." (PDF p. 24).
- **Diagnosis of the existing methods** [stated, 1983]: branch and bound is expensive because "each node involves the solution of a large scale NLP" (PDF p. 24), and "The main drawback in Generalized Benders method is that the number of iterations that are required to find the optimal solution is usually large." (PDF p. 25).
- **Why then** [inferred, with stated support]: by 1983 MILP codes were reliable enough to serve as a master problem (the report: MILP codes "have the important property of being able to determine global optimum solutions"), and large-scale NLP codes (MINOS) were emerging. The co-authored 2025 history describes the earlier state: "At that time, solving even small convex NLPs was challenging and the capability to solve MILPs was limited, making MINLP more theoretical than practical for OR practitioners." (EJOR egon copy PDF p. 3; this refers to the early 1970s, when Balas and Geoffrion proposed their algorithms). The algorithm was presented at the TIMS/ORSA meeting in Chicago in 1983 (reference "Duran and Grossmann (1983a)" in the DRC report; not independently verified).
- **Key insight** [stated, 1983, PDF p. 25]: "the master problem is given by an MILP problem which performs outer-approximations on the feasible region of the superstructure by using primal rather than dual information from the NLP problem. Furthermore, this NLP is usually of much smaller size than problem (1) since with a fixed choice of the 0-1 variables many constraints and variables in the superstructure are trivially satisfied." The second sentence ties the algorithm to the superstructure: a fixed binary vector switches off whole units.
- **Minimum evidence** [stated, 1983, PDF p. 25]: "This has been confirmed in a test problem involving 8 binary variables, 9 continuous variables and 23 inequality constraints. The proposed algorithm required one-fifth of the CPU-time of the branch and bound method and one-half [OCR: one" half] of the CPU-time of Generalized Benders decomposition." Also a bound comparison with GBD: "Duran and Grossmann (1983b) have proved that the lower bound of their master problem is always greater or equal than the lower bound predicted by Benders method" (same page). [practice: the small network example returns in the 2002 review, see SW4]
- **Abandoned paths and corrections**:
  - The GBD-relation paper ("... II. The Relation with Generalized Benders Decomposition Method", DRC Report 06-68-84, 1984; own list no. 28) never became a journal paper; the bound property reappears inside his later reviews (2002, 2004). Reason not found. [practice, primary list; the reappearance is my reading]
  - Infeasible integer assignments: "In Duran and Grossmann (1986) infeasible integer assignments were excluded from (OA-master) by adding no-good cuts." Fletcher and Leyffer (1994) replaced this with a feasibility problem, and "The resulting gradient cuts are generally stronger, as the no-good cuts only exclude a single integer assignment." (2025 EJOR, egon copy PDF p. 6) [stated, primary]. Fletcher & Leyffer: *Math. Program.* 66(1–3):327–349, 1994, DOI 10.1007/BF01581153 (Crossref).
  - **Erratum** (*Math. Program.* 39(3):337, DOI 10.1007/BF02592081, read in full): "The solution that was reported in Table 4A with z* = 7.7891 is suboptimal." The corrected solution "was found by resolving Test Problem No. 4 on an IBM 3090/400 with 5 FPS Array Processors at the Cornell Theory Center, Ithaca, NY", with MPSX for the MILP master problems and MINOS for the NLPs (6 iterations, 14.6 s). So the correction came from re-solving with the Cornell toolchain that became DICOPT (SW2). [practice, primary]
- **Reception** [stated + observed]: the 2025 co-authored history calls it "A milestone for MINLP" and says "The algorithm is easy to grasp and implement, yet computationally efficient." (PDF p. 3); it is the conceptual basis of DICOPT, Bonmin B-OA, FilMINT (K. Abhishek, S. Leyffer, J. Linderoth, *INFORMS J. Comput.* 22(4):555–567, 2010, DOI 10.1287/ijoc.1090.0373) and the single-tree solvers SHOT and AOA (2019 review). Scholar rank 2 (2,246 citations). A colleague-written profile calls it "the first rigorous decomposition algorithm for solving MINLPs" (CEE 2019, PDF p. 2); see Contradictions. The same year he published the engineering use in *AIChE J.* (Duran & Grossmann 1986a, 1986b). [observed, secondary]
- **Method it shows** [inferred]: (1) take the application's structure (binaries linear and separable, superstructure units switched off by binaries) as the algorithm's assumption; (2) judge the new method against the two existing ones (BB, GBD) on one small engineering instance and on a bound comparison; (3) publish the algorithm in a mathematical journal and its engineering use in the engineering journal in the same year.

### SW2. Making OA work on nonconvex engineering models: equality relaxation, the two-phase strategy and the augmented penalty in DICOPT (Kocis & Grossmann 1987, 1988, 1989; Viswanathan & Grossmann, *Comput. Chem. Eng.* 14(7):769–782, 1990, DOI 10.1016/0098-1354(90)87085-4)

- **Origin** [stated, primary]. OA's guarantees need convexity and inequality constraints; flowsheet models are made of nonlinear equations. Equality relaxation (1986 EDRC report abstract, KiltHub 10.1184/R1/6467390.v1): the algorithm "has the important capability of being able to explicitly handle nonlinear equations within MINLP formulations", and through this explicit treatment it "avoids computational difficulties (e.g. singularities, destruction of sparsity) that are experienced with algebraic or numerical elimination schemes" [abstract only]. Two-phase strategy (1987 report abstract, KiltHub 10.1184/R1/6467033.v1): "in phase I nonconvexities that may cut off the global optimum are systematically identified with local and global tests. In phase II, a new master problem is proposed that attempts to locate the global optimum which may have been overlooked in phase I." [abstract only]
- **Why then** [stated, EDRC-06-43-88, January 1988]: the Cornell sabbatical (PDF p. 3, quoted in §1.1), GAMS, MINOS and MPSX; the report credits "advances in nonlinear program (NLP) solvers (e.g. MINOS ...) and mixed-integer linear program (MILP) solvers (e.g. MPSX ...)" for "the capability of solving MINLP problems quite efficiently" (PDF p. 4).
- **Key insight** [inferred from the stated texts]: keep the OA decomposition, and where convexity fails, accept heuristics that are openly declared as such: relax linearized equations by the sign of their multipliers (ER); test linearizations for validity (two-phase); later, put penalized slacks on the linearizations in the master (APER) and stop when the NLP subproblems stop improving.
- **Minimum evidence** [stated, 1988 report]: "Computer times on an IBM-3090/600 are typically of the order of only 2-100 seconds for problems with up to 60 0-1 variables and 410 variables and constraints." (PDF p. 5), over fifteen test problems (batch design, flowsheets, planning, flexibility, reliability). For nonconvex problems the two-phase strategy found "the global optimum ... in 24 out of 28 cases" (PDF p. 14), with an explicit limit: "However, no rigorous guarantee on global optimality can be given since no special structure on the functions has been assumed to guarantee validity of the modified outer-approximations and uniqueness of the solutions of the NLP subproblems." (PDF p. 14)
- **Abandoned path** [practice/inferred]: the two-phase convexity-test strategy was "recently implemented in DICOPT" in 1988 (PDF p. 14), but the 2004 retrospective describes DICOPT as "based on the master problem (M-APER) and the NLP subproblems (NLP2)", where "since bounding properties of (M-APER) cannot be guaranteed, the search for nonconvex problems is terminated when there is no further improvement in the feasible NLP subproblems. This is a heuristic that works reasonably well in many problems." (author copy PDF pp. 24–25). The convexity tests survive only as an alternative "modification" in the 2002 and 2004 reviews. I found no statement of why the two-phase strategy was dropped.
- **Reception** [observed, secondary]: the GAMS manual (maintained by GAMS) keeps the stopping heuristic as default: "stop as soon as the NLP subproblems start worsening ... This is also the default stopping criterion in the implementation of DICOPT", and warns "Although the algorithm has provisions to handle non-convexities, it does not necessarily obtain the global optimum." In the 2019 benchmark of 15 convex-MINLP solvers on 366 MINLPLib instances (Grossmann a co-author), "SHOT and AOA were the overall fastest solvers" (preprint PDF p. 30), while "The OA approach seems to be well suited for the problems with a low discrete density, where DICOPT is one of the most efficient solvers" (PDF p. 21). His group then improved DICOPT with a feasibility pump (2020). Viswanathan & Grossmann 1990 has 1,003 Scholar citations.
- **Lesson he drew about modeling** [stated, 1988, PDF p. 14]: "in the linearization of some nonlinear functions (e.g. bilinearities), terms may vanish when variables take zero values and as a result, substantially cut into the feasible region ... However, the modelling of MINLP problems still remains an area that is not very well understood. This is currently a subject of our research work". This is the seed of SW4.
- **Method it shows** [inferred]: build the algorithm into a modeling language early; test on a portfolio of the group's own application models; where theory gives no guarantee, ship a declared heuristic and say so in print ("no rigorous guarantee").

### SW3. LP/NLP-based branch and bound (Quesada & Grossmann, *Comput. Chem. Eng.* 16(10–11):937–947, 1992; DOI 10.1016/0098-1354(92)80028-8; EDRC report December 1991, KiltHub 10.1184/R1/6466757.v1)

- **Origin** [stated, primary: 1991 report]: the bottleneck came from the group's own models. "Examples include retrofit of heat exchanger networks (Yee and Grossmann, 1991) and synthesis of heat integrated columns (Floudas and Paules, 1988). In both of these applications the optimization of the 0-1 variables at the level of the master problem is at least one order of magnitude more expensive than the solution of the NLP subproblems." (PDF p. 6; the two references are cited there, not checked).
- **Key insight** [stated]: "The key feature in the algorithm is the dynamic generation of the linear approximations which are derived at integer solutions in the tree." (PDF p. 6). In the 2025 wording: "The core idea in LP/NLP-based BB is to solve only one MILP problem." (EJOR egon copy PDF p. 7).
- **Why then** [inferred]: in 1991 there were no MILP solver callbacks, so the report describes its own branch-and-bound (depth-first search, both branches examined, backtracking along the same path, PDF pp. 14–15). The 2025 history notes: "Today, many MILP solvers allow cut-callbacks, which makes implementing a single-tree OA straightforward." (PDF p. 7). The idea came some 15–20 years before the infrastructure that made it the default (Bonmin B-QG 2008, FilMINT 2010, SHOT and AOA by 2018).
- **Minimum evidence** [stated, 1991 report]: abstract: "Typically fewer than 50% of the total number of nodes need to be enumerated, while in most cases the number of NLP subproblems to be solved remains the same." (PDF p. 4); results: "the proposed method achieves reductions between 46% and 84% of the nodes that need to be enumerated" and "in problem ex4 80% of the total time in the OA algorithm is required to solve the MILP master problems" (PDF p. 23). Conclusion on scope: "The proposed method should prove to be most useful in cases where the MINLP problem involves large number of 0-1 variables and the NLP subproblems are relatively inexpensive to slove [sic]." (PDF p. 24).
- **Abandoned paths** [practice/inferred]: the report covers nonconvex problems with penalized linearizations and admits "Both criteria are not rigorous" (PDF p. 21); the report's title is "for MINLP optimization", the published title is "for convex MINLP optimization problems". I did not read the published version, so whether the nonconvex section was dropped is unconfirmed. The report also proposes "partial surrogate" constraints to keep the LPs small; I did not trace what became of them.
- **Reception** [stated + observed]: the 2004 retrospective: "Therefore, this method is better suited for problems in which the bottleneck corresponds to the solution of the MILP master problem. Leyffer (1993) has reported substantial savings with this method." (author copy PDF p. 22; Leyffer's thesis cited there). The 2019 solver review ties the best performers to this design ("Both of the solvers are based on a single tree approach similar to LP/NLP-BB", PDF p. 30). The 2025 history is more guarded: "A single-tree version of an OA-algorithm is typically faster than a "vanilla" multi-tree variant", but "With acceleration techniques, the computational result in Lundell et al. (2022) shows little performance difference between the multi- and single-tree variants." (PDF p. 7; Lundell et al. cited there, not checked). Scholar: 672 citations.
- **Method it shows** [inferred]: find where the computing time actually goes in your own application models and restructure the algorithm to move work away from that place; report node counts and NLP counts, not only CPU time.

### SW4. Generalized disjunctive programming (Raman & Grossmann, *Comput. Chem. Eng.* 18(7):563–578, 1994, DOI 10.1016/0098-1354(93)E0010-7), through Türkay & Grossmann 1996, Lee & Grossmann 2000 and Grossmann & Trespalacios 2013 to Pyomo.GDP (2022)

- **Origin** [stated, primary]: three statements across 25 years point the same way. 1988: modeling "still remains an area that is not very well understood" (SW2). 1994 (Floudas & Grossmann, EDRC 06-174-94): the report addresses "previous limitations that have been experienced with algorithmic methods in process synthesis: combinatorics and local optima." (PDF p. 3); logic went first into the MILP solver: "the symbolic integration of logic both in DNF and CNF form have been automated in a special version of OSL, the MILP solver from IBM (Raman and Grossmann, 1993a)" (PDF p. 22). 2013: "deriving "good" formulations or finding potential improvements in existing models is commonly regarded as an art and strongly depends on the modeler skills", and GDP "originated with the goal of facilitating the modeling of discrete/continuous optimization problems through the use of higher level logic constructs" (author copy PDF pp. 1–2).
- **Why then** [inferred, with practice evidence]: CMU in the late 1980s and early 1990s combined AI-and-design work at the EDRC (A. Westerberg, I. E. Grossmann et al., "Applications of AI in design research at Carnegie Mellon University's EDRC", *Artif. Intell. Eng.* 5(2):110–124, 1990, DOI 10.1016/0954-1810(90)90005-O, DBLP), logic-based OR (John Hooker; the 1994 "Logic cuts" paper), and access to IBM's OSL. Balas's disjunctive programming supplied the theory; the 2025 history notes that Balas's convex-hull result "appeared in a technical report in 1974 but was not published until 1998" (PDF p. 3).
- **Key insight** [stated, 2013 and 2025]: write the model with Boolean variables, disjunctions and logic propositions, then derive the MILP/MINLP (big-M or hull) systematically, or solve the disjunctive form directly with logic-based methods. A practical reason for logic-based OA is given in 2025: when a disjunct means a unit is not selected, "some flows will then be forced to zero. This can cause numerical instability, as the functions may not be intended to be used with zero flow." (EJOR egon copy PDF p. 13). This matches the 1988 remark about terms vanishing at zero values. [stated]
- **Minimum evidence** [stated, 2002 review, author copy]: the same 8-process network that Duran & Grossmann formulated as an MINLP in 1986, re-solved as a GDP: "the branch and bound (BB) algorithm by Lee and Grossmann (2000) finds the optimal solution in only 5 nodes compared with 17 nodes of standard branch and bound method when applied to the MINLP formulation with big-M constraints", with a root bound of 62.48 against 15.08 (PDF p. 26); "As predicted by the theory, the logic-based OA method yields the lower bound 8.541, which is stronger than the one of the GBD method", 3 major iterations against 8 for GBD and 7 for ECP (PDF p. 26). His conclusion: "The numerical results of the small example have confirmed the theoretical properties that were discussed in the paper." (PDF p. 27). The text introduces the example as formulated "later by Türkay and Grossmann (1986) as a GDP problem" (PDF p. 23); the Türkay paper is from 1996.
- **Abandoned paths and limits** [stated/practice]: the first logic-based OA was narrow: Lee & Grossmann write that Türkay & Grossmann's method "can handle only disjunctions with two terms and is restricted to process networks" (author copy PDF p. 3), and their convex-hull relaxation generalized it. An erratum to Lee & Grossmann 2000 followed in 2001 (not read). The 2013 paper concedes that dedicated GDP algorithms are not the usual route: "GDPs are normally directly reformulated as MILP/MINLP to exploit the developments in these solvers" (author copy PDF p. 2). The group kept working on both routes (reformulations: Trespalacios 2015; special algorithms: logic-based discrete steepest descent, 2025).
- **Reception** [observed + practice]: Scholar 750 citations (Raman & Grossmann 1994); 406 (Türkay & Grossmann 1996); 395 (Lee & Grossmann 2000). The 2025 invited EJOR review frames the whole field as MINLP plus GDP. Implementations: LOGMIP (GAMS), Pyomo.GDP (*Optim. Eng.* 2022), DisjunctiveProgramming.jl (JuliaCon 2023). Conic extensions: Bernal Neira & Grossmann (COAP 2024).
- **Method it shows** [inferred]: modeling first: build a representation that keeps the logic, derive formulations and relaxations from it, and confirm every new method on the same small canonical example before moving to applications.

### SW5. Enterprise-wide optimization (Grossmann, *AIChE J.* 51(7):1846–1857, 2005; DOI 10.1002/aic.10617), the turning point in topic

- **Origin** [stated + observed]: abstract: "Enterprise-wide optimization (EWO) is a new emerging area that lies at the interface of chemical engineering and operations research, and has become a major goal in the process industries due to the increasing pressures for remaining competitive in the global marketplace." (journal p. 1846). The colleague profile places the start in CAPD's industrial program: the EWO interest group "came from the realization that process systems engineering must be applied at larger time and length scales, with logistics and planning across sites throughout the company." (CEE 2019, PDF p. 4) [observed]
- **Why then** [stated]: MILP solvers had become fast ("it is now possible ... to solve in a few seconds MILP instances that were unsolvable just five years ago"), "In contrast, for the type of problems that arise in process industries, the available LP-based branch-and-bound software is not always capable of solving industrial-size MILP models." (journal p. 1850). The process models he had built for synthesis (nonlinear, disjunctive) were what planning and scheduling at the enterprise scale lacked.
- **Key insight** [stated]: "We should note that collaboration between researchers in process systems engineering and operations research should be most fruitful in this area." (p. 1850); and "it is clear that chemical engineers can play a major role not only in the modeling part, but also in the algorithmic part given the strong and rich tradition that chemical engineers have built in mathematical programming." (p. 1854). GDP is named as a modeling tool for EWO (p. 1851).
- **Minimum evidence**: a perspective, not a result. It argues from industrial cases (e.g., a BASF supply-chain project reported with 10% cost reduction, p. 1846–1847) and from the group's planning and scheduling models. [practice]
- **Abandoned paths** [not traced]: the paper proposes computational grids (the MW master-worker library) for EWO; I did not check whether the group followed this up.
- **Reception** [practice + observed]: 728 Scholar citations; the 2012 follow-up review (*Comput. Chem. Eng.* 47:2–18); the 2009 Springer chapter with Furman; special issues on EWO (*Comput. Chem. Eng.* 32(11), 2008; *Optim. Eng.*, 2019, own list "Other" nos. 46 and 70). Planning, supply chain and energy titles go from 6% of his titles (1985–89) to 22–25% (2000–2024) and over 40% in 2025–26 (§1.2).
- **Method it shows** [inferred]: open a research program with a field-defining perspective, then staff it with industrial consortium problems and a stream of students; keep MILP/MINLP/GDP as the common tool while the application scale grows (unit, flowsheet, plant, enterprise).

## 4. Cross-cutting patterns (candidates for Phase 2; all [inferred] from the evidence above)

1. **Algorithm from the application's bottleneck.** Each core method answers a defect seen in the group's own models: discretized operating conditions (OA, 1983), nonlinear equations and nonconvexities in flowsheets (ER/APER, 1987–90), an expensive MILP master in heat-exchanger-network retrofit (LP/NLP-BB, 1991), weak formulations and zero-flow instabilities (GDP, logic-based OA, 1994–2000).
2. **One small canonical example, reused for decades.** The 8-binary process network appears in the 1983 report, the 1986 OA paper and the 1996 and 2002 GDP work; the 2013 GDP paper motivates with a two-product toy problem. Theory is "confirmed" on the small example; scale comes from the application papers.
3. **Unified reviews as a research instrument.** He writes a major review or tutorial on MINLP/GDP roughly every five years (1990, 1995, 2002, 2004, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2019, 2025), each deriving all methods from the OA subproblems ("the different methods can be derived from three basic NLP subproblems and from one cutting plane MILP problem, which essentially correspond to the basic subproblems of the Outer-Approximation method", 2002 review, author copy PDF p. 2). Several are among his most cited works, and in 2026 he received the INFORMS Saul Gass Award for Expository Writing.
4. **Rigor where the structure allows it, declared heuristics where it does not.** Convex case: proofs and bounds. Nonconvex case: penalties and stopping rules labeled as heuristics in print, with a separate line of rigorous global optimization for special structures (bilinear, concave, linear fractional: 1995–2017).
5. **Software in the modeling language of the day.** GAMS (DICOPT 1986–90, LOGMIP 1997–99), COIN-OR (Bonmin 2008), Pyomo (2018–22), Julia/JuMP (2023).
6. **Engineering venue first.** Core MINLP algorithms were published in *Comput. Chem. Eng.*; the OR and mathematics venues came later, often with co-authors from those communities.
7. **Student-as-step lineage.** Duran → Kocis → Viswanathan (associate) → Quesada → Raman → Türkay → Lee → Sawaya → Ruiz → Trespalacios → Chen and Bernal → Pérez and Ovalle: each thesis extends the previous OA or GDP step, and older students return as co-authors (Duran 2021).
8. **Scale ladder.** Unit (1970s) → flowsheet and heat integration (1980s) → batch plant scheduling (1990s–2000s) → enterprise and supply chain (2005–) → energy systems and decarbonization (2010s–2020s), with MILP/MINLP/GDP as the constant tool.

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **"First" claims vs the history he co-wrote.** The 2019 colleague profile says OA "was the first rigorous decomposition algorithm for solving MINLPs" and that DICOPT was "the first computer code for solving MINLP problems" (CEE 2019, PDF p. 2). The 2025 history co-authored by Grossmann places two earlier MINLP algorithms "based on concepts similar to Bender's decomposition" in the early 1970s: Balas (*Linear Algebra Appl.* 4(4):341–352, 1971, DOI 10.1016/0024-3795(71)90005-X) and Geoffrion's generalized Benders decomposition (*J. Optim. Theory Appl.* 10(4):237–260, 1972, DOI 10.1007/BF00934810); his own 1983 report treats GBD as an existing MINLP method. "First code" was not checked.
2. **Who developed DICOPT.** The 1988 report presents DICOPT as Kocis and Grossmann's implementation, written at Cornell in 1986–87; the GAMS manual says DICOPT "was originally developed by Jagadisan Viswanathan, Ignacio E. Grossmann, and Aldo Vecchietti at the Engineering Design Research Center (EDRC) at Carnegie Mellon University" and does not name Kocis.
3. **Erratum date.** Crossref and DBLP date the OA erratum to *Math. Program.* 39(3), 1987; the printed running head of the page reads "Mathematical Programming 39 (1989) 337".
4. **Single-tree advantage.** 2019 review: the fastest solvers are single-tree, which "support the strong belief in the single approach [sic]" (PDF pp. 30–31). 2025 history: single-tree "is typically faster than a "vanilla" multi-tree variant", but with acceleration techniques Lundell et al. (2022) show "little performance difference" (PDF p. 7).
5. **Scope of LP/NLP-BB.** The 1991 report title ("for MINLP optimization") and text include a nonconvex extension; the 1992 paper title restricts it to "convex MINLP optimization problems".
6. **Counts.** Papers: >500 (2017), >600 (2019), >700 (2026), 785 + 75 items (own list), 945 works (OpenAlex), 246 (DBLP). Students: 56 PhD + 11 MS (2017), "over sixty Ph.D. and ten MS" (2019), 68 + 34 (2026), 66 + 37 entries on his alumni page, 8 in MGP. The 2019 "ten MS" is below the 20 or so MS graduates up to 2019 on his own alumni page. Citations: Scholar 80,981, OpenAlex 55,129, Semantic Scholar 54,058 (main ID only); per paper, OA 1986 has 2,246 / 1,458 / 1,244 (Scholar / OpenAlex / Crossref).
7. **Book dates.** Scholar dates *Global Optimization in Engineering Design* 2013; Crossref and his own list give 1996 (Kluwer).
8. **Citation error inside the 2002 review**: the GDP formulation of the example is attributed to "Türkay and Grossmann (1986)"; the paper is from 1996.
9. **His own list vs the publication record**: entries marked "submitted" in July 2025 that were published in 2009 and 2012 (§3).
