### S061 · Correction to: Exact semidefinite formulations for a class of (random and non-random) nonconvex quadratic programs (Mathematical Programming 2021, 190:845–848, DOI 10.1007/s10107-021-01684-5)
**Read level**: full (file pp. 1-4: the whole note, including the one-item reference list and the publisher's note). The title on p. 1 matches, and the authors are Samuel Burer and Yinyu Ye (p. 1). This replaces the c07 card (wrong text) and the a2-01 card (metadata only).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Yinyu Ye (Stanford, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: erratum to S019 (Math. Program. 181:1–17, 2020; correction header p. 1). It withdraws Theorem 3 of S019, the high-probability exactness result for random QCQPs, and proves a narrower replacement for diagonal instances only (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is an external report: "an error in Theorem 3 of [1], which was pointed about by Prof. Fatma Kılınç-Karzan and Mr. Alex Wang of Carnegie Mellon University" (p. 1). The note first locates the error in the proof's analysis of system (12), where diagonal data {C, Bᵢ, Dᵢ} with C, Bᵢ ⪰ 0 (glyph reconstructed) and Dᵢ indefinite are used. Several variants of (12) are analysed, each with one free scalar in {X, Yᵢ} and the rest nonnegative, and the proof needs all of them to be feasible simultaneously with high probability (p. 1). The diagnosis is stronger than "there is a gap": "the systems cannot be simultaneously feasible", so the probability is zero (p. 1).
  - Transferable technique: when a proof by "with high probability all these systems hold" is challenged, test whether the systems can hold together at all for any data. One deterministic infeasibility argument kills the whole probabilistic route (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: (a) Refutation, pp. 1-2: assume all systems are feasible. Fix i and the minimal ω > 0 with Dᵢ + ωBᵢ ⪰ 0, so some diagonal entry [Dᵢ + ωBᵢ]ⱼⱼ = 0. In the system where [Yᵢ]ⱼⱼ is the free variable, that variable has coefficient zero, so a positive combination of nonnegative variables would have to be ≤ −ω < 0, a contradiction. (b) Replacement, pp. 2-3: for diagonal QCQPs (1), each index j is mapped to two points of R^{1+m}, fⱼ = (Cⱼⱼ, [A₁]ⱼⱼ, …, [Aₘ]ⱼⱼ) and dⱼ = (cⱼ, a₁ⱼ, …, aₘⱼ). The epigraph form (‡) makes the separable structure visible (p. 2). With Kⱼ = cone{fₖ − fⱼ : k ≠ j} (the cone of feasible directions at fⱼ in the polytope Q = conv{f₁, …, fₙ}) and Lⱼ = the line spanned by dⱼ, the Lemma says: if int(Kⱼ) ∩ Lⱼ ≠ ∅ for all j (≠ reconstructed), then r* = 1, i.e. the Shor SDP is exact (p. 2). The random Theorem draws fⱼ/‖fⱼ‖ uniformly on the sphere and dⱼ at an angle with fⱼ outside π/2 ± θ, and concludes Prob(r* = 1) → 1 as n → ∞ for fixed m and θ (p. 3).
  - Transferable technique: for a separable QCQP, encode each variable's quadratic and linear coefficients across all constraints as one point fⱼ and one direction dⱼ in R^{1+m}. A system of LP feasibility conditions then becomes a cone-meets-line condition at every vertex of a polytope (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The model is the diagonal QCQP (1) of S019 under its Assumptions 1-3 (p. 2: "Assumptions 1–2, which guarantee that the feasible set of (1) is bounded"; p. 3: b is chosen so that Assumptions 1 and 3 hold). A redundant ball xᵀx ≤ ρ² is added without knowing ρ explicitly (p. 2; and in the Theorem "for some finite ρ> 0", p. 3). The random model is new compared with S019: m is fixed while n → ∞ (S019's Theorem 3 allowed m ≤ n^q, per the c06 card for S019), normalized coefficient points are uniform on the unit sphere, and an angle condition is placed on dⱼ (p. 3). The Remark says i.i.d. standard Gaussian diagonal entries give the uniform distribution, and the angle condition can be met by accept-reject sampling (p. 4). Scope is narrowed explicitly: "it only applies to diagonal instances of (1)" (p. 2).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (i) Proof of falsity by a zero-coefficient extreme: choose ω minimal so that one diagonal entry of Dᵢ + ωBᵢ vanishes, then look at the system where exactly that variable is free (pp. 1-2). (ii) Restrict to a sufficient subsystem: S019's system (5) is feasible if a "more restrictive system" with diagonal X is feasible. Eliminate the free Xⱼⱼ through I • X = 0 to get Σₖ≠ⱼ Xₖₖ(fₖ − fⱼ) + xⱼdⱼ < 0 (p. 2). (iii) Translate to geometry: that system is feasible iff Kⱼ ∩ (Lⱼ + int(R^{1+m}₋)) ≠ ∅, and int(Lⱼ + R^{1+m}₋) = Lⱼ + int(R^{1+m}₋) because Lⱼ is a line (p. 3). (iv) A full-dimensionality and interior argument: int(Kⱼ) ∩ Lⱼ ≠ ∅ makes Kⱼ full-dimensional, so Kⱼ meets the interior of the full-dimensional polyhedron Lⱼ + R^{1+m}₋ (p. 3). (v) Scale normalization: substitute xⱼ = x̂ⱼ/‖fⱼ‖, because "the tightness of the Shor SDP relaxation is not affected by simple positive variable scalings", which allows ‖fⱼ‖ = 1 without loss of generality (p. 3). (vi) An asymptotic geometric limit: as n → ∞, Q approaches the unit ball and each Kⱼ approaches the halfspace {v : fⱼᵀv ≤ 0}, so the angle condition makes Lⱼ meet int(Kⱼ) with probability → 1 (p. 3). The last step is stated qualitatively. My reading, not the authors': it gives no rate in n and no explicit union bound over the n indices.
  - Transferable technique: before a probabilistic argument, use a scaling invariance of the property being proved to normalize the random data onto a sphere. Then the event becomes a statement about a polytope inscribed in the ball (p. 3).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The note has no numerical check of the replacement theorem. The Remark on Gaussian sampling and accept-reject (p. 4) is a recipe for generating instances, not a reported experiment.

The geometry gets one interpretive sentence per object ("K j is the cone of feasible directions at f j in the polytope Q ... L j is the line spanned by the directions ±dj", p. 2). The note does not say whether the arXiv version (1802.02688) was updated. That is not checked here.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is corrective theory. It withdraws S019's Theorem 3 (a general random-QCQP exactness claim), shows that its proof strategy cannot be repaired (pp. 1-2), and adds a new deterministic sufficient condition (the Lemma, p. 2) plus a random diagonal-QCQP exactness theorem (p. 3). Limits visible in the note: only diagonal instances; m held fixed; an angle condition on the linear terms (dⱼ must stay away from orthogonal to fⱼ, p. 3); a qualitative limit argument (p. 3). The note does not say whether S019's general-QCQP random claim is false or merely unproved. It replaces the theorem without settling that, and gives no future-work statement.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Disprove a "with high probability all systems feasible" step by choosing the extreme multiplier (minimal ω with Dᵢ + ωBᵢ ⪰ 0) that zeroes one coefficient. Then show that the system in which that variable is the free one reduces to a nonnegative combination required to be negative (pp. 1-2).
- Turn n LP feasibility systems into geometry: with fⱼ, dⱼ from the separable epigraph form, feasibility of system j ⇔ Kⱼ ∩ (Lⱼ + int(R₋)) ≠ ∅, and a clean sufficient condition is int(Kⱼ) ∩ Lⱼ ≠ ∅ (pp. 2-3).
- Normalize random data by an invariance of the property (positive variable scaling leaves Shor tightness unchanged), so the coefficient points lie on the unit sphere and asymptotics reduce to the polytope approaching the ball (p. 3).
- In a correction note, state the error in the first sentence, credit the finders in the same paragraph, prove that the old route is not repairable, and announce the narrowing ("except that it only applies to diagonal instances") before stating the replacement (pp. 1-2).
