### S049 · A slightly lifted convex relaxation for nonconvex quadratic programming with ball constraints (Mathematical Programming 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10107-024-02076-1; arXiv 2303.01624)
**Read level**: full. All of the arXiv v2 text, pp. 1-26, was read, including the Section 5 proof and the references. This is not the published version. Wording that differs from the v1 quotes in SKILL.md is noted under D5 and D7.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author); Kurt Anstreicher is thanked for "an important observation" that led to Theorem 1 (p. 24) · **Place in trajectory**: continues the TTRS/extended-TRS line, reusing his 2013 TTRS set (Burer & Anstreicher, ref [9]) and the 2023 Eltved & Burer instances (ref [17]) (pp. 15, 20). The proof borrows a lemma from his 2015 copositive survey (ref [8], p. 22).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The problem is a nonconvex quadratic over the intersection of m balls. It is polynomial-time solvable for fixed m (ref [6]). Shor is exact for m = 1, and Kelly et al. obtain exactness for m = 2 only with a disjunctive SDP that uses two PSD variables. "there is no known explicit, tractable, exact convex representation for m ≥ 3" (abstract, p. 1; pp. 2, 8). The entry point is to avoid both data-dependent exactness conditions and enumeration. The diagonal-QCQP literature gives sign conditions under which Shor is exact, which he sets aside (p. 2). Global B&B approaches are left aside for "a single strong bound" (p. 2). He states the goals as (i) a non-disjunctive, polynomially sized SDP with one PSD variable, (ii) exactness for m = 2, and (iii) empirical strength for m ≥ 3 (p. 2). He says the original hope was exactness for all m, and he reports how that hope ended (p. 3).
  - Transferable: when an exact result exists only in disjunctive form (one copy per piece), set yourself the goal of matching it with a single non-disjunctive object of polynomial size, and state that goal in the introduction.
- **D2 Core idea**: Rewrite each ball as xᵀx ≤ (linear in x), then insert one artificial scalar β so that xᵀx ≤ β ≤ ρᵢ² − cᵢᵀcᵢ + 2cᵢᵀx. This "exchang[es] m − 1 cone constraints in the original problem with m linear constraints" (p. 3; p. 11). After homogenizing with α, the set K has one rotated SOC and m linear constraints (p. 12). The standard toolkit Shor ∩ RLT ∩ SOCRLT then applies in dimension n+2. Kron is no longer needed because only one SOC remains, and the relaxation is called "Beta" (pp. 12-13). For m = 2, the complementarity (ℓ₁ᵀw)(ℓ₂ᵀw) = 0 pins β to the minimum of the two linear functions, and its linearization ℓ₁ᵀWℓ₂ = 0 gives the exact set R′ (pp. 13-14, Theorem 1). Two extensions follow. The first is ∥x∥ ≤ min{1, g + hᵀx}, exact by the same proof (Theorem 2, p. 18). The second is TTRS: diagonalize H₂, insert n artificial βⱼ ≥ xⱼ², then add Kron among the n small SOC₃ blocks (pp. 18-19). What is unchanged is the relaxation machinery (Shor, RLT, SOCRLT, Kron) from §2.1 (pp. 5-7). What changes is the formulation it is applied to.
  - Transferable: before adding cuts, reformulate. If several nonlinear constraints share a nonlinear term, sandwich one auxiliary variable between that term and the linear parts, so that the structure becomes one cone plus linear constraints. Then apply standard RLT/SOCRLT to the new formulation.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Q symmetric and not necessarily PSD. The balls have ρᵢ > 0, and without loss of generality c₁ = 0, ρ₁ = 1. (QP) is assumed feasible (p. 2). The generic §2 cone K is assumed nonempty and bounded at x₁ = 1 and to imply x₁ ≥ 0 (p. 5). For TTRS, the Hᵢ have full rank and changes of variables make H₁ = I and H₂ diagonal (p. 19). Exactness is claimed only for m = 2 and for the min{1, g+hᵀx} set (Theorems 1-2). For m ≥ 3 the claim is empirical strength only (pp. 3, 16).
- **D4 Proof devices**: The aim is to show that every extreme ray of the relaxation has rank one (Section 5, pp. 20-24), "our proof follows a technique from [8]" (Burer 2015). (i) Lemma 1: J maps SOC to SOC, and vᵀJv = 0 on the boundary (p. 20). (ii) Lemma 2: the Shor-type set has rank-one extreme rays by Pataki's rank bound (p. 21). (iii) Lemma 3 splits into three cases by where PᵀWℓᵢ lies (interior, boundary with Wℓᵢ = 0, boundary with Wℓᵢ ≠ 0). In the last case a rank-one perturbation W − εvvᵀ with v ∈ Range(W) stays in the set (citing Burer-Anstreicher-Dür, ref [10] Lemma 2), so the extreme ray W must be a multiple of vvᵀ (pp. 21-22). (iv) Lemma 4: an extreme ray of a cone intersected with a half-space is either extreme in the cone or a sum of two extreme rays (ref [8] Lemma 5, p. 22). (v) Main proof: the case vᵢ = 0 reduces to Lemma 3. In the interior/equality case rank(W) ≤ 2, and a bordered matrix U shows Range(W) = span{v₁, v₂}, which contradicts extremality (pp. 22-23). In the boundary case at least one of the two perturbations W − εᵢvᵢvᵢᵀ is feasible (pp. 23-24). The paper notes a "closely related result" in Ye-Zhang Theorem 2.7 (p. 20).
  - Transferable: to prove that an SDP relaxation equals a conic hull, prove that every extreme ray has rank one. Split cases by whether each linearized constraint is tight, and in the tight case subtract εvvᵀ with v = Wℓ; this works because v ∈ Range(W) keeps the matrix PSD.
- **D5 Experiments**: Python with the MOSEK 10.0.37 Fusion API on an M2 MacBook Air. Code, scripts and results are on GitHub (sburer/ballconstraints) (p. 4). "Solved" requires a relative gap < 10⁻⁴ and an eigenvalue ratio > 10⁴, with the second signal "to bolster our confidence" (p. 10). The gap is measured against the solution embedded in X* (p. 10). Example 1, (n, m) = (2, 2), gives Shor −0.5876, Kron −0.5487 and Beta exact at −0.54. A figure of lower envelopes over x₂ shows Beta giving the convex envelope (pp. 13-14). For m = 2 he uses 1,000 "Martinez" instances per (n, m) ∈ {(2,2), (4,2), (6,2)}, built with the 2013 generator: solve m = 1, then add a ball that cuts off the optimum. Instances "already solved by Shor" are excluded (p. 15). Kron solves 955/884/882 and Beta all 1,000, at close to Shor's time (Table 1, p. 15). Beta solves all 267 instances that Eltved & Burer left unsolved (p. 15). For m ≥ 3 he uses max-norm instances (Q = −I, NP-hard), again excluding Shor-solved ones (p. 16). Beta solves 977/973/908 against Kron's 208/412/2 (Table 2, p. 17). Gap closure is broken down by joint solve status (Table 3, p. 17): 142 instances are unsolved by both methods, with Beta closing 29% on average, and no instance is solved by Kron but not by Beta. Timing scales from (2,2) to (64,64), with 40.6 s at the largest size (Table 4, p. 17). On TTRS, R′ solves all 212 of the 2013 instances that Shor ∩ RLT ∩ SOCRLT leaves unsolved, against 77 for Anstreicher's Kron, 29 for Burer-Yang and 211 for Consolini-Locatelli, "in 0.374 seconds on average" for n = 20 (p. 20). A Zhen et al. constraint was tried and "did not provide added strength", which is reported (p. 9). Version note: v2 says "we specifically excluded ones that were already solved by Shor" (p. 15) and "we also require a large eigenvalue ratio" (p. 10). The SKILL.md quotes from v1 use different wording. The number of seeds generated before filtering is not reported. Table 1's caption speaks of "3,000 randomly generated Martinez instances, which were not already solved by Shor", while the text says 1,000 were generated per size and then filtered (p. 15).
  - Transferable: break gap closure down by joint solve status (both unsolved / only new solves / only old solves / both solve) so that a "never worse" observation can be read off directly (Table 3, p. 17).
  - Transferable: before the large-scale tables, show a 2-D example and plot the projected lower envelope of each relaxation against the true objective (Figure 1, pp. 13-14).
- **D6 Positioning**: Kelly et al. are presented as the m = 2 benchmark, and the contrast is drawn without criticism: disjunctive, two PSD variables (pp. 2, 8). Eltved & Burer is "more similar to the current paper" (p. 8) and supplies the hard m = 2 set (p. 15). Anstreicher's Kron is credited as "a valuable tool" even though "we are unaware of any case in which enforcing Kron makes a relaxation exact for all objectives" (p. 8). The exactness results he builds on are cited in their own lifted form: Sturm-Zhang Shor ∩ SOCRLT (p. 8), Burer-Yang non-intersecting cuts (p. 8), and Ye-Zhang for Theorem 1 (p. 20). Consolini & Locatelli's 211/212 result is reported as the prior best on TTRS (p. 20).
- **D7 Writing**: The goals are enumerated (i)-(iii) in the introduction (p. 2). The paper is candid about how the project started ("As we began this project, we were motivated by the idea of constructing an exact relaxation for (QP) for all m", p. 3). An intuition paragraph comes before the formalism (p. 3). He gives an explicit caution about overloaded notation (p. 5) and moves the main proof to the last section (pp. 3-4, 20). Unproved regularities are marked as empirical (p. 16). The conclusions list four open questions (p. 24), and the acknowledgment credits Anstreicher's observation precisely (p. 24). One claim is stated more strongly than the evidence: p. 3 says "we will show that our relaxation is exact for m = 2 but inexact for m ≥ 3", but §3.4 shows only numerically unsolved instances (Tables 2-3, pp. 16-17) and gives no certified counterexample.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: The contributions are a polynomially sized non-disjunctive SDP for all m, exact for m = 2 (Theorem 1) and for ∥x∥ ≤ min{1, g+hᵀx} (Theorem 2), with strong and fast empirical results for m ≥ 3 and a TTRS relaxation that solves all 212 benchmark instances (abstract; pp. 17, 20). Limits are stated concretely: the relaxation is inexact for m ≥ 3, there is no proof that Beta ⊇ Kron, there is no comparison proof against Eltved & Burer, and the relaxation has not been embedded in a B&B (pp. 2, 16, 24). The 0.374 s TTRS timing is printed here without qualification (p. 20).

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅: the "slight lift" to n+1 (n+2 after homogenization) puts the m = 2 problem exactly into one PSD variable with linear and SOC constraints (Theorem 1, pp. 11-14). Method 2 evidence ✅: §2.1 builds the kitchen sink Shor ∩ RLT ∩ SOCRLT ∩ Kron explicitly (pp. 5-7) and tries one more ingredient, Zhen et al., which added nothing (p. 9). The result is an explicit non-disjunctive description, exact at m = 2 and not beyond (pp. 2-3), against a disjunctive prior (Kelly et al.). Method 3 evidence ✅: the two-signal "solved" rule (p. 10), and "suggests empirically ... we do not have a formal proof" for Beta ⊇ Kron (p. 16). Method 3 variant ⚠: inexactness for m ≥ 3 is announced as something "we will show" (p. 3) but rests on numerical "unsolved" counts (pp. 16-17), not on a certificate. Method 4 evidence ✅: the Martinez generator with Shor-solved instances excluded (pp. 15-16), and the ladder kept by reusing the 267 Eltved-Burer and 212 TTRS hard instances (pp. 15, 20). Method 4 variant ⚠: the filter is disclosed but the scan count is not, and the 1,000 vs 3,000 counts on p. 15 read inconsistently. New-pattern candidates: "artificial-variable sandwich" (pp. 3, 11, 18-19); "rank-one extreme-ray proof by rank-one perturbation" (pp. 21-24).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Sandwich an artificial scalar β between a shared quadratic term and the linear parts of several constraints. This turns m cone constraints into one cone plus m linear constraints, so RLT/SOCRLT apply (pp. 3, 11-12).
- Fix the extra freedom of β with a complementarity product (ℓ₁ᵀw)(ℓ₂ᵀw) = 0 and linearize it as ℓ₁ᵀWℓ₂ = 0 to get exactness (pp. 13-14).
- Prove hull equality by showing that every extreme ray has rank one, using case splits and an εvvᵀ perturbation with v = Wℓᵢ (pp. 21-24).
- Report gap closure by joint solve status, so that "no instance solved by the old method but not by the new" can be read straight from the table (Table 3, p. 17).
**Quotes**:
- "In this paper, however, we seek a relaxation that is strong irrespective of the data." (p. 2)
- "This suggests empirically that Beta is always at least as strong as Kron, although we do not have a formal proof establishing this." (p. 16)

### S048 · A semidefinite relaxation for sums of heterogeneous quadratic forms on the Stiefel manifold (SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications 2025, DOI 10.1137/23m1545136; arXiv 2205.13653)
**Read level**: full for the main text (pp. 1-15) and Appendices A-B (pp. 15-20). Appendix C was read for its statements and assumption checks (pp. 20-23); the proof of Lemma C.4 was skimmed (pp. 23-24), and the main reference list (pp. 24-27) was skimmed. Supplement Appendix D (pp. 27-28), E.2-E.3 (pp. 30-31), G (pp. 38-43) and H (pp. 43-45) were read. Appendix E.1 (dual derivation, pp. 28-30) and Appendix F (proofs of Lemmas C.1-C.2 and supporting lemmas, pp. 31-38) were skimmed for statements only. The supplement reference list (pp. 45-47) was skimmed. The text is arXiv v3.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Kyle Gilman and Laura Balzano (University of Michigan, Electrical and Computer Engineering, p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: an application of the SDP-relaxation and duality toolkit to a signal-processing and machine-learning problem (HPPCA), with Michigan EECS collaborators. The text does not say which parts each author wrote.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The problem is to maximize Σᵢ uᵢᵀMᵢuᵢ over U ∈ St(k, d) with Mᵢ ⪰ 0 (p. 1), which arises in HPPCA, generalized PPCA, radar clutter and robust sparse PCA (p. 1). For L ≥ 2 Brockett terms no analytical solution is known, and guarantees exist only for commuting Mᵢ (p. 2). Local solvers (MM, Riemannian ascent) carry no global guarantee, and "no previous convex methods exist to solve (1.1)" (p. 2). The entry point is a gap in practice: practitioners already run local solvers, so a cheap certificate for their output is valuable (pp. 5-6).
  - Transferable: when a field already has scalable local solvers, aim the convex relaxation at certifying their output rather than at replacing them.
- **D2 Core idea**: Lift Xᵢ = uᵢuᵢᵀ. Write orthonormality as binary eigenvalues of ΣXᵢ (2.2), relax this to ΣXᵢ ⪯ I with tr(Xᵢ) = 1 and Xᵢ ⪰ 0, and drop the rank constraints, which gives (SDP-P) (p. 3). This is read as a decomposition of the Fantope variable into k trace-one pieces, so that the objective is captured exactly (p. 3). Three results follow. (a) A dual certificate (Theorem 4.1) built from a candidate U alone: an LMI feasibility problem in k scalar variables ν (p. 6), with an ε-version that bounds suboptimality by ε*d (Corollary 4.2, p. 7). (b) Tightness near jointly diagonalizable data. When the Mᵢ commute, the SDP is an assignment LP (p. 7). Continuity of the dual optimal set then keeps rank(Zᵢ) ≥ d−1 in a neighbourhood, and with it the rank-one property (Theorem 4.6, p. 8). Lin's theorem turns "almost commuting" into "near commuting" (Corollary 4.7, p. 9). (c) HPPCA data matrices concentrate towards commuting as n grows or as the noise variances become equal (Proposition 4.9, pp. 9-10). The standard Shor relaxation is shown to be "trivially not tight" here (p. 5; Appendix G.1, pp. 38-39).
  - Transferable: when the standard Shor lift is weak, lift each column separately and put the coupling constraint (here ΣXᵢ ⪯ I) on the sum. Shor on the stacked vector cannot express that coupling (p. 39).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Mᵢ ⪰ 0, without loss of generality because tr(Xᵢ) = 1 lets a diagonal shift change only the objective constant (p. 8), and k < d (Lemma 2.1, p. 4). The coefficients lie in the bounded set C = {0 ⪯ Mᵢ ⪯ μI} (p. 8). A unique optimal solution of the diagonal LP is assumed and defended as generic (citing ref [17], p. 8). Theorem 4.1 needs an exact second-order stationary point, which the authors flag as unattainable numerically, hence Corollary 4.2 (p. 6). The HPPCA model is Gaussian, with distinct λᵢ and distinct vℓ (p. 9). Theorem 4.6 is an existence result: "the results do not quantify an exact δ" (p. 9).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) A one-line weak duality proof in the main text (p. 4). (ii) Strict feasibility at Xᵢ = I/d, which gives Slater's condition (Lemma A.1, p. 16). (iii) ROP ⇔ optimality for (1.1), using a trace-of-square argument for orthogonality (Lemma A.2, p. 17). (iv) Complementary slackness: rank(Zᵢ) = d−1 forces rank(Xᵢ) = 1 (Lemma 2.4, p. 17). (v) The certificate is built from KKT conditions by defining Y = U(Λ − D_ν)Uᵀ and Zᵢ = Y + νᵢI − Mᵢ and verifying ⟨Zᵢ, Xᵢ⟩ = 0, "Similar to the work in [42]" (pp. 17-18). (vi) Nonnegativity of ν is shown by contradiction, shifting ν so that the objective drops when k < d (Lemma B.2, p. 18). (vii) ε-KKT: lift the approximate dual by ε*I, and the duality gap equals ε*d (pp. 19-20). (viii) A general parametric conic-duality result: under interior feasibility and an extra bounding constraint that cuts off no optimum (Assumptions C.0.1-C.0.2), the dual optimal sets converge and the nearest-point selection y(c) is continuous (Lemmas C.1-C.2, Proposition C.3, pp. 20-21). The assumptions are verified with the explicit interior point Y = μI, Zᵢ = (μ+ε)I − Mᵢ, νᵢ = ε (pp. 21-22). (ix) The Goldman-Tucker strict complementarity theorem for the assignment LP gives rank(Zᵢ) ≥ d−1 (p. 22). (x) The extension of Lin's theorem to k-tuples, Lemma F.13 (p. 9). (xi) An explicit counterexample at d = 4, k = 2 shows that the feasible set is not the convex hull. The proof bounds the rank of X when λ_max[X] = 1 forces the top eigenvector into every Vⱼ (Appendix G.2, pp. 39-41). (xii) Proposition G.2 gives Mᵢ that are not almost commuting yet still have the ROP (pp. 41-42), which shows that the sufficient condition is not necessary.
  - Transferable: to prove tightness near a nice case, show that strict complementarity holds at the nice case (here an LP, via Goldman-Tucker). Then prove continuity of the dual optimal set in the data. The rank condition is open, so it persists in a neighbourhood.
  - Transferable: the extra-constraint trick. Bound the dual feasible set by an objective-level constraint built from a data-independent interior point, so that compactness holds uniformly over the coefficient set (pp. 21-22).
- **D5 Experiments**: MATLAB R2018a with CVX/SDPT3 on a MacBook Pro. Code is on GitHub (kgilman/Sums-of-Heterogeneous-Quadratics), with a warning that results may vary with tolerances (p. 10). An instance counts as "tight" when the eigenvalue error to e₁ is below 10⁻⁵, a single numerical signal (pp. 11, 13). The ROP is assessed on random HPPCA instances: 100 trials for d ≤ 50 and 20 for d ≥ 100 (Tables 1-3, pp. 11-12). The ROP fraction rises to 100% as n grows or the variances equalize, and drops to 0.35 at d = 200-300, k = 10 with small n (Table 2, p. 12). Random-PSD Mᵢ fall to 0 for k = 10 (Table 4, p. 43), a failure reported only in the supplement. StMM local solutions are certified on synthetic CJD data (d = 10, k = 3) and on HPPCA data (d = 50, k = 5). Markers separate certified, SDP-not-tight and tight-but-local-max outcomes, and StMM is globally optimal in more than 95% of tight instances (pp. 13-14). Medians of 10 trials show StMM plus certificate scaling about 60× better (k = 3) and 15× better (k = 10) than the full SDP (p. 14). One protocol inconsistency: the projection onto the Stiefel manifold is described as orthogonal Procrustes (p. 11) and as a QR decomposition (p. 44).
  - Transferable: when plotting results for a local solver, use separate markers for three outcomes: certified global, relaxation not tight, and relaxation tight but a local maximum. The third outcome shows how often the local solver misses (p. 13).
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper extends Bolla et al., which covers only the commuting case, to "almost commuting" data (pp. 2, 5). The second-order conditions of ref [35] are judged "difficult to check in practice" (p. 5; p. 27). The constraint set is presented as related to the Fantope but distinct from it (pp. 2-3). The certificate follows the Fantope certificate work of Won, Zhou and Lange (pp. 6, 17). The difference from their orthogonal-trace-sum setting (several Stiefel manifolds against one basis) is spelled out on p. 28. Burer-Monteiro landscape results are cited as distinct, because orthogonality couples the columns (pp. 5, 28). The perturbation theory of ref [16] is distinguished on two grounds: it uses Shor, which is not tight here, and a single matrix block (p. 5).
- **D7 Writing**: A footnote fixes the meaning of "heterogeneous" (p. 1). A hedging footnote reads: "while our work does not guarantee that a local solution is globally optimal, we propose a certificate based on a sufficient condition" (p. 6). Sufficiency is stated as "by no means necessary", with a counterexample in the supplement (p. 9). The main text is 15 pages and the proofs and extra experiments are in appendices and a supplement. The acknowledgment credits the finder of an earlier error by name (p. 15).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: The contributions are a new SDP relaxation, a cheap global-optimality certificate for local solutions, and tightness in a neighbourhood of commuting data together with a proof that HPPCA approaches that regime (pp. 2, 14-15). Limits are stated: the LMI still needs O(d²) storage (p. 15); the size of the neighbourhood is not quantified (pp. 9, 15); no analytical certificate is known, because Zᵢ is not monotone in νⱼ (E.2, p. 30); and the feasible set is not the convex hull (G.2). Future work named: randomized scalable SDP, sharper ROP conditions, quantifying the region in Theorem 4.6, and applying the ideas of ref [16] (p. 15).

**Method links**: Method 1 variant ⚠: the problem is lifted into one convex object (SDP-P), but the object is a strict relaxation, not an exact reformulation (G.2, pp. 39-41). The research effort moves to characterizing data for which it is tight (pp. 3-4, 7-9). This is the "representation is not a solution" taste applied to a relaxation that is honestly inexact. Method 3 evidence ✅: a certified explicit counterexample (d = 4, k = 2) bounds the claim (pp. 39-41). The paper states that the sufficient condition is not necessary (p. 9; G.3) and credits by name the person who found an error in an earlier version (p. 15). Method 3 variant ⚠: "tight" rests on one numerical signal, the eigenvalue error below 10⁻⁵ (pp. 11, 13), not on the two-signal rule of S049 (p. 10). Method 4 variant ⚠: the instances are random draws from the application's generative model, and fractions are reported unconditionally. There is no engineered hard pool or baseline filter, and the losses (random PSD, large d and k) are in the supplement (p. 43). New-pattern candidates: "certify the local solver, don't replace it" (pp. 5-7, 14); "tightness by perturbation from an LP-exact case" (pp. 7-8, 20-22).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Build a global-optimality certificate from a candidate U alone. Set Y = U(Λ − D_ν)Uᵀ and Zᵢ = Y + νᵢI − Mᵢ; complementarity then holds by construction, and only an LMI feasibility problem in k scalars remains (pp. 6, 17-18).
- Make the certificate robust to inexact stationarity: minimize ε subject to the ε-relaxed LMIs, and report the bound p* − ε*d (Corollary 4.2, pp. 7, 19-20).
- Prove tightness in a neighbourhood of a solvable case. Strict complementarity holds at the LP case (Goldman-Tucker), the dual optimal-set map is continuous, and the open rank condition therefore persists (pp. 8, 20-22).
- Refute a convex-hull claim with an explicit small instance whose rank exceeds the bound any convex combination of feasible points must satisfy (pp. 39-41).
