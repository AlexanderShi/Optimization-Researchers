### S007 · A finite branch-and-bound algorithm for nonconvex quadratic programming via semidefinite relaxations (Mathematical Programming 2008, doi:10.1007/s10107-006-0080-6)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-24, including the reference list). The figures (pp. 19-22) are only axis labels and captions in the text file, so the plotted node and time values were not readable; I report the authors' prose summary of them.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Dieter Vandenbussche (Axioma, Inc., p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: received 27 June 2005, accepted 30 October 2006 (p. 1); the first Burer paper on globally solving nonconvex QP, built on the authors' earlier AL solver for Lovász-Schrijver SDPs ([4], p. 4); its box-constrained part is specialised in S018.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The entry is a theoretical gap in global QP: existing methods partition the polyhedron P and may need an infinite tree to verify optimality in exact arithmetic; the authors quote Sherali and Tuncbilek's convergence statement as typical and say they know of no method that verifies optimality in a finite number of nodes (pp. 2-3). They also say the gap is practically minor: ε-optimality is finite, and "concerns of an infinite tree are quite irrelevant" in floating point (p. 3). The concrete obstacle: finite KKT branching (Vandenbussche-Nemhauser for the box case) yields LP relaxations that are unbounded, and the box-case trick for bounding them does not extend (p. 3). Problem: max ½xᵀQx + cᵀx s.t. Ax ≤ b, x ≥ 0, Q indefinite or psd (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when a known finite method works only for a special case, identify the single property that breaks in the general case (here: boundedness of the node relaxation), and make fixing that property the paper.
- **D2 Core idea**: Keep the combinatorial branching on KKT complementarities (four index sets Fx, Fz, Fb−Ax, Fy, p. 6) and replace the unbounded LP node relaxation by an SDP relaxation in Y = (1;x)(1;x)ᵀ (SDP0, p. 9), joined to the multiplier constraints (y,z) ∈ Gx through the objective-equating constraint (3) Q̃•Y = (bᵀy + cᵀx)/2 (SDP1, p. 9). (3) is valid because of the Giannessi-Tomasin identity xᵀQx + cᵀx = bᵀy at KKT points (Prop. 2.1, p. 5). A stronger, larger SDP2 lifts (x,y,z) jointly and writes complementarity as linear "diag" constraints on the lifted matrix (pp. 14-15). What stays unchanged: finite KKT branching from Hansen et al. and Vandenbussche-Nemhauser (p. 3); the Lovász-Schrijver lift-and-project template (pp. 4, 8).
  - Transferable: when a relaxation is unbounded in auxiliary variables (multipliers), add one valid equation linking them to a bounded lifted quantity (here, objective value = dual value), and the recession cone collapses.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: P bounded and scaled into the unit box (Assumption 2.4) and with an interior point (Assumption 2.5), both called "mild" and justified by scaling and dimension reduction (p. 7). The finiteness theorem is stated for exact arithmetic and separately for the bit model (p. 12). The implementation drops (3) for speed and replaces it by preprocessed bounds on (y,z) plus an LP at leaf nodes (p. 16).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Unboundedness of the LP relaxation via LP duality with chosen objectives d = e and d = 0 plus complementary slackness at an interior point (Prop. 2.2, Cor. 2.3, p. 7); boundedness of SDP1 through recession cones: Yeᵢ ∈ K bounded by Assumption 2.4, and constraint (3) forcing bᵀy = 0 on a cone where bᵀy > 0 (Prop. 3.1, p. 10); "relaxation with no gap at a KKT point ⇒ optimal" (Prop. 3.2, p. 10); correctness defined as "every leaf is fathomed", with fathoming only on infeasibility or zero gap (p. 11); proof under two computation models, the bit-model case using that at a leaf the SDP coincides with an exactly solvable LP (Thm 3.3, p. 12); the surrogate function δ(x, Hx) and Prop. 4.1 (cut off by SDP1 iff δ(x,Gx) > Q̃•Y), from which two ways to tighten follow (pp. 12-13).
  - Transferable: prove the correctness of a finite branch-and-bound by showing that the relaxation is exact at every leaf, and handle floating point by showing that leaf relaxations reduce to an exactly solvable LP (p. 12).
  - Transferable: before strengthening a relaxation, write a scalar surrogate (here δ) whose comparison with the relaxed objective says exactly which points are cut off; its two sides give two separate ways to tighten (p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments**: AL method of Burer-Vandenbussche [4], with CPLEX solving convex QP subproblems (p. 15); maximum-normalised-violation branching, local NLP solver for incumbents, best-bound search, dual warm start, relative tolerances 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻² (p. 17). Every instance is run four times (SDP1/SDP2 × two tolerances) on a Pentium 4, 2.4 GHz (p. 17). Test sets: the 54 Vandenbussche-Nemhauser box QPs, n = 20-60 (p. 18); 12 Lootsma-Pearson inventory and 15 Skutella scheduling instances generated at random (pp. 18-21); about 20 Globallib QPs with n = 10-50, where instances with CPLEX numerical failures after rescaling were dropped, and the text says so (p. 23); 24 random dense QPs (p. 23). Results: few nodes, most instances solve at the root at 1%; SDP2 needs fewer nodes but SDP1 is always faster; CPU from seconds to "a few days"; the SDP solve is the bottleneck (pp. 17-18). There is no comparison with BARON or any other global solver: BARON is mentioned only as available software (p. 2). Results are given as log-log scatter plots of SDP1 against SDP2 (Figs. 1-6, pp. 19-22).
  - Transferable: when a new relaxation has two variants, run every instance under both variants × two tolerances and plot them against each other on log-log axes; it separates strength (nodes) from cost (time) at a glance (pp. 17-22).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on and extends Vandenbussche-Nemhauser's finite box-QP branch-and-cut ("It is straightforward to extend the finite KKT-branching", p. 3); claims novelty against the SDP literature ("no one has studied in detail the specific SDP relaxations that we consider", p. 4). Lovász-Schrijver, Kojima-Tunçel, Bomze-de Klerk, Sherali-Fraticelli and Anstreicher are reviewed neutrally (pp. 3-4). Scope disclaimer: if a good local solution is all you want, the methods of Ye or Absil-Tits are quicker (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing**: The contribution is stated as two goals, theoretical (p. 4) and practical (p. 4), each mapped to sections. The theorem is announced with the warning "We stress that the theorem is concerned with the solution of (QP) in exact arithmetic." (p. 11). Empirical observations are hedged ("we have actually never observed ... We believe this is evidence", p. 13). The practical findings are given as a bulleted summary before the details (pp. 17-18).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theoretical: the first finite branch-and-bound for general bounded QP (pp. 4, 8). Algorithmic: two SDP relaxations of the KKT system (p. 4). Limits are stated concretely: SDP solution time is the bottleneck; "improved SDP solution techniques will have a significant impact" (p. 18); SDP2 numerical inaccuracy (pp. 17-18, 22); Globallib instances dropped for CPLEX failures (p. 23); how much tighter SDP1 is than SDP0 is unresolved: "we have been unable to obtain a precise answer" (p. 12).

**Method links**: Method 5 evidence ✅: theory requirement (3) dropped in practice for speed, with a printed safeguard (bounds on (y,z) from loosely solved preprocessing SDPs, LP at leaves) and a constructed counterexample showing why the safeguard is needed (p. 16); this is the source of H7's "theoretically unwise" quote. Method 4 variant ⚠: the 54-instance BoxQP set and the disclosure that numerically failing Globallib instances were omitted (p. 23) are present, but the pool is not filtered to where a baseline fails, and no external baseline is run. Method 1 variant ⚠: the lift Y = (1;x)(1;x)ᵀ and the lifted KKT system (pp. 8-9, 14) are here, but as node relaxations inside branch-and-bound, not an exact single-cone reformulation. Method 3 variant ⚠: a computation-led belief is labelled as belief (SDP1 never beat SDP0, p. 13), but floating point is called "quite irrelevant" (p. 3), the pole of Tension 2 opposite exact certificates. New-pattern candidates: "finite branching on complementarity + a bounded relaxation that is exact at the leaves" (pp. 6, 11-12); "proof under two models of computation" (p. 12).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Bound a relaxation that is unbounded in its multipliers by adding one valid equation that ties the lifted objective to the dual objective (constraint (3), pp. 9-10).
- Define "correctness" of a finite branch-and-bound explicitly as "every feasible leaf has zero gap", and prove it separately (p. 11).
- When you drop a constraint the theory needs, compute substitute bounds once in preprocessing with the constraint included, and keep an exact fallback at the leaves (p. 16).
- Normalise branching violations by a priori bounds on slacks and multipliers so that "most violated complementarity" is scale-free (p. 17).
**Quotes**:
- "We are unaware of any methods that can verify optimality in a finite number of nodes (using exact arithmetic)." (p. 3)
- "Of course, it is theoretically unwise to ignore (3) because both the boundedness of (y, z) and the correctness of branch-and-bound depend on it." (p. 16)

### S015 · Coordinating the supply chain in the agricultural seed industry (European Journal of Operational Research 2008, doi:10.1016/j.ejor.2006.12.015)
**Read level**: full (file pp. 2-25: body, Appendix A and references). File page 1 is Elsevier's copyright notice. Fig. 1 (cash-flow diagram, p. 6) and Fig. 2 (p. 15) are only labels in the text file; Fig. 2's three scenarios are described in prose and parameters on p. 16.
**Role**: core (applied OR outside the team field; read for transfer) · **Coauthors**: Philip C. Jones, Timothy J. Lowe (both Iowa Management Sciences, p. 2) · **Place in trajectory**: received 28 September 2006 (p. 2), the same Iowa period as S007/S004; an application paper in the "O.R. Applications" section (p. 2), unrelated to his QP machinery.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entered from industry practice: a seed supplier with whom the authors worked uses a "pure bonus" contract and, for new hybrids, a "mixed" bonus-penalty contract (p. 3). Traditionally dealers return unsold seed at full credit, so they over-order (pp. 3-4). Question: can these contracts, as actually used, coordinate the channel (dealer's optimal order = channel-optimal newsvendor quantity) (p. 3)? The industry intuition that the bonus system "breaks down" for new hybrids is the hypothesis to check (p. 3).
  - Transferable: take a mechanism already in use, write it exactly as practiced (including its arbitrary-looking thresholds), and ask the theory question about that mechanism rather than about an idealised one.
- **D2 Core idea**: Model the dealer's expected profit with threshold functions hβ(Q) = max{ℓ, βQ} for the bonus and hπ(Q) = max{ℓ, πQ} for the penalty, and differentiate piecewise (eqs. (5)-(7), pp. 10; (13)-(15), p. 12). With the wholesale price w fixed (as in the industry), the contract parameters (b, β, p, π) are the only levers (pp. 7, 8). General demand: a sufficient condition under which no pure bonus coordinates (Prop. 3.1, p. 10); a mixed system that always coordinates (Prop. 3.2, p. 12). Uniform demand: an iff condition Q*c > u/2 (Prop. 4.2, p. 15) and a full classification of coordinating parameters as a hyperbola ∪ ray (Thms. 4.3, 4.7, pp. 16, 19-20).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Single supplier, single dealer, single product; production cost sunk (g = 0); 0 < s < w < r; s, w, r, t, c exogenous; w the same for all dealers (p. 8); demand on [ℓ, u] with smooth f, F (p. 9). Uniform demand is called "strong" and justified twice: in practice (short life cycles, only bounds known, p. 13) and in theory (Perakis-Roels: with known support, the minimax-regret order equals the uniform-demand newsvendor order, p. 14).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Piecewise derivatives of expected profit at threshold kinks (eqs. (6), (7), (14), (15), pp. 10, 12); a lower bound on the right derivative at Q*c to rule out coordination (Prop. 3.1, p. 10); a truncated-normal counterexample to the converse of Prop. 3.1 (§3.3.1, p. 11); concavity of the piecewise profit on each piece, so the optimum is max{ℓ/β, critical point} (Prop. 4.1, p. 14); a comparison Eb = Em + EP with EP convex and increasing, which orders the maximisers (Prop. 4.4, pp. 18-19); a derivative-sign argument along the coordinating curve to locate minimum dealer profit (Thm. 4.8, pp. 21-22); cash-flow replication arguments (perfect-information state of the world) to show non-equivalence with Pasternack, Taylor and Tsay (App. A, pp. 22-24).
  - Transferable: to show your model is not a special case of an existing one, compare realised cash flows under every demand realisation, and pick an extreme "state of the world" (perfect information) in which the two models must agree if one contains the other (p. 24).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The only numbers are an illustrative parameter set for Fig. 2 (ℓ, u, r, w, c) = (500, 1500, 90, 80, 20) with three (s, t) scenarios (p. 16) and a back-of-envelope threshold c ≥ 53.75 for a new hybrid (p. 17). There is no field data.
- **D6 Positioning**: Positions against Pasternack (buyback), Tsay (quantity flexibility), Taylor (target rebate) and Cachon-Lariviere (revenue sharing) (pp. 5-7), arguing both that the seed model differs and that those models are not implementable in the seed industry (w cannot vary, uniform w across dealers, no demand update before ordering, pp. 7-8). Pasternack's model is shown to be a special case of the mixed system (p. 13, App. A.1 p. 23). A university football-program consignment contract is offered as a generalisation found in another industry (pp. 4-5).
- **D7 Writing**: Explicit scope statements: "We claim neither that these are the only mechanisms possible nor that they are in every sense optimal." (p. 3); on Tsay, "we do not claim to have accomplished this miraculous feat" (p. 8). Industry voice is reported with sources ("According to our sources in the seed industry", p. 4). The paper ends with a "how to use the formulas in practice" paragraph and a hedged closing thought (p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Contribution: modelling plus analysis (characterisations and classification), with practical formulas (Thm. 4.7 gives "a simple, linearly constrained set", p. 22). Limits named: single retailer; limited supply across retailers is "extremely complicated" and left out (footnote 2, p. 7); unbounded dealer profit in Thm. 4.8 is stated not to be realistic (p. 21). There are no explicit future directions.

**Method links**: Method 3 variant ⚠: claims are written at their level (a sufficient condition in general, a counterexample to its converse, then an iff under uniform demand, pp. 10-11, 15), but the counterexample is analytic, not computational. No evidence for Methods 1, 2, 4, 5: the work is outside his conic-relaxation programme. The scope-disclaimer habit of H9 appears (pp. 3, 8). New-pattern candidate: "model the practice as it is: treat industry-fixed levers as exogenous and characterise the full set of remaining parameters that achieve the goal" (pp. 8, 16, 19-20).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Justify a strong distributional assumption twice, once by practice (what data actually exist) and once by a theorem (minimax-regret equivalence) (pp. 13-14).
- Present a full parameter classification as a table of cases (empty / trivial / impossible / hyperbola ∪ ray) before proving it (p. 18).
- Use the classification to answer the practitioner's actual design question, e.g. fix an existing bonus and add a penalty, or guarantee last season's dealer profit (pp. 20-22).
**Quotes**:
- "We claim neither that these are the only mechanisms possible nor that they are in every sense optimal." (p. 3)
- "While such an outcome would be startling indeed, we do not claim to have accomplished this miraculous feat." (p. 8)

### S004 · On the copositive representation of binary and continuous nonconvex quadratic programs (Mathematical Programming 2009, doi:10.1007/s10107-008-0223-z)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-17, including acknowledgements and references).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Place in trajectory**: received 27 November 2006, accepted 26 March 2008 (p. 1); cites S007 as [5] for the KKT route (p. 11) and the Anstreicher-Burer low-dimensional hull manuscript as an application (p. 16).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: A short list of NP-hard problems (standard QP, max stable set, QPs over transportation matrices) were known to have copositive/CP representations, which Burer calls "a complete list" as far as he knows (p. 1). The question is how far the list extends (p. 2). The motivation is uniformity: a CP representation lets one "package" the difficulty inside one cone, so knowledge about that cone, such as Parrilo's inner hierarchy, applies to all problems at once (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when a handful of special cases share a striking representation, ask for the largest class with that representation, and argue the payoff as transfer of future knowledge about the common object.
- **D2 Core idea**: Write all linear constraints as equations, add each squared equation aᵢᵀXaᵢ = bᵢ² as a redundant homogeneous quadratic equation, impose xⱼ = Xⱼⱼ for binaries, and replace the rank-one matrix by a completely positive matrix (C) (p. 4). Under the "key assumption" that the linear constraints imply 0 ≤ xⱼ ≤ 1 for j ∈ B (eq. (1), p. 3), (C) is exact: Feas+(C) = Feas+(P) + L∞⁺ (Prop. 2.1, p. 4) and opt(C) = opt(P) (Thm. 2.6, p. 6). Extensions: eliminate x into C*ₙ under condition (8) (Thm. 3.1, pp. 7-9), and add complementarity constraints on bounded variables (Thm. 3.2, pp. 10-11). What did not change: the standard lift (1,xᵀ; x,X) (p. 4).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Linear constraints in equation form, x ≥ 0, binaries B, Feas(P) nonempty (p. 3). The key assumption (1) is without loss of generality (add xⱼ + sⱼ = 1, p. 3). Unbounded L is allowed through the recession cone L∞; the stronger equality Feas+(P) = Feas+(C) needs boundedness (Cor. 2.5, p. 6). Condition (8) for eliminating x is equivalent by Farkas to infeasibility of a related system and can be forced by an explicit bound on a sum of variables (p. 7). The quadratic-constraint extension needs two generalised assumptions (p. 10).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Completely positive decomposition of the feasible matrix into rank-one nonnegative terms, split into K+ (ζₖ > 0) and K0 (ζₖ = 0) (Lemma 2.2, p. 5); the equality case of Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the original and squared equations forces aᵢᵀzₖ = bᵢζₖ for every term (eqs. (4)-(6), p. 5); "sum of nonnegatives is zero" for binaries, λₖ(vₖⱼ − vₖⱼ²) = 0 (Lemma 2.3, p. 6); a negative recession direction gives opt = −∞ (Lemma 2.7, p. 7); no interior for (C) shown by one psd matrix orthogonal to the feasible set (p. 9), contrasted with the interior of (C') via (1/n)I (p. 9); a 2-variable counterexample (from Bomze et al.) shows the remnant constraints aᵢᵀXα = bᵢ cannot in general be dropped (p. 9).
  - Transferable: to prove that a conic lift is exact, decompose a feasible point into rank-one pieces and use a tight inequality (Cauchy-Schwarz equality case) on paired constraints, original and squared, to force each piece to be feasible (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. §5 is a discussion of computation (approximating C* by (Kʳ)*, of size about O(nʳ); adding valid constraints in (x,X)), with no runs (pp. 15-16).
- **D6 Positioning**: Section 4 restates each earlier result in the paper's notation: Bomze et al. (standard QP), de Klerk-Pasechnik (stable set), Povh-Rendl (graph tri-partitioning, QAP) and Sturm-Zhang (generalised cones) (pp. 11-14). It places itself "naturally between" Bomze et al. and Sturm-Zhang (p. 14). It concedes the limits of subsumption: "our analysis cannot be said to subsume previous analyses" because general quadratic constraints are not covered (p. 14). It notes a shared device with Povh-Rendl (Cauchy-Schwarz) (p. 14).
- **D7 Writing**: Short and theorem-driven: problem, representation, simplification, comparison, computation, conclusion (p. 2). The main computational insight is set off as a displayed statement (p. 16). The hedging is explicit: "not a "magic bullet"" (p. 15); "we feel the above insight is a critical contribution" (p. 16). It ends with three open questions (pp. 16-17).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory only. The limit is stated up front in §5: (C) is itself NP-hard, and the difficulty is moved into C* (p. 15). Future directions: more general quadratic constraints, extending Bomze-de Klerk approximation bounds or a PTAS, bounding the hierarchy level r (pp. 16-17). Applications claimed as "success stories": the Anstreicher-Burer closed-form hull for n = 2 box QP and Povh-Rendl's QAP SDP (p. 16).

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅: this is the founding text: the "package" sentence (p. 2), the key assumption as the hypothesis that makes the cone representation exact (p. 3, step 2), the special cases covered (§4, pp. 11-14, step 4), and turning to tractable approximations of the cone plus valid (x,X) constraints (§5, pp. 15-16, step 5). Method 1 stop rule evidence ✅: "not a "magic bullet"" (p. 15). Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 6, credit by name): the acknowledgement thanks Bomze "for pointing out an earlier error in Proposition 2.3" (p. 17); the content of the error is not in this text. Method 3 evidence ✅ (step 8): open cases handed on as questions (pp. 16-17). Method 2 variant ⚠: the n = 2 box-QP hull (Anstreicher-Burer) appears as a downstream application of the CP view (p. 16), the first trace of the small-substructure programme. New-pattern candidates: "square each linear equation and add it as a redundant quadratic equation" (pp. 4, 14); "restate every prior special case in your notation and say exactly where you do not subsume it" (pp. 11-14).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Make an assumption "without loss of generality" by showing the one-line reformulation that enforces it (slack xⱼ + sⱼ = 1, p. 3; a bound on a sum of variables to obtain (8), p. 7).
- After an exact reformulation, look for a reduced version that has an interior (eliminate x into C*ₙ), since Slater-type conditions matter for duality and solvers (p. 9).
- Give a two-variable counterexample to show that a simplification cannot go further (p. 9).
**Quotes**:
- "it does allow one to “package” the difficulty completely inside the convex cones of copositive and/or completely positive matrices." (p. 2)
- "Overall, it seems that the original equation and its squared version are critical for Theorems 2.6 and 3.1." (p. 14)

### S011 · On non-convex quadratic programming with box constraints (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2009, doi:10.1137/080729529)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-17, including references).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Adam N. Letchford (Lancaster) (p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: received 7 July 2008, revised June 2009 (p. 1); continues the Anstreicher-Burer n ≤ 2 hull result ([3]) toward n = 3; Proposition 9 was conjectured by Anstreicher (p. 14).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: QPB is a fundamental NP-hard problem, but the convex hull QPBₙ of {(x,y) ∈ [0,1]^(n+…): yᵢⱼ = xᵢxⱼ} is little understood; "a complete linear description of QP B3 is not known" (p. 2). The paper asks for basic structural facts about the set (p. 2). Concluding remark: "it is surprising that many of its basic properties were not established before now" (p. 16).
  - Transferable: when a field has strong algorithms for a problem but no structural results for its hull, list the standard polyhedral questions (dimension, extreme points, vertices, symmetries, facets, validity checking) and answer them one by one.
- **D2 Core idea**: Treat QPBₙ with the tools of polyhedral combinatorics, and link it to Padberg's Boolean quadric polytope: BQPₙ is both the projection of QPBₙ (Prop. 5, p. 10) and the projection of the face yᵢᵢ = xᵢ (Prop. 6, p. 11). Then BQP facets are transferred: a BQP facet is a QPB facet iff there are n suitable extreme points with one fractional coordinate each (Thm. 4, p. 11). For the Boros-Hammer family this holds iff v ∈ {0, ±1}ⁿ, i.e. the cut-type inequalities (Thm. 5, p. 12). Valid inequalities are classified by the convexity of their quadratic form (concave → psd, convex → BQP + RLT, indefinite → recursion on facets of the box) (§6, pp. 13-15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Box [0,1]ⁿ without loss of generality (p. 1); variables yᵢⱼ for i ≤ j including the diagonal (p. 2). Polyhedral theory and convex analysis are assumed known (p. 2). Propositions 3-4 are stated without proof, "for brevity", via Pataki's psd-cone results (p. 9).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Affine independence of explicitly listed points for full dimension (Lemma 1, p. 5); every member of S is extreme, shown by a separable objective Σ(xᵢ − x̄ᵢ)² with a unique minimiser (Lemma 2, pp. 5-6); vertex iff binary, by perturbing a fractional coordinate by ±ε and showing that every normal vector satisfies an equation (Thm. 1, p. 6); invariance under switching through extreme points (Prop. 1, p. 7); face dimension of psd inequalities by multiplying vᵀx + s = 0 by each variable (Thm. 2, p. 8); preservation of codimension under canonical extension (Thm. 3, pp. 9-10); fractional points written as convex combinations of their two binary roundings (Prop. 5, p. 10; Lemma 6, p. 11); the maximum of an indefinite quadratic is on the boundary, which gives a finite recursive validity check (Lemma 8, Prop. 10, p. 15); a hand-checkable point with value 19/18 > 1 that separates QPB₃ from Q₃ (p. 16).
  - Transferable: to show that a candidate outer description Q is not the hull, find an inequality valid for the hull by an exact finite procedure (here the facet recursion) and a rational point of Q that violates it; this gives an exact certificate (p. 16).
  - Transferable: carry the symmetry group of a known combinatorial polytope (permutation, switching) over to the continuous hull to turn one facet into a family (pp. 6-7).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The n = 3 separation uses the specific (α, β, γ) and point on p. 16; "the optimal objective value is approximately 1.0929" is reported without method (p. 16).
- **D6 Positioning**: Extends Yajima-Fujie ("We extend this result significantly", p. 5; "We now give a much stronger result", p. 12) and builds on Anstreicher-Burer's n ≤ 2 result (pp. 4, 15-16). The connection to the Boolean quadric polytope (Padberg, Boros-Hammer, De Simone, Deza-Laurent) is the main bridge (pp. 4-5, 12). Folklore results (UBQP ⊂ QPB; UBQP ≡ max-cut) are attributed carefully ("possibly due to Rosenberg", p. 4).

### S018 · Globally solving box-constrained nonconvex quadratic programs with semidefinite-based finite branch-and-bound (Computational Optimization and Applications 2009, doi:10.1007/s10589-007-9137-6)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-15, including references). Figs. 1-4 (pp. 12-14) are captions only in the text file; I report the authors' prose about them.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Dieter Vandenbussche (Axioma, Inc., p. 1) · **Place in trajectory**: received 7 November 2006, published online 31 October 2007 (p. 1); the box-QP specialisation of S007 (cited as [5], with its revision dates, p. 14); its enlarged instance set is the second rung of the BoxQP ladder.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Specialise the finite SDP-based branch-and-bound of S007 to max ½xᵀQx + cᵀx over 0 ≤ x ≤ e, where Vandenbussche-Nemhauser's LP-based finite branch-and-cut is "one of the most successful" methods (pp. 1-2). The aim: an SDP relaxation that "better exploits branching information, while still maintaining a compact size", and a thorough comparison showing that larger problems can be solved (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: In the box case the multipliers need not be variables: y := max(0, Qx + c), z := −min(0, Qx + c) is always in Gx, and fixing yⱼ = 0 or zⱼ = 0 becomes a linear inequality in x alone ([Qx]ⱼ + cⱼ ≤ 0 or ≥ 0) (pp. 7-8). So the node relaxation is the smaller SDP0 over P′ = P(F0,F1) ∩ S(Fy,Fz) with cone K′ (eqs. (6)-(7), p. 8); constraint (3) and the explicit (y,z) are gone. What is kept from S007: KKT branching, the AL solver and its implementation details (pp. 3-4, 10-11).
  - Transferable: in a specialisation, check whether auxiliary variables (here multipliers) are explicit functions of the primal; if so, eliminate them and turn their fixings into linear constraints on the primal (pp. 7-8).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: The box [0,1]ⁿ; Q indefinite or psd (p. 2). Logical implications F0 ⊆ Fy and F1 ⊆ Fz are added (p. 4). Fathoming uses a relative tolerance, so floating point is handled by tolerance (pp. 7, 11).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Because the relaxation changed, correctness is re-proved: at a feasible leaf, every j lies in F0, F1 or Fy ∩ Fz, and each case forces Qᵀ·ⱼX·ⱼ = (Qᵀ·ⱼx)xⱼ (Prop. 3.1, pp. 8-9). The weaker cone K̄ (without the F0/F1 equalities) still implies them, through PSD principal-minor arguments: the 2×2 minor forces Xⱼⱼ = 1, and a rescaled 3×3 minor with determinant −(x̄ᵢ − X̄ⱼᵢ)² forces Xⱼᵢ = xᵢ (Prop. 3.2, pp. 9-10). A remark explains why "fathoming by feasibility" from integer programming does not carry over: a KKT relaxed solution is not automatically optimal because the objective was linearised (p. 7).
  - Transferable: when a relaxation is modified for speed, re-prove the one property the algorithm needs (exactness at leaves) instead of citing the old theorem (pp. 8-9).
  - Transferable: to show that a PSD lift implies an equality, look at a 2×2 then a 3×3 principal minor whose determinant is minus a square (pp. 9-10).
- **D5 Experiments**: Baseline: the co-author's earlier LP branch-and-cut (Vandenbussche-Nemhauser), run "using the parameter settings suggested in their work" under both branching rules, taking the faster per instance (pp. 11-12). Test set: the 54 instances of [17] plus 36 new instances at n = 70, 80, 90, 100 × densities 25/50/75% × 3, integer entries in [−50, 50], 90 in total (p. 12). Two runs: default tolerance with a 45,000 s limit and 1% with a 20,000 s limit (pp. 12-13). Results: branch-and-cut faster on small instances, SDP faster on large; branch-and-cut fails on 25 of 90 (default) and 26 (1%); the SDP approach finishes all (pp. 12-13). Maximum nodes are 305 (SDP) against 181,958 (B&C) (p. 13). Termination gaps of the unfinished B&C runs are plotted (Fig. 2, p. 13). Hardware and software versions for this paper are not stated in the text (CPLEX 9.0 appears in the reference list, p. 15).
  - Transferable: when a baseline times out, plot its final optimality gap per instance (ordered by size, then density), so readers see how far it was from finishing rather than only a failure count (pp. 12-13).
- **D6 Positioning**: Two classes of global methods, infinite (partition P) and finite (KKT branching) (p. 2). It relates itself to Helmberg's SDP branch-and-cut for 0-1 QPs, Anstreicher et al.'s QAP and Braun-Mitchell's SDP for complementarity (p. 2). A "high level" comparison with An and Tao's d.c./ellipsoid method concedes that they solve n up to 550, more than this paper, and notes no public global QPB code exists (pp. 13-14).
- **D7 Writing**: Compact: recap of S007 in §2 (pp. 3-7), specialisation in §3 (pp. 7-10), computation in §4 (pp. 10-14). Results are given mainly as log-log plots with a few headline numbers (pp. 12-13). Claims are graded: "We conclude that the SDP-based approach scales better than branch-and-cut." (p. 12). The abstract calls the method "state-of-the-art" for box QP (p. 1).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Algorithmic and computational: a compact box-QP relaxation with re-proved correctness, plus evidence of better scaling (pp. 2, 12-13). Limits named: the strength-versus-solve-time trade-off (p. 13); B&C is faster on smaller instances (p. 12); An and Tao reach larger n (p. 14). No future-work section.

**Method links**: Method 4 evidence ✅: the ladder rung (54 → 90 instances by adding larger, denser random instances where the incumbent LP method is expected to fail, p. 12), and the baseline reported with its stopping state (gaps of unfinished runs, pp. 12-13). This is also H5 (the co-author's previous code at its suggested best settings, faster of two branching schemes per instance, pp. 11-12) and H8 (losses printed: B&C faster on small instances, An-Tao larger n, pp. 12, 14). Method 5 variant ⚠: implementation choices from S007 (loose AL solves, crude warm start "extremely helpful in practice", p. 11) are reused, but here the theory is re-proved for the modified relaxation rather than left open (pp. 8-10). Method 1 variant ⚠: lift-and-project SDP0 (pp. 4-5), used as a node bound. New-pattern candidate: "specialise a general method by eliminating auxiliary variables that are explicit in the primal, then re-prove the algorithm's key property" (pp. 7-10).
**Transferable techniques**:
