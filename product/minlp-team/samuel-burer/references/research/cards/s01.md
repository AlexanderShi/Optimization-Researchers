**Read level**: partial (abstract and §1–§3 pp. 1–5, §4 pp. 6–12 except the detail of §4.2 criteria on pp. 8–9, §5 pp. 12–16, §6 opening p. 16, §7 p. 18; Hessian formulas pp. 16–18 skimmed). First-page check: title and authors Samuel Burer and Jon Lee match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Jon Lee (introduced by Kurt Anstreicher, p. 18) · **Position in trajectory**: carries the low-rank factorization idea (refs [4–6], p. 4) into a nonconvex, nondifferentiable bound inside a combinatorial branch-and-bound.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Anstreicher and Lee's masked spectral bound generalizes eigenvalue, diagonal and spectral-partition bounds, but "They did not, however, consider efficient evaluation of the bound or use of the bound in a complete Branch-and-Bound algorithm." (p. 3). The goal is to get the strength of combinatorial partition bounds through continuous optimization (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Factor the mask X = VVᵀ with row normalization h(V), so the PSD and unit-diagonal constraints are implicit; minimize the spectral bound by steepest descent with acceptance of non-improving steps (Algorithm 1) or a normalized projected path (Algorithm 2) (pp. 3–5). In branch-and-bound, choose orig vs complementary bound by remembering, per (f, u) pair, which was stronger the first time, run two steps, extrapolate by quadratic interpolation whether fathoming is near, and warm-start from a shared V (p. 13).
- **D3 Assumptions**: C positive definite (p. 1); non-differentiability and nonconvexity accepted on four stated empirical grounds (global minimum not needed, nondifferentiability "not so serious", local solutions similar, initialization at fixed masks) (p. 3); gradient formula valid when λ_s > λ_{s+1} (p. 4).
- **D4 Proof devices (brief)**: gradient via symmetric functions of eigenvalues (p. 4); Proposition 1: −∇g(V̄) is a descent direction for f, by an angle decomposition (p. 5); exact Hessian formulas given for others' use (pp. 16–18).
- **D5 Experiments**: n = 63 environmental-monitoring data and an n = 124 set; metric is the entropy gap to a greedy + 2-opt heuristic (p. 6); fair comparison with Algorithm AS by obtaining its Matlab code and writing Algorithm 1 in Matlab too (p. 8); rank sweep k = 2…63 (pp. 10–11); branch-and-bound with a 3,600 s cap comparing gaps of four bounding strategies (pp. 14–16); the n = 63, s = 31 instance solved in 84 h (65 h with Algorithm 2), versus 3,300 s for a different NLP bound class in [2] after clock normalization (p. 14).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Algorithmic and computational; limits stated: no rank guideline (p. 10); Algorithm 2 better in isolation yet worse inside branch-and-bound (p. 14); "silver bullet" disclaimer (p. 14); second-order attempts (modified Newton, BFGS, L-BFGS) did not beat steepest descent and are reported anyway (p. 16); future: gradient sampling, effective low rank with second-order information (p. 18).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: known bound + known trick (low-rank factorization from his own SDP work, p. 4), simple descent despite nondifferentiability justified "in our experience" (p. 3), theory-light design choices (warm start, quadratic interpolation) tuned by experiment (p. 13), failures printed (p. 16).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant: when no strategy can solve the hard instances, the protocol switches to equal time caps and compares final gaps, with the caveat that this is not a claim of solvability (p. 14).
- Taste warning sign 3 ✅ evidence: "silver bullet" sentence parallels the 2009 "magic bullet" disclaimer (p. 14).
- new-pattern candidate: "Same-language re-implementation for a fair baseline" (obtain the competitor's Matlab code, write your own method in Matlab for the comparison, C for branch-and-bound) (pp. 8, 12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Inside branch-and-bound, cache per node class (here, the pair f, u of fixed-in and fixed-out counts) which of two bounds was stronger the first time and compute only that one afterwards (p. 13).
- Run two iterations of the bound optimizer, fit a quadratic through the three values, and continue only if fathoming is predicted within a few more steps (p. 13).
- If no method can finish the hard instances, compare remaining gaps after a fixed time cap and say explicitly what this does and does not show, including a stronger method from another bound class (p. 14).
- Report negative results on second-order variants with the reasons you observed (Hessian cost, rank-quality tradeoff, nonconvexity) and publish the formulas (p. 16).
