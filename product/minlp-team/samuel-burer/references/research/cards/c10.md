### S065 · Applications of the Lorentz positive cone in nonconvex quadratic optimization (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2609.32017)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–22, appendix pp. 21–22 read in full, not skimmed). First-page check: title and authors "Samuel Burer, Kurt M. Anstreicher" match (p. 1); version read is arXiv:2609.32017v1, 25 Sep 2026 (p. 1).
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Kurt M. Anstreicher (both Dept. of Business Analytics, Univ. of Iowa, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: latest step of the TRS/TTRS thread: SOC-RLT 2013 [ref 5] → Kronecker constraints (Anstreicher 2017) [ref 2] → beta relaxation (Burer 2025) [ref 4] → SEP/LOP cone (this paper), pp. 14–17.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry is a tool, not an application: Hildebrand's LMI for the Lorentz positive cone LOP(n, m), "a long-open question" first solved with an exponential-size LMI and then a polynomial one (p. 2). The question asked is what that cone buys in nonconvex quadratic optimization: (i) a class it solves exactly, minimizing a bilinear objective over two ellipsoids, shown to be a linear problem over SEP(n+1, m+1) and hence polynomial-time (p. 2, §3 p. 6); (ii) a strengthening of the Shor SDP for problems it does not solve exactly: quadratic objective over two ellipsoids, TTRS, and a noxious location problem (p. 2, §4 p. 9). The practical obstacle is stated up front: the polynomial LMI "is too large to be computationally practical for n and m beyond approximately 30" (p. 2), which motivates the separation oracle.
  - Transferable: when a cone gets a new exact description in a neighbouring literature, ask (a) which optimization class becomes exactly a linear problem over it, and (b) what it adds as a valid constraint on the lifted matrix of harder classes; write the computational obstacle into the introduction.
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Key idea: the off-diagonal block Z = [1 xᵀ; y V] of the Shor matrix must lie in SEP(n+1, m+1) whenever x and y lie in unit balls, because SEP with Z₀₀ = 1 is exactly the convex hull of (1; y)(1; x)ᵀ with ‖x‖, ‖y‖ ≤ 1 (Lemma 3, p. 5). Three solution routes for the bilinear case (p. 7): "Full SEP" (the explicit nm × nm SDP (11)), "Lazy SEP" (drop the nm(n−1)(m−1)/4 subspace equalities ⟨J, S⟩ = 0 and add violated ones as needed), and "Dual LOP/TRS", bisection on the single dual variable α with a TRS-based membership oracle, which gives a polynomial-time algorithm "without requiring the use of a framework such as the ellipsoid algorithm" (p. 2). The separation oracle reduces M ∈ LOP to a TRS min{(M₀ᵀx)² − ‖M̄x‖² : ‖x̄‖ ≤ x₀ = 1} ≥ 0 and returns the cut yxᵀ (pp. 4–5); the authors say the existence of such an oracle was suggested by Nemirovski and that they provide "an independent" one (p. 4). For §4 problems the same SEP constraint is added to Shor (15), (23), (28); for TTRS Z is formed as G·Uₓ with y = Ax + b (p. 14); for noxious location the reverse-distance constraints are first convexified via the sphere lift of [ref 6] and turned into Lorentz-cone memberships, then pairwise SEP(4, 4) constraints are added (pp. 17–18). Unchanged: the Shor SDP stays the base relaxation throughout (p. 9).
  - Transferable: if a dual has one scalar variable and the cone has a cheap membership oracle, bisect on the scalar and recover an ε-optimal primal point from the oracle's violating pair (p. 7).
  - Transferable: implement a large linear-subspace constraint lazily: solve without it, add at most a fixed number (here 50) of the most violated equalities per round, stop at a scaled tolerance (pp. 7–8).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: min(n, m) ≥ 2 throughout §2 (p. 2); full-dimensional ellipsoids, reduced to unit balls by an affine change of variables "without loss of generality" (p. 6, p. 10); TTRS with A nonsingular and Q indefinite (p. 13) — note that the paper's own counterexample (24) uses a negative definite Q (p. 16), so the "indefinite" phrase in the problem statement is not a restriction actually used; noxious location: points in the unit disk after a minimum-enclosing-disk rescaling, hull given by Ax ≤ b (p. 17). Polynomial-time claims rest on the TRS being solvable in polynomial time [ref 8] (p. 4) and on the polynomial LMI of Proposition 1 (p. 3), which is cited from Hildebrand, not reproved. A rank-one recovery step for the oracle's SDP uses a random objective on the optimal face, valid "With probability one" (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: (1) Proposition 2 (SEP LMI) derived from Proposition 1 by writing membership in the dual cone as an optimization problem and taking its conic dual via a minimax/Lagrangian chain (3) (pp. 3–4). (2) Separation by TRS with an explicit separating matrix A = yxᵀ, ȳ = −v̄/‖v̄‖, eq. (4) (p. 4). (3) Normalization decomposition (Lemma 3): rescale each rank-one Lorentz atom to (1; yⁱ)(1; xⁱ)ᵀ with weights λᵢ = αᵢβᵢ summing to one (p. 5). (4) Lemma 4: ‖V − yxᵀ‖∗ ≤ √((1−‖x‖²)(1−‖y‖²)) by writing V − yxᵀ as a weighted sum of deviation outer products and applying Cauchy–Schwarz plus a variance identity (pp. 5–6). (5) Corollary 5: equality in Lemma 4 forces the boundary, via ‖·‖F ≤ ‖·‖∗ and a perturbation of V (p. 6). (6) Lemma 6: Pataki's rank bound gives extreme points of Shor of rank ≤ 2 (p. 10); at rank 2 both trace constraints are tight, the Schur complement is rank one, so the Lemma 4 bound holds with equality and Z cannot be interior to SEP; read as "very likely" violated, i.e., a motivation lemma rather than a strict-improvement theorem (p. 10). (7) Lemma 7: SEP dominates the Kronecker (KRON) constraint because every rank-one atom satisfies KRON "by construction" (p. 11); the authors then explain why the same argument fails for TTRS: the atoms do not satisfy yⁱ = Axⁱ + b (p. 15). (8) Lemma 8: reduction of TTRS to Lemma 6 via the linear map G that preserves PSD-ness and rank (p. 14). (9) Bisection bracket from norm inequalities: α ∈ [−‖c‖−‖d‖−‖R‖₂, −max(‖c‖, ‖d‖)] (p. 7). (10) Appendix: KRON implemented by cuts using a block elimination W(Z) to reduce K(Z) ⪰ 0 to a Schur complement K′(Z) ⪰ 0 and lift a violating vector a to a linear cut ⟨C, Z⟩ ≥ 0 (pp. 21–22). (11) Exactness counterexample for TTRS at n = 2 with rational data, optimal value certified by enumerating stationary points and the intersections of the two ellipse boundary arcs, confirmed by Gurobi to nine digits (p. 16); no exact rational certificate is printed, only decimal values. (12) Exact ground truth for noxious location by O(m³) Voronoi-candidate enumeration, argued to be a superset of the vertices of P ∩ Cᵢ (p. 18).
  - Transferable: to show a new constraint dominates an old one, decompose the new cone's elements into rank-one atoms and check the old constraint atom by atom; when coupling constraints (like y = Ax + b) are not inherited by the atoms, say so and drop the dominance claim (pp. 11, 15).
  - Transferable: use Pataki's rank bound plus a norm-equality boundary lemma to argue that the Shor optimum sits on the boundary of the new cone, so the new constraint is likely to cut it off (pp. 6, 10).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: Setup stated in full: Mac mini M4, macOS 26.5.2, MOSEK 11.2.2 via Fusion, Gurobi 13.0.2, Python 3.14.6, NumPy 2.5.1, SciPy 1.18.0, solver defaults with thread counts not fixed, code archived at github.com/sburer/lorentz-positive (p. 8). Families: (a) bilinear, 120 instances over 12 sizes up to 20 × 20 and 15 × 25, with c, d aligned to the leading singular vectors of R plus noise, normalized so z∗ ≤ −1, keeping "the first ten candidates for which the Shor relative gap ... exceeded 0.01" (p. 8); the number of seeds scanned is not reported for this family (p. 8). (b) Max-distance between two ellipsoids, 60 instances n = 2–15 plus 30 larger n = 20, 25, 30, kept only after a rank-one screen (eigenvalue ratio λ₁/λ₂ > 10⁴ discards, then a re-solve with fixed Shor objective and random linear objective) with "up to 16970 seeds" scanned (pp. 11–13). (c) TTRS: the 212 instances of [ref 5] unsolved by SOC-RLT (38/70/104 at n = 5/10/20) plus 30 new instances "filtered to ensure that the Shor relaxation was not tight" (pp. 15–16). (d) Noxious location: regular m-gons (true value one) and 10 random normalized instances per m, plus a hand-picked 4-point instance where SEP nearly closes the gap (pp. 19–20). Baselines: Shor, RLT, KRON, Lazy KRON, beta relaxation, Gurobi (with explicit box bounds and adaptive limit max{5, 3·t_max}) (pp. 8, 12, 17–18). Metrics: median wall-clock time; "tight" if the relative gap (19) to a method-specific recovered feasible point is at most 10⁻⁵; max gap; Lazy diagnostics (rounds, cuts, final violation); conic cross-checks between Full SEP, Lazy SEP and Dual LOP/TRS (max diff ≤ 3.2·10⁻⁷, Table 2, p. 9). Results: Dual LOP/TRS needs 23 oracle calls everywhere and scales to 20 × 20 in 60 ms vs 481 s for Full SEP (Tables 1–2, pp. 8–9); SEP tight on all max-distance and all 212 + 30 TTRS instances, KRON leaves gaps on 3 of 60 (p. 13) and on many of the new TTRS instances (Table 8, p. 16); Gurobi certifies global optimality on no bilinear instance beyond 4 × 4 and no max-distance instance beyond n = 4 (Tables 1, 3, pp. 8, 12), and on TTRS is tight on 69 of 70 (n = 10) and 93 of 104 (n = 20) instances, at 487 s median for n = 20 vs 2.18 s for Lazy SEP (Table 6, p. 15). Losses printed: Lazy SEP slower than Full SEP on small bilinear instances (p. 9) and on regular m-gons (1,218 of 1,224 equations added, about 35 s vs under 2 s, p. 19); SEP's relative improvement over Shor "appears to be decreasing with m" (p. 19); one Full KRON run needed two MOSEK threads after memory failures, disclosed (p. 12).
  - Transferable: compute the gap of each relaxation only against a feasible point recovered from that relaxation's own solution, never against another method's incumbent (p. 12).
  - Transferable: screen out instances whose SDP optimum is only spuriously high-rank by re-solving with the objective fixed and a random linear objective before declaring a gap (p. 12).
  - Transferable: give Gurobi an instance-adaptive time limit tied to the slowest conic method (max{5, 3·t_max}) and report the count of instances it certifies (pp. 8, 12).
  - Transferable: for 2-D problems, get exact ground truth by finite candidate enumeration (Voronoi vertices, bisector–edge intersections, hull vertices) so bound quality is measured against the true optimum (p. 18).

- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contributions: theory (exact SEP formulation and polynomial-time algorithm for bilinear optimization over ellipsoids, pp. 6–7; Lemmas 4–8), algorithm (TRS oracle, Lazy SEP, bisection, KRON cuts in the appendix), computation (four test families with archived code). **Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence: the bilinear problem over two ellipsoids is rewritten exactly as min⟨C, Z⟩ over SEP(n+1, m+1) with Z₀₀ = 1 (eq. (8), exact by Lemma 3, p. 6); the hardness then sits in one cone, attacked by its LMI, by lazy equalities and by its dual separation oracle (pp. 6–7). The Method 1 stop rule also shows: the explicit LMI is too big, so cheaper routes (Lazy SEP, LOP/TRS) are built (pp. 2, 7).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: SEP is tested against the strongest known combination on TTRS: the beta relaxation that already contains "the Shor, RLT, SOC-RLT, and Kronecker constraints" (p. 17), and in noxious location the ladder Shor ≥ RLT ≥ KRON ≥ Full SEP ≥ true, held on every instance (p. 18); the claim is bounded at the smallest dimension with a counterexample (n = 2, p. 16).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: non-exactness for TTRS is shown by a hand-built counterexample with rational data and two independent certifications (enumeration of stationary points and arc intersections, plus Gurobi to nine digits) (p. 16); a counterexample is also published against the author's own 2025 beta relaxation (p. 17); "tight" uses a 10⁻⁵ gap to a recovered feasible point (p. 12). ⚠ variant: no exact rational certificate is printed, only decimals (p. 16).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: Shor-gap filter > 0.01 for bilinear instances (p. 8); rank-one screen with "up to 16970 seeds" (p. 12); the 2013 unsolved TTRS set (212) reused and a harder rung n = 20, 25, 30 added, filtered so Shor is not tight (pp. 15–16). ⚠ variant: the scan counts are not given for the bilinear family or the 30 new TTRS instances (pp. 8, 16), so the "disclose the scan count" step is applied to only one family.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Membership in a "maps cone into cone" set (LOP) reduces to a TRS on the unit ball; the TRS minimizer gives an explicit separating rank-one matrix yxᵀ (p. 4).
- Bisection on a one-dimensional dual with a membership oracle, with initial bracket from norm bounds and primal recovery from the oracle's certificate of non-membership (p. 7).
- Lazy addition of linear-subspace equalities (≤ 50 per round, relative tolerance 10⁻⁷ scaled by ‖S‖F) as a cheap substitute for an O(n²m²)-equation representation (pp. 7–8).
- Atom-by-atom dominance proofs, with an explicit note of where they break (pp. 11, 15).
- Rank-one screen with random-objective re-solve on the optimal face before an instance counts as having a gap (p. 12).
- Exact 2-D ground truth by finite enumeration of candidate points (p. 18).
