### S039 · Convex hull representations for bounded products of variables (Journal of Global Optimization 2021, doi:10.1007/s10898-021-01046-7; arXiv 2004.07233)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-28, arXiv v1 preprint). The reference list (pp. 27-28) was skimmed.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Kurt M. Anstreicher, Kyungchan Park (all three at Iowa Business Analytics, p. 1); Burer is the second author, and the only acknowledgement is Anstreicher's ("Kurt Anstreicher would like to thank Pietro Belotti, Jeff Linderoth and Mohit Tawarmalani", p. 27), which suggests that Anstreicher started the project (inference, not stated). · **Place in trajectory**: continues the Anstreicher-Burer low-dimensional hull line (ref. [3], the 5-variable system from two box-constrained variables, pp. 2, 26). It is listed in SKILL.md Method 2 as a "bounded products" case.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Representing a product of two variables is called "a fundamental problem in global optimization". It arises from bilinear terms and from the factorable decomposition used by BARON (p. 2). The box hull of (x, y, xy) is given by the four RLT/McCormick inequalities (p. 2). The question is the hull of F' = {z = xy, box on (x, y), lz ≤ z ≤ uz} (p. 2). Belotti et al. and Miller et al. already gave a hull with an infinite family of "lifted tangent inequalities" that needs separation (p. 3). Nguyen-Richard-Tawarmalani cover z = x^b1 y^b2 but with more complex representations, and their normalisation lx = ly = 1 makes the important case lx = 0 or ly = 0 "difficult to extract" (p. 3). Santana-Dey give SOC-representability only through a disjunctive lifted formulation (p. 3). The entry point is to turn a known but implicit hull (infinite family or disjunction) into an explicit one in the original variables.
  - Transferable: when a hull is "known" only as an infinite family or a lifted disjunction, set as your target an explicit description in the original variables, and name the special case (here lx = 0) that the general result makes hard to extract.
- **D2 Core idea**: The authors "do not use the inequalities per se" (p. 3). They re-interpret the procedure that builds each lifted tangent inequality, which is tight on a segment joining a point on xy = lz to a point on xy = uz (pp. 5-6). For a given (x, y), solve for the convex-combination weight α as a function of (x, y); then z ≤ αlz + (1-α)uz is one nonlinear constraint equivalent to the whole family on that domain, and it is shown to be SOC (p. 6). Results: with only an upper bound, the hull is RLT + z ≤ uz + z² ≤ uz·xy (Prop. 1, p. 7). With only a lower bound, it is RLT (2a) + z ≥ lz + one SOC with a 2×2 PSD matrix M (Prop. 2, p. 8). With both bounds it needs three SOC constraints on a dissection into "center" and two "side" domains (Prop. 3, pp. 9-12). Only the center constraint (6) is globally valid (p. 12); together with RLT it "appears" to give a very close approximation (p. 14, Figs. 6-7). An exact disjunctive form is mentioned with details omitted (pp. 14-15). Volumes of the hulls versus RLT regions follow (§3.4, pp. 15-17), then general lower bounds lx, ly ≥ 0 (§4: Props. 4-9, pp. 17-24).
  - Transferable: to collapse an infinite family of cuts that are tight on segments, parametrise the segment through the query point, solve for the mixing weight in closed form, and substitute it into the bound on z; the result is a single nonlinear (often conic) inequality.
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: 0 ≤ (lx, ly, lz) < (ux, uy, uz), F' nonempty, rescaled so that ux = uy = 1 (p. 2). Bound tightening (1), lz ≤ lx, ly ≤ uz, holds WLOG but is assumed only from §4 on (pp. 2-3, 17). In §4.3, lxly < lz, since otherwise the lower bound is redundant (p. 20), and lx ≤ ly by symmetry (p. 21). Nonnegative lower bounds are essential throughout. The case-by-case structure depends on where (lx, ly) falls relative to √(lz·uz) and √(lz/uz) (regions A-F, pp. 20-21).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) The hull from [5, 11] (RLT + bounds + lifted tangents) is used as a black box, so each proof only has to show that the new SOC implies every lifted tangent inequality on its domain and is valid (Props. 1-3, pp. 7-12). (ii) Solving a quadratic equation for α and taking "the appropriate root" (pp. 9, 11-12, 19). (iii) A matrix identity rewrites the radicand as a quadratic form in (1-x, 1-y) with M ⪰ 0, which makes the constraint SOC (p. 9). (iv) Global validity of the center cone by the chain (√uz - √xy)(√xy - √lz) ≥ 0 ⇒ (6) (p. 12). (v) For (10), (9) is written as vᵀQv ≤ 0 and symbolic congruence transformations SQSᵀ give a Q̂ with exactly one negative eigenvalue, from which the SOC form is read off. The proof itself needs only the equivalence of (9) and (10) (p. 20). (vi) The (lx, ly) parameter space is dissected into regions A-F, and symmetry halves the casework (pp. 20-22). (vii) Closed-form volume integrals (7)-(8) (p. 15). Props. 6-9 are stated without proofs ("We omit proofs of these results", pp. 21-22).
  - Transferable: when a valid quadratic inequality ought to be conic, look for a congruence that leaves one negative eigenvalue (symbolic algebra helps), and report the discovery route separately from the short proof (p. 20).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (no computational study). The evidence is visual and analytic. 3-D hull plots (Figs. 2-5, 10-12, 15) and cross-sections at z = 0.3, 0.45, 0.6 show the gap between the exact hull and the center-cone approximation (Fig. 7, p. 14). Volume curves follow (Figs. 8-9, pp. 16-17). The sum of the two child hull volumes under spatial branching on z at b is minimised near b ≈ 0.2, where ln(b) = 2(b - 1), and the volume is reduced by about 32.4% (p. 16). At b = 0.3, about 30% of the RLT region is removed (pp. 16-17).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds directly on Belotti-Miller-Namazifar's lifted tangent inequalities and uses "the process by which they are constructed" (p. 3). The contrast with Nguyen et al. is respectful and specific (two named differences, p. 3), and so is the contrast with Santana-Dey ("In contrast, we will show ... directly in the variables (x, y, z)", p. 3). Volume comparison is placed after Anstreicher's SDP-vs-RLT volumes [2] and the Lee-Skipper-Speakman survey [9] (pp. 4, 17). It cites Linderoth's triangle hulls and Dey et al.'s SOC result as neighbouring SOC-representability results (p. 2).
- **D7 Writing**: Short propositions, each followed by a figure of the hull (pp. 7-25). Routine algebra is flagged as "straightforward to verify" (pp. 9, 12). Empirical judgements are hedged: "empirically gives a close approximation" (pp. 4, 7), "It appears" (p. 14), "We would expect" (p. 26). The paper says where a step is hard: the SOC form "is nontrivial" when lx, ly > 0 (p. 19). It then explains "how (10) was obtained" even though the proof does not need it (p. 20). Simpler cases (lx = ly = 0) come first, then general bounds (§§3-4).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory: in every case the hull is RLT + bound(s) on z + at most three SOC constraints (p. 26). There is also a volume-based reading of spatial branching on z (pp. 15-17). Stated limits: several SOC pieces are only locally valid, so an exact global description needs a disjunction, or else one or two globally valid SOCs as an approximation (p. 26). Props. 6-9 have no written proofs (pp. 21-22). Future work: multilinear terms (n > 2), adding a bound on xy to the 5-variable system of [3], and z = xᵀy with x, y ∈ R^n_+ ("would be very significant", p. 26).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: a small substructure that recurs (bilinear terms in factorable codes, p. 2), the goal of an explicit description in the original variables rather than a lifted disjunction (p. 3), case-by-case results by parameter region (pp. 20-24), and the disjunction kept only as a fallback (pp. 14-15, 26). Method 3 variant ⚠: empirical regularities are labelled at their level ("empirically", "It appears", pp. 4, 14), but Props. 6-9 are printed as propositions with proofs omitted (pp. 21-22), with no exact certificate or counterexample search reported. New-pattern candidate: "collapse a cut-generating process into one closed-form conic constraint" (pp. 3, 6). New-pattern candidate: "relaxation volume as a data-independent quality measure and branching rule" (pp. 15-17).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Re-derive a known infinite family of cuts as a single inequality by solving for the segment weight α(x, y) (p. 6).
- Show that a constraint is SOC by writing its radicand as a quadratic form with an explicit PSD matrix (p. 9), or by a symbolic congruence to a single negative eigenvalue (p. 20).
- Turn a hull result into a branching-point rule: compute the child-hull volumes in closed form and minimise their sum over the branching value (pp. 15-16).
- Check an approximation visually with cross-sections at several fixed values of the lifted variable (Fig. 7, p. 14).
**Quotes**:
- "We do not use the inequalities per se, but rather show how the process by which they are constructed can be re-interpreted to generate nonlinear inequalities." (p. 3)
- "If α can be expressed as a function of (x, y) then the result is a single nonlinear constraint that is equivalent to a family of lifted tangent inequalities." (p. 6)

### S050 · Strengthened SDP relaxation for an extended trust region subproblem with an application to optimal power flow (Mathematical Programming 2023, doi:10.1007/s10107-021-01737-9; arXiv 2009.12704)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-31, arXiv v2). The reference list (pp. 29-31) was skimmed.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Anders Eltved (DTU; this work comes from his visit to Iowa in 2019-20, p. 29, and feeds his PhD thesis [12], p. 6) · **Place in trajectory**: continues the SOC-RLT line of Burer-Anstreicher 2013 (ref. [5]) and Anstreicher's KSOC (ref. [1]). SKILL.md cites it under Taste 5 and H3.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: The problem is min xᵀHx + 2gᵀx over the hollow ball r ≤ ‖x‖ ≤ R intersected with an SOC ‖x - c‖ ≤ bᵀx - a whose Hessian matches the ball's (problem (1), p. 2). Bienstock's algorithm makes (1) polynomial-time solvable, but the authors want tight convex relaxations, and "(1) has no known tight convex relaxation" (p. 2). The structure covers a CDT special case and arises in OPF (p. 2). The real trigger was a paper whose cuts nobody had explained: "our initial motivation for this paper was the desire to understand the inequalities in [9] more fully" (Chen-Atamtürk-Oren, fn. 2, p. 6).
  - Transferable: start from a published, application-specific cut that works, and ask which general structure it is a special case of.
- **D2 Core idea**: The baseline is the strongest known combination. RLT and SOCRLT are equivalent once implicit linear constraints are allowed, and KSOC (a Kronecker product of the two SOC matrix forms) dominates them: Rshor ∩ Rksoc ⊆ Rshor ∩ Rsocrlt = Rshor ∩ Rrlt (pp. 3-4). The new inequalities combine three ingredients: the self-duality of SOC, the RLT-type product (R - ‖x‖)(‖x‖ - r) ≥ 0 encoded as an SOC membership (Prop. 1, p. 7), and nonnegative multipliers q(x) (quadratic) and l(x) (linear) that scale the SOC vectors, in the spirit of Lasserre's polynomial multipliers (p. 8). The resulting quartic inequality is relaxed to a quadratic one by replacing q + l with a constant lower bound [q + l]lowbd, and then linearised (Theorem 1, pp. 8-9). Since the cut is linear in r̂ ∈ [0, r], the two extremes dominate (Cor. 1, p. 9). Slab choices give concrete members (§2.1-2.2, pp. 9-11). Separation over q and l "bootstraps" any existing SDP relaxation through weak duality on its dual cone (pp. 6, 18-20).
  - Transferable: when multiplying valid inequalities produces a degree-4 term, replace the offending nonnegative factor by a constant lower bound over the feasible set to drop back to a quadratic you can linearise (p. 8).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Identity Hessian shared by the ball and the SOC (p. 2; named as the main limitation, p. 28). [c]max is precomputed by binary search on a convex subproblem that drops the nonconvex r ≤ ‖x‖, which may not return the smallest value (p. 7). F must be full-dimensional, with a known interior point x̂, for the separation problem to be bounded (p. 19). For OPF, L22 > 0 and U12 > L12 are assumed and called "realistic for power networks" (p. 14).
- **D4 Proof devices**: (i) SOC self-duality: the inner product of two SOC vectors is nonnegative, which gives the base cut (7) (p. 6). (ii) A scaled SOC membership derived from (R - ‖x‖)(‖x‖ - r) ≥ 0 (Prop. 1, p. 7). (iii) Degree reduction by a constant lower bound (Theorem 1, pp. 8-9). (iv) Dominance by extremes, from linearity in a parameter (Cor. 1, p. 9). (v) OPF equivalence: a rotation in C fixes z2 real (Prop. 3, pp. 13-14), and a 2-D polyhedral cone becomes an SOC (Lemma 1, p. 14; Prop. 4, pp. 14-15). Chen et al.'s cuts (21) are then shown to be (18) (Theorem 2, pp. 15-16), with the "Simple, although tedious" algebra checked by Matlab code in the repository (fn. 3, p. 16). (vi) Locally valid SOC cuts obtained by minimising over the slab direction s on a sign-pattern domain D_IJ (Theorem 3, p. 17). (vii) A boundedness lemma for the truncated dual cone via its recession cone (fn. 4, p. 19). (viii) Numerical non-implication: minimise the new cut's slack over Rshor ∩ Rksoc, and a negative optimum (-0.1248, -0.088562) shows that the cut is not implied (Examples 1-2, pp. 5, 17-18).
  - Transferable: to show that a new inequality is not implied by a relaxation, minimise (inequality right-hand side minus left-hand side) over that relaxation on a tiny instance and report the negative value and the solution (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**: Matlab R2019a, CVX, MOSEK 9.1, one Xeon E5-2650v4 core, 2 GB (p. 21). Full run times are not reported ("we are most interested in the strength of the added cuts"), but sample times at n = 10 are given: Shor 0.6 s, Shor ∩ KSOC 50 s, one separation 64 s (p. 22). The random generator guarantees a known interior point (p. 22; full procedure on GitHub, fn. 5). τsep = -10⁻⁵, and "exact" means λ1/λ2 > τrank = 10⁴ (one signal, p. 22). Gap closure is measured against the best of 100 fmincon local solves, which over-approximates the true closure, as the paper says (p. 23). The design: 15,000 instances for each n = 2..10 in each of three cases (general; r = a = 0, c = 0; TTRS), each bootstrapped from Rshor and from Rshor ∩ Rksoc (p. 23). Results: on the general case at n = 2, the cuts improve more than a third of the instances where Shor is inexact and close about 9% (p. 24, Table 1). From Rshor ∩ Rksoc, the cuts improve nothing for n ≥ 4 (p. 24, Table 2). The special case shows no drop with n (p. 25, Tables 3-4). TTRS looks like the general case (pp. 26-27, Tables 5-6). Two extra tests: 110,000 further n = 2 instances, of which 109,938 were exact under Rshor ∩ Rksoc and the remaining 62 were closed with about 3 cuts on average (p. 25); and 100 instances "found ... by generating many random objectives", all closed by the locally valid cuts alone (p. 26). The number of seeds scanned for those 100 is not given.
- **D6 Positioning**: The paper is built on SOCRLT (Sturm-Zhang, Ye-Zhang, Burer-Anstreicher) and on KSOC (Anstreicher 2017), and Jiang-Li [13] are credited for comparisons (p. 4). Chen-Atamtürk-Oren's OPF cuts are re-read as "actually just special cases of our cuts" (abstract p. 1; §3.1, pp. 11-16). Bienstock's polynomial-time algorithm is acknowledged, and the paper says its own aim is different (tight relaxations, p. 2). Lasserre's multipliers are named as the analogous idea (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing**: A two-line motivating inequality (6) and a 2-D example come before the general theory (p. 5). The code repository is announced in the introduction (p. 6). Example 3 walks through three rounds of separation with the computed q, l and the final rank-1 solution (pp. 20-21). The implementation is called "rudimentary" (p. 22). Trends are hedged ("suggesting", p. 24; "seem", p. 25). Computational regularities become labelled conjectures (Conjectures 1-2, pp. 25-26). The conclusion restates the claim modestly: the cuts strengthen "the strongest known SDP relaxation ... especially in low dimensions" (p. 28).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: The contribution is a polynomial-time separable cut class (theory and algorithm), an OPF application (a unifying explanation of Chen et al.'s cuts), and new locally valid SOC cuts for {x ≥ 0 : ‖x‖ ≤ 1} (p. 28). Limits are stated concretely: identity Hessians only, with a workaround sketched for a general J (pp. 28-29); the cuts weaken as dimension grows (pp. 24, 28); for general F the gap is not fully closed (p. 28). Future work: streamline separation, test higher dimensions, find other applications, and use the self-duality of other cones (p. 29).

**Method links**: Method 2 evidence ✅: the baseline is the intersection of the strongest known relaxations (Shor ∩ KSOC ⊇ ... SOCRLT = RLT, p. 4); it is beaten on a substructure that recurs in OPF (pp. 11-16); and the n = 2 case is where the hull is conjectured (p. 25). Method 3 evidence ✅: regularities seen on 110,000 and 100 instances are printed as Conjectures 1-2, not theorems (pp. 25-26), and the OPF algebra is machine-checked (fn. 3, p. 16). Method 3 variant ⚠: exactness here rests on one signal, the eigenvalue ratio > 10⁴ (p. 22). The two-signal rule SKILL.md cites is from the later arXiv 2303.01624. Method 4 variant ⚠: the main pools are unfiltered random instances, though the tables report counts conditional on "Inexact initial" (pp. 23-27). One filtered hard pool appears (100 instances where Shor ∩ KSOC fails, p. 26) without the scan count. Heuristic H3 evidence (the Chen et al. cuts as special cases, pp. 1, 6, 11) and H10 evidence (code repository with examples and results, p. 6). New-pattern candidate: "bootstrap separation through the dual cone of whatever relaxation is current" (pp. 6, 18-20). New-pattern candidate: "self-duality of a cone as a cut generator" (pp. 6-8, 29).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Separate a parametrised family of quadratic-multiplier cuts by putting each multiplier's nonnegativity certificate in the dual cone of the current relaxation (weak duality), then truncate with an interior point so the problem is bounded (pp. 18-19).
- Use a known generator design that plants an interior point x̂ and sets a = bᵀx̂ - ‖x̂ - c‖ - θ to guarantee feasibility with nonempty interior (p. 22).
- Report each instance as exact-initial / improved / closed / no improvement, with average cuts and gap closure for each category (pp. 23-24).
- Verify a "tedious" symbolic equivalence with code in the public repository and cite the file (fn. 3, p. 16).
**Quotes**:
- "Indeed, our initial motivation for this paper was the desire to understand the inequalities in [9] more fully." (p. 6)
- "We believe this is strong evidence to support the following conjecture as well:" (p. 26)

### S055 · A strengthened SDP relaxation for quadratic optimization over the Stiefel manifold (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 2024, doi:10.1007/s10957-023-02168-6; arXiv 2208.03125)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-16, arXiv v1). The reference list (pp. 14-16) was skimmed. Figures 1-4 are images not in the text layer; only their captions and the prose describing them were read.
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Kyungchan Park (Iowa Business Analytics, p. 1); thanks to Kurt Anstreicher and Laura Balzano (p. 14) · **Place in trajectory**: follows Gilman-Burer-Balzano's block-diagonal Stiefel SDP (ref. [15], p. 2, later published as S048) and applies Anstreicher's Kronecker idea (ref. [4]), as S050 does.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: (QPS): min uᵀHu + 2gᵀu over u = vec(U), U ∈ St(n, p) (p. 1). For p = 1 this is the trust-region subproblem (TRS), which is polynomial; for general p it is NP-hard (p. 2). The applications are listed (Procrustes, subspace tracking, clustering, electronic structure, p. 2). The entry point is a recent relaxation by Burer's own group that is strong for block-diagonal H (ref. [15]). The question is whether a further strengthening helps for general H (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: A ladder of three relaxations of non-decreasing strength (p. 3). Shor is the trace constraints plus Y ⪰ 0 (p. 4). DiagSum adds Σ_j X_jj ⪯ I_n, adapted from [15] to general H through UᵀU = I ⇒ [I Uᵀ; U I] ⪰ 0 ⇔ UUᵀ ⪯ I (pp. 4-5). Kron takes the Kronecker product of that redundant LMI with itself and linearises it to M(u, X) ⪰ 0, of size (n + p)² (pp. 5-6). The authors call the contribution an observation: "the Kronecker-product idea applies in this setting" (p. 3). Variants were explored and dropped: symmetric permutations give only a permuted M (p. 7), and a Hadamard-product relaxation (4) never strengthened DiagSum "through extensive numerical experiments" (p. 7).
  - Transferable: take a redundant LMI your problem implies and multiply it by itself (Kronecker or Hadamard), then linearise in the lifted variable. Also test the cheaper Hadamard principal submatrix before paying for the full Kronecker form (pp. 5-7).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: n ≥ p (p. 9). Data H and g general, except that the block-diagonal class uses g = 0, following [15] (fn. 1, p. 5; p. 9). There are no theorems, so there are no technical assumptions beyond validity.
- **D4 Proof devices**: Validity of each rung by Schur complements (chain (2), p. 4) and the PSD-ness of Kronecker products (p. 5). An explicit linearisation through the basis matrices K_ji (p. 6). A symmetry argument that every symmetric permutation gives the same constraint (p. 7). The Hadamard LMI (4) is a principal submatrix of M, so its strength lies between DiagSum and Kron (p. 7). Primal bounds come from projecting mat(u) onto St(n, p) by the thin SVD (U0V0ᵀ), then running Manopt trust-regions, which gives a certified two-sided gap (pp. 7-8). No theorems are proved.
  - Transferable: when the feasible set is a manifold, get the upper bound by projecting the SDP's first-order part onto the manifold (polar factor), then polishing with a manifold local solver, so every instance has a provable gap (pp. 7-8).
- **D5 Experiments**: Four synthetic classes: random Gaussian; block-diagonal Gaussian (unlike [15], whose blocks were negative semidefinite); random orthogonal Procrustes; random Penrose regression (pp. 9-10). n ∈ {6, 9, 12} and p ∈ {2, ⌈n/2⌉, n-1}, with 1,000 instances per (n, p) per class (p. 10). "Solves" means relative gap < 10⁻⁴ (p. 10). Setup: MATLAB R2021b, Mosek 9.3.10, Manopt 7.0, YALMIP for Kron, one Xeon E5-2680v4 core, 8 GB (pp. 10-11). The YALMIP model was checked two ways (dualize, and Mosek's reported problem size, p. 11). Gurobi 9.5.1 was tried in preliminary tests and dropped: at (6, 3), its gaps after 15 minutes were "significantly worse" than the SDPs' within 1 second (p. 8). Times (Table 1, p. 11): Kron takes 250.233 s at (12, 11) against Shor's 0.707 s. Gaps are shown as frequency-polygon histograms (Figs. 1-4, pp. 12-14). Kron is strongest on random instances, shows "little to no improvement" over DiagSum on block-diagonal ones, and solves nearly all Procrustes and Penrose instances, with "a few instances not solved" (p. 12).
- **D6 Positioning**: Builds on his own group's DiagSum [15] and Anstreicher's Kronecker constraints [4] (p. 2). Jiang-Li [18] are credited for the Hadamard approach (p. 7). Nemirovski [21] is cited for NP-hardness (p. 2). The global solver is mentioned once and set aside on evidence (p. 8).
- **D7 Writing**: Short and plain. The cost trade-off is in the abstract ("although at the expense of longer solution times", p. 1). The contribution claim is modest ("conceptual contribution ... observation", p. 3). An unproven implication is labelled as such ("it appears that DiagSum implies (4), although we have so far not been able to prove this", p. 7). Losses are stated in the introduction (no gain on block-diagonal instances, p. 2).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A new relaxation and an empirical comparison. Limits: much higher solve time (p. 11), no gain on block-diagonal H (pp. 2, 12), and some Procrustes/Penrose instances not solved, which might be solver inaccuracy (pp. 12-14). Future work: get Kron's strength more cheaply (cutting planes, PSD on critical principal submatrices of M); "either exhibit analytically an instance for which Kron is inexact or to prove that Kron is tight" on Procrustes/Penrose; extend the optimality certificate of [15] to general H (pp. 12-14).

**Method links**: Method 2 variant ⚠: the same "stack the known relaxations, then recombine an ingredient" move (Shor ⊆ DiagSum ⊆ Kron, a Kronecker product of a redundant LMI, pp. 3-6), but on a whole problem rather than a small substructure, with no hull characterisation and no dimension-by-dimension result. Method 3 evidence ✅: computational regularities are labelled as unproven (p. 7), and tightness on Procrustes/Penrose is passed on as an open question whose possible answers are a counterexample or a proof, with numerical inexactness admitted (pp. 12-14). Method 4 variant ⚠: unfiltered random pools of 1,000 per cell, with no hard-pool engineering (pp. 9-10). Method 5 evidence ✅ (weak): the paper is a combination of a known theorem (Kronecker PSD) and a known relaxation, tested computationally with the theory left open (pp. 3, 12-14). Heuristic H4 evidence (cost reported per rung, intermediate Hadamard rung considered, pp. 7, 11). H5 evidence (a general global solver run directly, then dropped on evidence, p. 8). H8 evidence (the block-diagonal loss is printed, pp. 2, 12).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Before leaving out a general-purpose global solver as a baseline, run it on one small cell, report its gap after a fixed time against yours, and state the reason for leaving it out (p. 8).
- When a modelling layer (YALMIP) builds your constraint, check that the model passed to the solver is efficient: compare against the dualised form and check the reported problem size (p. 11).
- Use a grid of (n, p) with p ∈ {2, ⌈n/2⌉, n-1} to cover thin, square-ish and near-square shapes (p. 10).
**Quotes**:
- "Our conceptual contribution in this paper is the observation that the Kronecker-product idea applies in this setting" (p. 3)
- "Said differently, it appears that DiagSum implies (4), although we have so far not been able to prove this." (p. 7)

### S067 · Convex Hulls in Quadratic Space (Encyclopedia of Optimization 2024, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-54621-2_892-1)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1-6, the published entry, including the reference list).
**Role**: core (encyclopedia entry; the expository voice of Boshi Yang and Sam Burer) · **Coauthors**: Boshi Yang (Clemson, p. 1; a former coauthor, see refs. 4 and 15, p. 6) · **Place in trajectory**: a reference entry that collects the quadratic-hull results of Burer's TRS line (Burer-Anstreicher 2013, Burer-Yang 2015, Yang-Anstreicher-Burer 2018) together with others' results (pp. 4-6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Convexification turns a nonconvex problem into a convex one and can be applied to substructures to get better bounds (p. 1). The integer hull of integer programming is the analogy given (p. 1). For a quadratic objective, the relevant object is the hull in the space of (x, xxᵀ), the "quadratic hull", which "plays an important role in solving" QCQPs (pp. 1-2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Define C(F) = conv{(x, xxᵀ) : x ∈ F}. Then QCQP equals minimising Q • X + 2cᵀx over C(F) or its closure (eq. (4), p. 2). A one-dimensional example with a figure shows the reformulation (F = {0.5 ≤ x² ≤ 4}, pp. 2-3). Explicit descriptions are in general as hard as the QCQP itself, so the Shor relaxation S(F) is the standard outer approximation (p. 2). The entry then catalogues the known cases where C(F) = S(F) or is S(F) plus RLT/SOC-RLT constraints (pp. 3-6).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: F nonempty throughout (p. 3). Compactness gives a closed C(F) (p. 2). Each special case carries its own condition, which the entry names. These include the two-sided Slater condition (Xia et al.), "A ≠ 0" (Joyce-Yang), parallel or non-intersecting linear constraints (Burer-Anstreicher; Burer-Yang), proper triangles, tetrahedra and quadrilaterals (Anstreicher-Burer), and the boundary of H contained in F (Joyce-Yang) (pp. 3-6).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Expository; devices are reported, not proved. SOC-RLT: multiply a nonnegative linear slack into the SOC form of the ball, then linearise (p. 4). RLT on (b - Ax)(b - Ax)ᵀ ≥ 0 (p. 6). The m × m completely positive cone is SDP-representable for m ≤ 4, which gives explicit hulls for polytopes with at most four facets (p. 5). A small counterexample shows why the alternative quadratic expression is needed when A = 0: for F = [-2, 1], the hull is {x + X ≤ 2, X ≥ x²}, which differs from S(F) (p. 4).
  - Transferable: when stating that a condition (here A ≠ 0) is needed, give the one-line counterexample right after it (p. 4).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a.
- **D6 Positioning**: Neutral survey voice. Burer's own results (refs. 1-4, 15) appear next to Sturm-Zhang, Xia-Wang-Sheu, Pong-Wolkowicz, Jin et al., and Joyce-Yang, with no ranking (pp. 3-6). It cross-links to the entries on copositive optimization and RLT (p. 6).
- **D7 Writing**: The cases run from easiest to hardest: one quadratic constraint, a ball with linear constraints, only linear constraints, then non-intersecting constraints (pp. 3-6). A picture comes first (Fig. 1, p. 3). Each case is a displayed formula for C(F) with its condition. It ends on a short open-problem sentence (p. 6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Expository: a map of the cases where the quadratic hull is known. Its stated limit is the state of the field: "there are only a few cases for which the quadratic hull is known explicitly" (p. 6), and new techniques are needed (p. 6).

**Method links**: Method 1 evidence ✅: QCQP is packaged as linear optimisation over one convex object, the quadratic hull C(F) (eq. (4), p. 2), and the open question becomes a property of that set (p. 6). Method 2 evidence ✅: the catalogue is a list of small substructures (one quadratic, ball + 1-2 linear constraints, small polytopes) whose hull is Shor plus explicit RLT/SOC-RLT pieces (pp. 3-6). Heuristic H9 evidence: an entry-point text, easiest to hardest with geometry first (pp. 2-6).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Present a catalogue of convex-hull results as "hull = Shor + named extra constraints + the condition that makes it exact", case by case, so a reader can see which ingredient each case adds (pp. 3-6).
- Open with a 1-D example whose lifted hull can be drawn, so the reformulation is visible before the general theory (pp. 2-3).
**Quotes**:
- "In general, determining an explicit expression for C(F) or C(F) is at least as difficult as solving the corresponding QCQP." (p. 2)
- "Additional novel techniques are needed to generate strong valid inequalities for quadratic hulls." (p. 6)
