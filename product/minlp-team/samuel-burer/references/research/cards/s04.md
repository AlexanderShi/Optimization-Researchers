### S059 · A branch-and-bound algorithm for instrumental variable quantile regression (Mathematical Programming Computation 2017, DOI 10.1007/s12532-017-0117-2)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–24 main text and conclusion; appendix proof of Lemma 1 read pp. 24–25; the rest of the appendix pp. 25–27 and the reference list not read). First-page check: title and authors "Guanglin Xu · Samuel Burer" match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Guanglin Xu (Dept. of Management Sciences, Univ. of Iowa, p. 1); problem and instance generator supplied by Alexandre Belloni (acknowledgements, p. 24) · **Position in trajectory**: a statistics application solved with the 2008–12 "B&B on a convex relaxation" toolkit; first of two Xu–Burer papers in this batch (see S044).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Entry came from a statistician: IVQR, in which the estimate x̂₁ should make the instrument coefficients x₂ = 0 optimal in an ℓ₁ regression LP (1) (p. 3), a "partial inverse optimization problem" (p. 4). The existing enumeration of [7] works for n₁ = 1 but suffers the curse of dimensionality for larger n₁ (p. 3). Because x₂ = 0 may be unattainable, the target becomes the x₂ of minimum Euclidean norm, giving a convex QP with complementarity constraints (CQPCC), "generally NP-hard" (p. 3; formulation (8), p. 6).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**: Write the LP optimality conditions of (1)–(2) with complementary slackness (3)–(6), make x₁ a variable and minimize ‖x₂‖² (pp. 5–6); eliminate y with slacks to get (9) (p. 7). Root relaxation = drop all complementarities; it is bounded below by 0, so B&B needs no bounds on x₃± (p. 7). Branch on one complementarity [x₃⁺]ᵢ[s⁺]ᵢ = 0 by fixing either factor to zero, "analogous to branching on a 0–1 binary variable" (p. 12). Implementation choices (pp. 13–15): CPLEX dual-simplex CQP for warm re-optimization after added constraints; maximum-violation branching (like most-fractional); initial GUB from least squares then LP (1); warm starts from parent solutions; implied complementarities ([s⁺]ᵢ = 0 forces [x₃⁻]ᵢ = 0); a new "bi-priority" node rule: depth-first among nodes with LB ≤ v̂, best-bound otherwise, with v̂ = 0 because IVQR optimal values "are typically very close to 0" (p. 15).
  - Transferable: when complementarity variables are unbounded but the objective is bounded below, branch on complementarities with linear equalities instead of writing a big-M MIP, so no a priori bounds are needed (pp. 7, 12).
  - Transferable: if a problem class has a known typical optimal value (here near 0), use it as the threshold of a two-list node selection (depth-first below the threshold, best-bound above) to find a good incumbent early without letting the tree explode (pp. 14–15).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**: Median quantile u = 1/2 only (p. 2); m > n₂ ≥ n₁ from the statistics literature (p. 3). Bounds for the competitor's big-M are asymptotic: x₃± ≤ 5‖b‖∞, justified for a light-tailed normal error (Example 1, pp. 8–10) and a heavy-tailed example (Example 2, pp. 10–11); the authors warn the bound "might cut off some or all optimal solutions in practice" for finite m (p. 11).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Feasibility of (8) by LP strong duality (Proposition 1, p. 6). Lemma 1: triangle inequality and a union bound (pp. 8, 24–25); Lemma 2: Gaussian tail bounds for maxima (p. 8); Example 1: split the event into α + γ + δ and use Azuma's inequality plus Lemma 2 (pp. 9–10); Example 2: Hölder plus Markov (pp. 10–11).
- **D5 Experiments**: 500/300/100 random instances (m = 50/100/150, n₁ = n₂ = 5) from Belloni's generator (Algorithm 1, Table 1, p. 16); against Couenne 0.4, QPBB is faster on 454/500, 283/300 and 99/100, and Couenne times out on 7 and 6 (p. 17); against CPLEX 12.4 on the big-M MIQP (13) with the derived bounds, QPBB is faster on 343/500 and CPLEX proves optimality on only 159/300 and 17/100 (pp. 19–21); big-M sensitivity at M, 2M, 5M, 10M with M taken from QPBB's own optimum (pp. 21–22); failed variants (strong branching, pseudo-cost, depth-first, breadth-first) reported (p. 23). Optimal values agree across solvers to relative 10⁻⁶ (p. 15).
  - Transferable: when a competitor needs data your method does not (here variable bounds), derive defensible bounds for it, give the same bounds to your own code "for a fair comparison", then run a sensitivity study on the competitor's parameter (pp. 19, 21–22).
  - Transferable: treat "solved" as confirmed only when independent solvers agree on the optimal value to a stated relative accuracy (p. 15).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contribution: a CQPCC formulation and a special-purpose B&B (algorithmic plus computational), and statistical bounds "of independent interest" (p. 4). Limits and future work (p. 24): only the 2-norm objective (1-norm or ∞-norm would give LP subproblems); the trade-off between ‖x₂‖ and estimation error was not studied. The m > n₂ > n₁ case is said to give "the same" results but is not shown (p. 16).

**Method links**: Method 5 ✅ evidence: benchmark against the best public global codes (Couenne, CPLEX) and print the variants that failed and why (strong branching gives no LB information because node values are ≈ 0, p. 23). Method 3 ✅ evidence (two signals for "solved"): optimal values cross-verified by the other algorithms to six significant figures (p. 15). Method 4 ⚠ variant: the instance pool is a size ladder (small/medium/large) of unfiltered random instances from a domain generator, with no hardness filter (p. 16). New-pattern candidate: "fair-comparison handicap": give the baseline what it needs and apply the same restriction to your own method (pp. 19, 21).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Branch on complementarity pairs like binaries, with no big-M (p. 12).
- Threshold-based bi-priority node selection when the typical optimal value is known (pp. 14–15).
- Derive bounds for a competitor's big-M, impose them on yourself too, and report the competitor's sensitivity to M (pp. 19–22).
**Quotes**:
- "However, we have found by testing code supplied by the authors that this general-purpose algorithm was not competitive with the special-purpose algorithm that we will present in this paper." (p. 4)
- "In other words, when an algorithm claims to have solved an instance optimally, it is independently verified by the other algorithms up to six significant figures." (p. 15)

### S036 · Quadratic programs with hollows (Mathematical Programming 2018, DOI 10.1007/s10107-017-1157-0)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–13, all sections and references). First-page check: title and authors "Boshi Yang · Kurt Anstreicher · Samuel Burer" match (p. 1); "SHORT COMMUNICATION" (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Boshi Yang (Clemson, p. 1; "student Yang" in SKILL.md Method 3), Kurt Anstreicher (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: follows the TRS-with-cuts line (Burer & Anstreicher 2013 [7]; Burer & Yang 2015 [9]) and answers an open case of Burer & Letchford [8] (p. 10).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Is minimizing a quadratic over F with disjoint ellipsoidal "hollows" removed (G = F ∩ H) harder than over F? The entry is a simple intuition: if the optimum over F falls inside a hollow, q must be convex and the optimum over G lies on that ellipsoid's boundary, an equality-constrained TRS (pp. 2–3). The note "formalizes this intuition" through SDP relaxations (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Theorem 1: if a valid SDP relaxation R(F) is tight for q, then R(F) ∩ L(H), which adds only l linear constraints Wₖ•X + 2wₖᵀx + ωₖ ≥ 0, is tight over G (pp. 5–6). Theorem 2: R(F) is tight for all quadratics q if and only if R(F) = C(F) (p. 6). Corollary 1: C(G) = C(F) ∩ L(H) (p. 7), proved twice: from Theorems 1–2, and directly via a rank-one decomposition plus induction on l (pp. 7–8). §4 transfers the picture to integrality: elongated hollows approach the one-variable splits for x₁ ∈ Z, which suggests that MIQ₁,₁ is PSD plus splits; this is proved directly for MIQ₁,ₙ₂ (Theorem 3, p. 12), the open case of [8] (pp. 10–11).
- **D3 Assumptions**: F bounded, full-dimensional, quadratically constrained, possibly nonconvex (p. 2); each Eₖ ⊆ F with pairwise disjoint interiors, checkable by lm + l(l+1)/2 TRSs (p. 5). Both conditions are shown necessary by Counterexamples 1–2 (pp. 8–10). A "valid SDP relaxation" need not have a known PSD representation (footnote 2, p. 3).
- **D4 Proof devices**: Extreme points of C(F) have the form (x, xxᵀ), so linear objectives over C(F) are exact (Proposition 1, p. 4). Separation lemma in minimization form for the only-if direction of Theorem 2 (p. 6). Sturm–Zhang rank-one decomposition with yᵖᵀVyᵖ = 0 to place the atoms on bd(E₁) (pp. 7–8). Pataki's rank bound for extreme points (p. 12). Lemma 2, completing the square in j (pp. 11–12). Necessity shown by explicit 2-D counterexamples with numbers: v(q, G) = −0.25 vs SDP −1.575; 1.21 vs 0.86 (pp. 8–10).
- **D5 Experiments**: n/a (no computational study; counterexample values are computed by hand).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Theory only: a hull description and a tightness-transfer theorem that applies "uniformly" to most known TRS extensions (p. 5), and it upgrades a result of [9] to cuts that intersect on the boundary (p. 7). Left open: a proof of §4 through §2 is blocked by the unboundedness of Z × R; "we believe a proof based on Sect. 2 should be possible" (p. 11).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence: the object is the whole lifted hull C(·) = conv{(x, xxᵀ)}, and the result is a statement about that one convex set: adding l linear constraints maps C(F) to C(G) (pp. 4, 7). Method 2 ✅ evidence: an explicit, non-disjunctive hull for a small mixed-integer substructure (MIQ₁,ₙ₂, Theorem 3, p. 12), with the boundary marked by certified counterexamples where the assumptions fail (stop rule, pp. 8–10). Method 3 ✅ evidence: the regularity is first stated as a conjecture from a picture (Fig. 5), then proved directly, and the paper says which route was not completed (p. 11). New-pattern candidate: "analogy across areas": a geometric limit (hollows elongated into slabs) used to guess a hull in a seemingly unrelated problem (pp. 10–11).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove a hull identity by combining "tight for one objective" with the equivalence "tight for all quadratic objectives ⇔ R = C" (Theorem 2, p. 6); it turns objective-wise tightness into a set equality.
- Give a second, constructive proof by rank-one decomposition so that the result connects to the standard hull toolkit (pp. 7–8).
- Pair each assumption of a theorem with a minimal 2-D counterexample showing it is needed (pp. 8–10).
**Quotes**:
- "Inspired by these results, we resolve a related question in a seemingly unrelated area, mixed-integer nonconvex quadratic programming." (p. 1)
- "While we believe a proof based on Sect. 2 should be possible, we have instead opted to prove the generalization of this conjecture to arbitrary n2 using a direct approach that closely mimics the alternate proof of Corollary 1 presented in Sect. 2." (p. 11)

### S044 · A data-driven distributionally robust bound on the expected optimal value of uncertain mixed 0-1 linear programming (Computational Management Science 2018, DOI 10.1007/s10287-018-0298-9; read as arXiv:1708.07603v1)
**Read level**: full, arXiv v1 (pp. 1–25 main text and conclusion; acknowledgements and references pp. 26–29 skimmed). First-page check: title and authors "Guanglin Xu, Samuel Burer" match (p. 1). Version note: the published CMS version was not read; pages are arXiv v1 pages.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Guanglin Xu (Univ. of Iowa, p. 1); thanks Anstreicher, Lin, Zuluaga, Yang (p. 26) · **Position in trajectory**: applies the 2009 copositive representation [11] to data-driven DRO, as part of the 2016–18 copositive robust/DRO bounds listed under Method 1.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Bound the expected optimal value of a mixed 0-1 LP whose objective coefficients are random with an unknown distribution observed only through samples (p. 1). Gap in prior work: moment-based ambiguity sets (Bertsimas et al.; Natarajan et al.'s cross-moment CP model) do not converge to the true distribution as the sample size grows (p. 3). Entry: a 2-Wasserstein ball around the empirical distribution (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Decompose the transport plan by conditioning on each sample (Lemma 1, p. 10), dualize to inf over λ ≥ 0 of λε² + (1/N)Σ supξ (v(ξ) − λ‖ξ − ξ̂ᵢ‖²) (eqs. (6)–(8), p. 10). Each inner sup is a mixed 0-1 bilinear program (10), which becomes a copositive program over CP(Ξ̂ × Rⁿ₊) by Burer's 2009 result under Assumption 3 (pp. 11–12); conic duality gives (17) (p. 12). An inner approximation IA = {S + M} of the copositive cone (RLT-type for polyhedral Ξ̂, exact for a second-order cone) gives a tractable SDP upper bound (21), Theorem 1 (pp. 13–14). The radius is chosen by a cross-validation-style "empirical confidence level" (Algorithm 1, pp. 8–9) instead of the conservative theoretical radius (p. 8).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Assumptions 2–5 (p. 9): X nonempty and bounded; Assumption 3 (Ax = b, x ≥ 0 ⇒ 0 ≤ xⱼ ≤ 1 for binaries), the "key assumption" of the 2009 CP paper, said to be easily enforced (p. 9); Ξ convex, closed and tractable; Ξ bounded, stated separately "to highlight its role in proving the exactness" (p. 10); without it (17) is still an upper bound (pp. 12–13). Light-tailed P (Assumption 1, p. 7) is needed only for the theoretical radius.
- **D4 Proof devices**: Max-min inequality plus strong duality for moment problems (Shapiro, Prop. 3.4) (p. 10); a redundant ball constraint I•Z ≤ r gives Slater for the copositive dual by taking ρ large (p. 12); Lemma 2 (inner approximation is copositive) checked term by term (p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments**: Three small applications (expected maximum of n = 3 lognormals, a 7-arc project network, a 4-item knapsack) with N = 10 to 1280, 100 trials each, a 100,000-sample Monte Carlo reference, and a moment-based baseline given the exact moments ("this choice favors MB", p. 14). The Wasserstein bound tightens as N grows while the moment-based bound stays flat (pp. 17, 21, 23); empirical coverage is ≥ 0.97 (Tables 1–3, pp. 18, 22, 25); for the knapsack the bound does not converge because the SDP relaxation is not tight (p. 24). MOSEK 8 via YALMIP (p. 14).
  - Transferable: when your claim is about a trend (a bound improving with data), give the baseline its most favourable information (exact moments) so that the trend cannot be blamed on a weakened baseline (p. 14).
  - Transferable: choose a data-driven radius by resampling (train/validate splits, coverage percentage per candidate radius) and then report the realized coverage on fresh trials (pp. 8–9, 18).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: A copositive reformulation and an SDP bound for a Wasserstein DRO expected-value problem (pp. 4, 25). Limits: tiny instances (n = 3, 4; 7 arcs, pp. 15, 20, 23); no convergence for integer problems when the relaxation is loose (p. 24). The conclusion is a summary only, with no future directions (p. 25). Positioned against Hanasusanto & Kuhn: max-max rather than max-min, and natural SDP approximations rather than a hierarchy (p. 4).

**Method links**: Method 1 ✅ evidence: the hard inner problem is rewritten exactly over one cone CP(Ξ̂ × Rⁿ₊), and the work then goes into an approximation of that cone (pp. 11–14); the 2009 hypothesis that makes the reformulation exact reappears as Assumption 3 (p. 9). Method 4 ⚠ variant: the testbed is not a hard pool; instead the baseline is deliberately advantaged (p. 14). New-pattern candidate: "fair-comparison handicap" (also in S059).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Per-sample conditioning plus Lagrangian duality turns a Wasserstein DRO bound into N copies of a deterministic inner problem, each open to a conic reformulation (pp. 10–11).
- Add a redundant norm-ball constraint to obtain a Slater point in the conic dual (p. 12).
**Quotes**:
- "Actually, this choice favors MB, but the goal of our experiments is to demonstrate that our approach provides a valid upper bound" (p. 14)
- "This is due to the fact that problem (21) is a relaxation of problem (17) and the fact the relaxation is not tight in this example." (p. 24)

### S062 · Three methods for robust grading (European Journal of Operational Research 2019, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2018.06.019)
**Read level**: full (pp. 1–8, all sections and references). First-page check: title and authors "Samuel Burer, Veronica Piccialli" match (p. 1); section "Innovative Applications of O.R." (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Veronica Piccialli (Rome Tor Vergata; research started during Burer's visit to Rome, p. 8) · **Position in trajectory**: an applied side project; it reuses MILP disjunctive modelling and a tailored spatial B&B outside the QP line.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: From his own teaching: choose letter-grade breakpoints (a, b) so that final letter grades are not overly sensitive to uncertainty in assignment grades (pp. 1–2). The authors claim "little to no literature" on algorithmic grading under uncertainty (p. 2). Modelling stance: uncertainty only raises grades ("benefit of the doubt"), based on the authors' experience with students (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Three strategies (p. 2). MaxGap: maximize the distance from the breakpoint to the next grade below, solved by enumeration (Proposition 1, p. 2). Basic: minimize the number of "borderline" students; the discontinuous indicator L is replaced by a continuous "pseudo-borderline" P = Q(a, ϕ + ε) − Q(a, ϕ), with each Q modelled as a union of three polyhedra by a disjunctive MILP (pp. 3–4); because the integer-valued objective has many optima, a divide-and-conquer pass breaks ties by gap (p. 4). Robust: min over (a, b) of the max over a MIP-representable uncertainty set U, solved by a tailored spatial B&B on the (a, b) rectangle: an upper bound from fixing the midpoint and solving the inner max; a lower bound from a "bunch" B of worst-case f̂ collected across nodes, ordered by helpfulness; halving branching, best-bound selection, stop at width ω (p. 5).
  - Transferable: in a min-max B&B, reuse the inner-max solutions found while computing upper bounds as a pool of scenarios; the max over the pool of the node-wise min is a valid lower bound that improves as the pool grows (p. 5).
  - Transferable: to model a discontinuous indicator that must be both maximized and minimized, replace it by a continuous piecewise-linear δ-approximation written as a difference of two monotone pieces, each a disjunctive union of polyhedra (pp. 3–4).
- **D3 Assumptions**: Only two breakpoints (a, b), with ub + ε < la so the problems separate in the nominal case (p. 2); U compact, mixed-integer representable, G ≥ Ḡ (p. 5); U(k, c) bounds the 0-norm or 1-norm of the perturbation, each entry raised by at most 5 points (pp. 5–6).
- **D4 Proof devices**: n/a beyond two short propositions (optimal breakpoints lie at data points or at the upper bound, pp. 2, 4); B&B validity is argued informally (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**: 50 synthetic instances sampled from the per-assignment empirical distributions of a real course (m = 41, n = 5), to protect student privacy (pp. 5–6); 8 parameter settings; Matlab + Gurobi 6.5.1, one core (p. 6); Robust run times log-normal with base-10 means 2.70 and 3.73, i.e. up to ≈ 5370 s (p. 6). Cross-evaluation: each method's solution is plugged into the others' objectives (bubble plots, Figs. 3–4, pp. 6–7); exception instances are shown individually (Tables 1–2, pp. 7–8). Code on GitHub, MaxGap as an Excel file, Basic via NEOS (p. 6).
  - Transferable: when methods optimize different objectives, compare them by the sub-optimality of each solution in every other method's objective, and then show the instances that break the overall trend (pp. 6–8).
  - Transferable: build test data by sampling from the empirical marginals of a real but private dataset, and say what this ignores (correlations) (p. 6).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Modelling plus algorithm plus a practical recommendation: the simple strategies are "quite robust themselves", so a professor should look at all three and inspect the worst-case f (p. 8). Limits stated concretely: Robust may be impractical for large classes and needs optimization expertise; a possible speed-up via Vielma–Nemhauser compact disjunctions (p. 8).

**Method links**: no direct link to Methods 1–5 (an application with no cone representation, hull or hard-pool design). Heuristic evidence: H8 (print losses) — the paper's own Robust method is reported as 11.5–14.6% suboptimal in MaxGap's objective and as worse "in addition to its increased computational requirements" (p. 6); H10 (release code) appears already in 2018 (GitHub sburer/grades, p. 6), earlier than the 2023–26 dating of H10 in SKILL.md. New-pattern candidates: "cross-evaluation of solutions across objectives" (pp. 6–7); "privacy-preserving synthetic instances" (p. 6); "scenario-bunch lower bound for min-max B&B" (p. 5).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Scenario-bunch lower bounds in min-max spatial B&B (p. 5).
- Cross-objective evaluation with outlier instances shown one by one (pp. 6–8).
- Ship each method at the user's level of expertise: spreadsheet, NEOS, full code (p. 6).
**Quotes**:
- "In particular, the original course data was not used to test the algorithms directly; it was simply the basis for randomly generating the test data." (p. 6)
- "Results on realistic instances show that the MaxGap and Basic strategies are quite robust themselves, even though there are some cases for which explicitly computing the robust solution is worthwhile." (p. 8)

### S038 · Quadratic optimization with switching variables: the convex hull for n = 2 (Mathematical Programming 2021, DOI 10.1007/s10107-021-01671-w; read as arXiv:2002.04681v1)
**Read level**: full, arXiv v1 (pp. 1–21, all sections and references). First-page check: title and authors "Kurt M. Anstreicher, Samuel Burer" match (p. 1). Version note: arXiv v1 dated February 9, 2020 (p. 1); the published Math. Program. version was not read, so whether Conjecture 1 survived to print is not known from this text.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Kurt M. Anstreicher (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: next to the 2010 low-dimensional hull paper ([2], QPB for n = 2) and the 2021 switching/bounded-products work under Method 2; a "smallest dimension where it is true" result.

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: Quadratic optimization in (x, y) with 0 ≤ x ≤ y and binary switching y, common in power, portfolio, scheduling and pooling (pp. 1–2). Represent H = conv{(x, xxᵀ, yyᵀ)}: n = 1 is the known perspective result, and even QPB (y = e) is intractable for general n with a known representation only for n = 2 (p. 2). Entry: the next smallest case, n = 2.
- **D2 Core idea**: (i) Reprove n = 1 as "PSD + RLT is exact": substitute t = 1 − y, use the CP representation with "CP = DNN for 4 × 4", and simplify (Theorem 1, pp. 4–5). (ii) n = 2: a disjunctive hull over the four y values with auxiliary α, β (Theorem 2, p. 6); (iii) eliminate β by setting each βⱼ to the largest of its lower bounds, at the cost of four PSD conditions (Theorem 3, pp. 8–9); (iv) show that only the 5 × 5 condition (5h) is needed (Theorem 4, p. 18) via Lemma 1 ((5e) redundant, p. 11), a reduction to α₁ = 0 (Lemma 2, p. 12), and α₂ intervals from determinant quadratics (Lemmas 3–6, pp. 13–18). (v) An alternative derivation through t = e − y and RLT shows that (5h′) is the natural PSD matrix (12) minus T₁₂uuᵀ, i.e. the y = 0 piece removed (p. 19), which leads to Conjecture 1: the plain PSD + RLT relaxation captures H′, the hull without y₁y₂ (p. 20).
- **D3 Assumptions**: n = 2 only; separable objectives and often no cross-terms (p. 2); H′ (without y₁y₂) treated separately (p. 2).
- **D4 Proof devices**: For projections with PSD constraints "we are unaware of any general methodolgy", so the proof is tailored (p. 3). Concavity of principal-minor determinants in one entry, so checking the two endpoints suffices (p. 11); Schur complements (p. 10); eigenvalue interlacing (at most one negative eigenvalue, p. 14); a contradiction using the maximizer α₂* of a concave determinant (pp. 16–18).
- **D5 Experiments**: none reported; the conjecture is "supported by extensive numerical computations" with no details given (pp. 3, 19).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: An exact representation of H for n = 2, still lifted in α (Theorem 4, p. 18). Open: Conjecture 1, "unproved" (p. 20); if true, standard PSD + RLT would suffice for H′ at n = 2, as at n = 1 and for QPB at n = 2 (p. 20).

**Method links**: Method 2 ✅ evidence: the hull of a small recurring substructure in the smallest open dimension, with step 2 visible (re-derive n = 1 in their own PSD/RLT machinery first, pp. 3–5) and step 7 (proved for n = 2, conjectured beyond, p. 20). Method 2 ⚠ variant on step 6: the disjunctive formulation is the starting point and is only partly eliminated (β removed, α kept), so the final description is a projection rather than one directly in the original variables (pp. 3, 18). Method 3 ✅ evidence: the regularity is printed as a conjecture supported by computation and explicitly "remains unproved" (pp. 3, 20). Method 1 ✅ evidence (small): the n = 1 proof goes through the CP representation and the fact that CP = DNN in dimension 4 (p. 4).
**Transferable techniques**:
- Eliminate an auxiliary variable that enters a PSD matrix only on the diagonal by monotonicity: set it to the max of its lower bounds, then split the max into cases, each giving one PSD condition (pp. 8–9).
- Show a PSD condition is redundant by noting that every relevant principal-minor determinant is concave in the free entry and checking both interval endpoints (p. 11).
- Reinterpret a surviving PSD condition as "natural matrix minus the piece of one disjunct" to generate a conjecture for the weaker hull (p. 19).
**Quotes**:
- "This conjecture is supported by extensive numerical computations but remains unproved." (p. 3)
- "we are unaware of any general methodolgy for characterizing such a projection, and therefore our proof technique is tailored to the structure of H for n = 2." (p. 3)
