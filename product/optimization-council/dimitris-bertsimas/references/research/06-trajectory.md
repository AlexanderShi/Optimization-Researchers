## 0. The trajectory in one paragraph (all [inferred], built from §§1–8)

Bertsimas has spent his whole career at one institution: a student at MIT from 1985 and on the faculty since 1988. His trajectory is a sequence of problem classes, each taken up when he judged the tools had made it tractable, and each later applied to a new field. He began inside his advisor Odoni's probabilistic-routing line: Jaillet's 1985 thesis on the probabilistic travelling salesman problem, then his own 1988 thesis on probabilistic combinatorial optimization. Through his first decade (ages 26–35) he worked in applied probability and queueing (the achievable-region and conservation-law program, with Tsitsiklis) and in LP-based approximation algorithms, and his teaching produced the 1997 LP textbook. By his own retrospective account, given in 2014–2016, around 1997–98, as a newly established full professor in his mid-thirties, he stopped choosing problems to impress senior colleagues and began choosing problems he judged important. The record from then on shows a company (Dynamic Ideas, 1998), work in finance, and from 2001 robust optimization (RO). He entered RO three to four years after the founding papers of Ben-Tal–Nemirovski and El Ghaoui–Lebret, and set his work apart from theirs by tractability: a robust LP stays an LP, a robust MIP stays a MIP. Health care came in through company data: the claims-data company D2Hawkeye (2001 or 2003, the sources disagree, to 2009) and a first paper received in January 2007. It became personal with the 2009 deaths of his parents, which he names as the trigger of his personalized-medicine program. He made the machine-learning turn early and against the consensus he describes: an integer-optimization classifier was submitted in 2002 and accepted in 2006. It took off only after he had quantified the speed-up of MIO solvers and hardware (about 200 billion-fold in 2014, 800 billion-fold in 2017) and recast statistics *under a modern optimization lens* (his title phrase): the Morse lecture (2014), *Annals of Statistics* (2016), optimal trees (2017), the book (2019) and the Lanchester Prize (2021). From 2013 he also built education programs and institutions: an edX course (2014), the MBAn (2016), an associate deanship (2019) and Open Learning (2024–). Since 2024 his output shows research following his role: Universal AI was launched in May 2026, and 2026 preprints treat LLM personalization and generated lecture videos. Meanwhile his health-care and machine-learning papers keep multiplying: 137 journal articles in 2020–2025 against 58 in 2015–2019 (CV count).

### T5. Robust optimization (2001 → present); RO
- **Trigger**: **no first-hand reason found** in the sources read. The 2021 "Subject to" interview has a chapter on *Robust and Adaptive Optimization*, but it was not read.
- **Stated framing at entry [stated-joint] P** (*The price of robustness*, pp. 35–36, paraphrase): the RO approach of the early 1970s (Soyster) can be too conservative. The ellipsoidal approaches of Ben-Tal–Nemirovski and El Ghaoui–Lebret reduce conservatism but give nonlinear models, which are computationally harder. Their aim is to keep the linear framework, control the conservatism of each constraint, and therefore extend naturally to discrete problems.
- **Program statements [stated] P**: by Sept 2008 the research page gives the goal of a tractable theory of optimization under uncertainty with two motivations: data uncertainty with tractable robust counterparts, and modelling stochastic and dynamic problems with uncertainty sets to avoid DP's curse of dimensionality (paraphrase). That page highlighted only RO from 2008 to 2014.
- **Context [practice]**: the paper was reviewed in the Financial Services area. He had worked in finance since 1998, and his first RO PhD thesis (2002) was RO in finance. The probabilistic analysis of protection levels reuses his applied-probability training [inferred].
- **Persistence [practice]**: RO is his longest unbroken line. Journal titles counted by keyword: 3 in 2000–04, 8 in 2005–09, 19 in 2010–14, 18 in 2015–19, 13 in 2020–25 (§5). It runs from adaptive (MOR 2010, DOI 10.1287/moor.1100.0444) to data-driven (Math. Program. 2018, DOI 10.1007/s10107-017-1125-8, arXiv 1401.0212), to ML-based two-stage ARO (EJOR 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2024.06.012), to the 2026 retrospective. ### T7. Statistics and ML under a modern optimization lens (1997/2002 → 2014–2021 → present); ML, IO
- **Early, against the stated consensus [stated-joint] P**: CRIO (submitted Nov 2002) says integer optimization had had very limited impact on statistical computation, because of a belief formed in the early 1970s that such methods are intractable. Its stated motivation is the significant advances in integer optimization over the preceding decade (paraphrase, pp. 252–253). The first contribution it claims is to make the statistics and data-mining community aware of integer optimization (paraphrase).
- **New-tool trigger, quantified [stated-joint] P**: the Morse lecture document (2014-11-11, pp. 6–7) takes solver speed-ups from Bixby: CPLEX 1.2 (1991) to CPLEX 11 (2007), more than 29,000×; Gurobi 1.0 (2009) to 5.5 (2013), more than 20×; a machine-independent total of 580,000× for 1991–2013. Combined with about 320,000× in hardware, the overall speed-up is about 200 billion-fold. It concludes that the belief that MIO is irrelevant to statistics, formed in the 1970s–80s and justified then, no longer holds (paraphrase). OCT (2017) updates the figures: about 1.4 million× in solvers to Gurobi 6.5 (2015) and about 570,000× in hardware, giving about 800 billion-fold. It also cites Breiman et al. that globally optimal trees did not then appear feasible.
- **Timing [practice]**: about 12 years from the first submission (2002) to the public reframing in the Morse lecture (2014). Acceptance in the ML and statistics venues followed: the Annals of Statistics (2016), Machine Learning (2017), NeurIPS earlier (2012). Then the doctoral class (2016), the book (2019), the Lanchester Prize (2021) and the company (Interpretable AI, 2018).
- **Identity signal [stated]**: on the Morse lecture's title page (2014) his title is *Boeing Professor of Operations Research and Statistics*. Other sources give no statistics title.

### T8. *Tractable stochastic analysis in high dimensions* by RO, as a replacement for probability (c. 2009 → c. 2016, then faded); SD, RO
- **Stated program [stated] P** (research page, Apr 2015, paraphrase): probability theory is not computationally tractable in high dimensions. Replace the Kolmogorov axioms by consequences of the CLT and the LLN, cast as uncertainty sets, and so obtain tractable analysis of queueing networks, network information theory (a generalization of Shannon), multi-dimensional option pricing (Black–Scholes–Merton) and multi-item auctions (Myerson). The goal is an alternative to classical probability that scales with dimension.
- **Practice**: the ISMP 2012 semi-plenary and the 2013 ECC plenary on this theme (CV); the Math. Program. 2012 paper; auctions (MOR 2014, DOI 10.1287/moor.2014.0645); robust queueing (OR 2015). **Unpublished**: *Channel Coding via Robust Optimization*, Parts 1 and 2 (with Bandi). The CV of Dec 2025 still lists both as submitted to IEEE Transactions on Information Theory in 2015. Neither has a Crossref record or an arXiv entry.
- **Fade [inferred]**: the program is absent from the 2026 homepage's research interests; its queueing part survives in the 2022 book.

### T9. From prediction to prescription (2014 → present); DA

- **Origin [practice]**: a student's single-author preprint, withdrawn in June 2014 for an error, then rebuilt as a joint paper (§1, §7). Published only in 2020.

## 9. Pointers for the synthesis (all [inferred] from §§1–8; candidate heuristics, not his words)

1. **Re-open an old problem when the tool changes, and quantify the change**: integer optimization for statistics (1970s belief → 2002 attempt → 2014 speed-up argument); LLMs for formulation (2024). Before re-entering, he measures the tool's progress in a number his audience can check (Bixby's version-on-version speed-ups).
2. **Enter second, win on tractability**: in RO he did not originate the idea. He made the robust counterpart stay in the class practitioners already solve (LP, MIP). The same move appears in *Robust queueing theory* (performance analysis made an optimization problem).
3. **Template reuse**: programs named *X under a modern optimization lens* (statistics 2014–19; probability 2012–15), reversed in the 2024 title *Robust and Adaptive Optimization under a Large Language Model Lens*. A recognizable naming of research programs that he carries across fields.
4. **Applied problems come through partners, often his companies, before the papers**: D2Hawkeye → health-cost prediction (2007); Dynamic Ideas → execution and portfolio work (1998–2002); OCP → food security and decarbonization (2022–25); Hartford HealthCare and Holistic Hospital Optimization (2022–) → hospital operations (2024–26).
5. **Consolidate each era in a book with former students or close colleagues**: LO/IO 1997, IO 2005, analytics 2016, ML 2019, RO 2022, queueing 2022, health 2025. He states the pipeline from teaching to notes to book (oral history p. 11).
6. **A personal event can retarget the application field without changing the method**: in 2009 the same MIO and RO toolset moved to personalized medicine.
7. **A role change retargets the research**: from Vice Provost (2024) to education-AI preprints (2026). The deanships and directorships tracked his research (analytics → MBAn) and then led it (Open Learning → education AI).
8. **Impact-horizon rule, stated repeatedly**: impact within 2–5 years, not 40 (2014–16); within two years, not 50 (2025); worth doing only if a 100% success changes something (2015). The numbers drift; the rule persists.
