### S597 · Stochastic and Dynamic Vehicle Routing in the Euclidean Plane: The Multiple-Server, Capacitated Vehicle Case. 1990 (MIT Operations Research Center Working Paper OR 224-90 1990, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (DSpace record, hdl 1721.1/5220). The record's abstract stops mid-sentence at "…into m equal subregions". The rest was not read.
**Coauthors**: Garrett van Ryzin.
**Position**: the August 1990 working paper behind S032 (Operations Research 41(1), 1993). His homepage lists this title as "Operations Research, 1993" (metadata). Its "previous paper" is, by the description, the DTRP paper S016 (OR 1991; 1989 working paper S261). That identification is inferred.
- **D1** {SD AP}: The entry is the authors' own dynamic traveling repairman problem (DTRP). A vehicle moving at constant speed in a Euclidean region serves demands whose arrival times, locations and on-site service times are stochastic. The goal is to minimize the expected system time. This paper extends the model (abstract). The title names multiple, capacitated vehicles (metadata).
- **D2**: The first extension gives new bounds and policies for m identical vehicles of unlimited capacity. In heavy traffic, system time falls by a factor 1/m² against a single server. The policies divide the region into m equal subregions (abstract).
- **D8**: The abstract recaps the predecessor's results: stability independent of the region's geometry, bounds on the optimal system time, an optimal light-traffic policy, and constant-factor heavy-traffic policies. It then announces extensions "in several directions". The capacitated case lies past the record's cut-off and was not read (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - New-pattern candidate: lower bound over all policies, then constant-factor policies ✅ (abstract: the recapped DTRP results, extended here to m vehicles).
  - New-pattern candidate: staged companion papers from one model ✅ (abstract: the same model "extended in several directions"; published as S032).
- Transferable technique: when you extend your own stochastic model by one resource (m servers), make the scaling law of the gain the headline (here 1/m² in heavy traffic), not just a new bound (abstract).
- Quotes (abstract): "In this paper, we extend these results in several directions." · "First, we propose new bounds and policies for the problem of m identical vehicles with unlimited capacity and show that in heavy traffic the system time is reduced by a factor of 1/m2 over the single server case."

### S836 · Signed Budget Uncertainty for Robust Mixed-Integer Optimization (Optimization Online 2026, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (Optimization Online, posted 2026-09-30).
**Coauthors**: Danique de Moor (MIT Sloan), Dick den Hertog (Amsterdam), Federica Donnini (Florence); affiliations from PDF p. 1.
**Position**: the newest work on the budget sets that S001 introduced. It comes from the de Moor–den Hertog group of S377 and S708 (metadata).
- **D1** {RO IO}: Classical budget uncertainty bounds the aggregate *absolute* deviation from nominal values. In many applications, however, the information available concerns aggregate *signed* deviations (abstract).
- **D2**: Bound signed aggregates instead of absolute deviations (abstract).
  - For robust MIO with binary uncertainty-affected variables, the robust counterpart needs only two extra variables and two extra constraints per uncertainty-affected constraint, whatever the number of uncertain coefficients (abstract).
  - A laminar variant encodes hierarchical information (geographical, organizational, categorical). Its counterpart grows with the number of aggregate sets, not with the number of coefficients (abstract).
- **D8**:
  - With a single uncertainty-affected constraint, the robust problem can instead be solved through 2^K nominal problems, where K is the number of aggregate sets (abstract).
  - Experiments show computational advantages, and solutions that no setting of the classical budget parameter produces (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 1 ✅ (abstract): the device keeps the class. The counterpart stays compact, and with one uncertain constraint the problem reduces to nominal solves, as in the n + 1 nominal solves of Method 1 step 3.
  - Method 3 ⚠ variant (abstract): the lever is the *shape* of the set (signed, laminar budgets), not the level of Γ. The last sentence argues that tuning the classical knob cannot reach these solutions.
  - New-pattern candidate: practitioners' maxims encoded as uncertainty-set geometry ✅ (abstract: the information held, signed aggregates over a hierarchy, becomes the set).
- Transferable technique: if your information comes as net totals by region or category, encode it as signed, nested budgets. Then show two things: the counterpart's size does not grow with the number of uncertain coefficients, and the new set yields a solution that no setting of the classical Γ yields (abstract).
- Quotes (abstract): "In many applications, however, information concerns aggregate signed deviations." · "Experiments demonstrate the computational advantages and show that signed budget uncertainty can produce solutions not obtainable by adjusting the classical budget parameter."

### S858 · A Robust Optimization Approach to Supply Chains and Revenue Management (MIT PhD thesis of Aurélie Thiele 2004, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (DSpace record, hdl 1721.1/16693). The thesis abstract is the student's text.
**Author**: Aurélie Thiele; **advisor**: Dimitris J. Bertsimas (DSpace record).
**Position**: Scholar credits the thesis to him, but it is his student's work. The corpus holds four Bertsimas–Thiele papers: S055 (IPCO 2004), S014 (OR 2006), S027 (Tutorials in OR 2006) and S122 (2005 working paper) (metadata).
- **D1** {RO AP}: Supply-chain and revenue-management problems under uncertainty need a tractable framework that shows the structure of the optimal policy and needs little knowledge of the probability distributions (abstract).
- **D2**: Two robust approaches (abstract):
  - Polyhedral uncertainty sets governed by budgets of uncertainty, which trade robustness against optimality. For supply chains, the robust problem equals a deterministic problem with modified parameters.
  - Historical realizations used directly, without estimation: a fraction of the best cases is removed, and the sample average of the rest is optimized. In revenue management, the optimal policy often reduces to ranking the historical data.
- **D8**: Robust optimization is claimed to give models of randomness that make few assumptions, stay numerically tractable, include the decision-maker's risk aversion and reveal the structure of the optimal policy, especially in dynamic settings (abstract).
- **Method links** (the student's text, so evidence for his programme, not for his own words):
  - Method 3 ✅ (abstract: budgets of uncertainty set as a robustness–optimality trade-off).
  - Method 1 ✅ (abstract: the robust problem equals a deterministic one with modified parameters, so the nominal structure carries over).
  - Method 6 ✅ (DSpace record: a 2004 thesis he supervised inside the RO programme, with the four joint papers named above).
  - New-pattern candidate: robust = nominal on modified data (structure inheritance) ✅ (abstract; the device of the S014 and S027 cards).
- Transferable technique: a robust device that needs only data. Discard a fixed fraction of the best historical outcomes and optimize the average of the rest, with no distribution estimated (abstract).
- Quotes (abstract): "The polyhedron is affected by budgets of uncertainty, which reflect a trade-off between robustness and optimality." · "We apply this framework to supply chain management, show that the robust problem is equivalent to a deterministic problem with modified parameters, and derive the main properties of the optimal policy."

### S867 · Distributionally Robust Optimization with General Uncertainty Structure (Optimization Online 2026, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (Optimization Online, posted 2026-06-11).
**Coauthors**: Judith Brugman (Tilburg), Dick den Hertog (Amsterdam), Johan S. H. van Leeuwaarden (Tilburg). Brugman is listed first on Optimization Online and starred on PDF p. 1.
**Position**: den Hertog's line of exact reformulations, carried into DRO. The Optimization Online tags include "Reformulation-perspectification techniques" and "cutting-set methods"; compare S708 (metadata).
- **D1** {RO CP}: Exact methods for DRO with moment- and confidence-set-based ambiguity sets are largely limited to max-of-affine functions under strictly nested confidence sets (abstract).
- **D2**: A new ambiguity set built from linearly defined confidence sets allows weak nestedness. For functions convex in the decisions, three cases are distinguished by how the function depends on the uncertainty (abstract):
  - Convex: a global optimization algorithm, under the nested general set.
  - General nonlinear: the same algorithm, under the new set.
  - Concave: an explicit conic reformulation, under the new set.

  Every case first reformulates the DRO problem as a robust problem and then applies robust-optimization techniques (abstract).
- **D8**: The result is an exact framework. Solving to optimality lets one gauge how conservative approximation-based DRO models are. Two applications are given: capital budgeting and appointment scheduling (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 1 ✅ (abstract): DRO is rewritten exactly as a robust problem, with a conic reformulation in the concave case, and the ambiguity set is changed so that tractability is preserved.
  - New-pattern candidate: exact optimum on a small solvable instance as the certificate for a heuristic ⚠ variant (abstract): exact DRO optima serve as the yardstick for approximation-based DRO models. The abstract does not say the instances are small.
- Transferable technique (abstract):
  - When exact methods depend on a structural assumption such as strict nesting, redefine the ambiguity set so that a weaker version suffices.
  - Then sort the functions by their convexity in the uncertainty, and give each case its own exact route.
- Quotes (abstract): "All three cases are handled by first reformulating the DRO problem as a robust problem and then applying advanced techniques from robust optimization." · "By solving these problems to optimality, our framework can offer valuable insights into the conservatism and behavior of existing approximation-based DRO models."

### S873 · Optimized Oversampling (manuscript, submitted to Machine Learning per his homepage 2022, no DOI/arXiv)

- Quotes (abstract): "In this paper we propose a new oversampling framework, Optimized-Oversampling (O2) that consists of solving optimization problems to get new synthetic points and balance the dataset." · "As the experiments illustrate, O2 is on par with or improves on the current state of the art oversampling methods, while it is both deterministic in nature and easy to modify."

### S884 · Fast and Scalable Inference of Dynamical Systems via Integral Matching (NeurIPS 2023 AI for Science Workshop 2023, OpenReview mn3wfSnWYG, no DOI/arXiv)

### S926 · LIDS Publication# 2659 Robust linear optimization and coherent risk measures (MIT LIDS report LIDS-P-2659, year not given, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json).
**Coauthors**: David B. Brown.
**Position**: the LIDS-report version of S024 (OR 2009, "Constructing uncertainty sets for robust linear optimization"), whose abstract makes the same argument from risk measures to sets. Brown's 2006 thesis "Risk and robust optimization" (DSpace 1721.1/37894, advisor Bertsimas) states the one-to-one correspondence (thesis abstract; metadata).
- **D1** {RO LO}: The robust optimization literature says little about how to construct uncertainty sets (abstract).
- **D2**: Take a coherent risk measure as the primitive, and use the duality theorem for such measures to build the uncertainty set. The setting has observations of the data but no detailed distribution (abstract):
  - Some classes of risk measures give polyhedral sets; the centrally symmetric ones induce a norm.
  - Tail-moment risk measures give norm-bounded sets.
- **D8**: The converse also holds: every convex uncertainty set corresponds to an equivalent coherent risk measure. Some computational evidence is shown (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 1 ✅ (abstract): the set is obtained by dualizing the risk measure, and polyhedral sets keep the robust LP linear.
  - Method 3 ⚠ variant (abstract): the set comes from the decision-maker's risk measure and the data rather than from a hand-set knob, which is the route the Method 3 correction asks for.
