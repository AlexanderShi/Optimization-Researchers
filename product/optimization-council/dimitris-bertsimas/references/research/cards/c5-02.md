### B014 · Data, Models, and Decisions (Bertsimas, Freund; 2nd ed., Dynamic Ideas 2004): publisher's book page (educational philosophy, distinguishing characteristics) (book material 2004, https://www.dynamic-ideas.com/books/ecbcnthsfb6hnzfdezpw0aldtvl9yr)

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {DA; AP; SD (probability, simulation); LO, IO and nonlinear optimization as tools}:
  - The gap: existing textbooks cover methodology. This one "introduces decision support systems through real world applications, and uses spreadsheets to model and solve problems", using management-science techniques "only as tools to facilitate problem solving" (p. 1).
  - Audience: the core MBA class Data, Models, and Decisions at MIT Sloan (p. 1). The stated aim is to prepare management students to become tomorrow's business leaders (p. 1).
  - The joint belief behind it: management-science tools and models represent "the future of best-practices for tomorrow's successful companies" (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea (stated principles)**:
  - Mission: Dantzig's test. The page quotes Dantzig's sentence on the final test of any theory, then states the mission of showing that management science passes it (p. 1; quoted below).
  - Three design principles (p. 1):
    - Rigor: fundamental concepts rather than formulas or commands. The page rejects plugging numbers into black-box models ("Simply teaching students to plug numbers into black-box models is not relevant to a student's education.") and stresses "how, what, and why" tools are useful and what their ramifications are in practice.
    - Relevance: every topic is applied at once to realistic business scenarios; the spreadsheet is the basic template; more than thirty cases come from the authors' consulting or their students' experience.
    - Readability: engaging, informal, conversational, with humour. Most chapters and cases were pre-tested in classrooms at MIT Sloan, Harvard Business School, Wharton, Columbia, British Columbia, Ohio State, Northwestern and McGill, plus several universities overseas.
  - Five distinguishing characteristics (p. 1): a unified treatment of probability/statistics and optimization models; concise writing; an appropriate use of spreadsheets; cases for tomorrow's managers; and a capstone chapter on integration (Chapter 10, three sectors).
  - Formulation before software, stated for Chapters 7–9: the optimization model is developed by defining decision variables and explicitly constructing the constraints and the objective. "Only then do we translate the problem into a spreadsheet for solution by appropriate software." (p. 1). Spreadsheets are a vehicle for working with models, not a substitute for building them (p. 1; quoted below).
  - Transferable technique: state the order formulation → software in the front matter, and keep it in every optimization chapter.
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - The fundamental concepts named (p. 1): "modeling and evaluating uncertainty explicitly", the dynamic nature of decisions, historical data and limited information, simulating complex systems, and allocating scarce resources optimally.
  - Spreadsheets are the template for almost every model. Command instructions are given for simulation, linear regression, and linear, nonlinear and discrete optimization. The book presumes basic spreadsheet familiarity and is not a spreadsheet manual (p. 1).
  - Cases: more than thirty, rich in context, often with a relatively young MBA graduate as protagonist facing a difficult management decision (p. 1).
  - Resources (p. 1; none fetched): spreadsheets for many cases; Crystal Ball simulation software, an Excel add-on, for the Chapter 5 cases; for instructors on request, slides, a test bank, a solution manual and case instructor notes.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis**: the page names topics, not theory: statistics, simulation, probabilistic modeling and optimization (p. 1). Decision variables, constraints and objective are built explicitly before solving (Chapters 7–9, p. 1). No algorithm is named; n/a beyond that.
- **D5 Evaluation**:
  - Points are made once or twice, with examples, then reinforced in the exercises and case modules (Concise Writing, p. 1).
  - Classroom pre-testing at eight named schools plus overseas (p. 1). The solution manual and test bank imply graded exercises [inferred].

  - Against textbooks that cover methodology (p. 1), and against textbooks whose spreadsheets interfere with the key concepts ("Unlike many existing textbooks", p. 1).
  - The book joins two subjects usually taught apart, probability/statistics and management-science optimization (p. 1).
  - Dantzig is introduced as "a founding father of modern management science" (p. 1). No other book is named.
- **D7 Writing**: an authority quotation opens the philosophy; principles and characteristics follow as named bullets. The page reports its own reception: "Many students and colleagues have praised the book's engaging writing style." (p. 1). Reading note, not a finding: on the page Dantzig's quotation opens with two backquotes and is never closed.

### B006 · Optimization over Integers (Bertsimas, Weismantel; Dynamic Ideas 2005): publisher's book page (purpose, distinguishing characteristics, four parts) (book material 2005, https://www.dynamic-ideas.com/books/x0g7bsm2nvnl6j7ebqodcrhsvlgbm7)

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {IO; RO (robust discrete optimization); CP (semidefinite methods, optimization over polynomials); HS (local search, simulated annealing); LO through polyhedra}:

- **D2 Core idea**:

  - The geometric spine (p. 1): "Develops the theory of integer optimization from a new geometric perspective via integral generating sets", and "Contains a unified geometric treatment of cutting plane and integral basis methods".
  - Eight distinguishing characteristics (p. 1):

    - enumerative and heuristic methods, including local search over exponential neighborhoods and simulated annealing;
    - approximation algorithms by primal–dual, randomized rounding, semidefinite and enumerative methods;
    - a unified treatment of mixed integer and robust discrete optimization;
    - many examples and exercises from classroom use.
  - Four parts, by name only (p. 1): Formulations and Relaxations; Algebra and Geometry of Integer Optimization; Algorithms for Integer Optimization; Extensions of Integer Optimization. The order puts the algorithms after the algebra and geometry; his old books page says the algorithms are built on them (B008 p. 2) [cross-reading].

- **D3 Model and data**: problem classes named: integer, mixed integer and robust discrete optimization (p. 1). Data: CPLEX formulations and data for hands-on exercises, organised by chapter (resources line, p. 1; not fetched). No case study is mentioned.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis** (topics as listed, p. 1): integral generating sets; integral polyhedra; duality; relaxations; lattices; Gröbner bases; optimization over polynomials; counting integer points in polyhedra; cutting plane and integral basis methods; approximation algorithms (primal–dual, randomized rounding, semidefinite, enumerative); mixed integer and robust discrete optimization. No proof device is visible beyond these names.
- **D5 Evaluation**: "a large number of examples and exercises developed through extensive classroom use" (p. 1); hands-on CPLEX exercises (resources line, p. 1). No computational study is mentioned.
- **D6 Positioning**: "We depart from earlier treatments of integer optimization" (p. 1), but no earlier treatment is named. The claimed novelty is "a new geometric perspective via integral generating sets" (p. 1).
- **D7 Writing**: the purpose sentence is in "we", hedged twice with "we feel" and ranked twice with "most importantly" (p. 1). The characteristics are verb-led bullets: Develops, Emphasizes, Discusses, Contains, Covers, Presents, Provides, Includes (p. 1). "Unified" recurs three times: the purpose, the cutting-plane treatment, and mixed integer with robust discrete optimization (p. 1).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**:
