- **D1 Problem and entry point** {DA + AP (cardiology); ML as engine}:
  - *Entry*: a clinical choice with no unified rule.
    - For patients not at extreme surgical risk, operators choose between two platforms: the balloon-expandable Edwards Sapien and the self-expanding Medtronic Evolut. The choice rests on operator preference, patient characteristics and CT anatomy (p. 4).
    - Existing studies name scenarios that favour one valve but "do not present a unified treatment strategy" (p. 4).
    - Guideline-style advice is "not evidence-based or quantitatively evaluated" and does not target a specific complication (p. 5).
  - *The outcome is chosen for its clinical and operational weight*: permanent pacemaker implantation (PPI).
    - PPI is associated with long-term mortality, heart-failure hospitalization and longer stays.
    - Two cited studies put its added cost at $23,588 and $10,213 (p. 4).
  - *Problem stated* (p. 9): covariates x_i ∈ R^p; treatments T = {Medtronic Evolut, Edwards Sapien}; a binary outcome y_i (PPI). Prescribe one valve per patient to minimize PPI risk.
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - *Two steps* (pp. 9–11):
    - Estimate a counterfactual reward matrix Γ (n × 2) with a doubly robust estimator.
    - Fit an Optimal Policy Tree that minimizes Σ_i Σ_t 1{τ(x_i) = t}·Γ_{i,t} (Eq. 2, p. 11). This is a weighted classification tree in the OCT framework, optimized by coordinate descent (p. 11).
  - *Leaf-level analysis* is the main evaluation (pp. 2, 6, 18). Inside each leaf, patients who received the non-prescribed valve are assigned the observed PPI rate of the patients who received the prescribed one. The policy is thus scored on observed outcomes, not on model-estimated counterfactuals.
  - *Selection from a pool* (pp. 12–13):
    - OPTs are trained on 20 random 50/50 splits.
    - The top 30 are kept by test-set performance, leaving 13 unique trees.
    - Three finalists are chosen by the size and train–test consistency of their improvement.
    - The final tree is the one "most consistent with the current medical insight" (p. 13).
  - *What changed*:
    - Against scenario-based clinical advice [6–8]: one data-driven rule with a stated objective (pp. 5–6).
    - Against causal-inference studies in health-care operations [20–22]: transparent decision paths (p. 5).
  - *What did not change*: OPT is used as published [14]; no new algorithm is proposed (p. 11).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - *Internal cohort*:
    - First-time TAVR at Hospital A, January 2011–September 2022.
    - Sapien or Evolut platform valves only; valve models are pooled into platforms to enlarge the sample.
    - Exclusions: prior pacemakers or ICDs, valve-in-valve procedures, repeated valves and incomplete records (pp. 6–7).
    - 1,779 patients: 1,306 Sapien (PPI 13.63%) and 473 Evolut (PPI 15.22%) (Table 1, p. 8).
  - *External cohort*:
    - Hygeia Hospital, Greece, January 2011–December 2021, with the same exclusions. Taxonomies and units were harmonised with doctors from both hospitals (p. 7).
    - 151 patients: 118 Sapien 3 (PPI 3.39%, 4 events) and 33 Evolut Pro (PPI 18.18%, 6 events) (pp. 7, 9).
  - *Outcome*: in-hospital PPI before discharge, by the TVT Registry definition, with pacemakers and ICDs pooled (p. 7).
  - *Features*: 47 in all, from demographics, history, medications and extractions from echocardiogram and CT reports (pp. 7–8, 11).
    - Only 496 of the 1,779 patients have complete records; mean imputation fills the rest (pp. 11–12).
    - The CT-derived variables are 29.79–38.84% missing (Table A4, p. 38).
  - *Assumptions the authors admit*:
    - Counterfactuals are estimated, not observed. The causal link between valve and PPI is uncertain, and unobserved confounders are possible (p. 25).
    - The leaf-level analysis assumes within-leaf exchangeability (p. 18).
    - The training population comes from one hospital and does not represent the US (p. 24).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis** (no theorems):
  - *Reward estimator*: Γ_{i,t} = ŷ_{i,t} + 1{T_i = t}(y_i − ŷ_{i,t})/p_{i,t} (Eq. 1, p. 11), after Dudík, Langford & Li [28] (p. 10).
    - Models: one XGBoost outcome model per valve and one XGBoost propensity model. They are cross-fitted, tuned by cross-validated AUC and fitted separately on the train and test halves (pp. 11–12).
    - The 50/50 split is justified by this separate fitting (p. 11).
  - *Reader's note* [inferred]: the paper uses "a min propensity score of one" (p. 12).
    - With that floor, Eq. (1) returns the observed outcome for the valve received and ŷ for the other valve, so the propensity model drops out of Γ.
    - This matches the remark that estimates for observed outcomes "match the historical ground truth exactly" (p. 14). That match then holds by construction, not as validation.
    - It also sits oddly with the statement that the cohort differences "further justify our use of doubly robust estimation" (p. 17).
  - *Tree training*: maximum depth 8; at least 50 patients per leaf (5.6% of the training set); a complexity parameter tuned on a 15% hold-out (p. 12). No optimality gap or certificate is reported (pp. 11–12, 42).
  - *Evaluation devices*:
    - Leaf-level analysis (p. 18).
    - Evaluation by the test-half estimators (Table 6, p. 19).
    - Bootstrap intervals from 1,000 resamples of 95% of the test set (p. 19).
    - Concordance between candidate trees (Table 9, p. 21).
    - Age subgroups (Table B2, p. 40).
    - Imputation sensitivity, and one vs two outcome models (Tables C1–C2, p. 42).

### S616 · A Causal DAG Prior for Synthetic Time-Series Classification Datasets (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv 2606.21776)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–11 of arXiv v1 (19 Jun 2026).
- The body (pp. 1–4) was read in full; the references (p. 5) were scanned.
- Appendices A–D (generator, training, inference, generation cost; pp. 6–8) were read in full.
- The per-dataset Tables 3–4 (pp. 8–10) and the exclusion Table 5 (pp. 10–11) were read row by row for the points cited below.
- Figure 1 (a DAG schematic) and Figure 2 (a critical-difference diagram) are images; their captions and the text were read.

**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Franco Martino O'Rourke (MIT; first and corresponding author) and Ana Trisovic (MIT) (p. 1). Bertsimas is the third and last author. The paper has no contribution statement or acknowledgements (pp. 1–11), so his role is not documented. · **Position in trajectory**:
- A workshop paper (its first-page footer: Proceedings of the 2nd ICML Workshop on Foundation Models for Structured Data, Seoul, South Korea, 2026; p. 1) in the AI line of the 2019–2026 period (SKILL.md Research Trajectory).
- It contains no optimization model, and none of its 16 references is a paper of the group (p. 5): the farthest from the lens in this batch.
- Compare card c28 S132, student-led LLM work on which, by its contribution statement, he supported the project and edited the paper.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {ML; no optimization formulation}:
  - *Entry*: a structural critique of the status quo (p. 1).
    - Foundation-model pipelines for time-series classification (TSC) are two-stage. An encoder pretrained on unlabelled series sees one sample at a time, and a separate classifier is then fitted on the frozen embeddings.
    - So the encoder cannot use the labelled training set as context, and "the two stages optimise different objectives".
  - *Alternative*: TabPFN-style in-context learning. One transformer is pretrained on synthetic datasets, so the synthetic prior sets the classifier's inductive bias (p. 1).
  - *Gap* (p. 1): "What is missing: a prior over complete labelled datasets."
    - Public labelled TSC benchmarks are small and narrow in domain.
    - KernelSynth and CauKer emit no labels.
    - The concurrent TiCT uses a binary mixup of two univariate templates. It is not built on a causal DAG, and it is not natively multivariate, multi-class or cross-modal.
  - *Structure to generate* (p. 1): tabular attributes that drive series (soil type → humidity); series that drive other series; temporal self-causation; trajectories that determine a tabular outcome (growth → yield).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - *Typed nodes*: TabPFN's causal-DAG prior (Hollmann et al. 2025) is extended with two node types, tabular (a scalar per sample) and series-valued (a length-T trajectory) (p. 2).
    - Edges carry effects within and across the two types.
    - The label is read off a discretised tabular node, so classification is a readout of the same structural causal model, not a separate generator.
  - *Temporal structure is injected without recurrence*, inside an acyclic graph, by one random 1-D convolution per series node (causal padding, random dilation). This avoids unrolled dynamic SCMs and their instability under an untrained prior (p. 2; Eq. 1, p. 6).
  - *Probe*: TabPFN v2.5 is finetuned with only the input/output edits needed for n × m × T inputs, so that any performance change can be credited to the prior (pp. 3, 7):
    - Channel-aligned patch tokens: each channel is padded to a multiple of G = 3.
    - Per-channel z-scoring, with the internal normalize_x step disabled.
    - Channel shuffling instead of column shuffling in ensembles.
    - The tokeniser, transformer, decoder and in-context objective are unchanged.
  - *What changed vs the nearest classic*: the prior, not the model. TabPFN's tabular prior becomes a typed DAG with series nodes, GP roots and Conv1D mechanisms (pp. 2–3).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - *Generator* (pp. 6–7):
    - Graph: a layered feed-forward DAG with log-uniform widths and depth, and edges dropped at random. Node types are sampled.
    - Three guarantees are enforced by resampling. A series root, a hidden series node and a discretised tabular target exist; every non-root node has a parent; and every series node has a series parent.
    - Mechanisms: small networks with random activations (tabular nodes), nearest-prototype discretisation (categorical features and the label), Conv1D (series nodes), and optional Gaussian noise.
    - Roots: normal or uniform scalars; GP trajectories with one random composite kernel per dataset from {linear, RBF, periodic} (Eq. 2).
    - Post-processing: value warps, temporal granularity, predictive truncation, and values missing completely at random. Classes are capped at 10.
  - *Ranges*: hyperparameter ranges are given as symbols (a_d, b_d, α_drop, β_drop, π_S, ...) without values, except n ∈ [30, 1400] and T ∈ [6, 2000] (pp. 6–8).
  - *Evaluation data*: the 75 UCR/UEA datasets with K ≤ 10 and m·T ≤ 500, which is TabPFN's operating regime. The 83 excluded datasets are listed with reasons (pp. 3, 10–11). Default train/test splits are used (p. 3).
  - *Training*: cross-entropy on held-out samples of synthetic datasets (the PFN loss), with AdamW, warm-up and cosine annealing (p. 8). The number of synthetic datasets, the steps, the seeds and the compute are not stated.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis** (no theorems):
  - A structural causal model z_i = f_i(z_PA(i), ε_i) on a DAG (p. 6); the Conv1D mechanism (Eq. 1, p. 6); GP root sampling (Eq. 2, p. 7); the PFN objective L = E[−log q_θ(y_test | X_test, D_train)] (p. 8).
  - *Attribution design*: four configurations separate the steps (p. 3):
    - (A) TabPFN as is.
    - (B) The input/output edits only.
    - (C) B finetuned on the full prior.
    - (D) B finetuned on the prior with every series component switched off (series nodes, GP roots, Conv1D, series augmentations; T = 1).
  - *Statistics*: paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over the 75 datasets, on ROC–AUC and accuracy; shares of wins; a critical-difference diagram (Demšar 2006) (pp. 3–4).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - *Main table* (Table 1, p. 4), mean accuracy / AUC:

    | Ensemble size | A | B | D | C |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | e = 1 | .8436 / .9372 | .8378 / .9368 | .8366 / .9354 | .8559 / .9440 |
    | e = 8 | .8525 / .9405 | .8489 / .9399 | .8493 / .9393 | .8575 / .9444 |

  - *Tests* (Table 2, p. 4):
    - C vs D: p_AUC = 3.0×10⁻⁸ (C wins 73.3% of datasets, D 12.0%), and 1.8×10⁻⁷ at e = 8.
    - C vs A: p_AUC = 4.4×10⁻⁴. This comparison is tested at e = 1 only.
    - D vs A: not significant (0.10).
    - B beats D on AUC (1.7×10⁻⁴).
  - *Effect size*: C exceeds A by about 0.007 AUC and 0.012 accuracy at e = 1, and by about 0.004 and 0.005 at e = 8 [card-writer's arithmetic from Table 1].
  - *Losses visible in the appendix*: e.g. FordB AUC .874 (A, e = 1) vs .727 (C, e = 1) and FordA .976 vs .944 (Table 3, p. 8); Wine accuracy .796 vs .704 (Table 4, p. 10). The text says only that "performance degrades on some individual datasets" (p. 4).
  - *The edits cost a little*: B loses some accuracy against A, which the authors attribute to dropping TabPFN's SVD whitening (p. 3).
  - *Generator cost*: 100 datasets in about 28 s on one CPU core (mean 0.24 s, median 0.03 s, max 8.3 s), driven by n·T (p. 8).
  - *Not reported*: finetuning compute, steps, the number of synthetic datasets, variance across finetuning runs, and code.
- **D6 Positioning**:
  - It builds openly on TabPFN (Hollmann et al. 2023, 2025; Grinsztajn et al. 2025) and on PFNs (Müller et al. 2022) (pp. 1–3).
  - It borrows the kernel bank from KernelSynth (p. 7), patching from PatchTST (p. 3) and value warps from Hollmann et al. (p. 2).

- **D2 Core idea**:
  - *An end-to-end system* (pp. 3–4, 8–11, 21–22):
    - Nightly EMR extracts (10 of them) reach an AWS database by 7:40 am and become patient-day features.
    - One XGBoost model per target and hospital produces calibrated probabilities.
    - Green alerts fire when discharge probabilities exceed thresholds. Red alerts fire on the mortality level or on its day-over-day rise.
    - Each prediction has a SHAP waterfall plot, shown in a web tool that takes feedback comments.
  - *A trust ladder for the model class* (pp. 9–10):
    - The project started with interpretable models (OCT; sparse classification) in Interpretable AI's software, and doctors examined the trees.
    - "As doctors got more receptive", it moved to XGBoost for accuracy.
    - Ten-model ensembles and tsfresh features were rejected as not worth the compute and the loss of interpretability.
  - *Human plus model* (pp. 17–18): the doctors' expected discharge date (EDD) is the comparator. AND or OR combinations with the green alert raise precision or recall.
  - *Rollout as method* (pp. 11–14):
    - A pilot with four physician champions at the main hospital (HA), from the second half of 2021, with weekly feedback.
    - Then wider use at HA (2022).
    - Then three hospitals, chosen for their diversity and their appetite for experimentation.
    - Then the remaining three.
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - *Data*: 180,682 patients, 280,593 admissions and 1,375,215 patient days. Records start in January 2016 (HA), January 2021 (HF) or January 2020 (the others) (pp. 8, 32).
  - *Features*: patient-day rows in six groups: current conditions, labs, clinical measurements, operational time-series summaries, prior history, and ward and hospital variables (pp. 8, 29–30).
  - *Splits*: chronological per hospital, 50/20/30 train/validation/test (p. 9). Isotonic calibration is fitted on the first half of the test period and assessed on the second (p. 10).
  - *Imputation* (pp. 30–32):

    - Columns over 50% missing are dropped, and OptImpute (the group's method) fills the rest.
