### S819 · On the large deviations behaviour of acyclic networks of G/G/1 queues (LIDS-P-2278, MIT Laboratory for Information and Decision Systems 1994, no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–45 (cover, abstract, §§1–9, references, Appendix). The proofs were followed through the prose, since the displayed formulas are OCR-garbled. First-page check: the title matches, the authors are Bertsimas, Paschalidis and Tsitsiklis (LIDS and ORC), and the report is dated December 1994 (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ioannis Ch. Paschalidis, John N. Tsitsiklis · **Position in trajectory**: the 1994 report version of S177 (Ann. Appl. Probab. 1998; card s04). The text refers readers to Paschalidis's thesis, listed as "in preparation" (pp. 16, 43). It reuses the distributional laws of Bertsimas–Nakazato and Bertsimas–Mourtzinou (pp. 4, 13, 41–42).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; AP (high-speed packet networks) as motivation}:
  - Packet networks are to run at loss probabilities of order 10⁻⁹, so tail estimates are needed (p. 3).
  - Single-queue large-deviations (LD) results exist; for networks there are only bounds whose tightness is unclear (p. 3).
  - The closest prior work (de Veciana–Courcoubetis–Walrand, dVCW93) handles deterministic servers in discrete time. But its departure-process result rests on a technical assumption that is not re-verified for the departure process, so the authors doubt it can be applied inductively (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - Impose LD Assumptions B and C on the external arrivals and the service.
  - Prove that each network operation maps processes satisfying them to processes satisfying them: passing through a queue, superposition of independent streams, Bernoulli splitting.
  - Induct over the acyclic network, which the paper calls a calculus of acyclic networks (pp. 8–10).
  - The argument is self-contained and avoids the Dembo–Zajic technical result (p. 4). Most likely paths are characterized concretely (pp. 4, 12, 22–23).
  - Side result: the Palm and time-stationary versions of a point process share LD behaviour (pp. 4, 31–32).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - Topology and traffic: a directed acyclic graph in which any two directed paths meet in at most one node (p. 7); a single class with FCFS, without loss of generality for one traffic type (p. 3).
  - Processes: stationary, possibly dependent interarrivals; iid service at each node; independence across nodes; Bernoulli routing independent of everything else (pp. 3, 7). Stability: E[A] > E[B] (p. 11).
  - Assumption B: a one-sided LDP for arrival partial sums (the "many arrivals" tail only) and Gärtner–Ellis for service (pp. 9–10).
  - Assumption C: sample-path LDP lower bounds, from Mogulskii's theorem under independence or from mild mixing (pp. 10, 43–45). Renewal, Markov-modulated and mixing processes satisfy both (p. 10).
  - The iid service assumption can be weakened to an approximate-independence mixing condition (p. 24). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Waiting time: Theorem 4.1 is taken from the literature, "This result is not new", with the proof omitted (p. 11). The most likely path is the optimizer of an infimum over empirical arrival and service rates; the matching upper bound is "omitted" (p. 12).
  - Queue length: the distributional-law relation, extended beyond renewal processes in the LD setting (Lemma 4.2, Theorem 4.3, pp. 13–16). The lower bound comes from dualizing the constraint Λ_A(θ) + Λ_B(−θ) ≤ 0 on θ (p. 16).
  - Departures:
    - a sample-path upper bound (Prop. 5.1, pp. 17–18);
    - a lower bound that needs Assumption C, built on a forcing set of paths on which every customer finds the server busy (Prop. 5.2, Lemma 5.3, pp. 19–22);
    - the two matched by conjugate calculus (scaling and infimal convolution, p. 20).
    
    Theorem 5.5 and Corollary 5.6 return the departures' log-MGF as a convex dual, so the induction can continue (pp. 24–25).
  - Special cases: the G/D/1 queue reproduces dVCW93's result "exactly", and the M/M/1 queue recovers Burke's theorem in the LD regime (pp. 26–27).
  - Superposition goes through a Palm–stationary bridge built from Walrand's relation and Hölder's inequality (Theorems 6.1 and 6.4, pp. 28–32). Splitting is handled by Stirling counting (Theorem 7.1, pp. 36–37).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: one Matlab example (pp. 39–41).
  - Setup: two tandem queues with deterministic servers (rate 3.87) and two-state Markov-modulated deterministic arrivals (mean rate 3.29).
  - Results: θ₁* = −18.62. The first queue has "a smoothing effect", since departures deviate less than arrivals. θ₂* = −∞, so no large queue builds at the second node.
  - No simulation check and no failures are reported.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Claim: the results are "among the few" LD studies of a network (p. 41).
  - Limits: single class, acyclic, paths meeting at most once (pp. 7, 41); two proofs omitted (pp. 11–12).
  - Open problems: feedback, several traffic types, and scheduling policies chosen in the LD regime (p. 41).
  - Versions [card s04, S177]: the journal version's card records deterministic splitting and routing fractions, θ₁* = −9.47 in the same tandem example, and the departure theorem numbered 5.4. Here the splitting is Bernoulli (§7, pp. 36–37), θ₁* = −18.62 (p. 40) and the theorem is 5.5 (p. 24). Nothing in S819 explains the later change of the exponent.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 12, 16, 20, 24): optimization is the analytic language, not a solved model.
  - Rate functions are infima over empirical-rate paths, and their optimizers are the most likely paths.
  - Constraints are dualized to match bounds, and convex duality returns the log-MGF the induction needs.
  - No solver class is involved, as card s04 found for S177.
  - Context (not counted): Tension T3, a probability paper whose proofs run on convex duality.
- Method 6 · evidence ✅ (pp. 1, 16, 43): written with the co-adviser Tsitsiklis and with Paschalidis, the student they co-advised (card s04); the student's thesis on the subject is cited as in preparation.
- New-pattern candidate: **closure calculus for decomposition** (pp. 8–10, 24, 30, 37), an existing name (card s04).
- New-pattern candidate: **most likely path as an optimizer** (pp. 12, 22–23, 38–39), an existing name (card s04).
- New-pattern candidate: **subsume the incumbent as a special case** (pp. 26–27): the G/D/1 case reproduces dVCW93, and the M/M/1 case recovers Burke's theorem.

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a network-wide tail result, define an input class by LD assumptions and prove that each elementary operation (queue, merge, split) maps the class into itself. Then induct over an acyclic graph, restricting the topology (paths meet at most once) so that merged streams stay independent (pp. 7–10).
- Get a matching lower bound from a set of sample paths on which the event is forced: every customer finds the server busy, so departures equal services. Then match it to the upper bound by convex conjugacy (pp. 19–20).
- Check a new calculus against the classics first: reproduce a published special case exactly, and a textbook theorem (Burke's) in the new regime (pp. 26–27).
- When one stream is seen from another stream's arrival epochs, first prove that the Palm and time-stationary laws share LD behaviour, then work with the time-stationary one (pp. 28–32).

**Quotes**:
- "However, we should note that it is not very clear to us how the large deviations result for the departure process in [dVCW93] can be applied inductively." (p. 4)
- "In this way, our approach provides a calculus of acyclic networks since we will be able to determine the large deviations behaviour of each individual queue inductively." (p. 10)

### S241 · A critical survey of optimization models for tactical and strategic aspects of air traffic flow management (NASA contractor report 1997, NASA/CR-97-206409 per the NTRS record in card a3-01; no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–35 (all prose, Models 1–3, the two-flight example, Tables 1–2, conclusions, references); p. 36 is blank. First-page check: the title matches, and the authors are Dimitris Bertsimas and Amedeo Odoni of MIT (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthor**: Amedeo Odoni, his PhD adviser (SKILL.md, Academic Lineage) · **Position in trajectory**:
- The survey reviews the group's own 1994 ATFM papers (Vranas–Bertsimas–Odoni; Bertsimas–Stock) (pp. 23–29).
- It announces a "Part II" on capacitated flow models (p. 8).
- S275 (2011, this batch) cites it for the field's survey and taxonomy (S275 p. 4).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {AP + IO; SD for the stochastic models}:
  - Congestion costs airlines "well over $5 billion per year" (p. 2). Traffic flow management, through ground holding and flow redistribution, is the best available short-term remedy (pp. 2–3).
  - The stated task, from more than 40 references: find the best available models, their typical contexts, illustrative formulations and solution methods (p. 1).
  - Taxonomy (pp. 4–5): single- and multi-airport ground holding (each deterministic or stochastic), and the generalized tactical problem, which adds airborne delay.
- **D2 Core idea**: a review as triage. For each class it names the best available models, their computational performance and their applications to date (p. 1), and gives a verdict:
  - Single-airport ground holding: the deterministic Model 1 is a transportation problem, convertible to an assignment problem, so polynomial (pp. 9–11). The stochastic static Model 2b (capacity scenarios with coupling constraints, Richetta–Odoni) and the dynamic Model 3 (stages from a probability tree) are "the most advanced existing models" (pp. 7, 11–19).
  - Generalized tactical problem: five model types. The authors rank Bertsimas–Stock (1994), their own, first: the first optimal approach and the most successful computationally (pp. 23–24).
  - Multi-airport ground holding: four models. Andreatta–Brunetta's independent comparison finds the Bertsimas–Stock special case best in most cases (pp. 28–29).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - Single-airport network: one capacitated arrival airport, deterministic travel times, known ground and air delay costs, T discrete periods (p. 7). Justified for the US, where over 95% of delays arise at airports, but not for Western Europe (p. 8).
  - Model 2 assumes few capacity scenarios (4 or less), 3–4 cost classes and a constant air-delay cost, each with an operational reason (pp. 12–13, 16).
  - Network models: all generalized-tactical and multi-airport models are deterministic, and uncertainty is handled by re-solving (pp. 23, 27).
  - Data cited: FAA-provided OAG schedules and a EUROCONTROL set (p. 26); Boston Logan for Models 2–3 (p. 22).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: formulation strength is the yardstick.
  - Model 2a's coefficient matrix is totally unimodular, so its LP relaxation suffices and it can be solved as a min-cost flow (p. 14).
  - Model 2b's unimodularity is a Richetta–Odoni conjecture from integral LP runs, "still an open question" (p. 15).
  - Bertsimas–Stock's LP relaxations came out integral, explained in part by the connectivity constraints being facets of the convex hull (p. 27).
  - A solve device: solve the network-flow part without the capacity constraints to get a basis, then add the capacities and continue with dual simplex (p. 27).
  - Lindsay et al.'s weaker relaxations need branch and bound plus strengthening techniques (p. 27).
  - The two-flight example: optimal static cost $1,000 against dynamic $920. Dynamic strategies favour long-range flights (pp. 19–22).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - Bertsimas–Stock on FAA data (SUN SPARC20, CPLEX 3.0, GAMS 2.25; p. 26):
    - 278 flights, 10 airports and 178 sectors (43,226 constraints, 18,733 variables): an integral LP optimum in about 30 min;
    - 1,002 flights, 18 airports and 305 sectors (151,662 constraints, 69,497 variables): about 2 h.
  - Against EUROCONTROL's CASA heuristic (Vranas 1995): about 40% less delay in 3 of 5 cases and about 5% in the other two. CASA answers in about 30 s, flight-level feasibility was not clearly checked, and the evidence is "very preliminary" (p. 26).
  - Multi-airport tests: 1,000 and 3,000 flights at the "infeasibility border", found by trial and error, solved with CPLEX MIP 2.1 in up to 9,411 CPU s. One instance had 4% of flights non-integral (pp. 29–30).
  - Models 2–3: tested on Boston Logan data, with no real-time test with controllers (p. 22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: conclusions (pp. 30–32):
  - single-airport models are a "fine paradigm" under uncertainty, but assume uncapacitated en-route airspace;
  - generalized-tactical optimization is a viable alternative to CASA, held back by computation and "the difficulty of gaining TFM manager acceptance" (p. 31);
  - multi-airport problems solve efficiently, and the relaxations were integral in all but one multi-airport case and in all tactical cases;
  - the models "represent the strongest formulations proposed to date" (pp. 31–32).
  
  Gaps named:
  - the stochastic single-airport work was never extended to the multi-airport problem (p. 27);
  - priority-rule heuristics may become a good alternative to exact models (p. 29).
  
  Draft state [my reading]: leftover labels (Model B, Model D, Models E and F) stand for Models 1–3 (pp. 10–11, 14, 19); Table 1's caption says 3,000 flights for the 1,000-flight table (p. 30); "Section 6" is cited for Section 5 (p. 29).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (pp. 14–15, 27, 31–32): models are judged by formulation strength (total unimodularity, integral relaxations, facets) and by solve time on realistic data. "Strongest formulations" is the closing verdict. This is 1997 practice of the strong-formulation mark that SKILL.md dates to the 2005 IO book (Domain Map IO; Tension T4).
- Method 5 · variant ⚠ (pp. 22, 26, 31):
  - Matches Method 5: the owner's operational rule (EUROCONTROL's CASA, first planned first served) is the baseline, and the optimizer's own cost (30 min–2 h against 30 s) is printed, with the evidence called very preliminary.
  - Departs from it: the comparison is a third party's (Vranas), no controller-in-the-loop test exists (p. 22), and the adoption obstacles are named, not tested (p. 31).
- Method 3 · variant ⚠ (pp. 4–5, 11, 22): the trade-offs a decision-maker would price are named, but no curve is drawn. They are:
  - conservative vs liberal ground holding (pp. 4–5);
  - equity among operators vs cost: equity constraints still leave significant savings (p. 11);
  - the dynamic bias toward long-range flights (p. 22).
  
  It precedes the group's price-of-fairness line (S076, card c16).
- New-pattern candidate: **review as triage: best available model per class with computational performance and use to date** (pp. 1, 7, 23–30), an existing name (card a3-01), now confirmed in the full text.
- New-pattern candidate: **feasibility-border test instances** (p. 30), an existing name (cards c04, s2-02). It recurs in S275 (this batch, p. 25).
- New-pattern candidate (new): **self-ranking in one's own field review, backed by a third-party comparison** (pp. 23–24, 26, 28–29, 31–32). The survey ranks its authors' model first and leans on Andreatta–Brunetta's independent comparison. It also prints the counter-evidence: savings of about 5% in two of five CASA cases, and feasibility left unchecked.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Organize a field review by problem class. For each class give the best available model, an illustrative formulation, its computational record with sizes and machines, its applications to date and the open gaps (pp. 1, 9–30).
- To speed an LP with side constraints, solve the network-flow core alone for a basis, then add the side constraints and continue with dual simplex (p. 27).
- Test integer models at the "infeasibility border", the tightest capacities that remain feasible, where the problem is most relevant and hardest. Locate it by trial and error and report times there (p. 30).
- Use a two-flight example with a probability tree to show what dynamic re-planning gains over a static plan, and which systematic bias it introduces (pp. 19–22).

**Quotes**:
- "On the other hand, this evidence is very preliminary." (p. 26)
- "These models represent the strongest formulations proposed to date for this class of problems." (p. 31)

### S452 · Applications of semidefinite optimization in stochastic project scheduling (Singapore-MIT Alliance report 2002, DSpace hdl 1721.1/3994 per card a3-02; no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–5 (abstract, §§I–IV, Tables I–IV, references). First-page check: the title matches, and the authors are Dimitris Bertsimas, Karthik Natarajan and Chung Piaw Teo (MIT and NUS) (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Karthik Natarajan, Chung Piaw Teo · **Position in trajectory**: carries the Bertsimas–Popescu moment-bound line (refs [2] and [3], p. 2) to PERT networks. Partly supported by the Singapore-MIT Alliance (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {CP + SD; AP (project scheduling)}:
  - Expected completion time is #P-complete even for independent two-valued durations (Hagstrom), which motivates bounds (p. 1).
  - Durations are dependent because activities share resources, and in practice only means, variances and covariances are known (p. 1).
  - CLT approximations give no bounds and fail on small networks (pp. 1–2). Birge–Maddox give formal bounds but do not handle correlations effectively, and they name that as future research (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - Write the worst-case expected tardiness over all distributions with given first and second (joint) moments as a moment problem.
  - Dualize it, with strong duality from Klein Haneveld.
  - Path by path, the dual constraint is co-positivity of a matrix. Replace co-positivity by the sufficient condition PSD plus entrywise nonnegative (p. 3).
  - The result is an SDP with |P| + 1 semidefinite constraints (pp. 2–3).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - An acyclic project network with known minimum durations a ≥ 0 and known E[x_i] and E[x_i x_j] (pp. 1–2). Tardiness (R(x) − T)⁺ is upper semicontinuous, so strong duality holds (pp. 2–3).
  - Test data: the two small examples of Birge–Maddox (10 activities and 3 paths; 5 activities), with a_i, E[x_i] and E[x_i²] (pp. 3–4).
  - The cross moments used in the examples are not listed [my reading].
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Dual variables y₀ for the probability mass and y_i for the moments; the semi-infinite dual constraint is written per path as a quadratic form in (w, 1) that is nonnegative on the orthant, i.e. a co-positive matrix (pp. 2–3).
  - Testing co-positivity is co-NP-complete, so the PSD-plus-nonnegative restriction is used and gives a valid upper bound (p. 3).
  - [my reading] The SDP restricts the exact co-positive program, so its bound is valid but not shown to be tight.
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: SeDuMi; nine due dates over the two examples. The SDP bound is below Birge–Maddox in all nine, e.g. 4.16 vs 5 at T = 0 in Example 2 and 0.48 vs 0.73 at T = 25 in Example 1 (pp. 3–4). There are no solve times, no large network and no correlated instance (pp. 3–4).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Stated limit: the number of SDP constraints grows with the number of paths, which "may be too huge for large networks". The remedies offered are heuristics that screen for likely critical paths and LP-based cutting planes for the SDP (p. 4).
  - Extensions: higher moments via sums of squares (Parrilo), piecewise-quadratic tardiness, and bounds on the variance and on the probability of overrun (p. 4).
  - [my reading] The abstract's "tight upper bounds" (p. 1) claims more than the body's valid upper bound from a sufficient condition (p. 3).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (pp. 2–4): a probability question owned by PERT analysis becomes a conic program through its dual. The boundary is printed twice:
  - co-positivity is co-NP-complete, so a tractable restriction is used (p. 3);
  - the path count limits scale (p. 4).
  
  This matches Method 1, steps 3–5, and the CP row of SKILL.md's Domain Map.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠ (pp. 3–4): the incumbent bound is dueled on its own published examples and loses all nine comparisons. But no times are reported, there is no large instance, and the advertised advantage (correlations) is never exercised.
- New-pattern candidate: **assume only what can be estimated, worst-case the rest** (pp. 1–2), an existing name (cards c08, a3-02).
- New-pattern candidate (new): **rival's stated open problem as the entry point** (p. 2): the paper quotes Birge–Maddox's own future-research sentence on correlation information, then answers it.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To bound the worst-case expectation of a max of linear functions under moment information:
  - dualize;
  - write each piece's dual constraint as co-positivity of a bordered matrix;
  - replace co-positivity by PSD plus entrywise nonnegative, which gives an SDP upper bound (pp. 2–3).
- Duel a new bound on the incumbent's own published instances first, so the comparison cannot be blamed on the choice of data (pp. 3–4).
- Name the scaling bottleneck in the conclusion with two concrete remedies, here path screening and cutting planes for SDPs (p. 4).

**Quotes**:
- "In contrast, we provide formal upper bounds that are distribution free." (p. 2)
- "The principle limitation of the current technique is that the number of semidefinite constraints it generates is proportional to the number of paths." (p. 4)

### S275 · Multistage air traffic flow management under capacity uncertainty: a robust and adaptive optimization approach (51st AGIFORS Annual Proceedings 2011, pp. 692–721; DSpace hdl 1721.1/87621; no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–31 (DSpace cover, abstract, §§1–6, Tables 1–4, Figures 1–10, Appendix A with the proof of Theorem 3, references). First-page check: the title matches, and the authors are Shubham Gupta and Dimitris Bertsimas, dated May 31, 2011 (p. 2); the DSpace cover gives the AGIFORS citation (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthor**: Shubham Gupta, first author (ORC) · **Position in trajectory**:
- Funded by NSF EFRI-0735905 (p. 2).
- Builds on the Bertsimas–Stock-Patterson model, augmented with Bertsimas–Gupta's fairness reversals (pp. 4–6), and on the affine-policy results of Bertsimas and Goyal (pp. 5, 21).
- Cites the 1997 survey S241 (this batch) for the field's taxonomy (p. 4).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO + IO; AP}:
  - Weather caused about 60% of US air traffic delays in 2009 (OPSNET) (p. 3).
  - Network ATFM models assume deterministic capacities, which "may lead to suboptimal and possibly infeasible solutions" (p. 3). Stochastic work exists only for single airports, so the paper claims the first network model under uncertainty (p. 4).
  - Choice of tool: stochastic programming is set aside because distributions are unknown and scenarios multiply. Robust optimization is chosen, with adaptive optimization added against its conservatism in multistage problems (pp. 4–5).
- **D2 Core idea**: a weather-front uncertainty set. For each airspace element, capacity drops to αC between an uncertain arrival time T_a and T_a + d; fronts propagate through phases with lags (pp. 9–13). Results:
  1. conv(U) has a polyhedral description (Theorem 1, p. 19). Only the worst α is needed, so two parameters govern the set (Remark 2, p. 19).
  2. The robust problem equals the deterministic TFMP at the row-wise minimum capacity b_min (Theorem 2, p. 20).
  3. The LP relaxation of the multistage adaptive problem is solved by affine policies when the set is a simplex, and by piecewise affine ones otherwise. The number of pieces is counted by the set's extreme points (Theorem 3, Algorithm 1, pp. 21–22).
  4. If b_min ∈ U, adaptivity gains nothing (Prop. 3, Corollary 1, pp. 23–24).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - A binary time-indexed TFMP with capacity, connectivity and reversal constraints (pp. 6–10). Only the right-hand-side capacities are uncertain; A and c are certain (p. 14).
  - Assumption 1, that αC is an integer, is adopted for practicality and to simplify the integrality proofs (p. 11).
  - Step-function capacity profiles are justified on two Lincoln Labs sector profiles as "an appropriate engineering approximation" (pp. 11–12). A typical day has 3–4 fronts (p. 12).
  - Experiments: a synthetic 10 × 10 grid of 100 sectors, with 55 major US airports mapped onto it and simulated fronts in three regions (pp. 24–25).
  - The abstract speaks of real-world schedules, but §5 does not name the source of the flight schedules (pp. 2, 24–25).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Integrality of the (y, z) description: randomized rounding with one uniform threshold gives a feasible solution whose expected cost equals the LP value (Prop. 1, pp. 16–17, citing the Bertsimas–Weismantel book). A convex-hull sandwich follows (Prop. 2, pp. 18–19).
  - Theorem 2 is proved by a row-wise minimum, stated for an arbitrary right-hand-side uncertainty set (p. 20).
  - Theorem 3 extends Bertsimas–Goyal's two-stage simplex result to T stages. Each stage's policy is written as a convex combination of the optimal responses at the simplex vertices, so the worst-case cost separates stage by stage (pp. 21, 28–29).
  - Boundary printed (Tables 1–2, pp. 21–22): extreme points grow exponentially in the number of affected elements and periods, while constraints grow linearly. An affine policy suffices only while E ≤ m + 1, e.g. when τ or Δ is below 3.
  - [my reading] Lemma 1 says the robust LP under a discrete set is NP-hard, with the proof left to a "full version" (p. 15). Its problem (3) has only right-hand-side uncertainty, where Theorem 2's row-wise argument would yield an LP at b_min (pp. 14–15, 20).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - Setup: ROME with CPLEX. Reported times include ROME's parsing, which takes most of the total (p. 25). No machine is stated.
