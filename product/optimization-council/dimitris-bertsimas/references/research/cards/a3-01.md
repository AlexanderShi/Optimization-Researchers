**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json). Its source is the NTUA repository record, which holds no PDF. · **Coauthors**: X. A. Papaconstantinou

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD}: an exact solution method for the stationary multi-server queue with Erlang-k interarrival times and a Coxian service-time density of order 2 (abstract).
  - The entry is a wider service-time class than exponential, not an application (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Erlang's method of stages, in purely real arithmetic (abstract):
  - first, explicit forms for the probabilities of the states with all servers busy;
  - then an algorithmic scheme that ends in a linear system of only s + 1 equations.
  - Transferable technique: get closed forms for the all-busy states first. The remaining unknowns then form a linear system whose size grows only with the number of servers (abstract).
- **D7 Writing**: the abstract sets the method against established methods with a lettered list of five advantages (abstract):
  - (a) easier to grasp and implement; (b) closed forms with more qualitative insight; (c) lower order of computational complexity;
  - (d) a possible route to an exact FCFS waiting-time analysis; (e) extension to more general systems.
  - Transferable technique: state a method's advantages over the established ones as a short lettered list of checkable claims. End with what it opens next and how far it extends.
- **D8 Contribution**: the advantages above. The abstract gives no numbers (abstract).
- **Position** (other cards):
  - The companion M/C2(a,b)/s paper S291 (Transportation Science 1988; card s2-01) cites this paper as accepted at EJOR [S291 p. 14].
  - S389 (NRL 1990; card s2-01) works out the Ek/C2/s case numerically [S389 p. 12].
  - S239 (Queueing Systems 1988; card s01) is titled an exact FCFS waiting-time analysis for G/G/s systems. Inferred from title: this is the step promised in (d).

**Method links**: none of Methods 1–6. It is an applied-probability solution method with no optimization model (abstract).
**Quote** (abstract): "In comparison to other established methods, the present approach (a) seems simpler to grasp and easier to implement, (b) provides closed form expressions offering more qualitative insight, (c) has a much lower order of computational complexity"

### S324 · Nonlinear formulations and improved randomized approximation algorithms for multicut problems (IPCO 1995, DOI 10.1007/3-540-59408-6_39)

### S798 · An asymptotically optimal algorithm for job shop scheduling (Proceedings of the 38th IEEE Conference on Decision and Control 1999, DOI 10.1109/cdc.1999.832823)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json, from OpenAlex) · **Coauthors**: David Gamarnik
- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD + IO}: job shop scheduling with the makespan objective, entered through asymptotics in the number of jobs (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea** (abstract):
  - a fluid relaxation replaces the discrete jobs by a continuous fluid;
  - its optimal solution, in closed form, gives a lower bound C_max;
  - a feasible schedule built from the fluid solution has value at most C_max + O(√C_max). The constant does not depend on the number of jobs but does depend on the processing times.
  - Transferable technique: relax a hard combinatorial schedule to a fluid model solvable in closed form and use its value as the lower bound. Then round the fluid solution to a schedule whose additive gap grows more slowly than the bound (abstract).
- **D5 (one line)**: in computational experiments the schedules are within 1% of optimality, even for moderately sized problems (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: asymptotic optimality as the number of jobs grows, and C_max + O(1) when the initially present jobs increase proportionally (abstract).
  - Stated limit: the constant depends on the processing times (abstract).
  - Journal version, by title: S130 (Journal of Algorithms 1999; card s04). Fluid-relaxation follow-ups: S205 and S207 (works.json).

**Method links**: Method 1 variant ⚠ (abstract). Relaxation first, but the bound is read from a closed-form fluid solution instead of an LP dual, and the boundary is printed (the constant depends on the processing times).
**Quotes** (abstract): "We propose a fluid relaxation for the job shop scheduling problem, in which we replace discrete jobs with the flow of a continuous fluid." · "In computational experiments our algorithms produce schedules which are within 1% of optimality even for moderately sized problems."
