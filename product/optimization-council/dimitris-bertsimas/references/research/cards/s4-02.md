### H001 · Adaptive Linear Algebra (author manuscript listed on his homepage 2023, no DOI/arXiv)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–26 (abstract, §§1–6 with Theorems 1–10, Proposition 1, Algorithms 1–3, Tables 1–2, references). Figure 1 has its caption only. First-page check: the title and the authors Bertsimas and Koukouvinos match (p. 1).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Thodoris Koukouvinos, MIT Operations Research Center (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the adaptive sequel of S458 "Robust linear algebra" (card s2-18), which it cites as "Submitted to European Journal of Operational Research, 2023". It solves its sub-problems with RPT-BB from S377, cited as submitted to Operations Research in 2023 (p. 24). The template running head "Author: Adaptive Linear Algebra" is unfilled (pp. 2–26), so this is an unfinished manuscript [my reading].

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO+LO; CP for the S-lemma and the RPT-BB sub-problems}:
  - Linear-algebra methods assume exact data and solutions that do not depend on uncertain parameters (p. 1). In the robust framework of S458 one solution must face all perturbations; here the solution may react to parameters as they are revealed, for example the realized errors of earlier periods (pp. 1, 14).
  - Motivating uses (p. 5):
    - compressed sensing with measurement error in the matrix;
    - static-equilibrium equations whose stiffness matrix is uncertain while loads depend on temperature or humidity.
  - Principal stresses motivate the eigen-pair version (p. 17).
  - Problem (2)/(5): over decision rules x(·), minimize the worst-case residual ‖A(Z)x(Z, z) − b(Z, z)‖ over a set U (pp. 2, 6). The eigen-pair version is problem (22) (p. 17).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - Restrict x(·) to linear decision rules: x = u + V1z + V2vec(Z) in the general model (p. 6), and x = u + Vz in the simplified model, where only z is revealed (p. 7).
  - Upper bounds:
    - closed form by the triangle inequality: (6) = ‖Au − b‖ + θ‖AV1 − I‖F + ρ‖AV2 − C‖F + ρ‖u‖ + θρ‖V1‖F + ρ²‖V2‖F (Theorem 1, p. 6), with an ℓ1 analogue (Theorem 2, p. 7);
    - the same bound re-derived by robust convex optimization; there the gap is read as the dropped rank-one constraints Q = wzᵀ (Theorem 3, pp. 8–9);
    - for polyhedral sets, a bound by decision rules on the dual variables (fixed recourse; Theorem 4, pp. 9–10);
    - with squared norms, an exact S-lemma form that is nonconvex (Theorem 5, p. 11).
  - **Exact solution by cutting planes**: a master problem over finitely many scenarios, plus a separation step that maximizes a convex function over a ball, solved globally by RPT-BB (Algorithm 1, pp. 12–13).
  - **Multi-period systems**: start from the robust solution (RLS); in each later period, the solution adapts to the vector of past errors (E1, …, Et−1) (Algorithm 2, p. 14).
  - **Eigen-pairs**:
    - eigenvectors follow linear decision rules, with linear sufficient conditions for orthogonality (Proposition 1, p. 18) and bounds (Theorems 7–8, pp. 18–19);
    - the eigenvalues' adaptive part µ is zero at the optimum of (27), so eigenvalues stay fixed (p. 19);
    - four sign cases on Σu and ΣV avoid the trivial zero solution (p. 20);
    - all pairs are computed by alternating optimization (Algorithm 3, p. 21).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - The data depend affinely on (Z, z) (p. 6). Uncertainty sets:
    - U2: a Frobenius ball of radius ρ for Z and an ℓ2 ball of radius θ for z (p. 6);
    - U1: the ℓ1 analogues (p. 7);
    - a polyhedral set for z (p. 9);
    - a budget set for eigen-pairs (p. 19).
  - **Synthetic data only**:
    - nearly singular matrices, whose last row is the sum of the others plus N(0, σ²) noise, with σ = 10⁻¹⁴ or 10⁻¹³ (pp. 15–16);
    - for eigen-pairs, A = A0ᵀA0 with A0 of size (n − 8) × n (p. 23).
  - **Test perturbations**: z̄ is the mean of N = 100 scenarios drawn from [0, 20], applied as A + diag(z̄) and b + z̄ (pp. 16, 23). This is far outside the training sets (ρ, θ ≤ 1). The paper itself credits the edge over the robust solution to perturbations "outside of the uncertainty set" (p. 23). [my reading] If the draws are uniform, every entry of z̄ is close to 10, so the test is close to one fixed diagonal shift.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Devices: bounds by the triangle inequality and support functions (pp. 6–9), the S-lemma (p. 11), and cutting planes with RPT-BB separation (pp. 12–13).
  - Theorem 6: the adaptive system (ALS) has relative error O((σ1 + ρ)/(σn + ρ)), against O(σ1/σn) for the nominal one (CLS). The proof runs through the block normal equations (AᵀA + ρI)[u, V] = [Aᵀb, Aᵀ] (pp. 13–14). [my reading] As with S458's Theorem 4 (card s2-18), the argument covers the squared upper-bound problem, not the exact ALS solved for Table 1.
  - Theorem 9: Algorithm 3 converges at O(1/T), by citation to block-coordinate-descent results (p. 21). [my reading] The sign-case selection and the normalization are not block minimizations of one objective; card s2-18 notes the same gap in S458.
  - Draft slips [my reading]:
    - ρ appears for θ in the proof of Theorem 1 (p. 6);
    - Theorem 4 refers to problem (13), which first appears in its proof (pp. 9–10);
    - §3.2 points to a non-existent §2.4.1 (p. 16);
    - Theorem 8 defines M with ‖µ‖2, but its proof uses ‖µ‖1 (p. 19).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - **Multi-period systems** (n = 100, ρ = 0.5, θ = 0.1, 10 periods, 10 instances): ALS equals RLS for two periods, then has the smaller error (Fig. 1, p. 15).
  - **Perturbed systems** (Table 1, p. 16):
    - CLS, by Gaussian elimination, has errors of order 10¹³–10¹⁴; RLS has 3.68–50.21; ALS is lower.
    - The exact RPT-BB solution beats the upper bound clearly at (n, ρ, θ) = (10, 0.5, 0.5) and (100, 1, 0.1). It does not run at n = 1000.
  - **Eigen-pairs** (n = 10, K = 10; Table 2, p. 23):
    - The exact adaptive solution (Ad-RPT) is best in all five (ρ, θ) settings, by small margins (e.g., 9.73 against 9.81 for the nominal).
    - The upper-bound solution (Ad-UB) is worse than the nominal in three of the five.
  - Not reported: solver version, machine, run times, variance across instances, code. Gurobi is named only as a possible solver (pp. 11, 21).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contributions: adaptive linear systems and adaptive eigen-pairs, each with bounds and an exact cutting-plane solution; benefits in multi-period systems and on perturbed ill-conditioned matrices (pp. 4, 23–24).
  - Stated limits:
    - the variables grow from O(n) to O(n²); a diagonal V restores O(n) (p. 14);
    - RPT-BB scales to hundreds of variables, the bound to thousands (p. 16);
    - all eigen-pairs come from an alternating scheme (p. 21).
  - Future: only "real-world applications" in general (p. 24).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (pp. 11, 14, 16, 21): every bound and sub-problem is SOCO or LO, except the squared S-lemma form, which the paper flags as nonconvex (p. 11). The boundary is printed in size: O(n²) variables, and hundreds against thousands of variables.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠ (pp. 16, 23): the cheap bound and the exact cutting-plane solution are reported side by side, which shows where the bound is tight and where exactness pays. But the duel is only against the nominal method and the authors' own robust solution, with no times, versions or machine.
- Method 3 · variant ⚠ (pp. 14–16, 23): ρ and θ vary over a small grid set by hand. There is no curve and no bound tying the knob to protection. The price of adaptivity is stated in problem size (p. 14), not in objective.
- New-pattern candidate: *adaptive sequel of one's own robust formulation* ✅ (pp. 1, 3, 5): the full text confirms what card a3-04 inferred from the title.
- New-pattern candidate: *exact optimum on a small solvable instance as the certificate for a heuristic* ✅ (p. 16): exact RPT-BB solutions at n ≤ 100 certify the upper-bound method, which alone reaches n = 1000.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Bound a min–max residual under a linear decision rule by splitting the norm with the triangle inequality. Each uncertain block contributes its set radius times a Frobenius norm, which gives a convex SOCO bound in closed form (Theorems 1–2, pp. 6–7).
- Make a cutting-plane method exact when its separation step maximizes a convex function. Replace the function by its biconjugate, then solve globally by RPT-BB (pp. 12–13).
- Keep orthogonality between decision rules linear. Impose the coefficient-wise sufficient conditions uᵀuj = 0, Vjᵀu + Vᵀuj = 0 and VᵀVj = O (Proposition 1, p. 18).
- Avoid the zero solution without nonconvex constraints. Solve four sign cases with the linear constraints Σu ≥ δ or ≤ −δ (and likewise for V), then keep the best (p. 20).

**Quotes**:
- "we observe that the upper bound for ALS is in general tight, however there exist instances where the exact solution obtained by cutting planes and RPT-BB is significantly better" (p. 16)
- "Finally, we note that RPT-BB scales to problems with variables in the hundreds, while the upper bound scales to problems with variables in the thousands" (p. 16)

### S597 · Stochastic and Dynamic Vehicle Routing in the Euclidean Plane: The Multiple-Server, Capacitated Vehicle Case. 1990 (MIT Operations Research Center Working Paper OR 224-90 1990, no DOI/arXiv)
Read level: **full**: file pp. 1–42 (series page, abstract, §§1–6, references). The OCR prose reads well, but most displayed formulas are garbled, so the constants below come from the prose. First-page check: the series page (p. 1, where the OCR misreads "Besimas") and the title page (p. 3) name D. J. Bertsimas and G. van Ryzin, OR 224-90, August 1990.

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Garrett van Ryzin, MIT Operations Research Center (p. 3) · **Position in trajectory**: the August 1990 working paper behind S032 (OR 1993, card c01). It builds on its reference [12], "submitted for publication to Operations Research" (p. 40), which is S016 (OR 1991, card c01). Funded by NSF grant DDM-9014751 (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; AP as motivation: utility repair, delivery, emergency services, couriers (p. 5)}:
  - Classical vehicle routing problems are static and deterministic, and they minimize travel. As models of practice they are "not always appropriate", and in dynamic settings delivery time is often the better objective (p. 5).
  - Rolling-horizon procedures are "inherently ad hoc" and give no strategic insight (p. 6).
  - Self-critique as the entry point: their own single uncapacitated vehicle is "somewhat unrealistic for most practical purposes" (p. 10).
  - Problem: policies that minimize expected system time for m vehicles from m depots, each vehicle serving at most q demands per tour (p. 10).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - The predecessor's bracket is kept: lower bounds over all policies, then policies within a constant factor of them in heavy traffic, and optimal in light traffic (pp. 8–10).
  - **m uncapacitated vehicles**:
    - in heavy traffic, system time falls by a factor 1/m² (p. 3);
    - the guarantee of randomized assignment grows like m (p. 18), and that of the G/G/m TSP policy like (m + 1)/2 (p. 21);
    - independent partitioning into m equal subregions keeps any single-server constant for every m (pp. 21–22).
  - **Capacity q**:
    - a radial collection term (after Haimovich and Rinnooy Kan) enters the stability condition, which therefore depends on the region's geometry (pp. 11, 24–25);
    - qTSP and qG/G/m policies achieve constant factors (pp. 25–29);
    - in heavy traffic the median is the optimal depot location (Theorems 4–5, pp. 28–29).
  - **Extensions**:
    - d dimensions and nonuniform demand (pp. 31–33);
    - three strategic models built from the asymptotic formulas: fleet sizing, districting with vehicles of different speeds, and the travel–waiting trade-off (pp. 33–38).
- **D3 Model and data**:
  - Poisson arrivals at rate λ; uniform locations in a bounded convex region with piecewise-linear boundary; general on-site service with finite first and second moments; constant speed v (p. 8).
  - Admitted simplifications:
    - the limiting means are assumed to exist (p. 12);
    - each tour visits exactly q demands, which "simplifies the analysis and seems quite reasonable for the heavy traffic case" (p. 22);
    - subregions are "reasonably similar and compact" (p. 21);
    - multi-depot policies need identical Voronoi cells and m = kp (pp. 28–31).
  - No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Light traffic: a relaxation to the m-median gives Theorem 1 (p. 13), and the mSQM policy is asymptotically optimal (p. 17).
  - Heavy traffic: Theorem 2 follows from two lemmas (pp. 14–16):
    - Lemma 1: a tagged demand, Wald's equation and Markov's inequality;
    - Lemma 2: stochastic dominance from the Haimovich–Magnanti k-median result;
    - the constant is γ ≈ 0.153.
  - Capacitated lower bound: two random points on a tour bound its length by two radial legs plus the hops between them (Theorem 3, Lemma 3, pp. 23–24). The resulting factor ⅓ is called a by-product of the proof (p. 24).
  - Policies are analysed with Kingman's G/G/m heavy-traffic limit and TSP asymptotics, after the set size is rewritten as y, the ratio of travel per demand to its critical value (pp. 18–21, 27).
  - Theorem 4 is proved by contradiction (p. 28), and Theorem 5's proof is omitted (p. 29). Theorem 7 (d → ∞) rests on a conjecture about TSP constants (p. 32).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - No new simulation. The single-server constants it reuses include simulated values from the predecessor (SFC ≈ 0.66, NN ≈ 0.64; pp. 9–10).
  - One numerical illustration: y* = 0.906 gives T = 578, and cutting travel per demand by 10% (y = 0.81) raises T by 21%, to 702 (Fig. 2, p. 37).
  - The claim that the SFC and NN policies run near critical travel rests on simulations that are not shown (p. 38).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contributions: stability and congestion results for m capacitated vehicles; light- and heavy-traffic policies; extensions to d dimensions and to arbitrary densities; strategic planning models (pp. 38–39).
  - Open problems:
    - Conjecture 1: partitioning an optimal single-server policy gives an optimal m-server policy (p. 22);
    - Conjecture 2: the lower bound for nonuniform demand (p. 33);
    - closing the gap in the constant "probably requires a new approach to the proof of Lemma 1" (p. 39);
    - networks in place of the Euclidean metric (p. 39);
    - for asymmetric depots, "More sophisticated bounds are no doubt needed" (p. 31).
  - **Comparison with card S032** (cross-card; S032 was not re-read):
    - γ ≈ 0.153 here (p. 16), against ≈ 0.266 in S032, which credits a new Lemma 2. This confirms the S032 card's inference that 0.266 came after mid-1990.
    - Here the region is convex with a piecewise-linear boundary (p. 8); S032 assumes only boundedness.
    - The radial-wedge (modified G/G/m) and D/G/m policies of S032 are absent here (pp. 17–22).
    - §4 (higher dimensions, general densities) and §5.1–5.2 (fleet sizing, districting) are not mentioned in card S032. Whether the journal dropped them was not checked.

**Method links**:
- Method 3 · evidence ✅, early form (pp. 36–38):
  - One interpretable knob, y, indexes the travel–waiting trade-off, with a worked price: 10% less travel per demand costs 21% more system time.
  - Radial costs are flagged as not tradeable.
  - Nonparametric policies are faulted precisely for lacking the knob (p. 38).
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 6, 34): a model replaces ad hoc rolling horizons, and its asymptotic formulas serve as "building blocks" for normative planning models. The class kept is an analysable queue, not LP or MIO.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠ (pp. 16, 22, 24, 39): the certificates are analytic lower bounds, and open gaps are printed as conjectures, not results.
- New-pattern candidates:
  - *lower bound over all policies, then constant-factor policies* ✅ (pp. 13–31);
  - *print the looseness of your own constants and the conjecture that would close it* ✅ (pp. 21, 24, 39);
  - *carry deterministic devices into the stochastic version and measure what survives* ✅: TSP asymptotics, k-median dominance and the radial collection cost (pp. 11, 16, 19–20);
  - *asymptotic formulas as building blocks for strategic models* (new): the fleet size from min c1T(m) + c2m, and areas proportional to vehicle speeds (pp. 34–36);
  - *working paper sharpened before the journal* (new): the differences in γ, region and policies listed under D8.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Lower-bound a dynamic routing policy with a tagged demand:
  - travel to the demand is at least the minimum distance from the locations that could precede it (the queue, the servers, the arrivals during its wait);
  - bound that minimum with Wald's equation and a stochastic-dominance lemma;
  - substitute the bound into the stability condition (pp. 14–16).
- Lift single-server guarantees to m servers by independent partitioning. Area and arrival rate both scale by 1/m, so the constant factor is unchanged (pp. 21–22).
- Lower-bound the length of a capacitated tour by two radial legs to random points of the tour plus the hops between them (pp. 23–24).
- Rewrite a policy's tuning parameter as the ratio of a cost to its critical value. In heavy traffic the optimum then sits near 1, and the curve becomes the decision-maker's trade-off (pp. 20–21, 36–37).

**Quotes**:
- "This appears to be just a by-product of the randomization used in the proof rather than a fundamental characteristic of the capacitated case." (p. 24)
- "Although these policies have advantages for systems where exact parameter estimation is difficult or parameters change frequently, this lack of travel/waiting cost control should be considered a disadvantage." (p. 38)

### S836 · Signed Budget Uncertainty for Robust Mixed-Integer Optimization (Optimization Online 2026, no DOI/arXiv)
Read level: **partial**.
- Read in full: pp. 1–17 (abstract, §§1–6), Appendix A's duality derivation (pp. 17–19), the start of Appendix B.1 (pp. 20–21), Appendices D–E (pp. 34–37) and the references (pp. 37–38).
- Not read: the rest of Appendix B (pp. 21–22) and Appendix C (pp. 22–34), which hold the laminar proofs and the big-M constants.
- First-page check: the title matches, and Bertsimas is the first of four authors (p. 1).

**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Danique de Moor (MIT Sloan), Dick den Hertog (Amsterdam Business School), Federica Donnini (University of Florence) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the newest work on the budget sets of S001 (Bertsimas–Sim 2004, cited p. 2). It builds uncertainty sets from limit laws following Bandi–Bertsimas 2012 (S065, card s09) (p. 11). Undated file; its software (Gurobi 12.0.1, Ubuntu 24.04.4; p. 13) fits the 2026 posting recorded in card a3-05.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO+IO}:
  - Classical budgets bound aggregate absolute deviations. In applications, reliable information often concerns aggregate signed quantities (p. 2):
    - forecasts and intervals for total regional demand or total population;
    - CLT bounds on sums;
    - hierarchies such as cities, provinces and regions.
  - Earlier tractability results with signed aggregates cover objective uncertainty and specific applications only: Kerkkamp 2014, Stienen et al. 2021, Goerigk–Lendl 2021, Yaman 2023 (p. 3).
  - Problem (1): MIO whose uncertainty-affected variables x are binary, with the constraint matrix A in a signed or laminar signed budget set (pp. 4–5, 8–9).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - **Worst case in closed form**: (a⁺)ᵀx + min{0, a⁺P − Σj(a⁺j xj + a⁻j(1 − xj))} (Theorem 1, p. 6). It is linearized with one binary variable, one continuous variable, two constraints and M = 2Σηj, whatever the number of coefficients (p. 7).
  - **Nominal solves**: with one uncertain constraint, the robust feasible set is the union of two nominal feasible sets, so two nominal solves suffice (Corollary 1, p. 7). With m uncertain constraints, 2^m are needed (p. 7).
  - **Laminar families**:
    - a recursive worst case, with downward corrections w ≤ 0 and upward corrections u ≥ 0 (Theorem 2, pp. 9–10);
    - 4K − 2K_L extra variables and constraints (p. 10), or 2^K nominal problems (Corollary 2, p. 11);
    - Example 1: 10,000 coefficients in 131 sets need 20,262 extra variables in the standard robust counterpart (RC), against 522 here (p. 10).
  - **Sets from data**:
    - CLT bounds µ(P) ± Cσ(P) with C ∈ {2, 3}, and stable laws for heavy tails (pp. 11–12);
    - reconciled hierarchical forecasts (MinT) give coherent aggregate intervals (p. 13), with a worked example (pp. 34–36).

    The baselines run in scikit-learn; the robust method uses JuMP in Julia with IPOPT.
  - Protocol:
    - baselines: 50/25/25 splits, tuned on validation F1 (p. 9);
    - robust method: trains on training plus validation data, and selects (k, λ, δ) by F1 "on the whole imbalanced training set" (Algorithm 1, step 11, p. 8; p. 9);
    - 5 random splits (p. 9).
  - Grids (pp. 9–10):
    - λ ∈ {0.1, 1} for weighted LR/SVM and for IHT (2 settings each);
    - GSMOTE: truncation factor (7 values) × neighbours (2) × deformation factor (6), 84 settings;
    - the robust method: k ∈ {1, IR/4, IR/2, 3IR/4} × λ ∈ {0.1, 1} × δ ∈ {0.05, 0.1}, 16 settings.
  - [my reading] Only the robust method is selected on in-sample F1. The tuning budget differs from baseline to baseline, and the threshold used for F1 is not stated.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: the convex hull of cardinality-constrained binaries, then LP duality on the inner problem (pp. 4–7). There is no further theory; the method "does not change the nature of the optimization problem complexity" (p. 11).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**:
  - **F1 gains** (p. 10): with LR, +0.046 (8.0%) over weighted LR and +0.045 (7.8%) over GSMOTE; with SVM, +0.033 (5.7%) and +0.039 (6.8%).
  - **AUC gains**: 0.6–1.0% (p. 11).
  - **Losses printed per data set**: for example, libras-move (p = 91) has F1 0.651 against GSMOTE's 0.849 under LR (Table 1, p. 11). Win–loss–tie counts are given, e.g. 14–6–0 for SVM F1 against weighted SVM (Tables 6–7, p. 15).
  - **Times**, on one Xeon core with 16 GB (p. 13): robust LR takes 2,008 s at n = 58,000, against 0.48 s for weighted LR and 391 s for GSMOTE (Table 5, p. 14).
  - No standard deviations or significance tests (pp. 10–15).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Stated results (p. 13): first on average; F1 gains significant, AUC gains slight; minutes at n ≈ 60,000.
  - No limitations or future work are stated (p. 13).
  - [my reading] The "one to two orders of magnitude" slow-down (p. 13) holds against GSMOTE. Against weighted LR, Table 5 shows three to four orders (p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (pp. 4–7, 11, 14): a combinatorial choice (which balanced subset to train on) is dualized away, so LR stays convex and SVM stays a QP. Solve times are measured up to n = 58,000.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠ (pp. 5–10, 14–15):
  - Present: duels with the incumbents on 20 data sets, with per-set losses and timings. The incumbent (weighted LR or SVM) serves as an anchor box rather than as a warm start.
  - Against it: only the robust method is selected on in-sample F1 (pp. 8–9), and its grid is larger than those of the weighted and IHT baselines (pp. 9–10). No certificate is needed, since the problems are convex.
- Research Taste mark 3 (not a Method) · evidence ✅ (p. 4): an optimal assignment of data to training and validation replaces random assignment, following stable regression. Tension T6 applies to the timing summary (pp. 13–14).
- New-pattern candidates:
  - *optimization over randomization (taste mark 3 as a method)* ✅ (p. 4);
  - *incumbent-anchored box when the robust model degenerates* (new) (pp. 5–6, 7–8);
  - *optimize the training sample, not the model* ⚠ variant: the hardest subset is selected, not generated (pp. 4–8).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace a random train/validation split by an adversarial one. Put cardinality-constrained selection indicators in an inner minimization that is linear in them; the convex hull and LP duality then give a single convex program (pp. 4–7).
- When a robust model degenerates to the zero solution, constrain it to a small box around the incumbent's solution, and tune the box width (pp. 5–6, 8).
- Next to the averages, report pairwise wins, losses and ties against each baseline, and a timing table that prints the price (Tables 5–7, pp. 14–15).
