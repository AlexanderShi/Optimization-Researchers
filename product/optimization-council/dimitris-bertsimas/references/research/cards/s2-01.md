### S291 · On the steady-state solution of the M/C2(a, b)/s queueing system (Transportation Science 1988, DOI 10.1287/trsc.22.2.125)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–15 (JSTOR cover, abstract, introduction with applications, §§1–7, Tables I–III, references). First-page check: the title and the authors Dimitris Bertsimas (MIT) and Xenophon Papaconstantinou (NTUA, Athens) match (pp. 1–2).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xenophon Papaconstantinou; D. Georgoulis (NTUA) is thanked for "constant help" (p. 14) · **Position in trajectory**: received January 1987 (p. 15). It reuses the root theorem of the authors' Ek/C2/s paper (ref. [4], EJOR, accepted 1986; pp. 7, 14). Its closing hope for a "general but tractable calculus of rational distributions for multiserver queues" (p. 14) is the programme of S168 and S663 (this batch).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; AP (shuttle transport) as motivation}: Multiserver bulk-service queues had been solved only with exponential service, which the paper reads as the field's reluctance to add analytical complexity (p. 2). It solves Poisson arrivals with Coxian-2 service under the general (a, b) rule: a batch needs at least a and at most b customers (abstract, p. 2). Two readings are offered:
  - applications (ferries, minibuses, shared taxis, medical evacuation and others), with the admission that "some key assumptions of the model may be violated" (p. 3);
  - for the journal's readers, s vehicles of capacity b that leave the terminal once at least a passengers wait (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: The C2 service channel makes the system a Markov chain on (busy servers, queue length, servers in stage 2) (p. 5). Saturated probabilities are sought in the separated form B_i w^n. The determinantal equation for w "leads to rather extreme computational problems" (p. 7), so a generating function over i turns it into s + 1 scalar equations, each with one real root in (0, 1) for ρ < 1 (Proposition 1, p. 7). The roots are distinct and ordered (Lemma 2, p. 7). A recursion (Fig. 3) reduces the remaining unknowns to a linear system of s + 1 equations (p. 8). The paper calls the combination of separation of variables and generating functions "a new approach" (p. 6).
- **D3 Model and data**: s identical servers, one FCFS line, C2 service with three parameters, ρ < 1 (pp. 2, 4–5, 7). C2 is justified two ways: it covers a wide range of squared coefficients of variation (SCV) yet stays tractable (p. 4); and two groups of destinations with exponential trip times produce it, so the usual belief that transport SCVs lie well below one "is not always justified" (p. 3). Cm with m > 2 is possible, but "the complexity is considerably increased" (p. 4). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Equivalence: with arrival stages read as customers and customers as groups, Eb/C2/s is M/C2(b, b)/s. This remark led to Proposition 1, whose proof is only outlined; the Rouché part is deferred to [4] (pp. 6–7). The roots are the same for every quorum a = 1, …, b (p. 7).
  - Closed forms for the queue-length pmf, the geometric tail P_{n+1}/P_n → w_0 (read off the root ordering), Lq, P_busy and P_delay (Propositions 3–4, pp. 8–9).
  - FCFS waiting time: split by the state the arriving customer finds, one case through the maximum of independent arrival and departure times; then next-event differential equations and Laplace transforms (Proposition 5) give a closed form and moments, and Little's formula is checked (pp. 9–12).
  - Idle-server distribution "almost trivial", batch-size distribution "considerably more intricate" (p. 12).
  - O(as³) time and O(s³) memory, with Step 3 (the recursion for g) dominating (p. 13).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: BASIC on a personal computer and FORTRAN on a CYBER 171. Table III gives per-step times for M/C2(10, 15)/s, from 79 ms at s = 1 to 28,232 ms at s = 25, with Step 3 dominant. Tables I–II show performance as the SCV varies (M/C2(4, 12)/15 at ρ = 0.9: Lq from 50.94 to 202.71) and as the quorum a varies (M/C2(a, 10)/5). The M/M(a, b)/s results agree with Sim and Templeton, and Asgharzadeh and Newell's conclusion that performance hardly depends on the trip-time law for large s "may not hold in general" (p. 13). No failures are reported.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Five contributions are listed (p. 2). Limits: C2 only (p. 4); assumptions that may fail in applications (p. 3); the cost optimization of dispatching and simple approximations are deferred to "a forthcoming paper" (p. 13). Future work: Cox's complex transition rates, hoped to be "the Ariadne's thread" to a calculus of rational distributions (p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 4, 7–8, 13): the class kept is an analysable distribution class (C2), not an LP or MIO. The separated root equations replace the determinant and keep the algorithm polynomial and in real arithmetic. The boundary is printed (Cm costlier) with measured per-step times. A co-authored SD paper with stated motives.
- Method 2 · variant ⚠ (pp. 2, 6, 13): the field's avoidance of non-exponential multiserver bulk queues is named and the problem reopened, with run times in support. But the reason given is a new analytic device, not a tool change, and the old belief is not conceded.
- Method 3 · variant ⚠ (p. 13): the operator's knob, the quorum a, is tabulated against queue length, utilization and batch size (Table II), but no cost or price curve is drawn; the optimization is deferred.
- New-pattern candidate: *dense rational-transform class as the tractability device* (pp. 3–4, 14), recurring from S239 and S346 (card s01).
- New-pattern candidate: *report where the algorithm spends its time, by step* (Table III, p. 13); it recurs in S168, S363 and S663 (this batch).
- New-pattern candidate: *subsume the incumbent as a special case* (pp. 6, 13): Eb/C2/s is identified with M/C2(b, b)/s, and Sim and Templeton's M/M(a, b)/s results are reproduced.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace a high-degree determinantal root condition by a generating function over the phase index; requiring it to be a polynomial forces an integer exponent and splits the condition into one scalar equation per root (p. 7).
- Identify a new bulk model with a solved one by reading Erlang arrival stages as customers and customers as groups, then reuse the solved model's root theorem (pp. 6–7).
- Use a proven ordering of the roots twice: to speed up root finding and to read the geometric tail rate off the largest root (pp. 7, 9).
- Report execution time per algorithm step across problem sizes, so that the bottleneck is visible (Table III, p. 13).

**Quotes**:
- "The fact that for multiserver BSQSs no attempts were ever made to tackle cases beyond the exponential service-times assumption (which is not representative of most situations encountered in practice), testifies to the researchers' reluctance to overshoot an already high level of analytical complexity." (p. 2)
- "We believe that the combination of separation of variables and the use of generating functions is a new approach, which seems to give very promising results." (p. 6)

### S833 · The Probabilistic Minimum Spanning Tree, Part II: Probabilistic Analysis and Asymptotic Results (MIT Operations Research Center working paper OR 184-88, 1988, no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–30 (cover, title page, abstract, §§1–6, references). Displayed formulas are badly garbled, so theorem statements are taken from the prose where the formulas are illegible. First-page check: the title and the sole author match; the title page reads "DiFitris Bertsimas" (an OCR error), and p. 5 gives Dimitris Bertsimas, Sloan School (pp. 3, 5).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (sole author); he thanks his thesis adviser Amedeo Odoni and Patrick Jaillet; NSF grant ECS-8717970 (p. 27) · **Position in trajectory**: August 1988 (p. 3), the sequel to Part I (ref. [3], "submitted for publication", p. 28) within his thesis programme (ref. [2]). The Networks 1990 paper S148 (card s01) covers the content of both parts [S148 p. 1]; its §6 carries these asymptotics in narrowed form (D8).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; IO}: Part I proved the problem NP-complete (p. 8). The a priori tree has two advantages: it is "robust" (it does not change the network's structure) and real-time (O(n) updates) (p. 8). The paper asks: "How does the idea of having an a priori solution to a combinatorial optimization problem compare with re-optimization strategies in terms of performance?" (p. 9). The answer is probabilistic, in the random Euclidean model (uniform points in the unit square) and the random length model (uniform arc costs), against re-optimizing the MST or the Steiner tree on each instance (pp. 7–9).
- **D2 Core idea**: Compare expectations, not instances (p. 27).
  - Re-optimization: condition on the number W of present points and concentrate it around np with a Chernoff bound, so that the deterministic limit theorems apply to the bulk (Theorem 1, pp. 13–15).
  - The PMST: show that the optimal a priori tree's expected length is a subadditive Euclidean functional, apply Steele's theory, and identify the constant (Theorem 2, pp. 15–22).
  - Random length model: Frieze's ζ(3) limit for the MST is carried to re-optimization (Theorem 4) and bounds the PMST (Proposition 4) (pp. 23–25).
- **D3 Model and data**: Independent presence with one common probability p, assumed because the analysis is asymptotic in n (p. 9); a present-node instance prunes the a priori tree provided it stays connected (p. 7); the cases np → ∞ and np → 0 (pp. 15, 23). Re-optimization costs quadratic time for the MST and exponential time for the Steiner tree (p. 26). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Chernoff concentration of W, then deterministic limits on the bulk (pp. 13–14, 20–21, 24–25).
  - Subadditivity by construction: an optimal PMST in each subsquare, one leaf per subsquare made an always-present "black" representative, and an MST on the representatives (p. 17). An induction bounds the extra expected length of making a leaf black by the longest edge times (1 − p)/p (pp. 18–19), which closes the subadditivity inequality (p. 20). Monotonicity is asserted as clear (p. 16).
  - The constant: the expected length depends on the present set only through its size, which gives β(p) = A√p; bounds valid for every p collapse as p → 1, so A equals the MST constant (pp. 20–22).
  - The PMST is asymptotically equal to MST re-optimization, and within 15% of Steiner re-optimization only "If we adopt the Gilbert and Pollak [8] conjecture" (p. 22).
  - Random length model: pζ(3) ≤ E[L_Tp] ≤ ζ(3) (Proposition 4, p. 25), with E[L_Tp] → pζ(3) left as Conjecture 5 (p. 26).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: none (no computation).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Claims: asymptotic equality with MST re-optimization and a constant factor to Steiner in the Euclidean model. For the random length model the abstract and introduction claim the PMST is asymptotically smaller than MST re-optimization (pp. 6, 10); the conclusion says "at least as good (we conjecture strictly better)" (p. 27), which is what Proposition 4 and Conjecture 5 support (pp. 25–26).
  - Limits: the Steiner strategy is not analysed in the random length model for lack of sharp theorems; the PMST is suboptimal on individual instances (p. 26).
  - Version note: the 1990 journal version narrows the claims. Its functional "is not monotone (the MST is not also monotone)", so only an "approximate monotonicity" is proved [S148 p. 25]. It asserts only some constant c(p, d) [S148 p. 24] and sums up "within constant factors from both strategies" [S148 pp. 1, 30]. Its random length results hold "in probability" [S148 pp. 28–29], where the working paper says "with probability 1" (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 · variant ⚠ (pp. 8, 22, 27): the price of a robust, real-time strategy is computed against re-solving: asymptotically zero against MST re-optimization, and about 15% against Steiner under a conjecture. There is no knob; p is data, as in S148 (card s01).
- Tension T6 (headline vs caveat) · evidence (pp. 6, 10, 25–27): the abstract and introduction state a strict ordering in the random length model that the body proves only as an inequality and leaves as Conjecture 5.
- New-pattern candidate: *a priori solution with a cheap update rule, priced against re-solving* (pp. 8–9, 26–27), recurring from S038 and S017 (card c01) and S148 (card s01).
- New-pattern candidate: *carry deterministic devices into the stochastic version and measure what survives* (pp. 15–20): Steele's theory is carried over; subadditivity has to be re-proved ("It is not clear that the subadditivity property holds for the PMST problem", p. 16), and the monotonicity asserted here fails [S148 p. 25].
- New-pattern candidate: *claim narrowed from working paper to journal* (pp. 6, 9, 15–23 against S148 pp. 1, 24–25, 28–30).
- New-pattern candidate: *limit case of the new parameter pins an unknown constant* (pp. 20–22), close to s01's *limit cases of the new parameter locate the hardness* (S148).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For expectations over random sub-instances, condition on the number of present points, discard counts outside np(1 ± ε) with a Chernoff bound, and apply the deterministic limit theorem to the rest (pp. 13–14, 24–25).
- Prove subadditivity of an a priori structure by gluing optimal pieces through always-present representatives and bounding the extra expected length the representatives cause (pp. 16–20).
- Identify an unknown limit constant by its scaling law plus bounds that hold for every parameter value, then send the parameter to the value where the bounds meet (pp. 20–22).
- Name the conjecture inside any sentence whose number rests on it (p. 22).

**Quotes**:
- "How does the idea of having an a priori solution to a combinatorial optimization problem compare with re-optimization strategies in terms of performance?" (p. 9)
- "It is quite surprising to find that the PMST strategy is asymptotically equivalent in terms of performance to the MST re-optimization strategy in the random Euclidean model and at least as good (we conjecture strictly better) in the random length model." (p. 27)

### S186 · Traveling salesman facility location problems (Transportation Science 1989, DOI 10.1287/trsc.23.3.184)
Read level: **full, WP version**: Sloan working paper 2068-88 (September 1988), WP pp. 1–24 (abstract, introduction, §§1–5, references); cited as [S186 WP p. N]. The journal print (pp. 184–191) was not available; only its abstract (abstracts.json) was compared. First-page check: the sole author Dimitris Bertsimas matches; the WP title is the journal title with "Two" in front, and the two abstracts match in substance (WP pp. 1–2; abstracts.json).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (sole author); he thanks his adviser Amedeo Odoni; NSF ECS-8717970 (WP p. 22) · **Position in trajectory**: carries the a priori approach of his thesis (ref. [6]) to location (WP p. 4). Its O(1) conjecture rests on Platzman and Bartholdi's conjecture, which Bertsimas and Grigni refuted in S161 (Oper. Res. Lett. 1989; card s01).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {IO + SD; AP (locating a service unit) as motivation}: Two problems (WP p. 3):
  - TSFLP: locate a service unit that each day tours the nodes that called, with an optimal tour;
  - PTSFLP: locate the unit and an a priori tour that skips the nodes that did not call.

  The entry points are Berman and Simchi-Levi's TSFLP heuristic, with relative worst-case error 1/2, and their O(n³) algorithm for a given a priori tour (WP pp. 4, 7, 16). The turning argument is the planner's: the heuristic proposes a good location but no estimate of its cost (WP p. 11). Computing the TSFLP objective g(i) needs 2ⁿ terms, each an NP-hard TSP (WP p. 21).
- **D2 Core idea**:
  - An exchange argument puts the facility at a node that always needs a visit (Theorem 1, WP pp. 6–7).
  - A sharper analysis of the incumbent heuristic (Theorem 4, WP pp. 10–11).
  - The PTSFLP reduces to n PTSPs by setting the candidate node's probability to 1 (WP pp. 12–13).
  - A spacefilling-curve location heuristic: sorting preserves order, exactly as the skipping rule does, so the curve tour of each instance is the a priori tour with absent nodes skipped. Platzman and Bartholdi's O(log n) TSP guarantee therefore holds on every instance (Theorem 5, WP pp. 14–15), and the heuristic's expected cost is known in closed form (WP p. 16).
- **D3 Model and data**: Independent presence probabilities p_i (WP p. 6). The triangle inequality is assumed for Theorems 1 and 4, and Theorem 1 is said to fail without it (WP p. 7). The facility sits at a node, optimal by Berman and Simchi-Levi (WP pp. 6, 12). Points are Euclidean for the heuristic; the asymptotics take equal p and uniform points in the unit square (WP p. 17). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Theorem 1: g(i) − g(j) = (1 − p_j) Σ p(S)[L(S ∪ i) − L(S ∪ i ∪ j)] ≤ 0 under the triangle inequality (WP p. 7).
  - Proposition 3 bounds the heuristic's surrogate L(S) + 2d(i, S) against the true objective on both sides, using a Hamiltonian-path argument (WP pp. 9–10). This gives the worst-case bound (1 − p_{i*})/2 of Theorem 4: "25% instead of 50%" at p = 1/2 (WP pp. 10–11).
  - For a given tour, consecutive candidate locations differ in two probability entries, so each expected cost follows from the previous one in O(n): O(n²) in total against Berman and Simchi-Levi's O(n³) (WP p. 16).
  - Conjecture 6: an O(1) guarantee, if Platzman and Bartholdi's conjecture is correct (WP pp. 16–17).
  - Asymptotics: where the facility is located does not matter (WP pp. 17–18); the incumbent heuristic is asymptotically optimal (Theorem 7, WP pp. 18–19); PTSFLP ~ TSFLP, proved "using exactly the same type of proof as in [6]" (Theorem 8, WP p. 19); the curve heuristic is about 25% above optimal, from an empirical β₁ = 0.956 and "the conjectured value" β_TSP = 0.765 (Theorem 9, WP p. 20).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: none in this paper. The conclusion cites thesis experiments in which PTSP heuristics came within 2–5% of the predicted expected cost on random problems (WP pp. 21–22).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contributions as listed in the abstract (WP p. 2). Conclusion: a priori strategies are "a serious and practical alternative" to re-optimization (WP p. 22).
  - Limits: only an O(log n) guarantee; the 25% rests on an empirical and a conjectured constant (WP p. 20); the PTSFLP still needs n PTSPs (WP p. 21).
  - Version note: the journal abstract drops the O(1) conjecture, adds a second heuristic for networks with the same O(log n) bound, and weakens "asymptotically equivalent" to "asymptotically very close" (abstracts.json; WP p. 2).
  - [inferred] With every p_i = 1 the heuristic's ratio is the curve heuristic's TSP ratio, so the line instances of S161 [S161 TR pp. 1, 5] refute Conjecture 6 as stated.

**Method links**:
- Method 5 · variant ⚠ (WP pp. 11, 15–16, 21): the planner's needs, knowing what the decision costs and getting each day's tour in real time, are the stated reason to replace a heuristic that has a good guarantee by a different problem. But the planner is constructed: there is no owner, data or current rule.
- Method 3 · variant ⚠ (WP pp. 19–20): the price of the a priori, real-time strategy is measured asymptotically (zero for the optimal PTSFLP, about 25% for the O(n²) heuristic), not set with a knob.
- New-pattern candidate: *a priori solution with a cheap update rule, priced against re-solving* (WP pp. 3, 11–12, 19–22).
- New-pattern candidate: *asymptotic optimality as the warrant for a simple heuristic* (Theorem 7, WP pp. 17–19).
- New-pattern candidate: *claim narrowed from working paper to journal* (WP pp. 2, 16–17; abstracts.json). Conjecture 6 is the claim that the tightness audit in S161 (card s01) undercut.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Prove that a facility belongs at a node that always needs a visit by a pairwise exchange of expected tour lengths under the triangle inequality (WP pp. 6–7).
- Reduce the joint choice of a location and an a priori tour to n a priori problems by setting each candidate's presence probability to 1 (WP pp. 12–13).
- Prefer heuristics whose output is unchanged by the instance's skipping rule (a sort along a curve), so that a worst-case guarantee holds instance by instance (WP pp. 14–15).
- When the candidates' closed-form expected costs differ in a few probability entries, update incrementally instead of recomputing: O(n³) → O(n²) (WP p. 16).
- Ask what the planner can do with a heuristic's output; if it gives no cost estimate, say so and reformulate (WP p. 11).

**Quotes**:
- "So the planner would be faced with the rather unusual situation of knowing a good location for the facility, but without knowing the cost of this decision." (WP p. 11)
- "because sorting has the property of preserving the order, which is exactly the property of the PTSP strategy as well" (WP p. 15)

### S261 · The dynamic traveling repairman problem (MIT Sloan School working paper 3036-89-MS, 1989, no DOI)
Read level: **full** (all surviving pages): pp. 1–33 (abstract dated July 10, 1989, introduction, §§1–5, Figures 6–8, references). The MIT Libraries notice says pages are missing from the original (p. 1); the running page numbers place the gap at running page 26, between file pp. 27 and 28, so the batch-means protocol of §4.4 and the start of the §4.5 example are not read. First-page check: the title and the authors Dimitris Bertsimas (Sloan) and Garrett van Ryzin (ORC) match (pp. 2–3).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Garrett van Ryzin (his PhD student per card S016, c01) · **Position in trajectory**: the working-paper version of S016 (Operations Research 1991; card c01, core). The problem came from Harilaos Psaraftis, who brought dynamic vehicle routing to Bertsimas's attention (p. 31). NSF ECS-8717970 (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; AP as motivation}: In practice the TSP is "a deterministic, static approximation" of problems that are probabilistic and dynamic, and minimizing travel ignores the cost of waiting (p. 4). The applications are chosen for the waiting objective: inventory replenishment, emergency service, mobile repair, landing sequencing, taxis and sales leads (pp. 4–5). The model has five characteristics (p. 5). Entry points (pp. 5–7):
  - Psaraftis's dynamic TSP;
  - the traveling repairman problem, the static analogue, which is NP-complete;
  - a priori optimization, real-time but "inherently static";
  - the stochastic queue median problem, a special case.
- **D2 Core idea**: Lower bounds for all policies come first; simple policies are then analysed against them (p. 7).
  - Light traffic: the SQM policy (wait at the median, serve FCFS, return) is asymptotically optimal (Theorem 1, p. 17).
  - Heavy traffic: a policy stable for all ρ < 1 must cut the travel per service as ρ grows (p. 17). Partitioning into cyclic queues (PART1, PART2), TSP tours over batches of N demands, and nearest neighbour do so (pp. 18–28).
  - System time grows like (1 − ρ)⁻² rather than (1 − ρ)⁻¹, because of geometry (p. 14).
- **D3 Model and data**: A square of area A, a restriction that "can be relaxed" (p. 11). Poisson arrivals, uniform i.i.d. locations, i.i.d. on-site service with known first two moments, unit speed, and routing decisions only at service completions (p. 11). Travel times are not i.i.d., so the M/G/1 method does not apply directly (p. 11). Two assumptions are not proved:
  - the heavy-traffic bound assumes that the nearest-neighbour bound for uniform points holds for every stable policy, conjectured but unproved (p. 14);
  - the nearest-neighbour analysis assumes a constant γ for which there is "no analytical guidance" (p. 27).

  No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Light-traffic bound: split the system time, give the server its best a priori position (the median), and apply Little's law (pp. 12–13).
  - Heavy-traffic bound: the stability condition, the assumed nearest-neighbour bound and Jensen's inequality give T* ≥ λA/(4(1 − ρ)²), as far as the OCR shows (p. 14).
  - FCFS behaves as M/G/1, the Pollaczek–Khinchin formula holding without mutual independence; its light-traffic ratio is at most c1/c3 ≈ 1.36 (pp. 15–16).
  - PART1 is an exhaustive symmetric cyclic queue with a projection rule that makes starting points uniform. It proves that a stable policy exists for every ρ < 1 (Theorem 2, p. 19); the best m approaches twice its critical value, and the ratio is about 4 (p. 20).
  - PART2 is bracketed between two cyclic queues in which the customers are "slid" toward or away from the server (pp. 21–22); it is about 4% better than PART1 (p. 24).
  - TSP policy: batches form a GI/G/1 queue with Erlang-N interarrivals; Kingman's bound plus the BHH tour asymptotics, with N reparametrized against its critical value, give a ratio of about 2.1 with Johnson's estimate β_TSP ≈ 0.72 (pp. 24–26).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: The nearest-neighbour policy is simulated by batch means (p. 27; the protocol page is missing), and Figure 6 plots simulated T_NN against the bound on log–log axes (p. 28). One numerical example compares all policies over ρ (Figures 7–8, pp. 28–30):
  - SQM is best below ρ = 0.05;
  - FCFS and NN are comparable up to ρ = 0.1, and NN dominates above it;
  - TSP is second where its bound is valid (ρ > 0.5);

### S168 · An analytic approach to a general class of G/G/s queueing systems (Operations Research 1990, DOI 10.1287/opre.38.1.139)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–17 (abstract, critical survey of methods, §§1–4, Tables I–III, Figure 5 caption, acknowledgment, references). First-page check: the title and the sole author Dimitris Bertsimas (MIT) match (p. 1).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: "a part of" his master's thesis at MIT under Amedeo Odoni (p. 17); received April 1987, revised December 1987 and July 1988, accepted September 1988 (p. 1). It is the general Ck/Cm/s theory behind three works: the FCFS waiting-time paper it announces as forthcoming (p. 2), which appeared first as S239 (reference list, p. 17; card s01); S389 (this batch, its ref. [2]); and the Ek/C2/s paper with Papaconstantinou (p. 11). S239's card guesses that its source report OR 156-87 is this paper; S168 prints no report number, so that stays unconfirmed.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD}: GI/G/s "has survived the attacks of many excellent mathematicians and operations researchers" (p. 1). A critical survey of four methods follows (pp. 1–2):
  - embedded Markov chains (matrix-analytic) run into dimensionality;
  - supplementary variables gave limited results;
  - complex-variable methods are of high complexity;
  - Erlang's method of stages "has been neglected for some time", out of a concern about equations that are "certainly not more formidable" than the others'.

  The problem: closed forms and an exact algorithm for the Coxian queue Ck/Cm/s (abstract, p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: A Markov chain by generalized stages (p. 3). Then separation of variables, a PDE technique "less well known" for partial difference equations (p. 5), and an m-variable generating function that satisfies a first-order linear PDE, solved by characteristics (pp. 6–8). The generating function must be a nonzero polynomial; this condition gives one scalar equation per occupancy vector, so each root w "satisfies a different equation" (p. 8). This is the separability property: saturated system-size probabilities are a linear combination of geometric terms whose equations involve only the two Laplace transforms (pp. 10, 13).
- **D3 Model and data**: Coxian arrivals (k stages) and service (m stages), chosen for versatility: the class contains the exponential, hyperexponential and Erlang laws and is dense in all distributions on (0, ∞) (p. 3); ρ < 1. Distinct roots are assumed: the condition fails only on a set of measure zero, no counterexample was found, it is conjectured always to hold and is proved for m = 2 (p. 10). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Theorem 2 (separability): no roots with Re x < 0; existence in the right half-plane by Schauder's fixed point theorem; uniqueness for the extreme occupancy vectors by the argument principle (pp. 8–9).
  - General uniqueness is not proved with "the present theory exclusively"; it follows by combining Theorem 2 with Pollaczek's and de Smit's root counts (p. 10).
  - Special cases: Ck/Cm/1; Ck/Em/1, whose complex roots make the computation costlier; Ek/Em/s by Rouché; Ck/M/s, which recovers the G/M/s equation; and Ek/C2/s, whose roots are all real (pp. 10–11).
  - Explicit root equations exist only for m ≤ 4 (Abel–Ruffini); the number of roots grows exponentially when s and m grow together (p. 12).
  - Two strategies for the unsaturated probabilities (pp. 12–13); prearrival and postdeparture laws (Proposition 4) and performance measures (p. 14).
  - Conjecture: separability holds for GI/R/s but not outside the rational class, since the structure of class R "and its probabilistic interpretation" separate the equations (p. 13).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: C2/C2/s in FORTRAN on a SUN 3 and Ek/C2/s on a CYBER 171. Tests reached V² = 100, k = 50 and s = 40, with internal accuracy checks and agreement with Groenevelt et al. and de Smit. Mean-wait tables are given at ρ = 0.9; results converge quickly in k to D/C2/s; Figure 5 plots CPU times against k and s, with no values in the text (pp. 14–16). Table I gives time and memory per step: step 3 is heaviest, and the algorithm is polynomial in s for fixed m, exponential when s and m vary together, and always polynomial in k (p. 15).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contributions: closed forms, the separability property and an exact algorithm (abstract, p. 1).
  - Limits: root uniqueness is borrowed and distinctness conjectured (p. 10); exponential growth (pp. 12, 15).
  - Practical advice: "the value of m = 2 is a tradeoff between accuracy and simplicity" (p. 12).
  - Future work: FCFS waiting times (p. 2); Cox's complex transition rates; order reduction of transfer functions, taken from electrical engineering, to approximate high-order distributions by low-order ones; the GI/R/s conjecture (pp. 12–13).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 3, 8, 12–15): the class kept is the Coxian (rational-transform) class, dense yet finite to analyse, not an LP or MIO. The boundary is printed: explicit only for m ≤ 4, exponential in s and m. The stop rule's logic is present: measure, then change the device (m = 2, order reduction) rather than push the general case.
- Method 2 · variant ⚠ (pp. 1–2, 14): a neglected method is reopened and the field's avoidance contested, backed by programmed and tested algorithms. The old view is called overstated rather than conceded, and there is no tool-change figure. This is a sole-author SD paper with stated motives (p. 14), against the Domain Map's "co-authored papers only" for SD.
- New-pattern candidate: *dense rational-transform class as the tractability device* (pp. 3, 13).
- New-pattern candidate: *revive a neglected classical method from a neighbouring field* (pp. 2, 5): Erlang's stages plus separation of variables from PDE theory.
- New-pattern candidate: *subsume the incumbent as a special case* (pp. 2, 10–11): GI/G/1 theory is generalized to many servers, and the G/M/s equation recovered.
- New-pattern candidate: *report where the algorithm spends its time, by step* (Table I, p. 15; Figure 5, p. 16).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For an infinite system of partial difference equations, assume a separated product form and require a generating function to be a polynomial; the polynomiality conditions give one scalar equation per root (pp. 5–8).
- Prove existence of roots by a fixed-point theorem on a compact region, and uniqueness in extreme cases by the argument principle or Rouché (pp. 8–11).
- When your own theory leaves a gap, close it with a known count from a rival method and state which part is borrowed (p. 10).
- Test an exact algorithm at extreme parameters (V² up to 100, k up to 50, s up to 40) against published tables from rival methods, with internal accuracy checks (p. 15).

**Quotes**:
- "Being fully aware of the need for the probabilist to be closely involved with the algorithmic analysis of a problem, we have undertaken the task of programming and testing our algorithms." (p. 14)
- "In the opinion of the author, in a practical situation the value of m = 2 is a tradeoff between accuracy and simplicity, which has the additional advantage of the rather unexpected real arithmetic." (p. 12)

### S363 · The departure process from a GI/G/1 queue and its applications to the analysis of tandem queues (MIT Operations Research Center working paper OR 245-91, 1990, no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–30 (cover, abstract, §§1–7, Figure 1, three examples, references). First-page check: the authors Dimitris Bertsimas and Daisuke Nakazato match, and the title is the full form of the truncated index title (pp. 1, 3).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Daisuke Nakazato (ORC, p. 3) · **Position in trajectory**: September 1990, funded by the MIT Leaders for Manufacturing program and Draper Laboratory (p. 3). It uses the pair's distributional Little's law (ref. [1], p. 20) and the rational-transform line of S168.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD; AP (manufacturing, communications) as motivation}: In networks the departures of one queue are the arrivals of the next. Beyond exponential assumptions departure processes are not renewal and exact results are few (Burke, Daley), and FCFS networks beyond Jackson's have no exact results, which turned the field to approximations (Whitt's QNA, Harrison–Dai) (p. 4). The problem: characterize GI/G/1 departures exactly, then use them for tandem queues (p. 5).
- **D2 Core idea**: The interdeparture time is the idle time plus the next service time, and the two are independent (pp. 5–6). Hilbert factorization of the Lindley process gives its transform. The general Cauchy-integral solution is set aside as numerically unstable; R/G/1 and GI/R/1 give finite forms that are "computationally very tractable" (Theorems 1–2, pp. 7–9). Correlations come from Cauchy integrals (adjacent pairs) and Keilson's Green's function method (any lag, R/R/1) (pp. 10–15); the counting process follows (pp. 20–23). Tandem queues are solved one after another under Assumption A, which keeps each departure transform rational (pp. 25–27).
- **D3 Model and data**: Single-server FCFS queues, restricted to R/R/1 "for tractability purposes" (p. 25). Assumption A (a queue's wait independent of its input interval) is exact for series of M/M/1 queues and for renewal departures; otherwise it is a renewal approximation that keeps the whole interdeparture law rather than two moments (p. 25). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Liouville's theorem on the two analytic half-planes of the factorization; roots located by Rouché (pp. 7–8).
  - Symbolic Mathematica checks on M/M/1: zero covariance and the counting-process formulas (pp. 12, 16, 22).
  - Lagrange inversion for M/G/1 and GI/M/1. M/D/1 correlations vanish in light traffic but, "Surprisingly", not in heavy traffic; D/M/1 is the reverse, explained by closeness to a Poisson process (pp. 16–20).
  - Tandem algorithm: root finding is the bottleneck, and the transform order grows along the line, d_i = d_{i−1} + M_i (p. 27).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: Mathematica, chosen for its root finding and symbolic algebra, with Hosono's Laplace inversion; inputs are transforms or first moments with an SCV (p. 27). Departure correlations were "very low, in the neighborhood of 0.01" (pp. 27–28). Two two-queue examples against Whitt's simulations: E[W1] 1.35 vs 1.360 and E[W2] 8.70 vs 8.131 in the first, 0.0964 vs 0.096 and 0.0265 vs 0.026 in the second (pp. 28–29). Answers were close to QNA (p. 28). A four-queue example is published without simulation (p. 29). No run times.
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contributions: an exact characterization of the departure process and a tandem algorithm (abstract, p. 3).
  - Limits: "We were unable to solve the problem exactly" (p. 25), hence Assumption A; inverting the counting-process transforms is "a nontrivial matter" (p. 22); the number of roots grows along the line (p. 27).
  - The 7% gap on E[W2] in the first example is called a slight difference and explained by its higher correlations (p. 29). No future work is stated.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 7, 25–27): a class-keeping device in queueing form. Assumption A makes each departure transform rational, so the recursion stays in class R; the general closed form is rejected as numerically useless, and the growth in the number of roots is printed.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠ (pp. 25, 27–29): the incumbent (Whitt's two-moment QNA) and published simulations are the opponents, and the computed correlations serve as an accuracy indicator. There is no certificate, and a 7% gap is called slight.
- New-pattern candidate: *a posteriori computational justification of a simplifying assumption* (pp. 27–29): correlations near 0.01 are read as almost-renewal departures, so Assumption A is accurate.
- New-pattern candidate: *dense rational-transform class as the tractability device* (pp. 7, 25–26).
- New-pattern candidate: *re-derive classical theorems with the new device* (pp. 12, 16, 22): the M/M/1 checks, including Burke's zero correlation.
- New-pattern candidate: *report where the algorithm spends its time, by step* (p. 27).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Write the interdeparture time as the idle time plus the next service time, which are independent, so that its transform follows from the waiting-time factorization (pp. 5–6).
- When a general closed form is numerically unstable, restrict one input distribution to rational transforms to obtain finite root sums (p. 7).
- In a decomposition approximation, compute the dependence you neglect (here the departure autocorrelations) and report it beside each result as an accuracy indicator (pp. 27–29).
- Publish a fully specified example without simulation, so that others can benchmark against it (p. 29).

**Quotes**:
- "Although this a result in closed form, we do not believe that it is numerically useful, since the inversion of such a transform is numerically unstable due to the multiple branch points in the integrand." (p. 7)
- "As it is quite difficult to find reliable simulation results in the literature we present a random example so that researchers can compare these results with simulation results and with their methods." (p. 29)

### S389 · Relations between the prearrival and postdeparture state probabilities and the FCFS waiting time distribution in the Ek/G/s queue (Naval Research Logistics 1990, DOI 10.1002/1520-6750(199002)37:1<135::aid-nav3220370109>3.0.co;2-f)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–15 (abstract, §§1–4, captions of Figures 1–5, references).   - Theorem 3 extends Haji and Newell's M/G/s relation (p. 8); Theorem 4 uses "Poisson arrivals see time averages" for the stage process (pp. 8–9).

Credit: an associate editor corrected the history of Burke's theorem, and referees pointed out Hébuterne's independent results, which include the same Theorem 1 (pp. 3, 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 2, 11–14): the Coxian class keeps the waiting time a finite mixture of exponentials (a class kept, not an LP or MIO). The boundary is reported as measured: seconds at s = 100 and k = 20, memory beyond. This is an early instance of Heuristic 4's printed memory limits.

### S663 · On the characteristic equation of general queueing systems (MIT Operations Research Center working paper OR 246-91, 1990, no DOI)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–17 (cover, abstract, §§1–5, references). First-page check: the title and the authors Dimitris Bertsimas and Daisuke Nakazato match (pp. 1, 3).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Daisuke Nakazato (ORC, p. 3) · **Position in trajectory**: September 1990, a companion of S363 with the same report series, funding and coauthor (p. 3). It extends the method of stages of S168 (ref. [1]) beyond rational transforms.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD}: "numerous papers" analyse specific queues through the roots of a nonlinear equation (abstract, p. 3). Existing exact analyses (Ramaswami–Lucantoni, de Smit, his own R/R/m) assume homogeneous servers and a single renewal arrival stream (p. 4). The aim is the structure of the solution for heterogeneous servers, several arrival streams, bulk queues and feedback, and a unification of the field (pp. 3–4).
- **D2 Core idea**: Extend the method of stages to arbitrary distributions with supplementary variables (elapsed times) and hazard rates (pp. 4–5). The Kolmogorov equations are written with operators and solved by separation of variables; the characteristic equation is read off the eigenfunctions of each operator (Lemma 1, pp. 5–7). In the physical reading, each independent arrival or service process contributes one operator, and the characteristic equation says that the spectrum of their sum is zero (pp. 12–14).
- **D3 Model and data**: Systems that start empty, for the transient analysis; FCFS for waiting times (pp. 5, 9). GI/G/1 first, then a queue with L renewal streams and m heterogeneous servers (p. 9), then bulk and feedback queues (pp. 14–15). The separated product form is assumed to be the general solution (p. 6); finitely many terms require rational service transforms (pp. 8, 11). No data.
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**:
  - Lemma 1: eigenfunctions of the arrival and service operators, with characteristic equation zα(θ) = 1 as far as legible (pp. 6–7).
  - Theorems 1–2: the queue-length transform is a combination of geometric terms in the roots (pp. 8, 11). There are exactly k roots by Rouché when service is rational of degree k, and a continuum of roots indexed by u for general service (p. 8), asserted without a completeness argument (my reading).
  - Waiting times by their distributional Little's law: mixtures of exponentials, hence exponential tails (pp. 9, 11). Theorem 2 agrees with his R/R/m results and generalizes Takahashi's Ph/Ph/c asymptotics (p. 11).
  - Keilson's compensation method and the operator rule give the characteristic equations of bulk and feedback queues (pp. 13–15).
- **D5 Computational and empirical evaluation**: none. One remark from experience: root finding is "computationally quite easy", and the large linear system takes most of the time (p. 12).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**: Contributions: one characteristic equation for a general class of queues, geometric and exponential tails, and a rule for writing the equation of new systems (pp. 3, 16). Stated limits: the results are "structural" for arbitrary distributions and only "potentially" algorithmic for class R (pp. 3, 16); the linear system "becomes very large very quickly" (p. 12). Future work: a finite algorithm for the rational class (p. 16).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · variant ⚠ (pp. 3–4, 11–12, 16): one formulation (the characteristic equation) covers a family that classical methods treat case by case. The boundary is printed (finite only in class R; the linear system is the bottleneck), but nothing is implemented or measured here.
- New-pattern candidate: *unify-then-extend* (pp. 3–4, 11–15): GI/G/1 and the multistream heterogeneous queue under one equation, then bulk and feedback queues by the operator rule.
- New-pattern candidate: *subsume the incumbent as a special case* (p. 11): his R/R/m results and Takahashi's Ph/Ph/c asymptotics.
- New-pattern candidate: *import a solution device from physics or engineering* (pp. 12–14): a generator-spectrum method, "one of the most commonly used tools in modern physics"; compare S168's transfer-function order reduction from electrical engineering (S168 p. 12).
- New-pattern candidate: *report where the algorithm spends its time, by step* (p. 12).

**Transferable techniques**:
