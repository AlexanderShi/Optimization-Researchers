- **D1 How the reviewer frames the book** {DA AP; teaching} (p. 1):
  - The book is a "unique text book primarily used by first year MBA students", recommended "for anyone interested in decision making".
  - The problem it answers, in the reviewer's telling: MBA programmes teach statistics and operations research "as subjects offered in two different courses (and often from two different departments)", and textbooks serve the two "as different dishes to be enjoyed separately!". This book is unique because it "combines the concepts of business statistics and operations research or management science in one book".
  - The book's primary focus is decision making (Quote 1).
- **D2 The reviewer's reading of the design** (p. 1):
  - Five "unique" features:
    1. decision making as the focus: frameworks, not standard topics (Quote 1);
    2. one book for statistics and OR/MS;
    3. selection: the authors "have started with decision making as the primary point and selected the concepts, techniques and tools that assist in decision making". The reviewer calls the result "an optimal mix" for "a tight MBA semester";
    4. for beginners: it "assumes very little background knowledge or experience and starts right from the basics";
    5. order: "Most textbooks have decision trees towards the end or middle of the text book.", whereas this one "begins with the building of decision trees in line with their focus on decision making".
  - Topic list, introduced as "The topics covered in the second edition": ten items, from "Decision analysis (introduction to decision trees)" through probability, sampling, simulation and regression to linear, nonlinear and discrete optimization, and last "Integration in the art of decision modeling".

The second edition's page puts simulation in Chapter 5, optimization in Chapters 7–9 and the capstone in Chapter 10 (B014 p. 1), so the chapter order probably did not change between editions [inferred].

**Transferable techniques**:
- When one course or text unifies two subjects around decisions, name in the preface the standard topics it drops and where to find them. This reviewer had to infer the omissions and asked whether depth suffered (p. 1). The authors' page states the unification and its concision but names no omission (B014 p. 1).
- Order a methods course by the decision: open with a decision model (here a decision tree) and let it motivate the tools that follow. The reviewer singles out this order as unusual (p. 1), and the first edition's contents follow it (B015 p. 1, title level).
