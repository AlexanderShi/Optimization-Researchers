### S264 · Bounds on linear PDEs via semidefinite optimization (Mathematical Programming 2006, DOI 10.1007/s10107-006-0702-z)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–24 (abstract, §1–§4, all five examples with Tables 1–10, references). Integral signs and many displayed formulas are lost in the extraction.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Constantine Caramanis (MIT LIDS, on an NSF predoctoral fellowship, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the moment–SDP programme (SKILL SW2) carried from probability bounds to PDEs; received September 2001, accepted December 2005 (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {CP; SD in Example 4; AP as motivation}: Applications want a functional of a PDE solution (an average boundary temperature, average inventory, expected queue lengths), not the solution itself (p. 1). Discretization costs grow like (1/ε)^d and yield approximations, which cannot guarantee bounds on the functional (pp. 1–2). Entry: guaranteed upper and lower bounds at lower computational cost (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Treat the unknown solution like a measure. The variables are its moments and boundary moments, and each monomial test function in the adjoint (weak) form gives one linear equation (pp. 5–6). PSD moment-matrix conditions are added; Haviland's and Schmüdgen's theorems make them necessary and sufficient on compact semi-algebraic domains (Theorem 3, pp. 8–9). Extension constraints indexed by K are added too. Maximizing and minimizing the functional then gives a guaranteed bracket for each (N, K) (pp. 10–11). What changed relative to earlier adjoint-moment work (Bertsimas–Paschalidis–Tsitsiklis, Kumar–Kumar, Schwerer): the PSD constraints, which those works did not use (p. 6). Maximizing a lower-bound variable u0 bounds min u from above (Proposition 1, p. 11). Trigonometric test functions handle trigonometric coefficients (p. 12).
- **D3 Model and data**: Linear operators with polynomial coefficients (p. 4); a separable test-function class, with u of compact support or exponentially decaying tails (p. 4); a known lower bound u0 (natural for densities and temperatures) strengthens the PSD constraints (p. 6); compact semi-algebraic domains for Theorem 3 (pp. 8–9). Test problems are textbook equations with known solutions plus one queueing diffusion; no field data (pp. 12–22).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: Haviland (a moment functional is valid if and only if it is nonnegative on polynomials nonnegative on the domain) plus Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz give one PSD condition per subset of the defining constraints, 16 per truncation level on the unit square (Theorem 3, pp. 8–10). Convergence of the bracket as N and K grow is stated without a rate (p. 11); the size is O(N^d) variables (p. 22).
- **D5 Evaluation**: SDPA 5.00 and SeDuMi 1.03 on a Sparc 5 (p. 13). ODE: SDPA needed a ±0.001 slack on the equalities; SeDuMi at N = 60 bounded 45 moments to 5 decimals in under five minutes (pp. 13–14). Bessel: severe instabilities when computing the maximum at N = 30 and 40 (p. 15). Helmholtz: LP-only bounds (AMPL with CPLEX) leave m0,0 unbounded even at N = 20, while the SDP at N = 3, K = 4 is informative (Tables 6–7, pp. 18–19). Reflected Brownian motion of two coupled queues: m1,0 in [0.5515, 0.5516] at N = 12 against the true 0.551506 (Table 9, p. 21). Instabilities and looser trigonometric bounds are printed (pp. 21–22).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A method with guaranteed brackets (theory and computation). The limits are concrete. SDP codes are less numerically stable than LP codes, and monomial moments may be ill-conditioned. General-purpose solvers also ignore that the moment matrices depend only on the domain (pp. 2, 22–23). Future: other spanning families or parameterizations, and SDP codes built for moment matrices (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: a problem owned by numerical analysis is written as an SDP. The device (moments, adjoint test functions, PSD moment matrices) keeps it in a solver class, and the boundary is printed: instability, O(N^d) variables, looser trigonometric bounds (pp. 2, 10, 21–23). There is no dual reading.
- Method 4 · variant ⚠: only the certificate step. Every (N, K) gives a guaranteed bracket, set against discretization's uncertified approximations; that comparison is argued, not run (pp. 2, 11, 22). The comparator actually run is the LP-only formulation (Table 6, p. 19). Supports Taste mark 7 (a result is stronger with a certificate).
- New-pattern candidate: *PSD moment-matrix strengthening carried across domains*, here to PDE functionals (pp. 6, 18–20; S255 in this batch; first logged for S185).
- New-pattern candidate: *constraint-family ablation*: LP-only bounds at N = 5, 10, 20 against the SDP at N = 3 with K = 0 and K = 4 isolate the PSD constraints as the source of informative bounds (Tables 6–8, pp. 18–20).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D2) To bound a functional of an unknown function fixed by a linear operator equation, make its moments the variables. Each test function in the adjoint form gives a linear equality, a Positivstellensatz gives PSD validity conditions, and the functional is optimized both ways (pp. 5–10).
- (D4) To bound a nonlinear functional such as min u with a linear method, add a lower-bound variable u0 linearly and maximize it subject to PSD conditions on the shifted moments (Proposition 1, p. 11).
- (D5) Show which constraints carry the result: run the LP-only version at large N, then the SDP at small N with and without extension constraints (Tables 6–8, pp. 18–20).
- (D2) Choose a test-function basis closed under the operator: monomials for polynomial coefficients, trigonometric functions for trigonometric ones (p. 12).

**Quotes**:
- "The obtained bounds are guaranteed upper and lower bounds, and not simply approximations." (p. 2)
- "the semidefinite constraints are critically important in improving the quality of the bounds, that is, without them the bounds are weak." (p. 1)

### S414 · Option pricing without price dynamics: a probabilistic approach (arXiv preprint 2006, arXiv math/0612075)
Read level: **partial**: abstract and §1 (pp. 1–3); §2 statements with the proofs of Theorem 1 and Proposition 3 (pp. 3–13); §3: Problem (b), Lemma 1, Theorem 5 and Proposition 5 statements, the LP of §3.3 (pp. 13–19); §4: set-up, the linearizing change of variables, the §4.4 example, Theorems 8–9 statements, §4.6–4.9 (pp. 20–34); §5 (pp. 35–36). Proofs of Theorems 2, 3 and 5–9 not read; formulas partly garbled.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Natasha Bushueva (an MIT mathematics graduate student; the paper is part of her April 2003 thesis, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the option-bounds line of Bertsimas–Popescu (2002), extended to several maturities and two assets. Posted in December 2006, more than three years after the thesis (p. 1); a 2004 geometric companion working paper is cited (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {CP + AP (finance); LO as engine}: Black–Scholes rests on geometric Brownian motion and a constant volatility, and implied volatility presumes the formula holds (p. 2). The question: the range of an option's price given only other options' prices and no arbitrage, with no price dynamics (p. 2). Earlier bounds (Lo, Grundy, Bertsimas–Popescu, d'Aspremont–El Ghaoui) cover one maturity and one stock (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: A probabilistic route rather than convex duality. The call-price transform Ψ(t) = E(X − t)^+ determines the law (Theorem 1, p. 5). Calls of several maturities admit a martingale if and only if a nondecreasing sequence of convex call-price functions fits them (Proposition 3, p. 11), which becomes a convex-hull no-arbitrage test (Theorem 4, p. 11). Markov martingales suffice (Corollary 1, p. 13). With prices bounded by L, a martingale supported on the strikes and payoff kinks suffices (Theorem 5, p. 16), so the bound is an LP over transition probabilities (pp. 18–19). Two assets: an exact LP of exponential size, after a change of variables linearizes a bilinear system (p. 28), and a polynomial ε-lattice LP that converges as ε → 0 (Theorems 8–9, pp. 28, 31). Baskets: atoms at the vertices of a hyperplane arrangement (Corollary 4, p. 35).
- **D3 Model and data**: A nonnegative martingale under the risk-neutral measure; r ≡ 0 without loss, so only the order of maturities matters (p. 4). Prices are bounded by L for the discretization, with monotone convergence as L → ∞ (Proposition 5, p. 18). Payoffs are continuous and piecewise linear (pp. 13, 20). Data: Verizon and Cisco call quotes from The Wall Street Journal of 22 October 2002 (p. 28).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: Uniqueness of Ψ by monotone convergence (p. 5). The martingale-existence result (Theorem 3) is Kertz–Rösler's, re-proved by a different approach (p. 7). Finite-support reductions (Theorems 5, 7) and lattice rounding of a two-dimensional martingale (Theorem 8, p. 28). No complexity results beyond size statements (pp. 19, 33).
- **D5 Evaluation**: One example, bounds on (V + C − k)^+ at maturity 52: using all maturities (Table 2) tightens the bounds against maturity-52 quotes alone (Table 3), and bounds for strikes below 20 are extremely tight (pp. 28–29). No solver, timing or LP size is reported.
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Characterization theorems plus LP algorithms (theory and method). Limits stated: the exact two-asset LP grows exponentially in t* (p. 28), and the extension to more assets grows exponentially in the dimension (p. 33); with piecewise-linear payoffs at later maturities, the authors doubt the two-asset problem can be made linear (p. 34). There is no conclusion or future-work section; the paper ends with §5 (pp. 35–36).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: a question owned by financial economics, where prices come from a dynamics model, is answered by LP. The boundary is printed: exponential size in time and dimension, and one case judged not linearizable (pp. 3, 28, 33–34). The route is probabilistic (transforms, support reduction), not a dual reading.
- New-pattern candidate: *assume only what can be estimated, worst-case the rest*: observed call prices and no arbitrage only (pp. 1–2).
- New-pattern candidate: *tight-or-nothing: bounds certified by extremal distributions*: best possible bounds, attained by discrete Markov martingales on the strike and kink grid (pp. 1, 16–19).
- New-pattern candidate: *re-derive classical theorems with the new device*: Kertz–Rösler's martingale-existence theorem by a different route (p. 7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) Encode a law on R+ by its call-price function, which is convex and nonincreasing with slope −P(X > t). Martingale consistency across dates then becomes monotonicity of these convex functions (pp. 5, 11).
- (D4) With piecewise-linear payoffs, restrict to laws supported on the kinks and strikes, then solve an LP over a Markov chain's transition probabilities (pp. 16–19).
- (D4) Linearize products of a probability and a conditional atom location by the substitution ẑ = z·P (p. 28).
- (D5) Run the bound with all available quotes and again with one maturity, to show what the extra data buys (Tables 2–3, pp. 28–29).

**Quotes**:
- "We show that the problem reduces to solving linear optimization problems that we explicitly characterize." (p. 1)
- "However the number of variables in the linear optimization problem will grow exponentially in the dimension." (p. 33)

### S118 · Constrained stochastic LQC: a tractable approach (IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 2007, DOI 10.1109/tac.2007.906182)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–16 (§I–§VIII, proof sketches, author notes). Nearly all displayed mathematics and table entries are lost in the extraction; statements are read from the prose.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: David B. Brown (MIT PhD 2006, then Duke Fuqua, p. 16) · **Position in trajectory**: robust optimization (the 2001–2013 programme) taken into stochastic control; received September 2004, revised 2005 and 2006 (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO + SD (control); CP as engine}: Riccati and dynamic programming solve stochastic LQ control. Yet a single nonnegativity constraint on the controls makes the cost-to-go piecewise quadratic with exponentially many pieces (pp. 1–2). The motivating case is the unconstrained optimal share-purchasing policy of Bertsimas–Lo, which both buys and sells (p. 1). DP also controls only the expected cost, not its distribution (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: Drop Bellman's recursion. Choose the whole remaining control vector to minimize the worst-case cost over a norm-bounded disturbance set; apply the first control and re-solve from the new state (p. 3). The problem is an exact SDP by the S-lemma and Schur complements (Theorem 3, pp. 4–6), and it recovers Riccati when the set shrinks to a point (Corollary 1, p. 6). Without constraints the SDP reduces to a convex search over one scalar by bisection plus matrix products, with the offline work separated from the online work (Propositions 4–6, Theorem 4, Corollary 2, pp. 6–9). With constraints, an SOCP inner approximation from Bertsimas–Sim robust conic results (Theorem 5, pp. 9–10) has a bounded gap to the SDP (Corollary 3, p. 10). Control constraints, probabilistic state constraints and cost-threshold guarantees all keep the problem in its class (pp. 10–13). Noisy state estimates fit by redefining the disturbance (Proposition 10, p. 14).
- **D3 Model and data**: A discrete-time linear system with known matrices and a known initial state (p. 2). Design uses a deterministic ellipsoid; how to choose it statistically is left outside the paper (p. 3). Guarantees assume i.i.d. normal disturbances, chosen for analytical convenience (pp. 10–11), and are proved for open-loop control only (p. 11).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: S-lemma and Schur complement give the SDP (pp. 3–6); an elimination lemma reduces it to a scalar problem (pp. 6–8). The online cost is linear in the horizon and the memory quadratic (Corollary 2, pp. 8–9). A Chernoff-type bound covers the quadratic cost under Gaussian noise (Proposition 9, p. 12). For SDP and SOCP solutions, the expected loss against Riccati is linear in the set size and comes with a probabilistic protection level (Theorem 8, pp. 12–13).
- **D5 Evaluation**: Matlab and SeDuMi (p. 14). A ten-stage unconstrained problem, 1000 closed-loop trials against Riccati: expected-cost increase and upper-tail variability across set sizes and disturbance distributions (Table II, pp. 14–15). The SOCP against the exact control law, 100 trials (p. 15). Open-loop run times with constraints on a 2.2 GHz Linux machine (Table III, p. 15). A five-stage constrained problem against the optimal policy computed by grid enumeration: the loss plateaus at 20–40% for large sets, and an apparent win at small sets is put down to grid error (p. 15).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A method with theory and computations. Limits stated: closed-loop analysis seems difficult (p. 11). The SOCP is myopic about current versus future costs (p. 15), and solving it every step becomes burdensome for large problems (p. 15). Open question: simplify the constrained case further (p. 15).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: the owning tradition (DP and the Riccati equation) is named, with where it fails (pp. 1–2). Class preservation is stated and used as a refrain (p. 10). The boundary is printed: open-loop guarantees only, and growth of the constrained SOCP (pp. 11, 15).
- Method 3 · evidence ✅: one knob, the set radius, indexes policies from Riccati to increasingly conservative ones (p. 3). Theorem 8 bounds the expected loss and gives the protection bought (pp. 12–13). The computed trade-off between expected cost and tail variability is shown with its plateau (pp. 14–15).
- New-pattern candidate: *subsume the incumbent as a special case*: Riccati at radius 0 (p. 6).
- New-pattern candidate: *receding-horizon robust re-solve in place of a DP recursion* (pp. 3, 6; also S059 p. 4).
- New-pattern candidate: *exact optimum on a small solvable instance as the certificate for a heuristic*: the grid-enumerated optimal policy on a five-stage problem (p. 15).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D2) When constraints break the DP recursion, optimize the remaining control sequence against a norm-bounded disturbance set and re-solve each period from the observed state (pp. 3, 6).
- (D4) Look for structure that turns the online solve into a scalar search plus matrix products, and count online operations and memory against the horizon (pp. 6–9).
- (D4) Design in a deterministic set, then audit under a probability model: bound the expected loss against the classical controller and the tail protection gained (pp. 11–13).
- (D5) On a small instance, compute the true optimum by brute-force grid enumeration and report the method's gap, including anomalies caused by the grid (p. 15).

**Quotes**:
- "Thus, a very simple constraint destroys the tractability of this approach." (p. 1)
- "By this, we mean the SDP remains an SDP and the SOCP remains an SOCP." (p. 10)

### S234 · Robust optimization in electromagnetic scattering problems (Journal of Applied Physics 2007, DOI 10.1063/1.2715540)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–7. Some Greek symbols are lost in the extraction.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Omid Nohadani (corresponding author) and Kwong Meng Teo, both at the MIT Operations Research Center (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the first, application-specific paper of the robust local search line, which S305 generalizes (S305 p. 3). Received November 2006, published April 2007, funded by DARPA (pp. 1, 6).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO + HS; AP (nanophotonic design)}: Designs described only by simulation are optimized heuristically, ignoring implementation errors that make them suboptimal or infeasible (p. 1). Earlier aperiodic dielectric designs matched a target to 95% but left robustness open (p. 1). The paper lists what robust-design tools lack: sensitivity analysis compares designs but finds no less sensitive one; designed experiments can fail for highly nonlinear systems with many variables; mean–standard deviation objectives need the error distribution; first- and second-order expansions do not suit sizeable perturbations. RO results need convex structure (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea**: Two steps (p. 3). First, a good nominal design: gradients by the adjoint method, reusing the LU factorization of the forward solve, with SPSA as a gradient-free alternative (pp. 3–4). Second, robust local search: explore the error ball with 101 gradient ascents to find high-cost neighbors. Then solve a small SOCP for the direction making the largest angle with all of them; if there is none, lower the cost threshold (p. 5). Only function values (and gradients) are needed (pp. 1–2).
- **D3 Model and data**: A two-dimensional Helmholtz finite-difference model of 50 dielectric cylinders at 37.5 GHz, checked against laboratory measurements at a 0.4 mm mesh (pp. 2–3). The design vector lies in R^100 over a nonconvex set; overlapping cylinders are avoided rather than modelled as constraints (p. 3). Errors are N(0, 40 μm) per coordinate, as measured in experiments; the ball radius of 550 μm covers 99% (p. 5).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: No theorems. The inner maximization has no closed form; the worst case is estimated from stored evaluations (pp. 5–6). Forward solve: a 70,000 × 70,000 sparse complex system by UMFPACK in 0.7 s on a 3.4 GHz Xeon (p. 2).
- **D5 Evaluation**: Nominal: SPSA needs 2500 iterations to reach 0.0052, while the modified gradient method passes it in 750 and ends at 0.0032 (p. 4). Robust: the estimated worst case is 0.05413 at the photonic-crystal-like start and 0.00646 after nominal optimization. Then 65 robust iterations cut it a further 8% at constant nominal cost, the final estimate using 110,000 neighbors (pp. 5–6). The 90% headline is measured from the photonic-crystal-like start, so most of it comes from nominal optimization (p. 6). Checks: 10,000 random perturbations show the final design up to 2% less sensitive, and 30,000 random samples reach 96% of the worst case found with 3,000 gradient-ascent evaluations (p. 6).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: An application plus a generic method. Limits: the worst case is only estimated, so worse errors may surface later (p. 6). A global optimum would need large-scale random search, judged out of scope (p. 4). Future: other electromagnetic and nanophotonic designs (p. 6).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 · variant ⚠: the nominal local optimum warm-starts the robust search (p. 3) and the multiple ascents act as restarts (p. 5). There is no certificate: the worst case is an estimate that can rise later (p. 6). The duel is against random sampling for the inner maximum, not against an incumbent design (p. 6). Practice evidence for Heuristic 3 (local search with restarts when exact models are out of reach).
- Method 3 · variant ⚠: one knob, the error-ball radius calibrated to 99% of measured errors, and a price: nominal cost unchanged for an 8% lower worst case (pp. 5–6). There is no bound and no curve over the radius.
- Method 1 · variant ⚠: the design problem stays a black box; only the direction subproblem is put in a solver class, as an SOCP (pp. 3, 5).
- Tension T6 evidence (not a method link): the results text and the conclusion lead with a 90% robustness gain, while Fig. 5's caption gives 8% for the robust step itself (p. 6); the abstract gives no number (p. 1). The OR manuscript of the same method reports only the 8% (S305 p. 17).
- New-pattern candidate: *staged companion papers from one model*: the applied paper first, the general OR method second (S305 p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) Reuse the forward solver's LU factorization for the adjoint gradient, so gradients come at practically no extra cost (p. 4).
- (D3) Size the uncertainty set from measured error statistics: a per-coordinate standard deviation from experiments and a radius covering 99% (p. 5).
- (D2) Estimate a worst case over a high-dimensional ball by ascents from the centre and from ±Γ/3 along each coordinate, and keep every evaluation for later iterations (p. 5).
- (D5) Check the inner-maximization estimator against random sampling with ten times the budget (p. 6).
- (D5) Validate the simulator against laboratory measurements before optimizing, and report the mesh needed for agreement (pp. 2–3).

**Quotes**:
- "Overall, we observe a 90% improvement in robustness of the final design, when compared to the initial PC-like structure." (p. 6)
- "Since we can only estimate the worst case cost by local searches, there is always a chance for late discoveries of worst implementation errors." (p. 6)

### S255 · A semidefinite optimization approach to the steady-state analysis of queueing systems (Queueing Systems 2007, DOI 10.1007/s11134-007-9028-7)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–13 (all sections and Tables 2–4; some formula fragments lost).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Karthik Natarajan (NUS mathematics, p. 1; in the CP lineage of SKILL.md) · **Position in trajectory**: the moment–SDP programme (SW2) applied to queueing steady states; received November 2005, revised November 2006 (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {CP + SD}: Steady-state distributions of Markov chains with general distributions are hard to compute. The iterative model W =d g(W, X) covers GI/GI/1 (Lindley) and GI/GI/c (Kiefer–Wolfowitz) (pp. 1–2). Kingman's two-moment bound is tight only in special cases and weak in light traffic. Little is known about using third and higher moments, or about bounding tails and second moments (pp. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea**: Relax equality in distribution to equality of moments, and independence to factorized moments. Split the support into pieces where g is linear and take conditional moments times piece probabilities as variables. Relax the moment cone by moment and localizing matrices, which gives an SDP hierarchy in r (pp. 4–6). The first-order relaxation reproduces Kingman's bound (Proposition 6, pp. 7–8); Daley's tightening is one more linear constraint (p. 9).
- **D3 Model and data**: g piecewise linear and f piecewise polynomial on semi-algebraic pieces (Assumption 1, p. 4); finitely many known moments of X, with X independent of W (pp. 3–4). Convergence as r → ∞ is claimed under three conditions, which the authors call strong (p. 5): all moments of X known, the moments determining X's law, and X's law fixing W's. Test cases: a Lindley process with normal X, and M/M/1 and M/M/2 queues with exact values (pp. 8–12).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: The relaxation chain inf(Ps) ≤ inf(P) ≤ sup(P) ≤ sup(Ps) (p. 6). Kingman is recovered by constructing a feasible point of the first-order SDP that attains his value (pp. 7–8). For GI/GI/2, the ordering map is piecewise linear over five polyhedral sets (Proposition 8, p. 10); the piece count for general c was pointed out by a referee (p. 12).
- **D5 Evaluation**: MATLAB 7.04 and SeDuMi 1.1 on a 1.72 GHz Pentium 4-M (p. 8). Lindley process with normal X (mean −0.25, variance 0.25) against a 500,000-replication simulation: the relative error of the upper bound on E[W] falls from about 90% (r = 1) to about 9% (r = 5) (Table 2, pp. 8–9). M/M/1 at ρ = 0.2–0.8 (Fig. 2, p. 9). Tails P(W ≥ w): relative errors are larger for small w (Table 3, p. 10). M/M/2: the first-order bound beats Kingman's two-server bound, for example 3.563 against 4.563 at ρ = 0.8, exact value 1.778 (Table 4, p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: A general algorithmic approach (theory and computation) that contains the classical two-moment bounds. Limits: the SDPs grow rapidly with the number of servers and the relaxation order (p. 12), and tail bounds stay loose for small w (p. 10). Open problem: a closed form for the first-order GI/GI/c relaxation. Future: tandem and fork–join queues and networks (p. 12).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: a queueing-theory problem (the GI/GI/c steady state) is written as an SDP hierarchy. The device (moments per linear piece, localizing matrices) keeps it in class, and the boundary is printed: strong conditions for convergence, and size growth (pp. 4–6, 12).
- Method 4 · variant ⚠: the incumbent bound is both the first rung of the hierarchy and the opponent. Kingman's bound is the r = 1 relaxation (pp. 7–8); it is beaten at higher r and, with two servers, already at r = 1 (Table 4, p. 12). The certificate is a two-sided bracket checked against simulation and exact values (pp. 8–12).
- New-pattern candidate: *re-derive classical theorems with the new device*: Kingman's and Daley's bounds as special cases (pp. 1, 7–9).
- New-pattern candidate: *PSD moment-matrix strengthening carried across domains*, here to queueing steady states (pp. 5–6; S264 in this batch).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D2) For a steady state defined by W =d g(W, X), split the support where g is linear and use conditional moments times probabilities as variables. Moment equality and factorized independence then become linear constraints (pp. 4–5).
- (D4) Show that the new hierarchy contains the classical bound by constructing a feasible point of the first-order relaxation that attains it (pp. 7–8).
- (D5) Report brackets against a high-replication simulation and exact formulas across traffic levels, and say where they stay loose (light traffic, small w) (pp. 8–12).

**Quotes**:
- "We show that the classical Kingman’s and Daley’s bounds for the expected waiting time in a GI/GI/1 queue are special cases of the proposed approach." (p. 1)
- "These conditions are however fairly strong typically." (p. 5)

### S305 · Robust nonconvex optimization for simulation-based problems (Operations Research manuscript 2007, no DOI or arXiv; homepage PDF)
Read level: **partial**: §1–§2 with Theorem 1, Proposition 1, Algorithm 1, Theorem 2 and Proposition 2 (pp. 1–9); §3.1 and §3.2.2–3.2.3 (pp. 9–12; §3.2.1 skimmed); §4–§5 (pp. 12–17); §6.1–6.2 and §6.4 (pp. 17–21; §6.3 skimmed); §7 (p. 22); the Appendix C proof of Theorem 2 (pp. 24–25). Appendices A–B not read in detail.
**Version**: the homepage PDF filed under this title prints a different title, *Robust Optimization for Unconstrained Simulation-based Problems*, on an Operations Research template dated June 2007 (p. 1). That is the published title of S090 (OR 2010), whose abstract in abstracts.json matches this one, so S305 and S090 are versions of one paper. All pages here are manuscript pages. A duplicated paragraph with a stray caption numbered Figure 2-13 remains (p. 21).
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Omid Nohadani, Kwong Meng Teo (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: generalizes S234's specialized search with a directional-derivative framework, a convergence analysis and parameter uncertainty (p. 3).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO + HS; AP as testbed}: Robust convex optimization fails when the cost is not given explicitly, and worse when it is nonconvex: PDE solvers, response surfaces, kriging models (pp. 1–2). Stochastic programming needs distributions that are seldom available (p. 2). Implementation errors and parameter uncertainty together have not received due attention (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea**: The robust analogue of descent. Let g(x) be the maximum of f(x + Δx) over an error ball. A direction is a descent direction for g if and only if it makes a negative inner product with every worst error (Theorem 1, p. 5). A robust local minimum is a point with no such direction (Definition 2, Proposition 1, p. 6), and the direction comes from an SOCP maximizing the angle to the worst errors (p. 7). The worst errors are unknown in practice, but known high-cost neighbors that span them suffice (Proposition 2, p. 8). This gives the robust local search: n + 1 capped gradient ascents with a barrier term, then an SOCP move whose step pushes the bad neighbors to the boundary (pp. 9–12). Parameter uncertainty is folded in by treating parameters as decision variables fixed at their nominal values (p. 18).
- **D3 Model and data**: Unconstrained, continuously differentiable f, with values and gradients from a subroutine (pp. 2, 5); an error ball, other p-norms possible (p. 4). The convergence theorem needs convex f with a bounded set of minima and diminishing steps (Theorem 2, p. 8). EM example: Γ = 550 μm from a measured standard deviation of 40 μm (p. 16).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: Danskin's min–max theorem gives the directional derivative (Appendix A, pp. 22–23), and KKT makes the gradient at a worst error parallel to it (p. 5). Theorem 2: given an oracle for the worst errors, Algorithm 1 is a subgradient method on convex g and converges under Shor's step rules (pp. 8, 24–25). For nonconvex f the practical algorithm is called a heuristic (p. 8).
- **D5 Evaluation**: Three examples. (i) A two-dimensional nonconvex polynomial with Γ = 0.5: the global nominal minimum −20.8 is certified with Gloptipoly; two starts reach the same robust local minimum, which the authors say cannot be generalized (p. 12). (ii) The 100-dimensional EM design: the worst case is 8% lower at the same nominal cost after 65 iterations, estimated with 110,000 neighbors; random sampling with 30,000 evaluations reaches 96% of the estimate from 3,000 (p. 17). (iii) Implementation plus parameter errors (18 dimensions): the search starts from the nominal global minimum, with worst case 450, and ends much lower. 10 + 1 ascents converge in 550 s against 600 s for n + 1, and 3 + 1 never meets the termination test (pp. 20–21).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Theory (descent and local minima for the robust problem, convex convergence), a generic algorithm and three examples. Limits: the worst case is only estimated (p. 17); the right number of ascents is unknown in advance (p. 21); only unconstrained problems are treated (p. 1); convergence is proved only for convex f (p. 8). No future work is stated (p. 22).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 · variant ⚠: a nominal local minimum warm-starts the robust search (p. 17), and the small example's nominal optimum is certified globally with Gloptipoly (p. 12). The robust result has no certificate beyond a numerical termination test. The conclusion nonetheless calls that test an optimality condition (p. 22): evidence for Tension T1.
- Method 1 · variant ⚠: the polynomial-SDP route for the two-dimensional example is weighed and declined as too large (p. 12); only the direction subproblem is kept in a solver class, as an SOCP (p. 7).
- Method 3 · variant ⚠: the radius is calibrated to 99% of measured errors, and the price is nominal cost unchanged for an 8% lower worst case (pp. 16–17). There is no bound and no curve over Γ.
- Skill update (not a method link): SKILL.md's Domain Map marks the convergence of the robust local search as not read. It is now read. Convergence is proved only for convex f, given an oracle for the worst errors and diminishing steps (Theorem 2, p. 8; proof pp. 24–25), and the nonconvex algorithm is labelled a heuristic (p. 8). This bears on Tension T2.
- New-pattern candidate: *robust analogue of another field's canonical concept*: descent direction and local minimum redefined for the worst-case cost (pp. 2–3, 5–6).
- New-pattern candidate: *staged companion papers from one model*: S234's application paper, then this general method (p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) For a min–max objective, characterize descent with Danskin's theorem: a direction descends if and only if it has a negative inner product with every worst-case perturbation. Compute it as a max-angle SOCP (pp. 5, 7).
- (D2) When worst cases cannot be found exactly, certify descent with a set of known bad neighbors that spans them (Proposition 2, p. 8).
- (D2) Fold parameter uncertainty into implementation error by making the parameters decision variables pinned to their nominal values (p. 18).
- (D4) Keep the inner search's time predictable: a capped gradient ascent, then a barrier term used only to keep iterates inside the ball, not driven to zero as in the standard barrier method (p. 10).
- (D5) Tune the depth of the inner search and report the time–termination trade-off, including the setting that fails (p. 21).

**Quotes**:
- "the following algorithm embodies a heuristic strategy to finding a more robust neighbor" (p. 8)
- "The numerically determined termination criteria of the algorithm acts as an optimality condition for robust optimization problems." (p. 22)

### S785 · Bounds on some contingent claims with non-convex payoff based on multiple assets (report 2007, no DOI or arXiv; venue unknown)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–11.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Xuan Vinh Doan (MIT Operations Research Center), Karthik Natarajan (NUS) (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the moment-bound option line (Bertsimas–Popescu) taken to non-convex multi-asset payoffs; an August 2007 report (pp. 1, 7), with no venue in the list.

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {CP + AP (finance)}: Moment bounds on options have covered convex payoffs, for which Jensen's inequality gives a simple tight lower bound (pp. 1–2). Entry: non-convex payoffs, a call on the minimum and a put on the maximum of several assets, given first and second moments only (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea**: Dualize the moment problem, following Bertsimas–Popescu (p. 3). Nonnegativity of a quadratic on the nonnegative orthant is exactly copositivity of the bordered matrix (Lemma 1, p. 3). Checking copositivity is NP-hard (Murty), so it is relaxed to PSD plus entrywise nonnegative (Remark 1, p. 3). The minimum inside the payoff is written as a maximum over the simplex and min and max are swapped by weak duality (Lemma 2, p. 4). The result is an SDP upper bound (Theorem 1, p. 5). Lower bound: Jensen on (·)^+, then the same relaxation of the dual (Theorem 2, pp. 5–6).
- **D3 Model and data**: Nonnegative prices with known mean vector and second-moment matrix, and no assumed distribution (p. 2); strong duality under a weak Slater condition (p. 3). Data: synthetic correlated lognormal prices for n = 4 assets (S(0) = $40, σ = 30%, ρ = 0.9, r = 10%, T = 1, values like Boyle–Lin's), with exact prices by Monte Carlo (p. 7).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: Lemma 1 by a scaling argument: a negative quadratic form on the orthant drives the quadratic to −∞ (p. 3). The chain P^c_u ≥ P_u ≥ P_max (p. 5). Two relaxations (the min–max swap; PSD plus nonnegative) mean no tightness is claimed. PSD plus nonnegative equals the copositive cone only up to size 4 (Diananda, p. 4), and the n = 4 experiments use 5 × 5 bordered matrices (pp. 3, 7); this last point is my inference and is not discussed in the report.
- **D5 Evaluation**: Matlab 7.4 with SeDuMi through YALMIP (p. 7). Call on the minimum of 4 assets, K = $20–$50: at K = 40 the exact price is 4.2340, the upper bound 8.0961 and the lower bound 0. Lower bounds are positive only below K = 40, and the upper bound is about 2.5 times the exact price at K = 50 (Table 1, p. 9). Put on the maximum, K = $40–$70: lower bounds are positive only above K = 50 (Table 2, p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Computational bounds (a method plus numbers). The bounds are judged reasonably good (pp. 1, 7) and significant (p. 11) without a gap analysis. No conclusion, limits or future directions are written (pp. 9–11).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: a pricing question is written as a dual moment problem. Its hardness is printed (copositivity is NP-hard), and a tractable inner device is chosen: PSD plus nonnegative, giving an SDP (pp. 3–5).
- New-pattern candidate: *assume only what can be estimated, worst-case the rest*: first and second moments only (p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D4) Turn a dual constraint that a quadratic be nonnegative on the nonnegative orthant into copositivity of the bordered matrix, then relax it to PSD plus entrywise nonnegative (pp. 3–4).
- (D4) Handle a minimum of linear functions inside a semi-infinite constraint by writing it as a maximum over the simplex and swapping min and max, with the simplex weights as new variables (Lemma 2, p. 4).
- (D5) Generate the moments from a known model (correlated lognormal) so that exact prices are available by Monte Carlo, and sweep the strike to show where each bound is informative (pp. 7–9).

**Quotes**:
- "The reformulation makes it clear that finding the (tight) upper bound Pu is a hard problem." (p. 3)
- "Computational results shows that these upper and lower bounds are reasonably good for call options on the minimum of multiple assets and put options on the maximum of multiple assets." (p. 1)

### S059 · Robust multiperiod portfolio management in the presence of transaction costs (Computers & Operations Research 2008, DOI 10.1016/j.cor.2006.02.011)
Read level: **full**: pp. 1–15 (all sections and Tables 1–6). The robust counterparts of §3 are partly garbled and were read from the prose.
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Dessislava Pachamanova (Babson College, corresponding author, p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the D-norm robust LP (Bertsimas–Pachamanova–Sim 2004) applied to multiperiod portfolios; online March 2006, in print 2008 (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem and entry point** {RO + AP (finance); LO as engine}: Markowitz's model is single-period. Dynamic programming gives closed forms only under strong assumptions and breaks with transaction costs, and scenario-tree stochastic programming grows exponentially with the number of periods (pp. 1–2). Ben-Tal–Margalit–Nemirovski's robust multiperiod model is an SOCP, convex but nonlinear. Tax and structure constraints may need integer programming, where linear MIP software is more developed (pp. 2–3). A second question: does multiperiod RO beat the single-period mean-variance rule used in practice (p. 3)?
- **D2 Core idea**: Polyhedral (D-norm) uncertainty sets keep the robust counterpart an LP (pp. 3, 6). Cumulative returns cut the uncertain constraints from N·M to N (pp. 4–5), and uncertain equalities become inequalities, since robust equalities are usually infeasible (p. 5). MPR1 combines intervals on later returns with a D-norm set on first-period returns and their covariance. MPR2 puts D-norm budgets on each period's cumulative returns with their covariances (pp. 5–6). Zero budgets give the nominal certainty-equivalent policy, and the L2 norm gives Ben-Tal et al.'s model (p. 6). Rolling horizon: apply the first step, then re-solve (p. 4). The objective is linear (risk-neutral); robust feasibility acts as an implicit risk measure (p. 4).
- **D3 Model and data**: Proportional transaction costs, no short sales or borrowing, a fixed riskless return (pp. 3–4); known means and covariances. Synthetic data only: 3 normal stocks plus cash, and 25 lognormal stocks from Ben-Tal et al.'s factor model (pp. 8, 10–11).
- **D4 Formulation and analysis (brief)**: Counterparts from the D-norm duality results of Bertsimas–Pachamanova–Sim (p. 6). Size counts for 500 stocks and 6 periods: MPR1 has 38,006 variables and 76,508 constraints, MPR2 14,006 and 23,016. These are called easily handled, but no solve times are given (p. 8). No theorems.
- **D5 Evaluation**: 1000 simulated paths over five periods, with 1% transaction costs (pp. 8–9). Baselines: single-period mean-variance, the industry standard, with its risk parameter set to 0.531 to match SPR's expected return and roughly its variance; single-period robust (SPR); and multiperiod nominal (pp. 8–9). Metrics: annualized mean, standard deviation, min and max, mean/standard-deviation ratio and loss probability, plus percentiles and pairwise win rates for lognormal returns (pp. 10–14). Misspecified arms shift the means down by 0.5σ or 10% (pp. 9, 11). A failure is printed: with i.i.d. normal returns in the 25-stock set, mean-variance has the better worst case and ratio. MPR1 there has min −0.6136 and a 12.5% loss probability against mean-variance's 5.4% (Table 4, p. 12). Results for other market-risk indices are omitted (p. 13).
- **D8 Contribution and limits**: Linear robust formulations plus a simulation study. Claims: excellent potential whatever the shape of the return distribution (p. 15); MPR1 is best under misspecification and MPR2 when parameters are right (p. 14). Limits left unstated: no real data and no solve times. No future directions (pp. 14–15).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅: the owning traditions (DP, stochastic programming) are named with what they cannot do (pp. 1–2). The set is chosen to keep the LP class so that integer side constraints remain possible (p. 3), and counterparts come from duality (p. 6). Tractability is argued by size counts, not timed (p. 8).
- Method 3 · variant ⚠: the knob maps risk aversion to tolerance of forecast error (p. 1), and the interval width controls conservativeness (p. 10). But no bound links the knob to the protection, and no curve over the knob is computed.
- Method 5 · variant ⚠: the baseline is practice's rule, single-period mean-variance (pp. 3, 8), and decisions are scored by realized returns. But there is no owner, partner or real data (pp. 8–14).
- New-pattern candidate: *knob-matched baseline*: the mean-variance parameter is set so that its portfolio's expected return equals SPR's (p. 9).
- New-pattern candidate: *misspecification duel*: all methods run on paths whose true means fall below the inputs (pp. 9, 11, 14).
- New-pattern candidate: *subsume the incumbent as a special case*: the certainty-equivalent policy at zero budgets, and Ben-Tal et al.'s model with the L2 norm (p. 6).
- New-pattern candidate: *receding-horizon robust re-solve in place of a DP recursion* (p. 4; also S118 pp. 3, 6).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D2) Rewrite multiperiod dynamics in cumulative returns so that only the cash-account constraints are uncertain, then robustify (pp. 4–5).
- (D2) Turn uncertain equalities into inequalities before robustifying (p. 5).
- (D5) Set the incumbent's risk parameter so that its portfolio matches the challenger's expected return, then compare realized distributions on the same simulated paths (p. 9).
- (D5) Add misspecified arms, with true means below the inputs, and report pairwise win rates on common paths (pp. 9, 11, 14).

**Quotes**:
- "Robust optimization models treat future asset returns as uncertain coefficients in an optimization problem, and map the level of risk aversion of the investor to the level of tolerance of the total error in asset return forecasts." (p. 1)
- "the MPR1 approach appears to be the most robust with respect to misspecifications in the parameters of the model, and clearly dominates the other approaches in situations that are most similar to real life." (p. 14)
