### D008 · Performance of multiclass Markovian queueing networks (Proceedings of the 39th IEEE Conference on Decision and Control 2000, pp. 534–539, DOI 10.1109/cdc.2000.912819)
**Read**: abstract (abstracts.json; source OpenAlex) · **Coauthors**: David Gamarnik, John N. Tsitsiklis
- **D1 Problem and entry point** {SD}: the steady-state distribution of queue lengths in multiclass queueing networks under a stable policy (abstract).
  - The entry is a gap: earlier results gave numerical bounds, and only on the expectation, not on the distribution (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: the Lyapunov functions that prove stability also bound performance (abstract).
  - If linear or piecewise linear Lyapunov functions show stability, they give geometric-type upper and lower bounds on tail probabilities, and so on expected queue lengths.
  - Example: a two-station re-entrant line under any work-conserving policy has E[L] = O(1/(1−ρ*)²). This extends Dai and Vande-Vate's stability result for ρ* < 1 (abstract).
  - Transferable technique: when a drift (Lyapunov) certificate of stability exists, try to extract geometric tail bounds from the same function before building a new performance argument (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: the abstract claims the first explicit geometric-type upper and lower tail bounds for networks of this generality (abstract). It states no limits. The journal version is S123 (Ann. Appl. Probab. 2001, card s05).

**Method links**:
- None of Methods 1–6 is visible; the abstract has no optimization model.
- New-pattern candidate: **stability certificate reused as a performance bound**. This is an existing name (card s05, for the journal version S123); this conference abstract states it too (abstract).

**Quotes** (abstract):
- "We establish a deeper connection between stability and performance of such networks by showing that if there exist linear and piecewise linear Lyapunov functions that show stability, then these Lyapunov functions can be used to establish geometric type lower and upper bounds on the tail probabilities, and thus bounds on the expectation of the queue lengths."
- "Previous results provide numerical bounds and only on the expectation, not the distribution, of queue lengths."
