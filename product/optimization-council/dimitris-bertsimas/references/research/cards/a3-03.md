### S745 · Channel Coding via Robust Optimization Part 2: The Multi-User Channel (manuscript Aug. 2015; homepage listing: submitted to IEEE Transactions on Information Theory; no DOI)
**Read**: abstract, from p. 1 of the homepage PDF. The PDF was saved for the next round (NEW-PDF).
**Coauthor**: C. Bandi (p. 1 check). Part 1 is S554, skimmed in an earlier round (INDEX).
- **D1** {RO CP IO; AP}: optimal finite-length (n) channel coding on multi-user Gaussian interference and broadcast channels at error probability ϵ (abstract).
- **D2**: The coding problem is written as an optimization problem. Its optimal values give exact upper and lower capacity bounds for finite n, and the bounds meet as n → ∞ (abstract). The class depends on the noise (abstract):

  - exponential and uniform noise: MILPs for commercial solvers.

- **D8**: The models are too large to solve to provable optimality. A feasible code gives a valid lower bound on the capacity region; the upper bound is only approximated (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (abstract): a problem owned by information theory is rewritten as SDP or MIO, and the class is traced to the noise model.
- Method 4 · evidence ✅ (abstract): the abstract says which bound is certified (the feasible code's lower bound) and which is not.

**Transferable technique**: when the exact model is too large to certify, say in the abstract which bound remains valid (the feasible solution's) and label the other as an approximation (abstract).

**Quotes**:

### S725 · A scalable algorithm for sparse and robust portfolios (arXiv 1811.00138v1, 31 Oct 2018)
**Read**: abstract, from the arXiv v1 PDF, p. 1. The PDF was saved for the next round (NEW-PDF).
**Coauthor**: R. Cory-Wright. v1 lists both authors; Scholar shows only Cory-Wright. The v1 first page marks it as submitted to Operations Research (p. 1 check).
**Version note**: this is v1 of the arXiv record of S136 (IJOC 2022, carded from a later arXiv version, now titled *A Scalable Algorithm for Sparse Portfolio Selection*). The homepage lists S725's title under the 2022 IJOC venue (homepage listing).
- **D1** {IO CP RO; AP}: Markowitz selection with a cardinality bound, minimum investments and linear constraints at real-world scale. Existing approaches give no provably optimal portfolios beyond 300 securities (abstract).
- **D2**: a cutting-plane method. A dual representation of the continuous Markowitz problem gives closed-form subgradients. Local-search warm starts are added, and the local search is also embedded inside the cutting planes. A rotated second-order-cone correspondence of the convexified problem gives a tight lower bound that is sometimes exact (abstract).
- **D5**: 3–4 orders of magnitude faster than existing methods. Provably optimal sparsity-constrained frontiers for the S&P 500, Russell 1000 and Wilshire 5000 (abstract).
- **D8**: algorithmic and computational. Limits are not stated in the abstract (abstract).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 · evidence ✅ (abstract): heuristic warm starts, an exact solve to provable optimality, and a relaxation bound.
- Method 1 · evidence ✅ (abstract): the structure (subgradients) is read off the dual of the continuous problem, and the relaxation stays conic (SOCP).
- Method 2 · variant ⚠ (abstract): the "why now" is the size at which existing methods stop (300 securities), not a solver × hardware figure.

**Transferable technique**: run the warm-start local search inside the cutting-plane loop, not only before it, to recover the optimal support sooner (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "Existing approaches to this problem do not provide provably optimal portfolios for real-world problem sizes with more than 300 securities." (abstract)
- "Third, we exploit a correspondence between the convexified sparse Markowitz problem and a rotated second-order cone problem to obtain a tight lower bound which is sometimes exact." (abstract)
