### B.2 His stated philosophy of advising [stated, P]

The homepage text "Path, philosophy and aspirations" is undated. He says in the oral history that he "Recently ... wrote a document, 3 pages" while "now 53". The homepage text says "the last 27 years as a university professor", which gives c. 2015 [inferred].

1. **Students first.** "I consider being the advisor of my graduate students far and away the most important aspect of my life as an academic. My graduate students have been and continue to be my first priority."
2. **Values over technique.** "The superficial objective is to teach them the principles of Operations Research, my area of expertise. I feel this is the minimal and not a particularly important objective. The most important thing I aspire to help them learn is why research is important and especially what research is important."
3. **The 100%-success test, applied before starting.** "Assume we succeed 100% in answering the research question we aim to address. Then, does something change in the world for the better among those things that are important in life?" It continues: "What is important is that these questions are answerable before we do the research."
4. **Self-criticism as the origin of the rule.** "It took me more than a decade to fully crystalize these principles. I see a lot of people, including myself in my early years, who aim to impress rather than change the world."
5. **Values list.** The list includes:
   - "the best idea should be followed, not whose idea it was";
   - loyalty: "They have entrusted their future in my hands and I take this responsibility very seriously";
   - positive reinforcement: "an encouraging word, a positive comment goes a long way to empower very talented but a bit uncertain young people to achieve their potential";
   - "first rate people surround themselves with other first rate people, but second rate people surround themselves with third rate people".
6. **Aim high.** "As a professor, I have tried to teach my students to aim high, even higher that they think they can achieve and dedicate themselves to achieve it." From the "Life Stories" speech; its typo is kept.
7. **The same words, 10 years later.**
   - "Working with my doctoral students is my principal and most favorite activity" (MIT News, 14 May 2025).
   - "It is far and away my favorite activity, to supervise my doctoral students … They actually make me a better scientist, and a better person." (MIT News, 23 March 2026, Killian Lecture).

**How students restate it** [observed, S]:
- Cory-Wright: he "taught me to always aspire to conduct impactful research that simultaneously improves our knowledge of mathematics and the human condition" (ORC testimonial).
- Koulouras: "I was also motivated by my advisor's philosophy of improving the human condition, through research and teaching, and by his eagerness to bring research to implementation in industry" (ORC testimonial).
- Gupta: "Your indefatigable positivity and unshakable belief that good research can have lasting impact have shaped me profoundly" (thesis, 2014).

### B.3 Intake: who gets in [stated vs observed; kept apart]

### B.5 Topic selection and the funding firewall [stated, P; observed echoes]

1. **Mutual persuasion.** p.6: "I need to convince the students that the topic I propose matters. The students can also propose a topic they are interested in. But both of us need to convince the other person."
2. **The test for theory and for application.**
   - Theory: "if it is a theoretical topic and you are fully successful in the research, can you teach this research in graduate-level courses? If you can teach it in undergraduate-level course, it is a big breakthrough. It happens maybe once or twice in a career."
   - Application: "used by companies in a reasonable amount of time. Not in 40 years, but 2 to 5 years."
   - In 2025 the horizon was shorter: "If you solve them, something changes, in what we teach or in industry, and in short order. Not in 50 years, but in two years." (MIT News).
3. **The topic share moves over the PhD.** p.13: "In the beginning, I have a higher role in choosing topics, but by the time they graduate they work in multiple topics, not only one." p.14: "In fact, some of them, I would say one-fourth of them, we work on topics as they have defined."
4. **Funding is separated from the thesis topic.** p.6: "I separate funding and PhD topic." p.7: "Very often, 50% of my students work on the topic of their research, and that's the price you pay for having complete freedom. We have another topic, which we will also write papers, and also good for them if they want to find academic jobs, where in a way pays the bills." The sentence is garbled in the transcript; the most plausible reading is that a student splits time between the thesis topic and a funded project that also yields papers [inferred].
   - Rationale (p.7): "I view funding as an enabler of doing excellent work, not a constraint that these are the only topics you should be working on if you want to have funding. Otherwise, you might not be aggressive enough, or visionary enough, or ambitious enough."
5. **What stays with them.** p.14: "In a way, the biggest contribution is not the specific topic they work, but the values of what research is important."

**What students report** [observed, S]:
- Two students, five years apart, say the topic was his: Popescu 1999, "who guided me along this path he had once initiated" (thesis p.3, transcribed from the page image); Thiele 2004, "this research path that he once initiated".
- Kallus 2015: "Dimitris inspired me to be the best academic I can be and to work on problems that matter. I have learned from him to choose research projects that I am passionate about and to pursue these projects tirelessly."
- Mišić 2016: "One of the most important skills that I have gained—and that I continue to develop—is choosing the right problem" (ORC testimonial; attributed to the ORC training in general, not to Bertsimas alone).
- Koulouras's thesis was electricity markets, but he also did healthcare work "though the latter was not part of his thesis" (homepage news, 18 Apr 2025) [practice]. This matches "multiple topics".

## G. Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Intake.**
   - "if somebody wants to work with me, I say yes, and I find the funding" (oral history p.6, c. 2014–16).
   - Against: the 2021 interview chapter "Criteria for selecting PhD students and postdocs" (listener's paraphrase: human connection, positivity, good heart).
   - Against: Brown's 2001 admission on "a good feeling" after one meeting.
   - The policy is to accept everyone who asks. The criteria imply selection.
2. **Meeting time.** The first three statements come from the same interview.
   - "about an hour or two hours a week" (p.6).
   - "once a week for about an hour. Maybe twice a week" (p.13).
   - "Thirty hours a week I meet with my students" (p.6).
   - Kallus (2015): "nowhere to be found at our scheduled meeting times".
   - Pauphilet (2020): "kept me energized week after week".
3. **Funding.**
   - "I find the funding" and "my constraint at the moment is not money. It's time" (c. 2014–16).
   - Students of 1995–2006 describe early support from other faculty, TA/RA posts with seven faculty, a semester rescued by another professor, and outside fellowships (D).
   - The first students were paid from his advisor's NSF grants (E). Different eras; the statements are not about the same years.
4. **Large organizations.**
   - "I'm not a huge fan of large organizations … the majority of them enter academia or smaller companies or start their own" (p.19).
   - His own 2025–26 posts announce graduates going to Meta (2), Amazon (2), Google and IBM (B.12).
5. **Counts and dates.**
   - "graduated 59" (oral history, nominally Oct 2014) against 53 on his roster by end-2014. This is probably a revision-date effect (E).
   - Companies: "ten" (2021 video description; Bodossaki bio; MIT News 2025 "10") against "13" (homepage 2026).
   - Dynamic Ideas: founded "1998" (CV; oral history) against "in 1999 I co-founded (with Dimitris)" (Mourtzinou).
   - MBAn: "In 2013, I developed and launched the Master of Business Analytics (MBAn) program" (homepage) against "inaugural faculty director of the Master of Business Analytics (MBAn) program since 2016" (MIT News 2025) against "we have recently launched a new Masters program in analytics for 60 students" (oral history). The homepage now says "just 80 positions".
   - The homepage says "currently advising 20 more" but lists 21 current students.
   - COVID-19 team size: Cory-Wright's ORC testimonial says "a 25-person team"; his thesis acknowledgements (2022) say "a 30-person team to tackle COVID within a week of MIT closing in March 2020". The paper has 21 authors.
   - Pierskalla 2013 team. The oral history (p.17) calls the 2013 health-care best-paper work "collaborative work with three of my doctoral students". His roster lists the award under O'Hair (PhD 2013) and Silberholz (PhD 2015), and under Stephen Relyea, who is on the **master's** list ("An Analytics Approach to Designing Clinical Trials for Cancer", 2013).
6. **Impact horizon.** "Not in 40 years, but 2 to 5 years" (c. 2014–16) against "Not in 50 years, but in two years" (2025).
7. **Advisor or committee member.** Goemans 1990, Teo 1996 and Sim 2004 thank him first as one of "the members of my committee" (Goemans, Teo) or "my thesis committee members" (Sim). Yet their abstract pages ("Thesis Supervisor: Dimitris Bertsimas" or "Dimitris J. Bertsimas", seen on the page images) and DSpace name him thesis supervisor. Sim then calls him "my advisor". This may be wording, or the early relationship may have been closer to collegial. Not resolved.
8. **Student first or alphabetical with the same student.** Bertsimas & Teo 1998 (alphabetical) against Teo & Bertsimas 2001 (student first). No rule found.
9. **Records disagree on theses.** Homepage titles and years differ from DSpace for at least six theses (A.1). The DSpace record is taken as authoritative here.
