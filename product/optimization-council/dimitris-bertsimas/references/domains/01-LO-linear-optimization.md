## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (Scholar counts in works.json), his own emphasis and the turns of §1. The most cited LO-tagged paper, the textbook aside, the robust network-flow paper with Sim [S004] (2,690 citations), is RO's signature (SKILL.md SW3); its flow device is in §3.

**SW-LO1 · *Introduction to Linear Optimization*, with Tsitsiklis (Athena Scientific 1997) [S002] · M6, M1 framing {LO IO}.** The body is unread; this entry rests on joint book material.
- *Forced by*: teaching; it was "developed while we took turns teaching a first-year graduate course at M.I.T." [B001 p. 1].
- *Key insight* (stated jointly): "Our general philosophy is that insight matters most." [B001 p. 1]: geometry for insight, algebra for algorithms; LP in general form; duality derived without the simplex method; one theme, exponentially many constraints, from modeling to a case study [B001 p. 1].
- *Minimum evidence* (titles): geometry before the simplex chapter; network flows the longest chapter (94 of 568 pages); polynomial-time methods 16%; the art chapter 6%; ⚠ the simplex-free duality is a starred section [B004 pp. 2–5].
- *Reception*: 5,688 citations, Scholar rank 2 (note 01 §1.7); Ramana adopted it for his course and found its geometric explanations "widely successful" [B005 pp. 13–14]; Bard's review survives as a publisher excerpt [B002 p. 1].
- *Method*: course → book → kept in repair (M6); formulation first among the four abilities the book builds (M1, joint).

**SW-LO2 · A technique for speeding up the solution of the Lagrangean dual, with Orlin (Math. Program. 1994) [S269] · M1 step 4 variant, M7 {LO IO}**, the Domain Map's LO anchor (DOI 10.1007/BF01582057).
- *Forced by*: NP-hard instances are attacked through LP relaxations and Lagrangean duals, yet the subgradient method is not known to be polynomial [S269 pp. 1–2].
- *Key insight*: write the easy polyhedron by its extreme points and rays and dualize; the dual has one variable per complicating constraint plus one, its separation problem is optimization over the easy polyhedron, so Vaidya's cutting-plane method runs in dimension m, not n [S269 pp. 3–4].
- *Minimum evidence*: operation counts for about a dozen relaxations, e.g. the Held–Karp bound in O(n^3.38 L) against O(n^6.76 L) for Vaidya's method applied directly [S269 pp. 12–13, 21]; no implementation.
- *Reception*: 33 citations, no critique on file; the device returns in 2021 as column generation with each unit's MILP as pricing oracle [S196 pp. 4–5].
- *Limits printed*: "to our knowledge Vaidya's algorithm has not been tested in practice" [S269 p. 22].

**SW-LO3 · On the worst case complexity of potential reduction algorithms for linear programming, with Luo (Math. Program. 1997) [S289] · M7 step 4 {LO}.**
- *Forced by*: potential reduction joins the best known O(√n L) bound to practical line searches, "a rare combination" [S289 p. 2]; so, "is the bound inherent in the algorithm or is it because of the weakness of our proof methods?" [S289 p. 2].
- *Key insight*: a deliberately degenerate network LP (two chains of equalities, b = 0, c = e) whose symmetry keeps the iterates in a two-value family; started at the ratio the exact line-search step maps to its reciprocal, the same step repeats forever [S289 pp. 6–11].
- *Minimum evidence*: Ye's algorithm with exact line searches needs Ω(√n log(1/ε)) primal steps for q = 2n + Θ(√n), matching the upper bound; Freund's and Gonzaga–Todd's variants too [S289 pp. 11–12].
- *Reception*: 27 citations; Ye commented on a draft, a referee simplified a proof [S289 p. 13]; no critique on file.
- *Limits printed*: one instance, one start; ν = Ω(√n) open; other potential-reduction and long-step methods possibly not covered [S289 pp. 11–12].

**SW-LO4 · A new algorithm for state-constrained separated continuous linear programs, Luo and Bertsimas (SICON 1998) [S156] · M1 variant, M4 certificate {LO SD}.**
- *Forced by*: much progress in finite-dimensional LP, little for infinite-dimensional LP, which models manufacturing and communication systems [S156 p. 1]; his 1994 survey listed an efficient continuous-LP algorithm as open [S580 p. 48] (link [inferred]).
- *Key insight*: for a fixed time partition the problem is an LP; a change of variables makes the breakpoints decision variables at the price of a nonconvex QP, of which KKT points suffice, because an LP dual discretization certifies global optimality [S156 pp. 8–10, 17].
- *Minimum evidence*: finite convergence, and "We prove algorithmically the absence of a duality gap for this class of problems without any boundedness assumptions on the solution set." [S156 p. 1]; each iteration row prints the dual bound beside the primal value (5963.27 against 5963.67 after three iterations) [S156 p. 32].
- *Reception*: 100 citations; by 2000 his group pairs such fluid-control policies with SDP lower bounds [S007 p. 18].
- *Limits printed*: time almost exponential in the accuracy; "It is our experience that (SCSCLP) is easier to approximate than to solve exactly." [S156 p. 32]; piecewise-linear or piecewise-constant data (Assumption 1) [S156 p. 4]; a bounded solution set and E = I for the structure theorem [S156 p. 29]. Not printed (the card's reading): no rival algorithm run, one random instance family, no rate bound [S156 pp. 30–32].

**SW-LO5 · The traffic flow management rerouting problem in air traffic control: a dynamic network flow approach, with Stock Patterson (Transp. Sci. 2000) [S043] · M1 variant, M7, M4 variant {LO IO AP}**, his most cited LO-primary paper (360 citations).
- *Forced by*: weather-driven rerouting runs through an iterative human process with no formal model, a problem the authors find unaddressed in the literature [S043 pp. 1–3].
- *Key insight*: a time-expanded multicommodity network, flights aggregated into commodity flows, binaries only for continued flights [S043 pp. 3–5]; relax sector capacities so subproblems are network-flow LPs, decompose fractional flows into paths by randomized rounding, pick paths with a packing IP, stop at an ε gap to the LP bound; the Lagrangian sweep only diversifies paths [S043 pp. 7–9].
- *Minimum evidence*: FAA-based instances with simulated capacities; gaps of 0.4%, 1.2% and 0.92% to the LP bound in 330 s, 116 s and 169 s on a Sun Sparc 20 [S043 pp. 10, 12, 14–15].
- *Reception*: no critique on file; "The idea of extracting solutions using randomization and combining these solutions using integer programming may be useful in other problems as well." [S043 p. 16].
- *Limits*: deterministic capacities though weather motivates the problem; no comparison with the command center's process [S043 pp. 4, 9–15].

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

**Picks**
- A problem another tradition handles without LP, then the LP that answers it: stochastic scheduling through conservation laws [S057 pp. 1, 3]; rerouting [S043 pp. 1–3]; inverting market outcomes [S194 p. 1]; convex-hull prices [S196 pp. 1–2].
- Within LP theory, whether a known bound is real [S289 p. 2], and speed-ups on "problems already known to be solvable in polynomial time" [S269 p. 2].
- Classes enlarged by what applications need, old results as special cases [S156 pp. 2, 4]; structure kept: aggregate, then disaggregate where coupling demands it [S043 pp. 4–5]; units' MILP models used unchanged [S196 pp. 4, 8].
- Admitted tractability assumptions: deterministic capacities [S043 p. 4]; piecewise-linear data [S156 p. 4]; convex market clearing [S194 p. 3]; one commodity, a cardinality budget [S191 p. 5]; fixed continuous recourse, polyhedral sets [S138 p. 3].
- Baselines: a bound (the LP relaxation [S043 p. 7]; the best known operation counts [S269 p. 21]); in several computing works also an incumbent: CPLEX on the compact MIP [S004 p. 18], greedy [S051 p. 10], maximum entropy [S359 p. 6], the practice approximation and a tuned subgradient method [S196 p. 9].

**Refuses**
- In the textbook (joint): the full simplex tableau beyond numerical examples, the standard form for concepts, encyclopedic coverage, network-flow refinements for better complexity estimates [B001 p. 1].
- In the complexity results of 1994–2003: rivals and devices without polynomial running time are left out of the worst-case comparisons (Plotkin–Shmoys–Tardos [S269 p. 13]; the parametric simplex [S004 p. 13]); the same years' computations use non-polynomial MIP solvers [S004 pp. 16, 18; S043 pp. 7–10].
- After 1998 [inferred from the cards]: LP-algorithm research and solver engineering; no card shows simplex or IPM code of his, and solvers serve as engines (LOQO, CPLEX, GLPK, Gurobi) [S156 p. 30; S043 p. 7; S153 p. 23; S138 p. 9].
- Absent from every card [inferred]: first-order or GPU LP methods, presolve, crossover.

[B005 p. 13]. The publisher quotes only praise [B002 p. 1]. No reply is known; the 68 errata items are local, none a wrong theorem by the card's reading, none on Ramana's points [inferred] [B003 pp. 1–5] (note 05, update row 18). Ye's 1998 review was located, not read; Bard's survives as an excerpt (note 05 §6 and its 2026-10-29 update).
- **Refinements by others**: two 2013–2014 papers cut the nominal solves of the 0-1 robust result (note 05 §4.2), not a refutation.
- **Limits the papers admit**, besides those in §2: no cancellations, fairness only sketched, parameters untuned [S043 pp. 5–7, 10]; "not effective" for non-convex markets, blind to non-marginal blocks [S194 pp. 3, 5]; no column-generation accelerations [S196 p. 10]; robust flows too conservative [S153 p. 23]; uniqueness claimed, not proved (card's reading) [S359 pp. 4, 10].
- **Headline before caveat (T6)**: the S156 abstract calls the algorithm effective on large realistic problems with several hundred continuous variables; the body's largest run has 250, with time almost exponential in accuracy [S156 pp. 1, 31–32].
- **Say–do on the evidence standard**: the 1994 "fastest" claim from operation counts [S269 pp. 1, 21–22] is what his later warning sign 2 rejects (C19); no paper of his re-measures it.
- **Failures and retractions**: none retracted (note 03 B5.2); the failed 1989 proof (note 02 F1).
