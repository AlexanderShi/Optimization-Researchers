## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (pack), the 2005 book's emphases and turning points.

**(a) Survivable networks and the parsimonious property, 1993, with the primal–dual and rounding papers** {IO LO} · M7, H10. *Forced by*: NP-hardness, which "Typically" sends researchers to approximate solutions [S052 p. 2], and an LP relaxation with no practical algorithm [S052 p. 4]. *Insight*: degree constraints leave the LP value unchanged; Held–Karp then gives the Steiner and k-connected LP values, and tree heuristics are bounded against the LP with tight families [S052 pp. 3, 5, 8–14, 20–21]. The sequels make inequality strength the ratio of a primal–dual algorithm [S214 pp. 3–4] and round all variables against one random threshold [S121 pp. 3–9]. *Evidence*: theorems and tight instances, no computation [S214 p. 4; S121 pp. 9–10]. *Reception*: 315 citations; generalizations by Agrawal–Klein–Ravi and Goemans–Williamson are credited in the paper [S052 pp. 18–19]; no critique was searched (note 05, Gaps). S052 has no rounding step (C2).

**(b) ATFM with en-route capacities, 1998, and its line** {IO AP} · M1, M6. *Forced by*: delay costs, a command center run on first-come, first-served holds and controllers' experience [S015 p. 1; S036 p. 1], and his own 1994 model, whose weaker relaxation needed rounding [S015 pp. 2, 7]. *Insight*: cumulative "by time t" binaries make the connectivity constraints facets, so the LP is almost always integral; the new LP bound dominates the old ones [S015 pp. 2, 4–5, 7, 10]. *Evidence*: real instances of 278 and 1,002 flights solved integral [S015 pp. 12–13]; in 2011, rerouting added by constraints only, with valid inequalities the solve needs [S037 pp. 2, 5–6, 13]. *Reception*: 883 citations, his most-cited primary IO work; an independent comparison, reported in his own survey, found its multi-airport special case best in most cases [S241 pp. 28–29]; the variables carry into fairness and scheduling models [S076 pp. 5, 35–36; S210 pp. 4, 9]. *Limits*: deterministic and static; decisions not scored against first-come, first-served [S015 pp. 11–13] until the 2012 replay of the FAA's two current rules [S114 p. 13].

**(c) Robust discrete optimization and network flows, 2003** {RO IO} · M1, M3. Anatomy in SKILL.md SW3; the IO part: with cost uncertainty in a 0–1 problem, n + 1 nominal solves suffice, so the robust version "retains the complexity of the nominal problem or its approximability guarantee" [S004 pp. 5–11, 22]; knapsack, sorting and shortest-path tests [S004 pp. 16–22]. *Reception*: 2,690 citations; others cut the number of nominal solves (note 05 §4.2); Burer's group challenged it on the TSP (§6); his own 2004 manuscript with Sim (MIT, no DOI) found that ellipsoidal sets with general covariance make the robust problem NP-hard, while identical variances keep n + 1 nominal solves [S167 pp. 4–6].

**(d) Exact first-choice product line optimization, 2019** {IO AP} · M1, M4. *Forced by*: heuristics dominate, and the closest exact work needed a week on a real instance [S093 pp. 2–3]. *Insight*: aggregate the preference constraints to keep n binaries, prove the formulation strongest by containment of relaxations, derive Benders cuts in closed form or from a greedy primal–dual pair [S093 pp. 6–13]. *Evidence*: average integrality gaps below 2% (cell averages up to 2.67%) against about 6%, 11% and 20% for the three rivals at n = 100 [S093 p. 17]; widths up to 10 proved optimal, 20 and 50 left at 1.74% and 3.09% [S093 p. 19]; code public [S093 p. 16]. *Limits*: a tighter rival formulation ran slower than a weaker one [S093 p. 19]; the hardware caveat sits in the body, not the abstract [S093 pp. 2, 20] (T6).

**(e) A unified approach to MIO with logical constraints, 2021** {IO CP} · M1, M4, H10. *Forced by*: big-M as the field's default; "we challenge this longstanding modeling practice" [S175 p. 1]. *Insight*: big-M is a regularizer; with v = Diag(z)x the inner dual makes f(z) convex under big-M or ridge, and ridge makes the dual solution unique, which removes cut degeneracy; single-tree outer approximation; perspective cuts reappear as OA cuts [S175 pp. 7–13, 16]. *Evidence*: better incumbents than CPLEX on 94% of 100 network-design instances, while CPLEX is faster on most facility-location instances [S175 pp. 18, 21]. *Reception*: 88 citations; it unifies the group's earlier sparse line, whose 2018 sparse-portfolio paper already dualized the inner problem inside one tree [S725 pp. 8–12] and whose journal version cites S175 for the general setting [S136 pp. 6, 17]; carried into stochastic network design [S347 pp. 9, 33]; no critique on file.

## 3. How he works here

IO is where most of his methods run as engines; it also shows several in textbook form.

- **M1 · Formulation first, kept in the class.** The formulation is designed so the IP behaves like an LP [S015 p. 2]; big-M links get problem-specific caps [S101 pp. 17–18]; robust 0–1 problems keep the nominal class [S004 pp. 5–7]; the regularizer is chosen for tractability [S175 p. 3]. In 1994 a commercial package, "rather than any custom-tailored algorithm" [S036 p. 7], though a custom branch-and-bound appears in 1995 [S108 p. 9] and its own Gröbner-basis code in 2000 [S213 p. 4]; from 2009 building the algorithm when the class fails recurs (C5): branch-and-bound on continuous variables [S040 pp. 2–6], custom pricing [S316 pp. 3–7], its own global solver with integers [S624 pp. 5–9]. *Say–do gap*: weak big-M in tree MIOs (T4, C10); a vaccine model left the class on a size count, with no timed attempt [S083 p. 15].
- **M2 · Quantified why now.** In IO the reason is a scale gap or a device: beyond 400 securities [S136 pp. 1, 4]; from about 400 to 10,000 targets [S316 pp. 2, 12]; 100-node designs "regarded as intractable" [S347 p. 2]; a week to minutes [S093 pp. 2, 20]. The solver × hardware figure is borrowed [S432 p. 2] or shown as separate LP tables [S561 pp. 10–12]. Earlier, the reverse: hardness answered by approximation [S052 p. 2], and CPLEX 6.0 losing his matched-quality test in 2002 [S070 pp. 13–15]. *Gap*: step 3 (rebuild the figure) is not practised here.
- **M3 · The price of X.** Fairness priced on a λ curve with a knee [S076 p. 27], the incumbent rule nested as one knob setting [S076 p. 17; S114 pp. 5, 11]; stability priced in blocking pairs against travel [S456 pp. 11–13]; the price of fixing variables bounded [S175 pp. 14–15]; a ridge priced at 1/(2γ) [S136 pp. 16–17]. *Gap*: applied IO prices with curves but rarely proves a solution-independent bound [S076 pp. 26–30], and the knob a decision-maker wants is traced only indirectly [S456 p. 11].
- **M4 · Warm start, exact solve, certificate, duel.** A heuristic warm start, limits and printed gaps [S093 pp. 16–19]; three duels with the lower bound beside the best value [S316 pp. 9–12]; one thread, failures printed [S136 pp. 24–29]; a matched-quality duel with CPLEX [S070 pp. 13–15]; exact solves auditing a heuristic [S108 p. 9; S558 p. 15; S397 pp. 19–21]. *Gap* (T1): no gaps reported [S040 pp. 11–15; S101 p. 10]; "optimal" without a certificate [S083 pp. 27, 29–30]; "Most of the MIOs did not solve to optimality" [S561 p. 29]; certificates of "secondary interest" in a paper that headlines proofs [S316 pp. 2, 7].
- **M5 · Judge by the decision.** GMO's thresholding rule rejected in its own terms and never run as a baseline [S101 p. 5]; the MIP then went live on 11 portfolios with 48.7% fewer names and 79.3% fewer tickets [S101 p. 13]; the FAA's two rules replayed on a levelled field [S114 pp. 12–17]; a baseline made stronger than practice [S304 p. 17]; a hospital's staffing rule [S416 pp. 13–14]. *Gaps*: the 1994–2011 ATFM models never scored against the FAA's current practice [S036 p. 7; S015 pp. 11–13; S109 p. 12; S037 pp. 14–15], which the 2012 paper above replays; a headline against the weakest constructed rule [S083 pp. 17–18, 23].
- **M6 · Programmes.** ATFM from 1994, begun with Odoni and carried on with and without him [S036 p. 13; S109 pp. 1–2; S076 p. 2]; approximation with Teo [S214 p. 11; S256 p. 1]; logical constraints passed from Van Parys to Cory-Wright and Pauphilet [S136 pp. 6–7]; a Lincoln Laboratory line from 2013 to 2025 [S558 p. 1; S907 p. 3; S316 p. 12].
- **M7 · Bound before policy.** Steps 1–4 are the field's standard here; his marks are tight families and grading against the same bound: tree heuristics against the LP value [S052 pp. 10–18, 20–21]; strength with a tightness theorem [S214 pp. 3–4]; a greedy bound shown tight [S108 pp. 5–8]; heuristics scored as deviation from the LP bound [S070 p. 9].
- **Heuristics.** H10: max-flow min-cut by rounding [S121 p. 3]; Roundy's 98% algorithm as a derandomization [S435 p. 3]; perspective cuts as OA cuts [S175 p. 16]; basis pursuit denoising as a perspective relaxation [S290 pp. 10–11]. H3: MIO as the move of a local search [S046 pp. 22–26; S137 p. 4]; an exact MIP as oracle in column generation [S304 pp. 10–15]; a small exact solve to certify a heuristic [S456 p. 15].

## 5. Critiques, failures and limits on record

- **Not searched**: note 05 checked no critique of his air-transportation or approximation-algorithm work and found no review of the 2005 book (note 05 §6, Gaps). Absence, not endorsement.
- **Robust discrete optimization**: the n + 1 nominal solves were cut by Álvarez-Miranda, Ljubić and Toth and by Lee and Kwon (note 05 §4.2); Poss's variable budget is less conservative for sparse solutions, and Büsing, Gersing and Koster note that Γ is set from arbitrary values (note 05 §4.1); Burer's group, §6.
- **Formulation strength (T4)**: Verwer–Zhang and Aghaei–Gómez–Vayanos showed the optimal-tree MIO grows with the data and has a weak relaxation; no reply was found (note 05 §2.3, §2.5, §2.8). His own papers name big-M's weakness [S040 p. 12] and instability [S175 p. 3] and drop it in the sparse line [S061 pp. 3, 6–10; S136 p. 6].
- **The speed-up figure** multiplies MILP solver factors by supercomputer peak speed and asserts the MIQO factor; no critic was found (note 05 §1.6); an IO paper borrows it [S432 p. 2].
- **The MIO engine's benchmarks**: Sarwar, Sauk and Sahinidis judged a timing comparison mismatched and found regularized L0Learn better and faster; Chen, Taeb and Bühlmann could not run the code (note 05 §1.4).
- **Limits the papers print**: IP times that "can become excessive" and a rounding heuristic poor at high cancellation costs [S036 pp. 8–10]; parametric and nonparametric ADP "not competitive" [S070 p. 1]; a speed-up whose key subroutine "has not been tested in practice" [S269 p. 22]; an algebraic algorithm without timings, faster only when infeasible or with few solutions [S213 p. 10]; a scheduling framework "far off from optimality" on job shops [S210 p. 13]; the 2009 branch-and-bound narrowed to good solutions found fast [S040 p. 16], then replaced [S010 pp. 5–6]; SCIP conceded as the leader [S624 pp. 1, 18]; plans not adopted [S137 p. 2; S243 p. 22].
- **Data**: no partner data in several studies, synthetic instances instead [S037 pp. 9, 15; S316 pp. 9, 12; S456 pp. 10, 15].
- **Retractions**: none in IO on the cards; the 1989 failed proof concerns an unnamed algorithm, LO or IO (note 01 §3).
- **Headline vs body (T6)**: "less than 15 minutes" at US scale beside a stated national mean of 987 s [S109 pp. 1, 11]; "certifiable optimality" beside printed gaps up to about 40% [S347 pp. 3, 7, 24].

## 6. Cross-team interfaces

*In print* below was checked by searching the guests' cards for his name and his cards for theirs.

### MINLP Team (minlp-team)

- **Grossmann · big-M, hull or a regularizer (complement).** *His*: big-M acts as a regularizer; prefer ridge or perspective forms when the objective has a strongly convex term [S175 pp. 1, 8–11, 23–24]. A ridge extracted from the objective's own quadratic term (1/γ = λ_min(Q), or a diagonal extraction) leaves the objective unchanged [S175 p. 8]; only an added ridge changes it [S175 p. 11]; under ridge the OA cuts are the perspective cuts [S175 p. 16]; earlier, cap big-M by problem knowledge [S101 pp. 17–18]. *Grossmann*: write the logic as GDP, derive big-M and hull mechanically, the hull in perspective form, measure bound against size with solver cuts and presolve off, push back on unbenchmarked big-M (ignacio-e-grossmann SKILL.md, "Method 1 · Model the logic before the equations", steps 3–4; Roundtable Card). *In print*: one way; his group runs Duran–Grossmann OA [S136 pp. 3, 14] in Quesada–Grossmann's single-tree form [S136 pp. 10, 13], which it credits by name [S400 p. 6]. *Contested*: an added ridge (change the objective) or a derived hull (change the formulation). *Test*: GDPlib and MINLPLib on/off instances with a strongly convex term, in two arms. (i) Ridge extracted from the objective: big-M, hull or perspective MISOCP, and ridge single-tree OA, by root bound (cuts and presolve off), size and time to a 1% gap on the true objective. (ii) Added ridge: incumbents re-scored on the unregularized cost at equal times, gaps taken against a bound on the unregularized problem, never compared across formulations [S175 pp. 17–18].
- **Leyffer · perspective forms for sparse models (agreement).** *Leyffer*: perspective constraints broke solvers at z = 0, where Slater's condition fails; preprocessing to w_j = 0 would fix this, but the solvers did not do it, so Guan, Gray and Leyffer fell back on big-M [sven-leyffer card S051 p. 3]. A later paper relaxed the constraints as rotated second-order cones with a cardinality budget, z relaxed to [0, 1] and integral at an optimum with ‖w‖₀ ≤ r, with neither big-M nor branching [sven-leyffer card S142 preprint pp. 4–6]. *His*: perspective cuts are OA cuts under ridge, each subproblem solved on the support only [S175 pp. 10, 16; S136 p. 13], which performs the preprocessing the 2009 paper named as the missing fix [sven-leyffer card S051 p. 3]; the group's sparse SVM runs ridge single-tree OA on the hinge loss [S170 pp. 4, 8–13]; basis pursuit denoising is the perspective relaxation [S290 pp. 10–11]. *In print*: one way; his group cites Fletcher–Leyffer for OA's finite convergence [S421 pp. 10–12; S725 p. 12]. *Contested* [inferred]: exact single-tree OA with a ridge, or one cardinality-budget SOC relaxation without branching. *Test*: the UCI sets of both Leyffer papers [sven-leyffer card S051 p. 4; sven-leyffer card S142 preprint p. 8]; ridge single-tree OA, CQ-SSVM (one SOCP) and the 2009 big-M MIQP, by share of integral z, support size, out-of-sample accuracy and time.
- **Sahinidis · global solver benchmarks (exchange in print).** *His*: the RPT-based branch-and-bound with integers is duelled against BARON (2021 release, through Pyomo) and SCIP on the group's own families; it concedes SCIP's lead and calls BARON limited on harder mixed-integer instances [S624 pp. 1, 10, 18]. On a public library, Sahinidis's yardstick, BARON did better: on 77 MINLPLib instances against the same BARON release, BARON solved 73, and the group's learned-surrogate method beat it on gap in 4 and on time in 3 [S266 pp. 24–25]. *Sahinidis*: benchmark whole public libraries against certified optima at defaults, end with a portfolio, count a relaxation's strength only at speed (nikolaos-v-sahinidis SKILL.md, "Method 4 · Enter with a benchmark; judge by certified ground truth and portfolios"; "Method 5 · A relaxation's strength counts only at speed"). *In print*: both ways; S624 cites BARON [S624 p. 2] and S266 cites BARON and ANTIGONE [S266 pp. 2–3]; in 2020 Sahinidis's group questioned the timing comparison of his sparse-regression paper, and his group's rejoinder acknowledged them without answering that point (note 05 §1.4; nikolaos-v-sahinidis note 05). *Contested*: what his wins on his own families, such as the dike problems [S624 p. 16], show when the public library favoured BARON [S266 p. 25]. *Test*: RPT-BB, RPT-SDP-BB, current BARON and SCIP at defaults on MINLPLib and QPLIB instances with integers, one thread, one limit; solved counts, gaps, failures, a portfolio verdict.
- **Misener · between big-M and the hull (complement).** *Misener*: the hull is large and numerically harder [ruth-misener card S046 p. 2], so P-split formulations keep the objective and climb from big-M (the 1-split is big-M with the smallest valid M) toward the hull, reaching it only for affine constraints over a box and never for fully disjoint, strictly convex disjunctions [ruth-misener card S046 pp. 6–8, 11–13]. They are built for many variables and few constraints per disjunct, and without that structure are "inadvisable" [ruth-misener card S046 pp. 3, 10]. *His*: replace the big-M box by a ridge term [S175 pp. 9–11]; a ridge extracted from an existing quadratic term leaves the objective unchanged [S175 p. 8], and only an added ridge needs re-scoring on the unregularized cost [S175 pp. 11, 17–18]. *In print*: none on this topic; elsewhere her robust pooling paper cites S088 [ruth-misener card S048 p. 7] (next item) and his S266 cites ANTIGONE [S266 pp. 2–3]. *Test*: her clustering, P_ball or ReLU-network instances, which have that structure [ruth-misener card S046 pp. 3, 20], with a convex quadratic objective term added and scaled as in his unit-commitment runs [S175 p. 22]; big-M, P-split (P = 2, 4) and ridge-OA with the ridge extracted from that term, by nodes and time on the same objective.
- **Misener · reformulation or cutting planes for robust models (exchange in print; agreement except on ellipsoidal sets).** *His*: no significant winner for robust MIO; run both, take the first to finish [S088 pp. 14, 22], a hybrid computed as the minimum of separate single-thread runs [S088 p. 20]; for robust LO with ellipsoids the reformulation won [S088 p. 13]. *Misener*: her robust pooling paper takes the question from that study and splits the verdict: cutting planes solve more, reformulation keeps intermediate solutions robustly feasible [ruth-misener card S048 pp. 7, 15, 19]; on her nonconvex pooling models the ellipsoidal reformulation did poorly [ruth-misener card S048 pp. 17–18]. *Contested*: the default when the global solver has no lazy callback. *Test*: her 14 instances × 3 geometries; charge the hybrid the CPU of both runs, or give each method half the limit, and compare it with cutting planes alone at the full limit; report share solved and the geometric mean of time ratios as in S088 [S088 pp. 10–11], with time-outs charged at the limit, plus the share of time-limited runs that hold a robustly feasible incumbent, her criterion [ruth-misener card S048 p. 19]; ellipsoidal sets shown apart.
- **Burer · robust TSP with interval costs (exchange in print; conflict on comparative statics, near-agreement on tours).** *His*: a Γ budget keeps the nominal class with n + 1 nominal solves [S004 pp. 5–10, 22]. *Burer*: one TSP with costs (1 − γ)l + γu has monotone comparative statics, which a 5-node counterexample shows the budget model lacks; the budget model took up to about 33,000 s at n = 175 [samuel-burer card S053 pp. 12–19, 21]. On the 72 standard instances the modified model's tours are mostly within 2.13% under the other models' objectives, it is offered as a quick heuristic for them, and γ and Γ are called not directly comparable [samuel-burer card S053 pp. 20, 22–23]; its worst-case win comes on constructed instances its authors call somewhat ad hoc [samuel-burer card S053 pp. 23–24]. *Contested*: focused (Γ) or balanced (γ) conservatism, and the cost of |E| + 1 solves. *Test*: Montemanni's 72 instances [samuel-burer card S053 pp. 19–20], with the printed cross-scores taken as given [samuel-burer card S053 p. 22]; the deciding part is simulated travel times, as S004 scored sorting [S004 pp. 18–19], and the solve counts of both models beside the reduced counts (note 05 §4.2).
- **Burer · bounds on random 0–1 problems (exchange in print).** *His*: a tight bound over all dependence structures with given marginal moments, one univariate SDP per variable [S169 pp. 3, 6–12]. *Burer*: moment-based sets ("Bertsimas et al." on the card) do not tighten as samples grow; a Wasserstein ball with a copositive reformulation does, unless the SDP relaxation is loose [samuel-burer card S044 pp. 3, 17, 24]. *Test*: Burer's three instances at growing sample sizes, both bounds, coverage on fresh draws.
- **Dey · the RPT relaxation ladder (exchange in print; complement).** *Dey*: RPT-derived inequalities, credited to Zhen, de Moor and den Hertog and to Bertsimas et al. [santanu-s-dey card S129 p. 18], form one rung of a ranked ladder; against BARON's optima for n ≤ 10 they reach 959–960 exact of 1,000 at about 121–129 s and are "not advisable" in higher dimensions [santanu-s-dey card S129 pp. 20, 23]. *His*: the LMI-strengthened variant is the main lever on dike problems (7 of 9 proved against 1 of 9), with no convergence proof [S624 pp. 8–9, 16]. *Contested*: where heavier rungs stop paying. *Test*: Dey's protocol (exact count, cumulative gap and time per rung) on S624's families, with and without the LMI, counting exact solves only where some solver certifies the optimum, or on small n as Dey did [santanu-s-dey card S129 p. 20]; several S624 instances have no certified optimum from any method [S624 pp. 11–13, 16], so report those as the gap to the best bound found, with current BARON and SCIP releases as certifiers.
- **Dey · cumulative binaries; strength is not speed (agreement).** *Dey*: nested cumulative binaries make an assignment block TU, and polyhedral comparisons do not settle solve time; the paper cites Bertsimas–Shioda on another point [santanu-s-dey card S124 pp. 2, 7–8, 10–11, 13]. *His*: "by time t" binaries made the ATFM relaxations strong [S015 pp. 4–5, 10], and a tighter product-line formulation ran slower [S093 p. 19]. *Test*: S093's product-line instances with formulations (3)–(5) and the new one [S093 pp. 17–19], and S015's MAGHP instances rerun with the 1994 and 1998 formulations, whose comparison used published numbers [S015 pp. 7, 11–12]; rank by LP bound, run under one limit, report win counts and cumulative time with time-outs charged at the limit [santanu-s-dey card S124 pp. 15–16].
