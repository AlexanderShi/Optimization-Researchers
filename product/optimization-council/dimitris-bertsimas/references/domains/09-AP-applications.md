## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (pack counts), his emphasis and turning points. SKILL.md's anatomies are not repeated; its dynamic-routing anatomy (SW6, labelled {SD AP} in SKILL.md) rests on S016 and S032, which the packs tag SD only.

**AP-SW1 · Air traffic flow management as strong 0-1 models, 1994–2016** (S036; S015, 883 citations; S037; S114; S076) · M1, M5, M6
- *Forced by*: ground holds by first-come-first-served and reroutes by controllers' experience, while models ignored en-route sector capacities [S015 pp. 1–2, 9].
- *Key insight*: "flight f has reached sector j by time t" variables make connectivity constraints facets, so the LP relaxation was almost always integral [S015 pp. 2, 4–5, 10]: "the key to solving large scale integer programming problems is to obtain strong formulations, which include facets of the convex hull of solutions" [S015 p. 2].
- *Minimum evidence*: real instances up to 1,002 flights solved integral, the larger in 29,920 s [S015 pp. 12–13]; in 2011 national instances (6,475 flights) reach a 1% gap in 743 s on average [S037 p. 13]; in "a few tests" without the valid inequalities (listed with the regional runs), no acceptable solution came within 3,600 s [S037 p. 13].
- *Reception and turn*: fairness entered by generalizing the FAA's own standard; RBS-EP adds no variables, runs under 10 s and cuts delay 4.0–4.5% against exemption RBS on historical days [S114 pp. 9–11, 17–18]. No field test is reported; the 2011 working-paper version of the 2016 paper opens on "the research has not been fully implemented in practice" [S076 p. 1].

**AP-SW2 · Optimal control of execution costs, 1998** (S009, 1,606 citations) · Heuristic 1, M5
- Anatomy in the [ledger](../research/09-evidence-ledger.md#anatomy-execution-costs): the practitioners' equal split is optimal "only in one very special case" (linear permanent impact, arithmetic random walk) [S009 pp. 8–9, 43], and again at θ = 1 under multiplicative impact with no-sales constraints [S009 pp. 36–37]; savings in cents per share against it, in simulation only [S009 pp. 13–25]. The finance line keeps the pattern: practice as some model's exact solution (Black–Litterman as inverse optimization [S086 pp. 1, 3, 5–9]) and a deployment write-up (GMO's portfolio MIP, adopted after "extensive historical simulations" [S101 p. 10]).

**AP-SW3 · From the price of fairness to national organ allocation, 2011–2025** (S012, 1,063 citations; S026; S044; S094; S259; S476) · M3, M5, M6
- *Forced by*: "the vast majority of these proposals remain unimplemented. The ostensible reason for this is fairness" [S012 p. 1].
- *Key insight*: lost efficiency bounded by a function of the number of players only [S012 pp. 7, 11–14]; the α-fair family nests the utilitarian, proportional and max-min rules, so one knob spans the debate [S026 pp. 2, 6–10]; for kidneys, the fairness duals of a hindsight LP become a point system of the committee's own form [S044 preprint pp. 14–16].
- *Minimum evidence*: 8.2% more life-years from transplant out of sample, in the policymakers' simulator [S044 preprint pp. 18–22]; OPOM in the regulator's simulator, about 418 fewer waitlist deaths a year [S094 p. 5].
- *Turning point*: lung weights chosen with the OPTN committee by a sample–surrogate–MILP framework became national policy on 9 March 2023, projected at about 20% fewer waitlist deaths (SRTR: about 40%) [S259 pp. 6–10, 12]; for kidneys: "KPSAM 2019 required 6 h and 42 min. MITSAM took 14.8 s, >1600 times faster." [S476 p. 6]. Limits: §5.

**AP-SW4 · From claims data to clinical decision tools, 2008–2026** (S050; S078; S079; S041; S094) · M5, Heuristic 6, M6
- *Forced by*: claims-based prediction mostly unvalidated on unseen populations and judged by R² [S050 pp. 1–2]; data and two co-authors from a company, D2Hawkeye [S050 pp. 1, 10].
- *Key insight and evidence*: off-the-shelf learners, intervention-aligned error measures and a strong naive baseline: "this baseline is much stronger than, for example, random assignment." [S050 p. 5]; absolute error down over 16% overall and 50–58% in the top cost bucket [S050 p. 8].
- *Turns*: build the missing asset ("such a database did not exist before this study" [S078 p. 3]) and design regimens by MIO that merit a trial [S078 p. 15]; a kNN rule for diabetes, counterfactuals only estimated [S079 pp. 4, 7]; from 2018 optimal-tree calculators (POTTER [S041]; validations [S179; S226; S242], all abstract-level).

**AP-SW5 · Boston school buses and bell times, 2017–2020** (S137; S262) · M5, M3, Heuristics 3, 4
- *Forced by*: routes drafted by hand in about 3,000 people-hours, so no policy could be costed [S262 p. 4].
- *Key insight*: the BiRD decomposition; start times as a GQAP with a routing proxy, solved by local improvement whose moves are MIO solves over random subsets of schools [S137 pp. 2–4].
- *Minimum evidence*: routing deployed in 2017, 50 buses (7%) fewer after the district added buses back, an estimated $5 million [S262 p. 8].
- *Turning point*: a priced curve shown to the district [S137 p. 5]: "Each additional point of community satisfaction in Boston costs dozens of additional buses and tens of millions of taxpayer dollars." [S262 p. 10]. The bell times were not adopted: "These start times have not been implemented yet due to parents concern with the magnitude of the change" [S137 p. 2]; the group's post-mortem names missing school-level engagement [S262 pp. 10–12].

**AP-SW6 · Hartford HealthCare and hospital operations, 2019–2026** (S115; S617; S416) · M5, M6
- *Forced by*: partners' bed and discharge decisions: "Our objective, however, is not to predict for the sake of prediction but, more importantly, to implement our innovations in hospitals for real-world impact." [S617 arXiv p. 7].
- *Key insight*: an expert-defined patient-day state fed to standard learners; the owner chose single trees "for they did not view the gain in accuracy worth the loss in interpretability" [S115 preprint p. 18]; at Hartford, optimal trees first, then SHAP-explained XGBoost, a physician-champion pilot and a staggered roll-out [S617 arXiv pp. 9–14].
- *Minimum evidence*: forecasts beat resource nurses' estimates (median relative error 11.5% against 16.0%) [S115 preprint pp. 21–22]; off-service placements down about 4 points by difference-in-differences, boarding delay not significant [S115 preprint pp. 22–24]; length of stay down 0.67 days, without a standard error [S617 arXiv pp. 23–25]; ED nurse scheduling in full use since April 2023 [S416 p. 18]. The skill's correction C4 rests here.

## 3. How he works here

- **M1 · Formulation kept in its class.** Strong 0-1 models (AP-SW1); devices that add no variables: fairness in objective coefficients [S114 pp. 9–11], routes as poset constraints [S037 pp. 2–5], an energy LP kept an LP by aggregating constraints before robustifying [S307 pp. 20–23]. ⚠ *Say–do gaps*: the 2025 kidney design simulates and filters, with no formulation [S476 p. 5]; DELPHI is a nonconvex calibration without certificate [S103 preprint pp. 8–9]; the diabetes rule has no optimization model [S079 pp. 3–5].
- **M2 · Why now.** A named data or industry change, not a solver figure [S074 pp. 1, 3; S046 pp. 1–2, 4, 6]; once a measured simulator speed-up [S476 p. 6]. In 1996 the argument ran the other way: "A priori optimization in VRPs is an attractive policy in the absence of intensive computational power." [S025 p. 17].

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

**Picks**
- An owner with a recurring decision, data, and a current rule that is first-come-first-served, hand-made or trial and error: ground holds [S015 pp. 1–2], hand-drafted routes [S262 p. 4], committee trial and error [S259 p. 5], a hand-made radiology schedule [S245 pp. 9, 13], siloed planning [S339 pp. 9–11].
- The strongest naive baseline, not random [S050 p. 5]; the industry's own fairness standard, extended rather than replaced [S114 p. 5; S044 preprint p. 6]; at one hospital "fairness is a non-negotiable business principle" [S245 p. 5].
- Interpretability where guidance changes treatment, black boxes tolerated for diagnosis [S685 pp. 2–3]; ⚠ his later hospital tools are SHAP-explained boosted trees (Hartford, after an optimal-tree stage [S617 arXiv pp. 9–11]; the COVID-19 calculator, in prospective validation [S054 pp. 4–10]), while the BIDMC flow tool ran single optimal trees chosen by the owner [S115 preprint pp. 18, 21, 25] (C21).
- Optimized assignment over randomization in small preclinical experiments [S124 pp. 2, 4–6].

**Declines**
- Data-calibrated fairness knobs for multi-party decisions: "the worst-case analysis is an instance-free approach, better suited for decisions in such an environment." [S026 p. 12].
- Price dynamics where moments suffice: model-free option bounds [S056 pp. 2–5] [inferred as a choice].
- Demand that reacts to the policy: "We will thus make the simplifying assumption that the demand process is independent of the control policy." [S034 p. 3].
- Optimization where it does not pay: "In this situation, optimization is not extremely useful and we recommend using greedy, which is the fastest and the most interpretable." [S046 p. 19].
- Claims beyond the data: no recommendations from predictive tools [S115 preprint p. 21]; "A fleet-wide result based on simplified assumptions would not be informative." [D022 p. 6].

## 5. Critiques, failures and limits on record

- **Boston** (C12). Goodman (2019) judged the plan a failure of political process in which families could neither see nor contest the trade-offs (note 05 §5.1). The PNAS paper says both "not implemented yet" and "delayed" [S137 pp. 2, 6]; the group's 2020 account names missing engagement [S262 pp. 10–12]; a 2022 course-scheduling paper reports non-adoption plainly [S243 pp. 17–28].
- **DELPHI.** The US Forecast Hub scored its mortality forecasts at the naive baseline (relative WIS 0.99) [S021 pp. 2, 4]; a German–Polish hub scored it worse than the naive baseline for cases and one-week deaths [S157 pp. 15–16]. His group's comparison uses its own metric and window [S103 preprint pp. 14–16], and the 2021 paper reads its MAPE backtest as "suggestive of excellent out-of-sample performance" [S099 p. 7]. No reply found. Small areas are disclaimed, not withheld: "not designed to perform well on areas with small populations", with eight few-death areas printed at death MAPEs of 44–193% [S103 preprint pp. 13–14].
- **Vaccine sites.** Headline against the weakest benchmark [S083 pp. 1, 17–18, 23]; "the full plan was not implemented" [S083 p. 25].
- **Execution costs.** Limits stated in the body: the §2 dynamics under which the naive rule is optimal are called illustrative and implausible, with negative prices, and replaced by a temporary-impact law [S009 pp. 7, 16–22] (C18); real-data estimation deferred to a companion paper [S009 pp. 6, 20, 42–43]; a limits section covers order type, parameter error, risk neutrality and partial equilibrium [S009 pp. 37–42]; the rival Almgren–Chriss text was not read (note 05 §5.3).
- **Fairness.** The only critique found is scope, a single decision period (Lodi et al., note 05 §6); his own: the worst case can be conservative on an instance [S026 pp. 15–16]. Robust inventory (AP-tagged RO): Park (2014) judged the 2006 formulation incorrect for its stated set; no reply (note 05 §4.3) [S014].
- **Organ allocation.** Fairness only approximately met out of sample [S044 preprint pp. 11, 22]; simulator overestimation [S094 p. 9; S230 p. 7]; projections, not outcomes [S259 pp. 11–12]; nonuse unseen at fixed volume [S476 p. 12].
- **Hospital operations.** The 4-point off-service headline against a within-period median effect of 0.00 [S115 preprint pp. 23–24]; 0.67 days without a standard error, and a pilot without controls [S617 arXiv pp. 23–25, 38]; his public length-of-stay figures differ (note 02 C6).
- **Unit commitment.** The inner worst case is solved by outer approximation to a local optimum only [S006 p. 4], while the abstract claims robustness against all realizations [S006 p. 1] (Tension T1).
- **Clinical tools.** Optimal-tree calculators inherit the OCT critiques (note 05 §2) and are called optimal without certificates (C9); announced regimen and diabetes trials have no reported outcomes (note 06 §7); he calls convincing physicians a long struggle (note 02 E7).

### 6.2 NLP Team

**Curtis, Wächter · Power-system operations and markets** (complement)
- *His*: adaptive robust unit commitment with the operator's network and transmission-security constraints and data, the dispatch an LP, Benders plus OA [S006 pp. 1–5, 9]; the power-flow model is not named on the card; convex-hull prices by column generation [S196 pp. 4–5].
- *Theirs*: Curtis clears multi-period markets as an implicit complementarity problem by semismooth Newton on the convex DC model, called better posed than Motto et al.'s nonconvex AC model, with unit commitment via convex hulls and AC power flow left to future work [frank-e-curtis card S071 pp. 5–6, 12–15, 22]; with Wächter and others he decomposes security-constrained AC optimal power flow, since operators fall back on DC approximations that lose voltage magnitudes and reactive power [frank-e-curtis card S061 p. 2]; Wächter decomposes AC optimal power flow with barrier-smoothed second stages (andreas-wachter SKILL.md, Signature Work Anatomy, two-stage AC-OPF decomposition).
- *Relation*: contrast; no citation either way; S071's DC model, like S006's dispatch LP, is linear. *Contested*: whether commitments chosen on linear security constraints [inferred for S006] stay feasible and cheap under AC physics. *Test*: the operator's data are not on the cards, so on a public AC case (for example a PGLib or MATPOWER system) commit by S006's robust model and by the reserve rule, dispatch both by AC optimal power flow, and compare violations and cost.

**Curtis · Hospital scheduling under uncertainty and equity** (two contrasts)
- *His*: ED staffing as a robust MILP over a data-calibrated set with a penalty on changes from the current schedule, deployed [S416 pp. 8–9, 18]; radiology schedules by a maximin with small multipliers on the totals, the price of fairness called "a moot point" because fairness is a business rule, gain projected [S245 pp. 5, 9, 13].
- *Theirs*, from two Lehigh-group papers (Tsang, Shehadeh, Curtis et al.; Curtis with Li, Shehadeh and Hochman), in which Curtis's share is not identified on the cards [frank-e-curtis card S034 p. 52; frank-e-curtis card S073 p. 39]: (a) operating rooms by stochastic and mean-support distributionally robust models, RO called overly conservative, judged out of sample under misspecified distributions [frank-e-curtis card S034 pp. 2, 10, 19–20, 43–47]; (b) resident rotations by a deterministic equity IP with an exact Pareto search and a price of equity from 0 to 0.44, uncertainty left to future work, adoption not reported [frank-e-curtis card S073 pp. 10–14, 17–18, 21–22].
- *Relation*: contrast; no citation found. *Contested*: (a) RO with a change penalty against mean-support DRO; (b) one maximin schedule against the whole front. *Test*: (a) both models on one hospital's later months, scored out of sample under misspecified demand as S034 does, on cost, shortfall and changes managers accept; (b) trace the revenue–mismatch front for S245's case and check whether its schedule lies on it.

**Curtis, Robinson · Fairness as hard constraint or priced knob** (complement)

- *Theirs*: hard constraints on bounded nonconvex surrogates so a stated tolerance is met, instead of tuned regularization [frank-e-curtis card S083 pp. 2–3, 8–9]; Robinson's Heuristic 10, hard constraints for tolerances (daniel-p-robinson SKILL.md, Research Heuristics 10).

**Nocedal (with Wright) · Calibrating DELPHI** (exchange in print)
- *His*: twelve parameters per area fitted daily by "the local truncated newton algorithm (Nocedal and Wright 2006)", weekly by dual annealing, without certificate [S103 preprint pp. 8–9].
- *Nocedal's*: for noisy functions, keep (L-)BFGS, with a difference interval set from an estimated noise level, a line search relaxed by twice that level and a recovery procedure [jorge-nocedal card S038 pp. 6–11]; among his first questions is the error level in values and derivatives, and whether the algorithm knows it (jorge-nocedal SKILL.md, Roundtable Card).
- *Relation*: one way, S103 cites the Nocedal–Wright textbook; the link to S038 is [inferred], since the card does not say whether DELPHI's gradients were differenced or how noisy its objective is [S103 preprint pp. 8–9]. *Contested*: whether the fit, not only the SEIR structure, explains forecasts at the naive baseline [S021 pp. 2, 4]. *Test*: first estimate the objective's noise level (ECnoise [jorge-nocedal card S038 pp. 7–8], Nocedal's third question); then refit DELPHI with a noise-aware quasi-Newton method and with its two tracks; compare residuals, parameter stability and WIS on archived data vintages (pseudo-prospective).

**Nocedal · Option pricing** (complement)
