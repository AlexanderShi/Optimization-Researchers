## 2. Signature works

**SW-DA1 · From predictive to prescriptive analytics** (with Kallus; Management Science 2020, read as arXiv v4) [S008] · Methods 5, 1
- *Forced by*: OR/MS data are mostly auxiliary covariates; SAA uses only the uncertain quantity, and a plugged-in point prediction ignores residual uncertainty [S008 pp. 1–2, 6–7].
- *Key insight*: read kNN, kernel, CART or forest predictors as weights over past observations and solve a reweighted SAA; tractability carries over, asymptotic optimality holds for kNN, kernel and local-linear weights, and P scores decisions as R² scores predictions [S008 pp. 10–16, 19, 21].
- *Minimum evidence*: a 150-week rolling backtest at a media distributor, P = 0.88 over just under 20,000 locations against SAA++ and perfect foresight [S008 pp. 30–31]; the applied write-up [S143 p. 4].
- *Reception*: 1,626 Scholar citations, six years from v1 to print (note 01 SW5); his group's bootstrap-robust follow-up [S139 pp. 3–4]; a survey cites its optimism with many covariates (note 05 §3).
- *Limits in the text*: for CART and forests, the methods of the case, "we do not have firm theoretical results" [S008 p. 19]; the baseline is a constructed data-poor rule, not the vendor's [S008 pp. 30–31].

**SW-DA2 · Optimal prescriptive trees** (with Dunn and Mundru; IJOO 2019) [S096] · Method 5, interpretability
- *Forced by*: personalization from observational data with missing counterfactuals, biased assignment and a need to see why [S096 pp. 2–3].
- *Key insight*: the leaves are the neighbourhoods that impute counterfactuals; a weight µ keeps a prediction term, because pure prescription error is optimistic out of sample [S096 pp. 6–7].
- *Minimum evidence*: four synthetic and four real settings [S096 pp. 9–20]; on diabetes and jobs, bracketed between current care and an oracle [S096 pp. 17–19].
- *Reception and limits*: Jo, Aghaei, Gómez and Vayanos built an example in which the objective favours the worst tree and found no code to replicate it; no reply was found (note 05 §2.6). The MIO "did not scale beyond shallow trees and small data sets" [S096 p. 7], so training is coordinate descent without a certificate; regress-and-compare beats every tree on warfarin, printed [S096 p. 17]. Later papers use Optimal Policy Trees (Amram, Dunn, Zhuo; not his [H005]) instead [S308 p. 7; S688 pp. 6–8].

**SW-DA3 · Learning the optimizer**: *The voice of optimization* (with Stellato; Machine Learning 2021) [S112] and *Online mixed-integer optimization in milliseconds* (IJOC 2022) [S092] · Heuristic 5
- *Forced by*: practitioners re-solve similar instances and treat solvers as black boxes; online control needs milliseconds on limited hardware [S112 p. 2; S092 p. 2].
- *Key insight*: "Instead, our approach encodes the optimal solution with a small amount of information that we denote as strategy." [S112 p. 2] Tight constraints plus integer values are learned as classes (trees for a voice, networks for speed); online, each candidate costs one KKT solve, its factorization cached when the parameter leaves the matrices fixed [S112 pp. 10, 14; S092 pp. 17–18]; a Good–Turing stopping rule governs sampling (Theorem 3.1's bound in the algorithm, the point estimate in MLOPT) [S112 pp. 19–21; S092 pp. 12–13].
- *Minimum evidence*: generated benchmarks; speed-ups up to 2,201× (facility location) and 6,846× (hybrid control), no significant one for portfolios, Gurobi faster on small transportation LPs [S112 pp. 24–31]; maximum times of 0.0096–0.0130 s against 0.0089–114.81 s for Gurobi on fuel-cell control [S092 p. 25].
- *Reception*: CoCo carries it into robotics [S204 pp. 3–7]; a prescriptive tree replaces the classifier [S308 p. 7]; the abstract's claims narrowed from v1 to v3 (note 03 B5.4), while the v3 body keeps "reliable" and "any" [S112 pp. 7, 32]; Ye's 2026 note doubts offline learning of LP bases (yinyu-ye note 01 §1.6).

**SW-DA4 · Bootstrap robust prescriptive analytics** (with Van Parys; Math. Program. 2022) [S139], and robustness against overfitting in multistage problems [S113; S150] · Methods 3, 1; Heuristic 1 as a variant [S113 p. 4; S150 pp. 3, 9, 24]; Heuristic 4 [S139 pp. 19–22]
- *Forced by*: his own method's failure: local-learner prescriptions overfit and turn optimistic with many covariates, with no built-in remedy [S139 pp. 3–4].
- *Key insight*: robustify the estimator over reweightings in a KL ball, the bootstrap's rate function, so bootstrap disappointment is at most exp(−nr) for kernels (no out-of-sample guarantee is claimed) and convexity is kept [S139 pp. 7–8, 12–16]; "the main practical advantage of our bootstrap distance function is that its size r can be calibrated in advance" [S139 p. 19].
- *Minimum evidence*: a synthetic newsvendor at one context; nominal methods disappoint about half the time; a near tie with Hanasusanto–Kuhn [S139 pp. 18–20].
- *Multistage sequel*: sample robust optimization "adds robustness to the historical data as a tool to avoid overfitting" [S113 p. 4]; with ML weights it removes the anticipativity of weighted SAA, and a 2×2 table makes SAA and two earlier methods of the group its special cases [S150 pp. 3, 22, 24].

**SW-DA5 · Mixed-integer optimization with constraint learning** (Maragno, Wiberg, Bertsimas et al.; Operations Research 2025) [S097], with the global line [S298; S266] · Methods 5, 1
- *Forced by*: outcomes with no formula (toxicity, palatability) but with data; earlier work fixed one learner or used predictions as inputs [S097 pp. 2, 4–5].
- *Key insight*: "we directly embed ML models rather than extracting predictions, allowing us to optimize our decisions over the model." [S097 p. 5] The data hull is a trust region; an ensemble violation limit guards against learning error [S097 pp. 13–17].
- *Minimum evidence*: without the hull, errors where the optimizer lands far exceed validation errors (CART 0.014 → 0.273), with it 0.059 [S097 p. 23]; prescribed chemotherapy regimens raise any-DLT satisfaction from 0.847 to 0.941 and predicted survival from 10.855 to 12.092 months, scored by a model ensemble [S097 pp. 32–33]. With an owner: simulator surrogates set the OPTN lung weights, national policy since 9 March 2023 [S259 pp. 6–10].
- *Global line*: learned trees or a menu of learners in an MIO, then local repair [S298 arXiv pp. 15–22; S266 pp. 7–8]; BARON solves 73 of 77 MINLPLib instances [S266 p. 25]; both papers concede no global guarantee [S298 arXiv p. 39; S266 p. 32].
- *Reception*: Burer–Zhu answer its enlarged hull [samuel-burer card S057 pp. 3, 6–7]; OMLT lists OptiCL [ruth-misener card S006 p. 4]; SKILL.md correction C13 moved this paper from Heuristic 5 to Method 5.

## 3. How he works here

- **M5, judge by the decision (the core).** Decisions are scored, not predictions: P [S008 p. 21]; a learned optimizer counts as accurate only when its point is feasible and near-optimal [S112 pp. 22–23; S092 pp. 22–23]; learners trained on decision cost [S308 p. 7]. The owner's rule is the baseline in the applied papers: ICE's decisions and the DHS written rule [S444 pp. 16–17], physicians' prescriptions [S370 pp. 13, 15–16], oncologists' phase III choices [S078 pp. 15–17], the WHO, FDA and EMA compositions [S407 pp. 11–12]; short of an owner, S906 re-tests an incumbent study's own estimators and segments (Besbes et al. 2010) [S906 pp. 31–32]. Missing counterfactuals get proxies with stated bias directions [S078 pp. 14–16] or a natural experiment [S444 pp. 13–19]. ⚠ *Say–do gap*: the methodological papers use synthetic data and score against SAA, the group's own methods, point predictions or published rivals (Hanasusanto–Kuhn and Ban–Rudin ERM [S139 pp. 19–22]), never against an owner's rule [S193 pp. 28–31; S133 p. 25; S688 pp. 9–10]; the flagship omits the vendor's rule [S008 pp. 30–31]; clinical prescriptions are scored on estimated counterfactuals [S457 pp. 8–10; S343 pp. 7–8].
- **M1, class kept.** Weights keep SAA's class [S008 p. 16]; the robust counterpart is convex whenever the nominal is [S139 pp. 8, 12–13]; shared dual variables make the multistage robust approximation an LP [S113 pp. 10–11]; one formulation solves in 40 s at n = 800 where the direct one does not finish in 2 h at n = 300 [S133 pp. 17–18]; norms 1, 2 or ∞ preserve the class [S097 p. 18]; a known strategy turns an MIQO into a linear system [S112 pp. 10, 14]. Boundaries printed: decision-dependent uncertainty is nonconvex [S008 p. 17]; the deterministic equivalent grows as O(N^T) [S193 p. 35]. ⚠ The global line approximates rather than reformulates [S298 arXiv pp. 3–4, 39].
- **M3, one knob.** A radius tied to bootstrap disappointment [S139 pp. 15–16, 19]; a trimming factor of 10–15% [S122 p. 27]; ε with both its failure sides printed [S113 pp. 18–19, 23; S150 p. 24]; α, ε and P handed to the user [S097 pp. 26–28]. ⚠ The price of interpretability is one averaged number, without a curve [H003 p. 1; S457 p. 4].
- **M4, duel without the exact solve.** Matched duels survive: same hypothesis class and grid [S308 p. 7], every method tuned by decision cost [S133 pp. 25–26], rivals run on their own problems [S097 pp. 60–62], BARON on its home benchmark [S266 pp. 24–25]. The exact solve does not: coordinate descent [S096 pp. 6–7], restarts to a local minimum [S418 p. 8], a stochastic heuristic under the name *optimal* [S688 p. 8], the solver moved offline as a teacher [S092 pp. 12–13, 36] (Tension T1).
- **M2.** The why-now is data and ML, without a figure [S008 p. 2; S193 p. 2]; learning to optimize borrows a solver speed-up to concede MIO is still too slow online [S092 p. 2; S204 p. 1].
- **M6.** Student lines reuse each other's lemmas [S193 p. 10; S150 pp. 18, 35–39] and start from each other's stated limits [S403 pp. 2–4]; the 2016 trial database is reused years later [S097 pp. 27–28]; company software is research infrastructure [S308 p. 9; S688 p. 10].
- **M7, thin.** Only ceilings valid for every policy (perfect foresight [S008 p. 21], an oracle [S096 pp. 17, 19]) or bounds from a known generator, for T ≤ 5 only [S403 pp. 16, 31–32]; a statistical SDDP bound is designed but not reported [S193 pp. 36–37].
- **Domain moves.** *Devices*: the asymptotic-optimality chain (pointwise, dense set, uniform on compacts, coercivity) [S008 pp. 45–47]; multistage regret telescoped into single-stage errors [S193 pp. 11, 13–14]; one new concentration lemma swapped into published proofs [S150 pp. 13–19]; duality gaps that make inverse equilibrium estimation one conic program [S058 pp. 8–10]; non-identifiability by two exact-fit models [S906 pp. 8–10]. *Algorithms*: weight-averaged SDDP cuts [S193 pp. 35–37]; a fresh subsample per outer-approximation cut [S443 p. 10]; a cross-fitted cost table with a policy-tree selector [S688 p. 8]. *Protocols*: rolling-origin replay [S008 p. 30]; SAA at 0 and the true optimum at 1 [S133 p. 25]; an ε = 0 column to expose overfitting [S150 p. 24]; extrapolation tests by truncated covariates [S133 pp. 29–30]; errors measured where the optimizer lands [S097 p. 23]. *Writing*: one headline number [S008 p. 1]; rivals as special cases [S139 p. 13; S193 p. 6].

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

**Picks**
- Covariates rather than samples of the uncertainty, decided conditionally and nonparametrically [S008 pp. 1–2; S193 pp. 3–4].
- The decision over the model: "predictive models may give biased causal estimates but still lead to good decisions." [S906 p. 3]; teaching voice, jointly: "Problems and data play a leading role in this book, while models play an essential but supporting role." [B017 p. 1]; decision support through applications [B014 p. 1].
- An exact optimizer around the learner: embed the model [S097 p. 5]; select one feasible policy per context, because "averaging feasible policies does not necessarily produce another feasible policy" [S688 p. 3].
- Robustness as regularization, its knob set from data or validation [S113 p. 4; S139 p. 19].
- Interpretability where people act: trees for prescription [S096 pp. 2–3]; OCT for high-stakes enforcement [S444 p. 10]; later, black-box prescribers distilled into trees [S457 p. 7].
- Global learners where local ones suffer the curse of dimension [S133 pp. 3–4].

**Refuses**
- Plugged-in point predictions; the prediction's bootstrap spread is "the wrong uncertainty" [S008 pp. 6–7].
- A generative parametric fit before SAA [S193 p. 3]; parametric ERM as default, though credited when its assumptions hold [S139 pp. 7, 22].
- Causal effects as the target: the question is practical, not causal [S444 p. 6]; effect estimators give no policy [S096 p. 4].
- Reinforcement learning for heavily constrained OR problems [S193 p. 4].
- End-to-end decision-focused training as a baseline: cited, never run (inferred from [S688 pp. 2, 12] and [S139 p. 3]).
- ⚠ Counter-practice: black-box ensembles explained after the fact in a clinical rule [S325 p. 6]; *optimal* attached to heuristic training [S096 p. 6; S688 p. 8].

## 5. Critiques, failures and limits on record

- **Named critics.** Jo, Aghaei, Gómez and Vayanos (Management Science 2026, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2021.02813): the prescriptive-tree objective can favour the worst tree, and no code replicates it; no reply found (note 05 §2.6). The survey of Sadana et al.: weighting is consistent but optimistic with many covariates; Xie, Abdin and Fang: weighted SAA ignores decision signals; Kannan, Bayraksan and Luedtke use kNN-weighted SAA as a strong baseline (note 05 §3). Burer and Zhu: every validity domain, Maragno et al.'s enlarged hull included, acts only in decision space [samuel-burer card S057 pp. 3, 6–7]. An unsigned, commercial review asks whether one decision course costs depth [B016 p. 1].
- **His replies.** A self-identified limit, answered before the named critics of weighted SAA wrote: the bootstrap-robust layer, v1 November 2017 by its arXiv identifier [S139 pp. 3–4]; the Sadana et al. survey cites it for the optimism point (note 05 §3). Its own test shows the gain at zero spurious covariates and shrinking room as they are added [S139 p. 21]. No reply to the tree counterexample was found.
- **Failures and retractions.** The 2014 withdrawal was Kallus's, before Bertsimas joined (note 06 T9). The prescriptive-tree MIO did not scale [S096 p. 7]. Pruning fails for portfolios and motion-planning accuracy is mostly below 10%, both printed [S092 pp. 29, 32–33]. OCT-HaGOn misses two of six best-known values [S298 arXiv pp. 29–31]. A decision-aware scenario reduction loses at larger m [S131 p. 36].
- **Limits the papers admit.** No out-of-sample guarantee [S139 pp. 7–8]; "Therefore, the current approach does not apply to problems that take hours to solve with Gurobi." [S092 p. 36]; online optimality guarantees forgone [S204 p. 5]; confounding assumed away [S097 pp. 29, 35]; constants too large for finite samples [S133 p. 14]; positive ε unnecessary with fixed decision rules [S113 p. 23]; results "not causal" [S325 p. 14]; ethical and legal review before any use [S444 p. 22].
- **Disclosure.** The versions read of S298 and S688 carry no competing-interest statement although both run Interpretable AI software [S298 arXiv pp. 45, 50; S688 p. 10]; journal versions not checked.
