## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (the pack), his own filing (the moment folder; SKILL.md's two CP anchors) and turning points.

1. **Moment problems and semidefinite optimization** (Handbook of Semidefinite Programming 2000, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4615-4381-7_16; 1,858 citations) [S007].
   - *Forced by, insight*: questions from four fields where moments appear (probability bounds, option prices, queueing lower bounds, rounding) [S007 pp. 2–3], answered by one fact: moment feasibility is a semidefinite condition, and a tight probability bound is an LP over measures with a polynomial dual [S007 pp. 4–6, 21–23].
   - *Evidence*: on a two-station network the SDP lifts the LP bound by 55–60% and comes close to the simulated LBFS-B policy; on a four-class single station it improves the LP "often by an order of magnitude"; each instance solves in under a minute (two-station) or two minutes (single station) [S007 pp. 16–18].
   - *Reception and method*: no critique found (note 05 §6); authorship differs between manuscript and published record (note 01, Contradictions 3). M1, M7, H10: "the semidefinite relaxation provides practically useful suboptimality guarantees that can be used to assess the closeness to optimality of heuristic policies" [S007 p. 18].
2. **Optimal inequalities in probability theory** (SIAM J. Optim. 2005, DOI 10.1137/S1052623401399903, with Popescu; SKILL.md SW2) [S018].
   - *Forced by, insight*: are the classical bounds best possible? The gap is put in the critics' words [S018 pp. 1–4]. Every dual polynomial is a valid bound; interval nonnegativity becomes a PSD Gram condition after a rational substitution, so the tight univariate bound is one SDP [S018 pp. 5–10].
   - *Evidence*: a landscape table of polynomial and NP-hard cases (NP-hard from k ≥ 2 on the orthant and k ≥ 4 on Rⁿ, via copositivity), closed forms up to three moments, no computation [S018 pp. 5, 13, 16–17].
   - *Reception and method*: 30 months in review (note 01 §1.10); read as settling the univariate case, no critique in 353 citing contexts (note 05 §6). M1, the gap it set out to close: "It is fair to say that a thorough understanding of the algorithmic aspects and the complexity of the (n, k, Ω)-bound problem is still lacking." [S018 p. 4]; its printed limits: NP-hardness from k ≥ 2 on the orthant and k ≥ 4 on Rⁿ, and no polynomial-size exact multivariate SDP [S018 pp. 13, 16–17].
3. **Semidefinite relaxations, multivariate normal distributions, and order statistics** (Handbook of Combinatorial Optimization 1998, with Ye; 430 citations) [S033].
   - *Forced by, insight*: no SDP guarantee for s–u–v max-cut [S033 preprint p. 4] and, implicitly, none for rounding under linear side constraints [S033 preprint pp. 3, 10]; read the SDP optimum as a normal covariance, sample, round by sign, and take every pairwise statistic from Sheppard's 1900 orthant formula [S033 preprint pp. 5–7].
   - *Evidence*: ratio theorems for cuts, constrained boolean QP and bisection; 25 of 54 G-set graphs at 0.78–0.98 of the SDP bound, a certificate per instance, without run times or a rerun of the incumbent rounding [S033 preprint pp. 8–16].
   - *Reception and method*: the card's reader notes that the basic rounding has the Goemans–Williamson distribution, so the novelty lies in the analysis [S033 preprint pp. 5–7]. M1 variant, the class relaxed rather than kept [S033 preprint pp. 3–6]; M4 variant, a ratio per instance with the incumbent roundings not run [S033 preprint pp. 15–16].
4. **Solving convex programs by random walks** (J. ACM 2004, DOI 10.1145/1008731.1008733, with Vempala; 354 citations) [S045].
   - *Forced by, insight*: oracle-based convex minimization "represents a frontier of polynomial-time solvability" [S045 p. 1]; replace Levin's intractable exact centroid by the mean of random-walk samples, kept valid by a perturbed Grünbaum theorem [S045 pp. 3, 6, 9–10].
   - *Evidence*: O(nL) oracle calls, matching Vaidya and best possible, in a table that prints the total-time rows it loses [S045 pp. 3–4].
   - *Reception and method*: it asks for an empirical evaluation [S045 p. 16]; no card records one. Complexity-first, his 2004 sense of *tractable* (M1 limitation).
5. **Mixed-projection conic optimization** (Oper. Res. 2022, DOI 10.1287/opre.2021.2182, with Cory-Wright and Pauphilet) [S250], with sequels [S274; S385].
   - *Forced by, insight*: an inherited intractability verdict and no mixed-integer convex form for rank [S250 pp. 1–2]; "projection matrices are to rank constraints what binary variables are to cardinality constraints" [S250 p. 10], and a regularizer lets outer approximation apply [S250 pp. 12–16, 19–21].
   - *Evidence*: ∃R-completeness [S250 p. 9]; rank-1 completion solved at n = 10–20, multi-tree outer approximation fastest, gaps reported as absolute values [S250 pp. 30–31], and sensor location (ranks 2–3) at n = 10–20 with absolute gaps ≤ 0.0149 [S250 pp. 34–36]; at n = 30, 15 of 18 rank rows stop at the 50-cut limit [S250 pp. 31–32]; a relaxation to n = 600 where Mosek runs out of memory, Mosek faster at n = 50–100 [S250 p. 28].
   - *Reception and method*: matrix perspectives with convex-hull results [S274 arXiv pp. 12–15]; a disjunctive branch-and-bound [S385 pp. 8–9]; certificates valid only up to the solver's piecewise approximation [S250 p. 30]. M1 variant (class left, algorithm built: C5), M4.
6. **Reformulation-perspectification and cone products** (2023–2026, with den Hertog, de Moor, Koukouvinos, Zhen) [S377; S708; S415; S689]; S415 is SKILL.md's second CP anchor (DOI 10.1287/ijoc.2023.0345).
   - *Forced by, insight*: RLT multiplies only linear or quadratic constraints, and BARON and SCIP own the class [S377 pp. 2–3]; a linear times a convex constraint becomes a perspective in (x, U) [S708 p. 8]; within five cones all 15 pairwise products can be written, and a robust-optimization lens picks the best of infinitely many [S415 pp. 3, 7–8].
   - *Evidence*: more instances solved than BARON and SCIP on the tested families, their wins printed [S377 pp. 1, 29–35; S708 pp. 37–41]; 40 variables at degree 3 and 20 at degree 4 in under an hour, on box-constrained instances solved at the root [S689 pp. 1, 19].
   - *Reception and method*: Dey and Kocuk's own RPT-derived inequalities, on a power-function set, cost more than BARON at n = 10 (§6.2); per its card, this anchor relaxes nonconvex problems rather than writing probability as convex programs [S415 pp. 2–3], states no threads, tolerances or BARON options, and its printed instance description does not reproduce Tables 3–4 (card reader's check) [S415 pp. 25–27, 46]. M1, M4, M6 (the joint RO book as proof engine [S689 pp. 6–7]).

Not chosen: the price of fairness (1,063 citations), tagged CP for one convex-geometry device [S012 pp. 7–8]; it is Method 3 evidence in SKILL.md.

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

Picks:
- **Best-possible bounds from what can be estimated**: "Instead of trying to characterize this exact structure, we optimize over all dependent structures with the given marginal moment information." [S169 p. 3]; the same stance in finance and scheduling [S056 p. 2; S452 pp. 1–2]; a bound counts as tight when it equals the supremum, even if only approached in the limit [S018 p. 2], certified by extremal distributions or sequences [S018 pp. 19–21].
- **Both sides of the complexity map in one paper** [S018 p. 5; S056 pp. 13–15; S250 p. 9].
- **Classes a commercial solver accepts** [S155 p. 7; S415 pp. 2–3].
- **From 2020, exact models over surrogates**: "NP-hardness is a worst-case analysis statement. In practice, NP-hard optimization problems are often tractable." [S250 p. 8]; heuristics fall short because they "do not provide an instance-wise certificate of optimality" [S385 p. 1].
- **Baselines**: classical closed forms (Arnold–Groeneveld, Aven, Boyle–Lin; Kingman and Daley) [S154 pp. 15–19; S255 pp. 7–9, 12]; the rival's own instances [S452 pp. 3–4]; BARON, SCIP, SOS packages and Mosek at stated versions [S689 pp. 17–19; S708 p. 35].

Refusals:
- **Independence assumptions and full distributions** [S169 p. 3; S056 p. 2].
- **The multivariate SOS hierarchy as the main route**: sidestepped [S169 pp. 10–11], later argued against on size and memory [S689 pp. 2–3, 18–19].
- **Convex surrogates as the answer**: the nuclear norm recast as a relaxation of the exact model [S250 p. 18].
- **Data-calibrated knobs where rivals must accept the choice**: "the worst-case analysis is an instance-free approach, better suited for decisions in such an environment." [S026 p. 12].
- **Complexity as the selector after 2010** [S302 p. 8; S315 p. 6], against his 2004 practice [S045 pp. 2–4] [inferred tension].
- *Declined* [inferred from absence]: the dedicated SDP codes S264 asked for [S264 p. 23]; an empirical study of the random-walk method [S045 p. 16].

- **Dey · RPT-type products tested in print.** *His position*: all constraint pairs multiplied plus an LMI, tractability falling with size [S377 p. 38]. *Dey's*: strengthen the cheapest relaxation by structure, no SDP at scale without a tractability argument (santanu-s-dey SKILL.md, Roundtable Card); with Kocuk, taking RPT (Zhen–de Moor–den Hertog; Bertsimas et al.) as a tool, he multiplies only the simplex equation by x_i^κ on a power-function set [santanu-s-dey card S129 pp. 18–19]; the resulting power-cone inequalities raise the exact count from 888 of 1,000 (weakest relaxation, 34 s) and 941 (PRS) to 959–960, at 121–129 s, while RLT has the largest marginal effect; at n = 10 they cost more than BARON on the nonconvex model, "not advisable" in higher dimensions [santanu-s-dey card S129 pp. 20, 23]. Also: S684 credits its principal-minor relaxations to Blekherman, Dey, Molinaro and Sun [S684 p. 11; santanu-s-dey card S035 arXiv p. 2]; S302 starts from SOC constraints on all 2 × 2 minors [S302 pp. 1–2], the k = 2 sparse PSD closure Dey and coauthors study [santanu-s-dey card S035 arXiv p. 2]; S250 cites Kocuk, Dey and Sun (p. 4). *Relation*: exchange in print. *Contested*: whether RPT-type products pay against a global solver beyond n ≈ 10, given that Dey's RPT step multiplies only the simplex equation. *Deciding test*: Dey's protocol (gap, time, count exact) on the RPT families at n ≥ 20, products added selectively [S415 p. 30]; count exact only where a certified optimum exists (from RPT-BB or a long BARON or SCIP run), since at those sizes BARON finds optima without proving them within an hour [S415 p. 27] or hits the limit [S689 pp. 18–19], and Dey's ground truth stops at n = 10 [santanu-s-dey card S129 p. 20].
- **Burer · moment sets against data.** *His position*: moments only, worst case over the rest, tight by an extremal distribution [S169 pp. 3, 8]; in 2026 moment sets defended for small samples [S867 p. 2]. *Burer's*: with Xu, moment-based sets (Bertsimas et al.; Natarajan et al.) do not converge as samples grow; on three small applications the Wasserstein bound tightens with N while the moment bound, given exact moments ("this choice favors MB"), stays flat [samuel-burer card S044 arXiv pp. 3, 14, 17]; on the knapsack their bound does not converge either, the SDP relaxation not being tight [samuel-burer card S044 arXiv p. 24]. Both sides restrict the copositive cone to tractable inner approximations: PSD plus entrywise nonnegative on his side [S452 p. 3; S785 pp. 3–5]; an S + M form, RLT-type for a polyhedral support and exact for a second-order cone, on Xu and Burer's [samuel-burer card S044 arXiv pp. 13–14]. Burer and coauthors study 5 × 5, where doubly nonnegative first exceeds completely positive [samuel-burer card S028 pp. 3, 12–13], the size of the bordered matrices in his four-asset bounds (card reader's inference [S785 pp. 3–4, 7]). *Relation*: exchange in print (Burer cites him; no reply found). *Contested*: the ambiguity model once samples exist. *Deciding test*: Xu and Burer's instances with moments estimated from the same N samples, coverage and width against N for both sets; the four-asset bounds with a 5 × 5 copositive cut.
- **Burer · low-rank factorization: heuristic or certificate.** *His position*: Burer–Monteiro as polishing step and opponent [S250 pp. 6, 27–29, 32]; alternating minimization feeds incumbents into a certified tree, since such methods' global guarantees rest on unverifiable data assumptions [S385 pp. 3, 14–15]. *Burer's*: the factorization solves large SDPs fast, yet dual methods are "much more valuable than our primal method" when a valid bound is needed [samuel-burer card S001 pp. 13, 22]; it adds no extraneous local minima and supports in-run lower bounds [samuel-burer card S003 pp. 3, 14]. *Relation*: complement; one-sided citation; convex SDPs against nonconvex fixed-rank problems. *Contested*: where certified search beats a factorized heuristic plus a bound. *Deciding test*: on S385's instances, node bounds from a low-rank SDP solver against Mosek in the memory-bound tree [S385 p. 26], gaps at equal time.
- **Misener · selective PSD strength inside a solver.** *His position*: avoid the Lasserre hierarchy (largest LMI of order n + 1; SOS out of memory from n = 30) [S689 pp. 3, 18–19]; Shor LMIs only on well-observed minors [S385 pp. 17–20]; RPT as the group's own Julia code [S415 p. 26]. *Misener's*: partial Lasserre relaxations chosen by cliques [ruth-misener card S078 pp. 2, 6]; PSD cuts scored to suit a host solver [ruth-misener card S021 pp. 2–3, 11–12]; gains must survive inside the full solver (ruth-misener SKILL.md, Roundtable Card). *Relation*: contrast of published methods; no citation either way on CP (elsewhere S266 cites ANTIGONE, Misener–Floudas, among rival solvers [S266 pp. 2–3], and her pooling paper takes its reformulation-versus-cutting-planes question from Bertsimas–Dunning–Lubin [ruth-misener card S048 p. 7]). *Contested*: which PSD strengthening buys most bound per second in a full branch-and-bound. *Deciding test*: the 99 BoxQP instances of her test [ruth-misener card S021 p. 20], a standard set that grew from Burer and Vandenbussche's test bed [samuel-burer card H001 p. 3], in one solver: RPT products with the LMI, scored PSD cuts, minors only; gap per cut and per second.
- **Grossmann, and Leyffer through the Belotti et al. survey · outer approximation on projection matrices.** *His position*: the mixed-projection method runs Duran–Grossmann outer approximation and Quesada–Grossmann single-tree search, with Grossmann's 2002 counterexample as the warning for nonconvex decomposition [S250 pp. 4, 20–21], and cites Belotti et al., Leyffer among them, for decomposition [S250 p. 19]; multi-tree was fastest [S250 pp. 30–31, 34]; the solver sometimes declared optimality with a 1–2% gap left, so absolute gaps are reported [S250 p. 30]. *Grossmann's*: outside the convex class, label the method and measure its gap to a global solver (ignacio-e-grossmann SKILL.md, "Method 6 · Guarantee or label"). *Leyffer's* (one of six authors of Belotti et al.; its sections cannot be attributed to him): RLT and SDP hierarchies grow "dramatically" [sven-leyffer card S002 p. 79]. *Relation*: complement; citations one way. *Contested*: whether bounds from a piecewise-approximated nonconvex master certify; single- against multi-tree as solvers mature [S250 p. 34]. *Deciding test*: re-certify the exact-solve instances with an exact spatial master and an independent bound check; rerun the tree comparison on a current release.
