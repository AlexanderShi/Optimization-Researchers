## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (pack), his own emphasis (SKILL.md SW4; the book's parts [B009 p. 1]) and turning points.

**2.1 Best subset selection via a modern optimization lens** (Ann. Statist. 2016, read as arXiv v1 [S010]; sequels [S081; S105]). *Forced by*: statistics had dismissed best subset as intractable, and the Lasso rests on hard-to-verify conditions [S010 pp. 2–3]. *Insight*: keep the exact estimator; a discrete first-order method warm-starts a MIQO with data-derived bounds; the constrained Lasso is a weaker relaxation than its root node [S010 pp. 1, 5–6, 9–10, 13–20]. An early stop carries a suboptimality guarantee for the bounded problem, and for best subset itself only under the provable bounds; the certified runs do not say which bounds they used, and box runs certify only inside the box [S010 pp. 17, 28, 34, 41]. *Evidence*: best upper bounds within a 500 s budget, gap curves warm against cold, Diabetes optima in seconds and certificates in 10–20 minutes [S010 pp. 11, 27–29]; duels with glmnet and Sparsenet, the Lasso's low-SNR wins printed [S010 pp. 30, 38–39]. *Reception*: 1,284 citations (pack); the low-SNR critique (§5). *Methods*: M2 (the belief "formed in the 1970s and 1980s and it was at the time justified" [S010 p. 8]), M1, M4.

**2.2 Optimal classification trees** (Mach. Learn. 2017 [S011]). *Forced by*: CART splits greedily on impurity and needs pruning; its founders called an optimal tree infeasible in 1984 [S011 pp. 4–5]. *Insight*: solve CART's own objective as one MIO, warm-started by CART; relaxed splits give hyperplane trees at no extra binaries [S011 pp. 9, 13–19]. *Evidence*: depth-matched gains on 53 UCI sets [S011 pp. 27–29]; printed limits: "most of our results for OCT and OCT-H do not carry explicit certificates" [S011 p. 26], depth-4 OCT 80.4% against unconstrained CART 80.7% [S011 p. 27]. *Reception*: 1,223 citations; four critiques unanswered (§5); the programme then runs on coordinate-descent local search, survival trees called globally optimized yet stopped at a local optimum [S162 arXiv pp. 1, 4]. *Methods*: M2, M4 with Tension T1, M6.

**2.3 Exact sparse regression at scale** (Ann. Statist. 2020 [S061]; Stat. Sci. 2020 [S095]; [S170]). *Forced by*: the 2016 big-M constant set the formulation's strength, and building the model grew burdensome with p [S061 pp. 2–3]. *Insight*: with a ridge term, eliminate the coefficients in closed form; the support problem becomes a big-M-free binary convex program solved by single-tree outer approximation [S061 pp. 6, 9–10]; Fenchel duality extends it to classification [S170 pp. 8–11]. *Evidence*: n and p in the 100,000s faster than the Lasso code, conceding that it returns a whole path [S061 p. 13]; phase transitions observed with the exact solver [S061 pp. 14–16]. *Reception*: BPV is his answer to the best-subset critique (§5). *Methods*: M1, M2 measured rather than borrowed, M4, Heuristic 2.

**2.4 Certifiably optimal sparse PCA** ([S184]; JMLR 2022 [S183]; [S429]). *Forced by*: with heuristics, "a practitioner using a heuristic method typically has no way of knowing when this has occurred"; SDP stops near p = 300 [S183 pp. 3–4]. *Insight*: a custom branch and bound after Couenne failed [S184 pp. 8–18]; an exact mixed-integer SDP with closed-form cuts, and relax-and-round with printed gaps beyond proof [S183 pp. 7–12, 22–26]. *Evidence*: p = 1300 certified at small k, nothing certified for p > 300 with k > 10 in 600 s, gaps under 2% [S183 pp. 20–21]. *Methods*: M4 at its fullest (one warm start for all, the comparator's code repaired first [S183 p. 19]); M1 with the class left and the algorithm built (C5).

**2.5 Robust classification** (INFORMS J. Optim. 2019 [S104]; [S225]; [S312]). *Forced by*: no principled model of data error in classification [S104 p. 3]. *Insight*: add an uncertainty set and dualize: feature robustness becomes a dual-norm term, label robustness a budget Γ of flips [S104 pp. 9–17]; the Lasso and SLOPE are robust (min-max) problems, and on SLOPE's uncertainty set the trimmed Lasso is the optimistic (min-min) twin [S225 pp. 5–7, 15]; the random split becomes a worst-case subset kept in LP or QP [S312 pp. 5–6]. *Evidence*: robust models ahead on most decided UCI sets [S104 p. 24]. *Limits*: the label-robust SVM leaves LP at up to three orders of magnitude more time [S104 p. 30]. *Methods*: Heuristic 1, M1, M3.

**2.6 From optimal imputation to "crude is good"** ([S030]; [S310]; [S391]), the domain's turning point. *2018*: imputation as one optimization over all missing entries, solved locally after the MIO took too long to certify [S030 pp. 6–8]; the cross-validated variant had the lowest geometric-mean MAE across 84 UCI sets, and some variant the lowest MAE on 76.2% of them at 30% MCAR [S030 pp. 24–27]. *Turn*: "In short, as far as prediction is concerned, crude is good." [S310 p. 5]; on real designs mice predicts worse than mean imputation on average [S310 p. 12]; in the 2025 adaptive sequel mean imputation stays best on real signals, tied in the text's own account with joint impute-then-regress [S391 pp. 1, 14, 30]. *Methods*: the ML analogue of M5 (judge downstream), Heuristic 4.

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

**Picks.**
- Exact sparsity over surrogates: ℓ1's selection ability "ought to be challenged" [S095 p. 9]; "robustness and sparsity should, in our opinion, be considered as two distinct properties" [S288 p. 7].
- Interpretable models where people act: rules a doctor can follow [S448 p. 2], features selected by name [S383 p. 3]; principle 1 of the book [B009 p. 1].
- Joint over sequential: all regression properties in one MIQO [S081 pp. 2–3]; whole retraining sequences [S401 pp. 2–3].
- Optimization over randomization: splits [S312 pp. 1–2], the bootstrap [S357 pp. 2, 13], oversampling [S873 p. 3], cohorts [S620 p. 4]; principle 5 [B009 p. 1].
- Prescription even inside ML: "This demonstrates the benefit of prescription over prediction in this setting." [S626 p. 24].
- *Assumptions and baselines*: k known in experiments [S061 p. 13; S300 p. 4]; default solver settings, disclosed [S010 p. 32]; the field's reference code as opponent (glmnet, rpart, MASS, EM, mice) [S010 p. 29; S011 p. 26; S178 p. 25; S358 p. 19; S310 p. 8], and the stronger naive rule when strong [S391 p. 13].

**Refusals.**
- Greedy one-split-at-a-time construction [S011 p. 4; S326 p. 3]; random training splits [S312 p. 1]; post-hoc explanations instead of interpretable models [S199 p. 2].
- Probability as ML's home: "ML is inherently linked to optimization not probability theory." [B009 p. 1] (joint); practice keeps statistical machinery [S666 pp. 7–8; S679 pp. 1, 8–10] (Tension T3).
- Learning-theory competition [inferred]: the flagships borrow their statistics [S010 pp. 56–58; S061 p. 14].
- Pretraining cost not justified against a cheap baseline: a training-free pipeline set as the bar for time-series foundation models [S474 pp. 2, 10].
- Counter-practice: a clinical tool on boosted trees with SHAP [S127 p. 4] (C21).

## 5. Critiques, failures and limits on record

**Named critics.**
- *Hastie, Tibshirani and Tibshirani*: best subset loses to the Lasso at realistic low SNR; the 2016 SNR range was unrealistic (note 05 §1.2). Reply by new paper (Heuristic 2): BPV took their grid, added a ridge term, retargeted to selection, and conceded "Our experiments suggest that their observations are still valid but less obvious" [S095 pp. 10, 23]; no sentence concedes the 2016 range (note 05 §1.3); the rejoinder is unread here. The MINLP team's Sahinidis notes record it acknowledging Sarwar et al.'s benchmark without answering their timing point (nikolaos-v-sahinidis note 05 §A8); whether it concedes the 2016 SNR range is not recorded there.
- *Statistical Science discussants*: path-against-point timing and a better, faster L0Learn (Sarwar, Sauk and Sahinidis); CIO code that would not run (Chen, Taeb and Bühlmann); co-author Mazumder's own low-SNR concession (note 05 §1.4–1.5).
- *Speed-up figures*: at least four figures between 2015 and 2019: 200 billion, about 450 billion and about 800 billion, each a linear-MIO solver factor times supercomputer peak speed [S010 pp. 7–8; S081 p. 4; S011 pp. 5–6], then 2 trillion over 1990–2016 with hardware [S184 p. 5]; no critic found (note 05 §1.6).
- *Optimal trees*: Hu, Rudin and Seltzer; Verwer and Zhang; Günlük et al. (§6.3); Aghaei, Gómez and Vayanos (weak big-M relaxation) (note 05 §2.2–2.5); no published reply (note 05 §2.8).
- *Cuvelier (2020)*: the stance not yet followed by the wider AI community; most algorithms without a widespread implementation [B010 p. 1].

**Failures and self-revisions.** CRIO, by his later verdict, gained little over CART [S011 p. 7] (the 2007 paper's own Table 7 puts CRIO above CRUISE's univariate trees on all four real sets [S073 p. 15]; the cards cannot settle which comparison the verdict means). Sophisticated imputation (mice) predicts worse on average than mean imputation on real designs, and the group's own 2018 and 2021 imputation papers are named as validated on synthetic removal [S310 pp. 12–13]; the 2021 paper's workshop version had reported med.impute ahead of mean imputation under synthetic MCAR removal [S228 pp. 3–4]. On real data the simpler incumbent often predicts better: the Lasso on 7 of 12 sets [S081 pp. 11, 13], sum-of-norms on every set [S317 p. 48], CART over axis-aligned OCT in all 9 cells of a clinician-led study, though OCT-H beat CART in 6 of 9 [S144 p. 8]. Unpublished (note 01 §3): the price-of-interpretability manuscript and the claim that hyperplane trees match neural networks, whose constructions "may be impractical to compute" [H009 p. 50].

**Limits printed**: hours to certify when p ≫ n [S010 p. 6]; tens of thousands of points prohibitive for OCT [S011 p. 25]; a commercial solver needed [S095 p. 31]; "a hard theoretical picture is not yet available" for the observed speed [S061 pp. 13–14].

### 6.1 MINLP Team

1. **Sparse-regression benchmarks — Sahinidis** (exchange in print, both ways). *His*: an exact ℓ0 + ℓ2 estimator wins on selection, under a 60 s limit justified by the warm start [S095 pp. 5, 23, 30], with no gap reported at 60 s [S095 p. 5]; ENet and SCAD dominate at high noise and correlation [S095 p. 12], and MCP makes fewer false guesses on the real design [S095 p. 21]. *Theirs*: best subset by integer programming is his own surrogate method, its price and noise weakness stated (nikolaos-v-sahinidis SKILL.md, "Method 3: Make the black box algebraic, and keep the algebra small"); the 2020 discussion he co-authored with Sarwar and Sauk says that BPV's timing figure appears to set one cardinality-constrained solve against a whole glmnet path, that fixing k at the truth somewhat exaggerates the Lasso's deficiencies, and that regularized L0Learn beats CIO/SS and runs faster, with the CIO/SS side taken from BPV's published numbers (note 05 §1.4; nikolaos-v-sahinidis note 03 §F3 reads this differently, as runs on the authors' open code). *Print*: BPV's rejoinder acknowledged the benchmark but left the timing point unanswered; HTT's rejoinder agreed on timing and called the discussants' own speed comparison misleading (nikolaos-v-sahinidis note 05 §A8, rows 8d–8e). *Contested*: whether exact ℓ0 pays at low SNR and equal budgets. *Test*: full (k, γ) paths timed path to path on one machine, SNR down to 0.05, L0Learn and the relaxed Lasso included, scored on prediction and support, every exact gap printed.
2. **Outer approximation as the engine — Grossmann, Leyffer** (complement; he cites them). *His*: sparse models as convex integer programs under OA, credited to Duran–Grossmann and Fletcher–Leyffer: single-tree OA with lazy cuts [S061 pp. 9–10]; OA with finite termination by Fletcher–Leyffer, the single tree mentioned only as an option [S317 pp. 13–15]; a bake-off of Bonmin, KNITRO, FilMINT, MINLP, SCIP and Couenne left one of six logistic MINLPs unsolved by all, which a tailored OA solved [S105 pp. 7–9]. *Theirs*: OA and LP/NLP branch and bound underlie DICOPT, Bonmin and FilMINT, single-tree where callbacks exist (ignacio-e-grossmann SKILL.md, Signature Work Anatomy; Roundtable Card); Leyffer distrusts "best solver" claims from one library (sven-leyffer SKILL.md, Roundtable Card); the authors of the Bonmin framework paper include Grossmann and Wächter [andrew-conn card S003 preprint p. 1] (andrew-conn SKILL.md, Research Trajectory). *Contested*: reformulation or tailored code as the source of the gain. *Test*: one convex integer program given to current Bonmin, an LP/NLP code and the tailored code, one machine and limit, gaps reported.
3. **Sparse SVM — Leyffer** (contrast: the paper cites Fletcher–Leyffer only for OA's finite termination [S170 p. 12], not Leyffer's sparse-SVM work). *His*: ℓ0 classifiers dualized into a binary convex program, Lasso-warm-started, gaps printed [S170 pp. 8–13, 23]. *Theirs*: the perspective MINLP failed in general solvers, read as a Slater violation and repaired by big-M [sven-leyffer card S051 p. 3]; a rotated-SOC perspective relaxation then avoids big-M [sven-leyffer card S142 preprint pp. 4–5] (sven-leyffer SKILL.md, "Method 6: Read the solver's numbers, then fix the formulation before the method"). *Contested*: root-bound strength and time to certificate. *Test*: same UCI sets and k; both relaxations as root bounds; time to a certified optimum.
4. **Sparse PCA certificates — Dey** (single component: contrast; common support: one-way citation, he cites Dey). *Single component, his*: certified optima at k = 5 up to p = 10,000 on gene-expression subsets [S184 p. 34] and at k = 5 and 10 on micromass, p = 1300 [S183 p. 20], printed gaps beyond [S183 pp. 20–26]; the later paper puts the Berk–Bertsimas limit at p = 250 [S183 p. 5], below the k = 5 proofs Berk–Bertsimas itself reports [S184 p. 34]. *Theirs*: heuristics rest on "very strong and often unverifiable assumptions", so certify by an ℓ1 relaxation proved within a factor of the ℓ0 optimum plus a small SOS-2 convex IP [santanu-s-dey card S017 arXiv pp. 2–4, 6–8, 11–14], with gaps bounded up to n = 2000 [santanu-s-dey card S017 arXiv p. 17] (santanu-s-dey SKILL.md, "Method 5: Certify with a cheap, strong relaxation"); the card leaves the specialised branch and bound it cites unnamed [santanu-s-dey card S017 arXiv p. 3], so whether Dey cites Berk–Bertsimas is open. *Test*: Dey's real instances and the UCI sets of [S183 p. 19] at k = 10 and 20, one machine, equal time, the dual bound and the gap to one best primal reported. *Common support, his*: all components at once on one support; Dey et al. named closest, no duel [S429 pp. 3–4, 23]; no citation back on Dey's cards. *Theirs*: an operator-norm relaxation plus a SOS-2 convex IP, the gap not yet closed on some instances [santanu-s-dey card S057 arXiv pp. 4–6, 30]. *Test*: his geometric method against that convex IP on Reddit1500 and Reddit2000 [santanu-s-dey card S057 arXiv pp. 24–25] at Dey's (r, k). *Contested* (both): the tighter dual bound per second.
5. **Piecewise regression — Dey** (exchange in print: Dey faults CRIO; no reply). *His*: CRIO's big-M assignment of clusters to groups, weak and slow by its own account [S073 pp. 8–10, 16], continuity at region boundaries left to future work [S073 pp. 16–17]. *Theirs*: a 2026 change-point paper faults it for ignoring contiguity and uses nested binaries that project integrally [santanu-s-dey card S124 pp. 2, 7–11], while warning that a tighter LP need not mean a faster solve [santanu-s-dey card S124 p. 13]. *Contested*: little; the objects differ, and on the assignment block the integral projection already settles the bound, so only time is open. *Test*: on one feasible set: on that paper's stock-price instances, CRIO's big-M assignment with added contiguity constraints inside its Basic model against the nested block, at equal K, T and loss, on one thread, reporting LP bound, nodes and time [santanu-s-dey card S124 pp. 14–15].
6. **Low-rank completion — Burer** (one-way citation: he cites Burer–Monteiro [S301 p. 2]). *His*: fastImpute eliminates one factor in closed form, set against Burer–Monteiro gradient methods [S301 pp. 2, 5]; certification deferred at scale [S475 pp. 7, 45]. *Theirs*: factorization X = RRᵀ sized by a rank bound [samuel-burer card S001 pp. 1–3], certified in the convex dual (samuel-burer SKILL.md, "Method 6: Solve in the cheap nonconvex space, certify in the convex dual"). *Contested*: certificates for factored solutions. *Test*: dual bounds for his outputs on small instances, gaps printed.
7. **Formulation strength of trees and clustering — Misener** (contrast). *His*: OCT's big-M encoding without certificates [S011 pp. 12–14, 26], its constants read off the feature scaling [S011 p. 12]; the clustering MINLP replaced by local search [S067 pp. 5–8]. *Theirs*: P-split beat big-M on every clustering and P_ball instance and, for a wide range of P, beat the hull on time on all but one instance; on two ReLU-network instances big-M won on time [ruth-misener card S040 arXiv pp. 10–13] (ruth-misener SKILL.md, "Method 2: Between formulations: the parameterised family between two textbook extremes"). *Contested*: whether strength restores certificates at OCT's depths, where the big-M constants are already tight (the critique in print is Aghaei et al.'s, note 05 §2.5). *Test*: OCT disjunctions in P-split form, root bound, nodes and time at depths 2–4.

### 6.2 NLP Team

Ipopt (Wächter) is the convex engine of his robust and stable logistic models [S104 p. 19; S365 p. 16]; no dispute is documented.

8. **The ℓ1 school — Nocedal, Wright** (contrast). *His*: sparsity imposed exactly; the cardinality-constrained inverse-covariance likelihood solved by OA from graphical-lasso starts, certified only on small instances [S288 pp. 6–10, 16, 29–30], with FDR 0.049 against 0.68–0.75 at 200–350 s against 1–4 s [S288 p. 17], while MB stays the most accurate and the headline runs carry no gap [S288 pp. 16–17]. *Theirs*: Newton-like ℓ1 methods duelled against the strongest code [jorge-nocedal card S043 pp. 2–3]; large-scale ML as a setting where traditional nonlinear optimization techniques "typically falter" [jorge-nocedal card S004 arXiv p. 3]; Wright lets the competitor set the target (stephen-j-wright SKILL.md, "Method 5: Fair duel, calibrated report") and prefers simple approximate solutions where the application allows (same SKILL.md, "Method 6: The application's ground rules pick the method class and the formulation"); neither guest's cards or skill state a view on ℓ0 selection, and Wright's skill puts mixed-integer outside his field (Honest Boundary). *Contested*: selection per unit time. *Test*: on S288's designs, equal support size k (or equal validation likelihood) and equal wall-clock budget, all tuned on validation; FDR, likelihood and gap reported.
9. **Fairness by constraints — Curtis** (contrast). *His*: label flips projected by a MILO, a convergence result that lapses with extra constraints, a priced knob [S480 pp. 7–10, 21–23]. *Theirs*: hard constraints on smooth surrogates built to meet a bound theorem's hypotheses, trained by stochastic SQP [frank-e-curtis card S083 arXiv pp. 2, 6–8] (frank-e-curtis SKILL.md, "Method 4: Audit the yardstick"). *Contested*: true out-of-sample fairness at the smaller accuracy cost. *Test*: same data and model, both tuned on validation, disparate impact and accuracy per data set.
10. **Smoothing and complexity — Nesterov** (complement; he uses Nesterov's device). *His*: a discrete first-order warm start with an O(1/ε) rate to ε-stationarity, not optimality [S010 p. 23], Nesterov smoothing for the LAD loss [S010 p. 25], and the MIO supplying the certificate; a sub-gradient rate called "disappointingly slow" [S095 p. 7]. *Theirs*: smoothing as a Golden Rule (yurii-nesterov SKILL.md, "Method 1: The Golden Rules"); pushback on heuristics without a complexity argument and on nonconvex local answers taken as final (Roundtable Card). *Contested*: solver certificate against complexity guarantee. *Test*: how often the rate-backed warm start already is the certified optimum, and the proof time after it.

13. **Hyperparameters as a blackbox — Audet, Scheinberg** (contrast with Audet; one-way citation of Scheinberg). *His*: validation grids and bisection on elbow-shaped curves [S317 pp. 17–20]; Bayesian optimization for soft trees [S477 pp. 10–11]; his group cites Ghanbari and Scheinberg 2017 for future nonconvex optimization of the imputation values [S391 p. 8]. *Theirs*: ML tuning as a constrained mixed-variable blackbox [charles-audet card S058 arXiv pp. 5–6], judged at equal evaluation budgets (charles-audet SKILL.md, "Method 5: Budget-aware, cross-community benchmarking"); the cited Scheinberg work is probably the DFO-TR black-box paper, which already runs the equal-evaluation-budget protocol this test asks for [katya-scheinberg card S052 pp. 6–8] (unconfirmed until the citing paper's reference list is checked). *Contested*: matched tuning: one paper refits its own methods and the static baseline with a √λβ tweak but not sum-of-norms [S317 p. 40], and credits the sum-of-norms lasso variant's edge partly to a more exhaustive grid [S317 p. 22]. *Test*: tune every method in that comparison (the paper's three methods, static_cutplane and the sum-of-norms variants) once with the paper's grid plus bisection and once with NOMAD, at an equal number of validation fits per method; apply the √λβ refit to all methods or to none; report test R² and support difference per data set.
