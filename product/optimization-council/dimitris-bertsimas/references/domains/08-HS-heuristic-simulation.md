## 2. Signature works

Chosen by citations (pack), his emphasis and turning points; the tree-search comparison (S340/S197) appears in §3 as a protocol.

**SW-HS1 · The simulated-annealing review, 1992/1993** (with Tsitsiklis; NRC chapter [B020]; *Statistical Science* 1993, DOI 10.1214/ss/1177011077 [S005]; 2,436 citations). *Forced by* a method in wide use with little justification, for finite sets only [S005 pp. 1–2]. *Insight*: convergence is settled, but time-asymptotic rates are "largely irrelevant"; the yardsticks should be hitting times over families of instances and comparison with the best specialized heuristic at equal computing time [S005 pp. 3–4]: "One can trace the inadequacy of the analytical methods mentioned so far to the fact that they deal with one instance at a time." [S005 p. 3] *Evidence*: no experiments of its own; the Johnson et al. results it reports make SA a "consistent loser" to Lin–Kernighan on the TSP [S005 p. 4]. *Verdict* (joint, 1992; C22): "As far as theory is concerned, there is at present a definite lack of rigorous results justifying the use of simulated annealing." [B020 p. 8]; yet it is "able to produce good solutions for an optimization problem, even if we do not understand the structure of the problem well" [B020 p. 10]. *Reception*: no critique found (note 05 §6); cited in 2023 by his group for a guarantee (SW-HS5). *Method*: Method 4's duel as a yardstick for others' experiments (C8); Method 7's demand for guarantees.

**SW-HS2 · Simulation-based booking limits, 2001–2005** (with de Boer; *Oper. Res.* 2005, received 2001, DOI 10.1287/opre.1040.0164 [S066 p. 1]; a 2000 working paper with a close title, probably its precursor [S502] [inferred from title and date]; 248 citations). *Forced by* airlines running virtual nesting with EMSR-BL or LP-BL limits while network LPs stay deterministic and exact DP explodes [S066 pp. 1–3]. *Insight*: keep the deployed framework and optimize only its booking limits, by simulation, a stochastic gradient on finite differences and offline value functions [S066 pp. 3, 5–10]; rival methods are often "directed at optimizing a different policy than the one that is actually implemented" [S066 p. 10]. *Evidence*: common simulated request streams [S066 p. 11]; 0.05–0.61% over EMSR-BL and 0.24–1.29% over LP-BL on hub networks [S066 p. 14]; within 0.17% of the exact DP optimum of a single leg at 95% confidence [S066 p. 13]; the gain turns negative without value functions under theft nesting [S066 p. 14]. *Reception*: none on record. *Method*: Method 5 (the airlines' rules as baseline); Method 4 as a variant; Method 1 contradicted, since the search "has no theoretical justification, but is a heuristic based on the intuition behind gradient descent methods" [S066 p. 7].

**SW-HS3 · Robust local search and robust annealing, 2007–2010** (with Nohadani and K. M. Teo; *Oper. Res.* 2010, DOI 10.1287/opre.1090.0715 [S090], after a physics-journal application [S234] and a manuscript [S305]; then constraints [S116], annealing [S141], optics [S278]; 180, 151 and 119 citations for S090, S116, S141). *Forced by* simulator-defined designs that fail under implementation errors, where convex robust optimization cannot be applied [S090 pp. 1–2]. *Insight*: descent theory for the worst-case cost: a direction descends if and only if it makes a negative inner product with every worst error, the best one solves a max-angle SOCP, and bad neighbours spanning the worst errors suffice [S090 pp. 3–6]; then constraints [S116 pp. 3–5] and an annealed log-sum-exp of bad neighbours' costs [S141 p. 4]. *Evidence*: a 100-variable electromagnetic design, simulator checked against measurements, worst case 8% lower in 65 iterations at practically unchanged nominal cost [S090 pp. 10–12]; in radiation therapy the radius is swept into frontiers for clinicians [S116 pp. 12–13]. *Limits printed*: convergence only for convex costs with an exact worst-error oracle [S090 p. 5]; a heuristic otherwise [S305 p. 8]; "Because we can only estimate the worst-case cost, there is always a chance for late discoveries of worst implementation errors." [S090 p. 12] *Reception*: critics in §5; Conn and Vicente reuse its example (§6). *Method*: Method 1 as a variant (only the direction subproblem stays in class); Method 3 with the radius set from measured errors [S090 p. 12]; no certificate (C3).

**SW-HS4 · General-purpose local search for binary optimization, 2007–2013** (with Iancu and Katz; working paper [S914]; *INFORMS J. Comput.* 2013, DOI 10.1287/ijoc.1110.0496 [S328]; 20 citations). *Forced by* large binary programs the leading code cannot solve: "In real-world applications, there is a stringent desire to find feasible solutions that improve current practice, without necessarily having a proof of their optimality." [S328 p. 2] *Insight*: one-flip moves through slightly infeasible solutions, pruned by a memory per constraint-status trace, one depth knob Q, no problem structure [S328 p. 3]; a pseudo-polynomial run time and a set-packing ratio for the ideal version [S328 pp. 4–8], lost in the hashed code, as the paper says [S328 p. 9]. *Evidence*: a duel with CPLEX at equal memory over checkpoints of 5 to 200 hours [S328 pp. 11–13]; "For shorter running times (one-two hours), CPLEX tends to have an edge, although not in all cases." [S328 p. 12]; library gaps of 8.04% and 4.96% [S328 p. 15]. *Reception*: none on record. *Method*: the 1992 yardsticks applied to his own heuristic [S914 pp. 2, 6–15, 22–24]; Method 3 as a variant (no quality curve over Q [S328 p. 11]); Method 4 with the roles reversed.

**SW-HS5 · Annealing as the trees' engine, 2023** (with Sujichantararat; manuscript [H002]). *Forced by* policy trees grown by local search that stall in local minima, after annealing had improved classification trees [H002 pp. 2, 4]. *Insight*: keep the tree moves and the annealing shell, swap the score, choose neighbours by rank annealing across temperature bands [H002 pp. 3, 6, 8–9]. *Evidence*: ten data sets; the abstract prints wins over OPT on 3 and over CART on 4 [H002 p. 1]; on one data set it runs 12 to 31 minutes against about one [H002 p. 18]. *Reception*: none on record (a submitted manuscript). *Method*: the incumbents as warm starts and opponents [H002 pp. 8, 13–14], but annealing "guarantees to provide a global optimal solution asymptotically under an appropriate cooling schedule" [H002 p. 2], an uncertified claim (Method 4 ✗). He posed the problem [H002 p. 19]. Tension T2's end point: the review, in its 1993 printing, cited for a guarantee, not for its reservations [H002 p. 2].

**SW-HS6 · Learned surrogates for black-box global optimization, 2022–2026** (with Öztürk [S298]; with Margaritis [S266]; *J. Glob. Optim.* 2026 and 2025; 23 and 33 citations). *Forced by* global solvers that accept only primitives they can relax, leaving simulation outputs out of reach [S298 p. 3; S266 p. 3]. *Insight*: learn each hard constraint with an MIO-representable model, embed it exactly, solve, and repair by projected gradient descent on the true functions [S298 pp. 3–4, 15–22]; the sequel adds learner classes, sampling where learners disagree and robustness to learning error [S266 pp. 7–8, 13–17]. *Evidence*: best-known values on 4 of 6 larger MINLPLib instances [S298 pp. 29–31]; the sequel improves its predecessor's gap on 39 of 77 instances, while BARON solves 73 [S266 p. 25]. *Limits printed*: "Since the method relies on ML-based approximations of the original problem, it does not necessarily produce globally optimal solutions." [S266 p. 32]; no true black box tested [S298 p. 39]. *Reception*: no critique on record. *Method*: Method 1 by approximation; Method 4 as a variant, certificate dropped and said to be dropped.

## 3. How he works here

**Methods (numbered as in SKILL.md).**
- **M1 · Formulation first, kept in class**: mostly a variant. The class is left on purpose because the cost is a simulation [S090 pp. 1–2; S278 p. 2], and polynomial-SDP relaxations are declined as too large or little used [S090 p. 8; S141 p. 7]. The class survives in subproblems: the direction SOCP [S090 p. 5]; exact counterparts for convex constraints, cutting a run from 3,600 s to 96 s [S116 pp. 5, 9]; an LP whose dual feeds gradients to an annealed search [S159 p. 4]; learned models embedded exactly [S298 pp. 15–16]. ✗ Booking limits have no exact formulation [S066 pp. 2, 7, 10]. ⚠ Say–do gap: the chirped-mirror paper leaves the class without a solve time at scale [S278 pp. 2, 4], against Method 1's stop rule.
- **M2 · Quantified why-now**: weak. Solver progress is told without a figure and motivates a heuristic [S914 pp. 1–2]; the MIO speed-up is asserted [S298 p. 1]; once, MIO's practicality is measured, upper tail printed [S340 pp. 14, 23].
- **M3 · Price of X**: one knob, no bound. The radius from measured errors (σ = 40 μm, 99% coverage, 550 μm) [S090 p. 12]; frontiers traced by sweeping it [S116 pp. 12–13]; lost nominal performance named the price of robustness [S278 p. 5]; Q with proved bounds [S328 pp. 4–8].
- **M4 · Warm start, exact solve, certificate, duel**: roles reversed: the heuristic is the method and the exact solver the opponent [S328 pp. 11–14], as when an exact MILP over candidate beam angles proves optimality yet ends 5% worse than the continuous hybrid [S159 p. 7]. The incumbent stays warm start and opponent [S066 pp. 10–11; S090 p. 12]. The certificate becomes a small exact optimum [S066 p. 13; S397 p. 19] or a mesh enumeration [S141 p. 7]. ✗ Gaps: "Therefore, applying the proposed RSA to this model warrants a global robust optimum." [S141 p. 9], for runs stopped at an empirical threshold [S141 p. 11]; the termination test called an optimality condition [S090 p. 15]; the borrowed guarantee [H002 pp. 2–3].
- **M5 · Judge by the decision, against the owner's rule**: where an owner exists: airline rules, with his own computing cost [S066 pp. 3, 11, 15]; planners' equispaced beam angles [S159 p. 4]; engineers' optimize-then-sample practice [S090 p. 12]; a published heuristic standing in for an operator's unknown rule [S397 p. 18]. Absent on synthetic grids [S340 pp. 17–19] and estimated rewards [H002 p. 13].
- **M6 · Programmes**: HS never ran as one [inferred]: one thesis (note 04), no course or book (§4). The staging shows: the application paper first, the OR method second [S305 p. 3]; annealed trees as a node of the optimal-trees programme, run against his company's software [H002 pp. 8, 19].
- **M7 · Bound before policy**: stated as the demand for complexity over families of instances [S005 p. 3]; practised as a tightness audit proving a heuristic's O(log n) ratio cannot be a constant [S161 TR pp. 1–5] and as a ratio for the ideal version of a heuristic [S328 pp. 6–9]. ⚠ In the robust and annealing line no bound exists, since the worst case itself is estimated [S090 p. 12]; S141 and H002 break the stop rule (no bound, no near-optimality claim).

**Domain moves.** *Proof devices*: a robust analogue of descent via Danskin's theorem and KKT, convergence as a subgradient method [S090 pp. 3–5, 16–17]; counting stored traces times objective improvements, then an exchange lemma and a reachability lemma [S328 pp. 4–8]; a self-similar tight instance [S161 TR pp. 3–5]. *Algorithm design*: neighbourhood ascents with a fixed barrier for predictable times, then a max-angle step [S090 pp. 6–8]; every evaluation reused [S090 pp. 7, 12]; staged deepening, each stage warm-starting the next [S328 p. 11]; a deterministic fluid MIO re-solved in rolling horizon [S340 pp. 11–14]. *Protocol*: equal memory over long checkpoints [S328 pp. 11–13]; one thread and 60 s per decision for both methods, the rival's overruns plotted [S340 pp. 16, 23]; a handicap test added in the journal version [S197 p. 12]; the worst-case estimator checked against random sampling given ten times the evaluations (30,000 against 3,000), which reaches only 96% of his estimate [S090 p. 12; S234 p. 6]; ablation of the component claimed essential [S066 pp. 13–14]; the simulator validated against laboratory data [S090 p. 10]; designs stressed beyond their set [S278 p. 6]. In the tree-search duel, the fluid MIO's fitted gain over a tailored heuristic exceeds every tree-search budget's [S197 p. 10]. *Writing*: the method framed by analogy to gradient descent [S090 p. 2], two-dimensional examples first [S090 p. 8]; ⚠ Tension T6: an abstract's "significantly lower worst-case cost" is the body's 8% [S090 pp. 1, 12]; gains of 0.05–0.61% called "practically significant" [S066 p. 14].

**Say–do gaps** (marked above): his 1992 yardsticks against his own annealers [S141 pp. 5–6, 11; H002 p. 18]; certificates (Taste mark 7) against S141, S090 and H002; Method 1's stop rule against S278; Tension T2 throughout.

## 4. Taste: what he picks and what he refuses

**Picks.** A heuristic or rule already in practice, with an owner: booking limits inside the airlines' framework [S066 pp. 3, 10]; beam angles chosen by experience [S159 pp. 1–2]. Costs given by a partner's simulator or model, validated against measurements where possible [S234 pp. 2–3; S116 p. 10; S259 p. 9]. In the robust-search line, one uncertainty model: implementation errors in a Euclidean ball sized from error statistics, measured for the EM design [S090 pp. 3, 12] and stated as σ = 0.5 nm at 95% coverage for the mirrors [S278 p. 3]; the learned-surrogate line puts p-norm balls on learned coefficients instead [S266 p. 16]. Generality over structure [S328 p. 3]. Deterministic smoothing of stochastic control, effective "in spite of how much they depart from the true dynamics" [S340 p. 31] (Tension T3). Heuristics as parts: warm starts, repair, recalibration (§1), and robust annealing as an estimator's global step, printed as the costlier choice [S222 p. 10]. Baselines: the owner's rule, CPLEX, random perturbation sampling [S090 p. 12].

**Refuses or declines.** Convergence theory for the practical nonconvex methods, called heuristics [S090 p. 6; S305 p. 8]; rates [S141 p. 6]; exact DP and the network integer program [S066 pp. 1–2]; continuous annealing in the review [S005 p. 1]; constraints in the first robust paper [S090 p. 3]; large-scale random search for a global optimum [S234 p. 4]. Derivative-free comparators are rare: SPSA appears once, beaten in a nominal design step [S234 p. 4]; the S298 card finds one black-box optimization reference, Bayesian optimization, dismissed in a sentence [S298 pp. 5, 50–52]; and an ML paper runs a cyclic coordinate search on its imputation values and names their nonconvex optimization as future work, citing Ghanbari and Scheinberg (2017), a DFO trust-region paper (§6.1) [S391 pp. 8–9] [inferred: DFO used and cited, never studied]. His robust search needs gradients [S090 pp. 2, 11].

**Teaching weight** (book material; the 1997 and 2005 books joint, the 2009 syllabus his own course page): three pages of heuristics in the 1997 LO text [B004 p. 5], about six of 550 pages in 2005 [B007 p. 3], one 2009 lecture shared with approximation algorithms [B021 p. 2]. [inferred] He teaches HS as a few integer-programming techniques.

## 5. Critiques, failures and limits on record

**Named critics** (note 05 §4.6), all on the robust local search: Menickelly and Wild (2020) adopt its test problem as standard, count his group's robust algorithms among the computationally heavy, and supply a stationarity guarantee for a derivative-free method; Zheng, Bao and Tan (2024): one objective only; Cakmak, Wu and Zhou (2021): it fails when only parameter uncertainty matters. No reply was found. Also citing it: Leyffer (MINLP Team) and co-authors, Menickelly and Wild among them, survey nonlinear robust optimization with a section on implementation-error methods [sven-leyffer card S021 TR pp. 18–19] that cites the algorithm (note 05 §4.6); the RO file treats the exchange ([03-RO](03-RO-robust-adaptive.md)). The annealing review drew no critique on record (note 05 §6).

**Admitted limits**, beyond those in §2: a search-depth variant that never terminates [S090 p. 15]; local minima only, random sampling "not a good gauge", 20-hour runs [S116 pp. 8, 13]; problem-specific parameters [S141 p. 10]; non-monotone progress [S066 pp. 12–13]. *Observed, not admitted*: neither side of the tree-search duel has a performance bound; the paper's only analysis is a branching-factor count [S340 p. 26].

**Over-claims on record** (08 §3 rows 4 and 16; C3, C9): the asymptotic theorem for stopped runs [S141 pp. 5–6, 9, 11]; the termination test as an optimality condition [S090 p. 15]; "the most general global optimization method in the literature" [S298 p. 45] beside "no guarantees of global optimality" [S298 p. 39]. **Retractions**: none found.

## 6. Cross-team interfaces

### 6.1 DFO Team (dfo-team)

All five members meet HS. SKILL.md's Roundtable Card predicts one fault line for Powell, Conn, Audet and Vicente, a convergence backbone against annealing and robust local search; the cards split it into seven meeting points, two of them exchanges in print. On HS topics, unless stated, neither side's cards record a citation of the other. Outside HS, his S289 cites Powell's lower bound for Karmarkar's algorithm [S289 p. 3], Vicente's risk-neutral density papers use the Bertsimas–Popescu characterization of nonnegative polynomials [luis-nunes-vicente card S028 pp. 10–11; luis-nunes-vicente card S087 p. 7], and Scheinberg faults the MIO formulation of his optimal trees [katya-scheinberg card S017 p. 4].

**Powell · annealing as a working engine** (contrast; conflict). *His*: annealing lacks theory but applies generally [B020 pp. 8, 10], and later drives robust design [S141 p. 4], tree search [H002 p. 6] and recalibration [S103 pp. 8–9]. *Powell's*: annealing is "a horrible method" [michael-powell card X001 p. 12], "very extravagant in their use of function evaluations" [michael-powell card X002 p. 4], deciding at random instead of exploiting precise values [michael-powell card S029 p. 2]. His objection is evaluation economy, not missing proofs: he holds that developers need not wait for theory [michael-powell card X001 p. 17]. Both surveyed the same convergence theory, and Powell adds a counterexample where asymmetric neighbourhoods mislead annealing [michael-powell card S014 pp. 38–42], against the review's symmetric neighbourhoods [S005 p. 1]. *Contested*: whether annealing earns its evaluations. Powell's lens leads on the smooth nominal f (michael-powell SKILL.md, Roundtable Card), which S141's simulator returns with its derivative [S141 pp. 2–3]; but S141 minimizes the worst case g globally, a maximum whose subdifferential, in the convex case, is the hull of the worst errors [S090 p. 16], so g is nonsmooth where they tie [inferred], and nonsmooth objectives and global search beyond multistart are blind spots on Powell's card. *Test*: on the two-dimensional robust polynomial whose global robust optimum S141 checks by mesh enumeration [S141 p. 7], both outer searches use S141's bad-neighbour ascents [S141 p. 3] as the common worst-case estimator, and every inner and outer call is counted; 30 robust-annealing runs against a model-based method started from 30 points; success means ending within a stated tolerance of the enumerated optimum; report the distribution of calls. A duel on the nominal f, where Powell's lens leads, can run beside it.

**Conn, with Vicente · robust optimization as bilevel DFO** (exchange in print, one way). *His*: gradient ascents estimate the worst errors and an SOCP gives the step, convergent only for convex costs with an exact oracle [S090 p. 5], gradients required [S090 p. 2]. *Theirs*: the robust counterpart as a bilevel problem with derivative-free trust regions at both levels [andrew-conn card S066 pp. 10–12, 16–17], inner accuracy tied to the outer radius [andrew-conn card S066 p. 8], existing theory reused [luis-nunes-vicente card S047 p. 3]. They run his two-dimensional example from his starts A and B, and both reach a local minimizer [andrew-conn card S066 p. 17; luis-nunes-vicente card S047 pp. 16–18]: "An astute reader will notice that there are no comparisons with competing methods — this is because such methods appear not to exist." [andrew-conn card S066 p. 19] *Exchange*: they cite and reuse his example; no card of his replies. *Contested*: not gradients: on the EM design the adjoint gives them at no extra cost [S090 pp. 2, 11], and Conn's lens would use them itself (andrew-conn SKILL.md, Roundtable Card), so the live dispute is controlled inner accuracy against estimated worst cases. Conn's lens also pushes back on heuristics with no convergence story (same card), and the nonconvex robust search has none [S090 pp. 5–6]; but their reuse of existing theory needs a unique lower-level solution and a C¹ reduced function, and their practical algorithm is not analysed [andrew-conn card S066 pp. 2–3, 10]. Where worst errors tie, both assumptions fail [S090 pp. 3–4, 16] [inferred], so neither side's practical method has a convergence result for the nonconvex robust case. *Test*: the head-to-head neither ran, on the shared two-dimensional example, counting simulator calls at both levels and scoring both final designs by enumerating the worst case on a fine mesh, the check S141 used [S141 p. 7]. If the 100-variable design is added [S090 pp. 9–12], withhold gradients from both methods, charge each finite-difference gradient n calls, and score both designs with one large budget of multistart gradient ascents inside the ball, the random-sampling score beside it, since random sampling is "not a good gauge" [S116 p. 13].

**Scheinberg · noisy simulation and stochastic gradients** (contrast; complement). *His*: booking limits improved by a stochastic gradient whose convergence conditions the paper says are not met [S066 pp. 6–7], checked against one exact optimum [S066 p. 13]. *Hers*: under an accuracy contract that holds with fixed probability, "a standard efficient unconstrained optimization method, such as a trust region method, can be applied, with very small modifications" [katya-scheinberg card S012 p. 2], with almost-sure convergence [katya-scheinberg card S012 pp. 14–22] and costs counted in evaluations [katya-scheinberg card S012 pp. 28–29]; finite-difference intervals set from the noise level [katya-scheinberg card S006 pp. 6–7]. Her co-author Menickelly [katya-scheinberg card S012 p. 1] later adopted his test problem with Wild (note 05 §4.6). *Contested*: the transfer to integers: revenue depends only on the integer part of the limits [S066 p. 6], and her lens leads on smooth objectives (katya-scheinberg SKILL.md, Roundtable Card). Her device for a step-like metric, AUC, is a smooth expectation under a data model the paper calls too strong for most practical data, with no convergence result for nonsmooth noisy functions [katya-scheinberg card S052 pp. 2–5]; it may not carry over, since here the expected revenue itself depends only on the integer part [S066 p. 6] and so stays piecewise constant [inferred]. *Test*: on the single-leg instance with a known DP optimum [S066 p. 13], his method against a probabilistic trust region on rounded continuous limits, common random numbers, equal simulated request streams; report each gap with its confidence interval.

**Scheinberg · a DFO trust region for a coordinate-search heuristic** (exchange in print, one way; complement). *His*: an ML paper, carded ML + RO, alternates learner fits with a cyclic coordinate search on the imputation values µ in steps of ±σj [S391 pp. 8–9], called "a heuristic, local-search process that could be improved in future work" [S391 p. 8]; it names nonconvex optimization of µ as future work, citing Ghanbari and Scheinberg (2017) [S391 p. 8]. *Hers*: on her cards that work is model-based DFO-TR for black-box ML, one new value per iteration, with a subsampled variant [katya-scheinberg card S052 pp. 3–4, 7] [inferred from authors and year]. *Exchange*: his paper cites her work for the step it leaves open; no card of hers replies. *Contested*: whether the loss as a function of µ, each evaluation a learner refit, is smooth enough for her models [inferred]; S052 justifies a step-like metric only through a smooth expectation [katya-scheinberg card S052 pp. 2–5]. *Test*: on S391's data sets, DFO-TR on µ against the coordinate search at equal numbers of model fits, held-out error reported.

**Vicente · what a heuristic may claim** (contrast; conflict). *His*: robust annealing as a stand-alone global method, its asymptotic theorem offered for runs stopped at an empirical threshold [S141 pp. 5, 9, 11]. *Vicente's*: keep the heuristic, add the guarantee (luis-nunes-vicente SKILL.md, Roundtable Card): "It is the poll step that guarantees the global convergence of the pattern search method." [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 p. 6], with stationarity as the guarantee [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 p. 26]; "A fair comparison among different solvers should be based on the number of function evaluations" [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 p. 16]. *Contested*: what a stopped run may claim: a global robust optimum for S141; only stationarity for PSwarm, also from a limit theorem, with termination conditions it concedes are hard to verify and a code that skips the mesh projection [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 pp. 10–11, 13, 15, 26]. His 1992 review, which called time asymptotics "largely irrelevant" [B020 p. 6; S005 p. 3], gives yardsticks for both: hitting times over families of instances and duels at equal computing time [B020 pp. 6, 9; S005 pp. 3–4]. *Test*: on the two-dimensional robust polynomial whose global robust optimum S141 enumerates on a mesh [S141 p. 7], robust annealing stopped by S141's empirical threshold [S141 p. 11] against annealing as the search step of a coordinate poll, with a poll alone as third arm, as in PSwarm's ablation [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 pp. 26–27]; 30 runs each [luis-nunes-vicente card S005 p. 18], a common inner worst-case estimator, equal call budgets; report the share of runs ending within a stated tolerance of the enumerated optimum, and the calls used.

**Audet · surrogates for black boxes** (contrast; conflict). *His*: learners chosen per function by held-out accuracy or R² [S266 pp. 7, 12; S259 p. 10]; black boxes claimed but untested, cheap evaluations and automatic differentiation assumed [S298 pp. 39–40; S266 pp. 32–33]. *Audet's*: "Our main concern in SBO is to find the correct optimizer, not necessarily the correct optimum." [charles-audet card S046 p. 6]; RMSE and PRESS can select a model with the wrong minimizer, so order error counts order and feasibility flips [charles-audet card S046 pp. 12–14]; surrogates sit in the Search [charles-audet card S046 p. 7], and "The poll rigorously ensures global convergence toward a solution satisfying some local optimality conditions" [charles-audet card S046 p. 5]. A 15-author comparison he co-signed (Fowler et al. 2008) brought direct search to a field that used genetic algorithms and annealing, because "there is no randomness in the method, and there are rigorous convergence results" [charles-audet card S022 pp. 1, 4]. *Contested*: for constraints both criteria count feasibility verdicts [inferred]; the dispute is objective surrogates and expensive, failing simulators. *Test*: rerun the 77-instance benchmark [S266 p. 24] with objective learners chosen by order error, and duel the method with NOMAD on a simulator with hidden constraints at equal call budgets.

**Audet · reusing past evaluations** (agreement). *His*: every evaluation kept; over 95% of the 110,000 neighbours behind the final worst-case estimate came from earlier iterations [S090 pp. 7, 12]. *Audet's*: Robust-MADS smooths noise with previously evaluated points instead of resampling [charles-audet card S055] (abstract level). *Contested*: robust means worst case for him, noise smoothing for Audet; his estimates can be overturned late [S090 p. 12]. *Test*: two measurements, each against the evaluations saved. On the deterministic EM design or the two-dimensional example, how often fresh neighbourhood ascents find a worse error than the cached worst case behind an accepted step [S090 pp. 7, 12]; on a noisy simulator, how often fresh evaluations reverse a Robust-MADS step.
